# Noncommutative geometry in particle physics: a dated record to November 2026

Written by Claude Opus 5.5 on 4 November 2026, to help decide which results of this project are new and which articles to write. Companions: [LITERATURE.md](LITERATURE.md) (targeted priority searches) and the author table at a.rivero.nom.es/research/ncactors.html (authors by year to 2003).

**Method.** Every identifier below was checked in this session against INSPIRE, the arXiv API or Crossref (curl and jq in bash). No Python was used; the author table was counted with awk in bash; no script is kept. Year = first public appearance; journal data follow.

**Labels.** [R] passages read in this session; [L] read earlier and recorded in LITERATURE.md (section given); [A] abstract read (and, where stated, the reference list held by INSPIRE or the publisher); [M] bibliographic data checked only. Statements about content rest on [R], [L] or [A]; the one-line descriptions of [M] entries are the standard ones and were not re-read.

## 1. Origins (to 1991) beside the Kaluza-Klein programme

### 1.1 The Kaluza-Klein programme, 1968-1987

- 1968. R. Kerner, Ann. Inst. H. Poincaré A 9 (1968) 143 [M]: non-Abelian Kaluza-Klein. Kerner later co-authors the matrix geometry of 1.3.
- 1979-1981. N. S. Manton, Nucl. Phys. B 158 (1979) 141; D. B. Fairlie, Phys. Lett. B 82 (1979) 97; P. Forgács and N. S. Manton, Commun. Math. Phys. 72 (1980) 15; G. Chapline and N. S. Manton, Nucl. Phys. B 184 (1981) 391 [M]: the Higgs as extra-dimensional components of a gauge field. Y. Ne'eman, Phys. Lett. B 81 (1979) 190 [M]: the SU(2/1) superconnection.
- 1981. E. Witten, *Search for a realistic Kaluza-Klein theory*, Nucl. Phys. B 186 (1981) 412 [A]: "It is possible to obtain an SU(3) × SU(2) × U(1) gauge group, but the proper fermion quantum numbers are difficult to achieve."
- 1982-1983. S. Randjbar-Daemi, A. Salam and J. Strathdee, Nucl. Phys. B 214 (1983) 491; C. Wetterich, Nucl. Phys. B 222 (1983) 20 [M]: elementary gauge backgrounds in six dimensions; reduction of Weyl spinors. Cited by Witten 1983 (refs. 13-14).
- 1983. E. Witten, *Fermion quantum numbers in Kaluza-Klein theory*, Shelter Island II (1985) 227, INSPIRE 193975 [R, preprint scan; L, "Sources read"]. Abstract: "In the absence of elementary gauge fields, a theorem by Atiyah and Hirzebruch states that the Dirac equation in 4+n dimensions always leads to vector-like fermion quantum numbers in four dimensions." Scope in the text: ground state M^4 × B with B compact; all gauge fields from isometries of the metric; the Dirac operator; for Rarita-Schwinger only homogeneous B ("a general impossibility theorem ... is not proved"); spin 5/2 has a counterexample. Escapes named there: "the character-valued index also need not vanish in the presence of elementary gauge fields" (pseudo-realistic models in its section VI), and "start with a unified theory (perhaps a supergravity or superstring theory) that determines the original gauge group". Its ref. 16 (Witten, J. Diff. Geom. 17 (1982) 661) first noted the theorem's relevance. Theorem: M. Atiyah and F. Hirzebruch, *Spin-manifolds and group actions*, in Essays on Topology and Related Topics (1970) 18, doi:10.1007/978-3-642-49197-9_3 [M].
- 1986. M. J. Duff, B. E. W. Nilsson and C. N. Pope, *Kaluza-Klein supergravity*, Phys. Rep. 130 (1986) 1 [opening sentence, quoted in the 2025 retrospective; full text not read]: "We do not yet know whether supergravity is a theory of the real world, nor whether the real world has more than four dimensions ... However, our research in this area has convinced us that the only way to do supergravity is via Kaluza-Klein and that the only viable Kaluza-Klein theory is supergravity." The bulk treats S^7.
- 1987. D. Bailin and A. Love, *Kaluza-Klein theories*, Rep. Prog. Phys. 50 (1987) 1087 [A]: the spectrum "with particular emphasis on the spectrum of light fermions", anomaly constraints "when explicit higher-dimensional gauge fields are present", cosmology, and "Finally, an introductory account of Kaluza-Klein supergravity is given leading towards superstring theory."
- 1987. T. Appelquist, A. Chodos and P. G. O. Freund (eds.), *Modern Kaluza-Klein Theories*, Addison-Wesley [M]. INSPIRE records reprints of Kerner 1968, Chapline-Manton 1983, Witten 1981, Witten 1983 and Randjbar-Daemi-Salam-Strathdee; the introduction (pp. 1-47) was not read.
- Retrospectives. M. J. Duff, *Kaluza-Klein theory in perspective*, arXiv:hep-th/9410046 [R]: "the sorry state of affairs until the September 1984 superstring revolution"; the heterotic string "eschews the route of getting the Yang-Mills gauge group from the general coordinate group"; "Does this mean that the idea of non-abelian gauge fields from extra dimensions is dead? Actually, no." M. J. Duff, B. E. W. Nilsson and C. N. Pope, *Kaluza-Klein supergravity 2025*, arXiv:2502.07710 [R]: the coset programme became "less and less tenable" for three stated reasons: the large negative cosmological constant, Witten's chirality result (cited as the 1981 paper), and the string models after Candelas-Horowitz-Strominger-Witten.

**Assessment of the view under test.** The texts read support it in part. Witten 1983 states the result with its hypotheses and names the escapes. The Bailin-Love abstract treats light fermions together with explicit higher-dimensional gauge fields, the escape route, and leads "towards superstring theory"; the heterotic string took that route. The retrospectives give chirality as one reason among three, and Duff 1994 denies that the idea is dead. We found no text of the period that treats the obstruction as closing the subject. The open-problem sections of the 1986 and 1987 reviews and the 1987 introduction remain to be read in print.

### 1.3 The first two-point and matrix models, 1988-1994

- Aug. 1988. J. Madore, *Kaluza-Klein aspects of noncommutative geometry*, Chester DGM proceedings (1989) 243, arXiv:1510.03915 [R]. "We can now generalize Kaluza-Klein theory by replacing C(F) by an associative algebra which is not necessarily commutative"; with M_n, "the internal laplacian has a finite spectrum and ... the total momentum space remains of dimension 4. This is important for the renormalizability of the theory."
- 1988-1990. M. Dubois-Violette, R. Kerner and J. Madore: Class. Quant. Grav. 6 (1989) 1709 [M]; Phys. Lett. B 217 (1989) 485 [A + INSPIRE references]; J. Math. Phys. 31 (1990) 316 [M]; J. Math. Phys. 31 (1990) 323 [A + publisher references]. Derivation calculus on C^∞(V) ⊗ M_n: Maxwell's theory gives U(n) Yang-Mills plus adjoint scalars with a quartic potential and a massive phase. J. Madore, Phys. Rev. D 41 (1990) 3709 [A]: Kaluza-Klein with a semisimple internal algebra.
- Mar. 1990. A. Connes and J. Lott, *Particle models and noncommutative geometry*, Nucl. Phys. B Proc. Suppl. 18B (1991) 29 [R, author copy]. GWS, Peccei-Quinn and the Standard Model as pure Yang-Mills on M × {two points}; the Higgs is the connection along the discrete direction; Section VI gives m_H = √2 m_t (top-dominance limit, pure Dixmier trace) and "it is impossible to get GUTs by our methods". Then *The metric aspect of NCG*, Cargèse 1991, 53-93 [M].
- June 1990. R. Coquereaux, G. Esposito-Farèse and G. Vaillant, *Higgs fields as Yang-Mills fields and discrete symmetries*, Nucl. Phys. B 353 (1991) 689 [A + INSPIRE references]: Higgs fields from "the gauging of discrete directions". Mainz-Marseille sequels: R. Coquereaux, R. Häussling, N. A. Papadopoulos and F. Scheck, Int. J. Mod. Phys. A 7 (1992) 2809; R. Coquereaux, G. Esposito-Farèse and F. Scheck, Int. J. Mod. Phys. A 7 (1992) 6555 [M]. Coquereaux came from Kaluza-Klein: R. Coquereaux and A. Jadczyk, World Sci. Lect. Notes Phys. 16 (1988) [M]. Y. Ne'eman and S. Sternberg, Proc. Nat. Acad. Sci. 87 (1990) 7875 [M]: superconnections.
- 1992-1994. A. Dimakis, F. Müller-Hoissen and T. Striker, J. Phys. A 26 (1993) 1927 [M]; A. Dimakis and F. Müller-Hoissen, J. Phys. A 27 (1994) 3159, arXiv:hep-th/9401149 [M], and J. Math. Phys. 35 (1994) 6703, arXiv:hep-th/9404112 [L, §A]: calculus on finite sets and lattices; Connes-Lott models as approximations of higher-dimensional gauge theory. A. Sitarz, J. Geom. Phys. 15 (1995) 123, arXiv:hep-th/9210098 [M]: gauge theory on discrete groups.

### 1.4 Did the founders present the discrete direction as a way around the chirality obstruction?

**None of the texts read does so, and none cites Witten 1981 or 1983.** The Kaluza-Klein reading is there from the start; the advantage claimed is finiteness; chirality enters by hand through the Z_2-grading of the finite Hilbert space.

- Connes-Lott 1990 [R]. The only Kaluza-Klein sentence (Section I): "The corresponding geometric space can be thought of as a spacetime of Kaluza-Klein type, where the fiber is formed of a two-point set." References: Connes (3), Dixmier, Ellis-Gaillard, Kasparov, Glashow, Peccei-Quinn, Salam, Weinberg (2), Wilczek.
- Connes 1988, CMP 117 [A]: INSPIRE's reference list cites Witten only for *Non-commutative geometry and string field theory*. The Essay was not accessible.
- Connes, *Noncommutative Geometry*, Academic Press 1994 (PDF, alainconnes.org) [R, full-text search]: "Kaluza" does not occur; Witten is cited once, for Morse theory. Chapter VI: "a double space-time", where "purely left-handed particles such as neutrinos live on the left-handed copy X_L".
- Madore 1988 [R]: Kaluza-Klein generalization motivated by the finite internal spectrum; spinors are built "in dimension 4 + n^2 − 1" with no discussion of chirality; no Witten among the references.
- Dubois-Violette-Kerner-Madore [A]: abstracts silent on Kaluza-Klein and chirality; the reference lists shown cite Manton 1979 and R. Kerner, L. Nikolova and V. Rizov, *A two-level Kaluza-Klein theory*, Lett. Math. Phys. 14 (1987) 333 [M], and no Witten.
- Coquereaux-Esposito-Farèse-Vaillant [A]: "The formalism presented here is therefore not a Kaluza-Klein-like approach to Yang-Mills theory, but is, in a sense, a discrete analog of it." INSPIRE's references cite Chapline-Manton 1981 and no Witten. Full text not read.

**Later Kaluza-Klein readings** (sources searched for "Witten", "chiral", "no-go"; none cites the obstruction):
- G. Landi, Nguyen Ai Viet and K. C. Wali, Phys. Lett. B 326 (1994) 45, arXiv:hep-th/9402046 [M]; Nguyen Ai Viet and K. C. Wali, Int. J. Mod. Phys. A 11 (1996) 533, arXiv:hep-th/9412220 [R], and 2403, arXiv:hep-th/9508032 [L, §A]. First "discretization of the fifth dimension"; the contrast drawn is the infinite tower and its inconsistent truncation (citing M. J. Duff, C. N. Pope and K. S. Stelle, Phys. Lett. B 223 (1989) 386 [M]).
- A. H. Chamseddine and A. Connes, arXiv:hep-th/9606001 [R, passage]: "our 'discrete Kaluza Klein' geometry M × F". T. Schücker, *Forces from Connes' geometry*, arXiv:hep-th/0111236, Lect. Notes Phys. 659 (2005) 285 [R, passage]: "Kaluza-Klein models whose fifth dimension is discrete". Martinetti-Wulkenhaar 2002 and Dai-Song 2001 [L, §A].
- Chamseddine-Connes, arXiv:0706.3690 [R]: "the advantages of both SO(10) and Kaluza-Klein unification, without paying the price of plethora of Higgs fields or the infinite tower of states". A. H. Chamseddine, arXiv:2511.05909 (2025) [R]: the mechanism is "different from Kaluza-Klein since it avoids a massive tower of states, and places the Higgs in the correct representation".

So the reading was made first by Madore (1988, matrix fibre) and Connes-Lott (1990, two-point fibre), called "a discrete analog" by Coquereaux-Esposito-Farèse-Vaillant (1990) and "a discretization of the fifth dimension" by Landi-Viet-Wali and Viet-Wali (1994). The nearest engagement with the obstruction's hypotheses that we found lies outside NCG: J. Baptista, JHEP 05 (2026) 008, arXiv:2506.09126 [L, §I] (non-Killing gauge fields with chiral couplings). Scope of this negative finding: the texts and reference lists above, plus INSPIRE full-text queries pairing "spectral triple" with "Atiyah-Hirzebruch" and "noncommutative geometry" with Witten's 1983 title (no NCG model-building paper among the hits).

## 8. Relevance to this project

**Kaluza-Klein reading of the two-point space; the Higgs as A_5.** By 1.4 the reading is as old as the subject (Madore 1988; Connes-Lott 1990; Coquereaux-Esposito-Farèse-Vaillant 1990), the "discretized fifth dimension" is Landi-Viet-Wali and Viet-Wali 1994, and the Higgs-as-connection idea descends from Manton, Fairlie and Forgács-Manton, which the matrix and Marseille papers cite. LITERATURE.md §A already quotes Connes-Lott §I. No founding text links the discrete fibre to the chirality obstruction, so a paper that states the obstruction in its scope and derives Connes-Lott from a chiral interval would make that link for the first time in print, within the searches of 1.4.

**Truncations and windows.** Section 5: truncation and convergence theorems exist for circles, tori, SU(2) and quantum groups, and collapse is treated by Farsi-Latrémolière; the chiral interval, whose minimal truncation is the algebra C ⊕ C, is absent (LITERATURE.md §A, §K).

**Real structure and KO-dimension.** J and the mod-8 table (Connes 1995); KO-6 for F (Connes; Barrett, same day in 2006); products (Dąbrowski-Dossena; Farnsworth; Ćaćić). We found no discussion of total dimension 9 or 11 as alternatives to 10 (LITERATURE.md §D).

**Number of generations.** Nothing in the Connes-Chamseddine line fixes it: *Why the Standard Model* says "the number of generations is still an input", and the 2007 PRL lists it among the "delicate points". The classification counts states per generation (2k^2 = 32); Krajewski diagrams and the Jureit-Stephan classification admit any multiplicity. The proposals located that derive three lie outside the spectral-triple axioms: the DFTUZ notes of 1999-2000 and the exceptional-Jordan line (Dubois-Violette 2016; Dubois-Violette-Todorov 2018; L. Boyle, J. Math. Phys. 67 (2026) 071701, arXiv:2006.16265 [M]). Whether these give three as a theorem was not checked.

**Lorentzian and boundary versions.** Lorentzian: Barrett 2007, Strohmaier, Franco-Eckstein, van den Dungen, Besnard-Bizi, twisted triples. Boundary: Chamseddine-Connes 2011 impose Π_−Ψ = 0 with a local chirality on the boundary of the manifold factor; Iochum-Levy, Forsyth-Goffeng-Mesland-Rennie and Bär-Ballmann give the mathematics. We found no spectral triple whose internal direction is a manifold with boundary (LITERATURE.md §I, lemma 22).

**Lines the project has not touched.** Twisted triples and grand symmetry; the Boyle-Farnsworth graded and Jordan reformulations; Besnard's algebraic background, which already contains σ and Z'_{B−L}; random NCG; quantum Riemannian geometry; propinquity beyond the collapse paper; fuzzy extra dimensions (Aschieri-Madore-Manousselis-Zoupanos), which give finite internal spaces with truncated towers and should be read before any claim about truncated Kaluza-Klein towers; Moyal field theory; the number-theoretic and solid-state ramifications.
