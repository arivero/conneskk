Explicit masses and the chiral limit in odd reductions of almost-commutative geometries

Draft, 3 November 2026

Abstract

A finite KO-6 triple with a grading-reversing involution factors as an odd triple times a point. We show that on the physical subspace the fermion action of the product with spacetime is exactly the action of the odd triple on an unconstrained spinor, with the Yukawa block becoming the constant matrix dd in DM+γ5dD_M+\gamma_5d. A paired sector gives one Dirac action of explicit mass for either sign of the involution, up to a change of Grassmann variables, and on a subalgebra commuting with DFD_F and the involution no scalar fluctuation remains on the kept mass blocks. At zero quark mass the symmetry of the colour-only quark sector is U(Nf)×U(Nf)U(N_f)\times U(N_f). The admissible involutions are the Σ∈U(Nf)\Sigma\in U(N_f) with Σ*M\Sigma^*M Hermitian, which are the stationary points of the mass term, and at M=0M=0 they form a homogeneous space of the chiral group. All of this is kinematic. A spacetime-dependent reduction of the fixed operator leaves the spectral action unchanged, so that a pion kinetic term needs another operator or a quantum effective action, and the condensate needs a measure on gauge fields, infinite volume and an order of limits.

1 Setting

The noncommutative Standard Model has a finite factor of KO-dimension 6 (Connes 2006; Barrett 2007; Chamseddine, Connes, and Marcolli 2007). Reference (Dąbrowski and Dossena 2011) treats products of real triples. A companion paper 1 studies when a KO-6 triple factors as an odd triple times a Clifford point, with formal KO-labels 4+5=94+5=9 and 4+7=114+7=11. Here we ask how the Yukawa coupling reads in the odd triple, and what the data say about the chiral limit. The labels are formal: the metric dimension of the product is 4, and the label 9 exists only for sectors with an involution of sign s=−1s=-1. Charged paired sectors have one. The strict light spectrum with one or three unpaired neutrinos has none, since its H0H_0 has odd dimension.

3 The chiral limit

Consider NfN_f flavours, qL,qR∈ℂNf⊗ℂ3q_L,q_R\in\mathbb C^{N_f}\otimes\mathbb C^3, with DF=(0MM*0)⊗13D_F=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&M\\M^*&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\otimes1_3 on (qL,qR)(q_L,q_R) and the conjugate on antiparticles.

Proposition 2. The unitaries on this sector commuting with both colour actions, γF\gamma_F, JFJ_F and DFD_F are the pairs (VL,VR)∈U(Nf)2(V_L,V_R)\in U(N_f)^2 with VLM=MVRV_LM=MV_R. So G(0)=U(Nf)×U(Nf)G(0)=U(N_f)\times U(N_f), G(mI)=U(Nf)VG(mI)=U(N_f)_V, and G(M)=U(1)VNfG(M)=U(1)^{N_f}_V for positive distinct masses. With the electric charge, G(0)=U(1)4G(0)=U(1)^4 for two differently charged flavours.

This holds iff Σ\Sigma is a stationary point of −Retr(MΣ*)-\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{tr}(M\Sigma^*). The reduced mass on (qL,q‾R)(q_L,\bar q_R) is ΣM*⊕sΣ*M¯\Sigma M^*\oplus s\,\overline{\Sigma^*M}, tensored with 131_3. At M=0M=0 the set is all of U(Nf)U(N_f), a homogeneous space of G(0)G(0) under Σ↦VLΣVR*\Sigma\mapsto V_L\Sigma V_R^*, with stabilizer U(Nf)VU(N_f)_V at Σ=1\Sigma=1. At M=mIM=mI it is {Σ=Σ*,Σ2=1}\{\Sigma=\Sigma^*,\Sigma^2=1\}; for positive distinct masses it is the 2Nf2^{N_f} sign matrices in a mass eigenbasis; for invertible MM, the polar part is the unique global minimum. With the electric charge, the set is the intersection with the commutant of the charge, with Σ*M\Sigma^*M still Hermitian: for u,du,d this leaves diagonal phases at M=0M=0, and signs for positive nonzero diagonal masses.

Proof. Commuting with both colour actions and reversing the grading gives the form, and UJF=sJFUUJ_F=sJ_FU fixes the rest. Commuting with DFD_F is ΣM*=MΣ*\Sigma M^*=M\Sigma^* and Σ*M=M*Σ\Sigma^*M=M^*\Sigma, which are equivalent. Along ΣeitX\Sigma e^{itX} the derivative of Retr(MΣ*)\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{tr}(M\Sigma^*) is Imtr(Σ*MX)\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma^*MX). The minimum is von Neumann’s trace inequality. ◻

The homogeneous space at M=0M=0 has the same stabilizer as in the symmetry breaking of QCD. The mass condition says that Σ*M\Sigma^*M is Hermitian, and it captures all stationary points, including saddles and maxima. Vacuum alignment (Dashen 1971) asks which of them minimizes a physical energy (Theorems 1 and 2 there give at least a local minimum, and a global one is left as a conjecture), and these data contain no such energy. So the theorem gives the shape of the set of reductions, and a map to a quantum order parameter is missing. The anomalous singlet direction also separates the classical U(Nf)U(N_f) from the pion manifold. Choosing another reduction of a fixed even triple changes a sign in the reduced block, and differs from a physical mass-sign change, for which the determinants at ±m\pm m differ by (−1)n0(-1)^{n_0}.

Proposition 3. Let U(x)U(x) be pointwise admissible, cμ=[DM,xμ]c^\mu=[D_M,x^\mu] and Bμ=(U∂μU)|H0B_\mu=(U\partial_\mu U)|_{H_0}. This points to a nonzero mm with a new operator, for instance the Higgs-type inner fluctuation Φ=mΣ(x)\Phi=m\Sigma(x) of an even triple whose algebra contains flavour algebras on LL and RR, with accompanying flavour gauge fields, or to a quantum effective action.

4 The condensate

Proposition 4. Let DM(A)D_M(A) have eigenvalues λn\lambda_n, with n0n_0 zero modes, on volume VV, and let cV(m;A)=V−1∑nm/(λn2+m2)c_V(m;A)=V^{-1}\sum_nm/(\lambda_n^2+m^2), for m>0m>0, regularized compatibly with the pairing ±λ\pm\lambda by a subtraction whose finite remainder is O(m)O(m), for example odd and analytic in mm. At fixed AA on compact MM, cV=n0/(Vm)+O(m)c_V=n_0/(Vm)+O(m) as m→0m\to0. If ρm(λ)=lim⁡V⟨V−1∑nδ(λ−λn)⟩m\rho_m(\lambda)=\lim_V\langle V^{-1}\sum_n\delta(\lambda-\lambda_n)\rangle_m is equicontinuous at 00 with ρm(0)→ρ0(0)\rho_m(0)\to\rho_0(0), with tails and subtraction controlled uniformly in mm and the infinite-volume limit taken inside the integral, then lim⁡m→0lim⁡V⟨cV⟩=πρ0(0)\lim_{m\to0}\lim_V\langle c_V\rangle=\pi\rho_0(0), the form of the Banks-Casher relation as stated in (Giusti and Lüscher 2009), Section 2; the original gives the integral and the condition ρ→const\rho\to\mathrm{const} at fixed AA, without the factor π\pi or an order of limits with the volume ((Banks and Casher 1980), eqs. (49), (50)); the hypotheses here are ours, and the sign and flavour normalization of a condensate depend on convention.

So the spectral triple supplies the operator DM+γ5mD_M+\gamma_5m, the density of each fixed operator, and the continuity of that operator and its square in mm at fixed background; the regularized function itself has the zero-mode pole above. The averaged density, the regulated determinant or Pfaffian measure, the sequence of volumes and the order of limits need further input. A proof of existence of QCD with fermions would supply a measure, and would not by itself give ρ0(0)>0\rho_0(0)>0. With the anti-self-adjoint D̃=−iDM\tilde D=-iD_M, det⁡(D̃+m)=mn0∏λ>0(λ2+m2)>0\det(\tilde D+m)=m^{n_0}\prod_{\lambda>0}(\lambda^2+m^2)>0 with a compatible regulator, which is part of the method of (Vafa and Witten 1984).

5 The anomaly on the reduced action

On the commuting subalgebra, let d=diag(mf)d=\mathrm{diag}(m_f) and let TT be a Hermitian flavour matrix with eigenvalues τf\tau_f, commuting with dd and with the connection. Then 𝒟2=DM2+d2\mathcal D^2=D_M^2+d^2.

Proposition 6. Let Nf=2N_f=2, M=0M=0, with colour background zero on the round S4S^4, and let Σ:S4→SU(2)\Sigma:S^4\to SU(2) generate π4(SU(2))=ℤ2\pi_4(SU(2))=\mathbb Z_2. This is a relative sign for that transport and endpoint identification; the pullback of the measure through RΣR_\Sigma keeps the value of the integral. It is the sign of the two-flavour discrete term (−1)Nc[σ](-1)^{N_c[\sigma]} (Lee, Ohmori, and Tachikawa 2021), and matching a pion field needs a sigma model and its anomaly-matching assumptions. By footnote 2 of (Witten 1982), the sign is the parity of the zero modes of a five-dimensional Dirac operator on the nontrivial SU(2)SU(2) bundle on S5S^5.

6 The chiral limit on an interval

In the scalar-algebra realization on a chiral interval, 𝒟=DM+γ5(D0+mΓ0)\mathcal D=D_M+\gamma_5(D_0+m\Gamma_0). The limit m→0m\to0 has two routes. With a bulk mass −μ-\mu, μl>1\mu l>1 and tanh⁡(κ0l)=κ0/μ\tanh(\kappa_0l)=\kappa_0/\mu, the mass m=μsech(κ0l)m=\mu\,\mathrm{sech}(\kappa_0l) is positive at finite parameters, with m∼2μe−μlm\sim2\mu e^{-\mu l} for μl→∞\mu l\to\infty, and at fixed μ\mu it tends to 00 as l→∞l\to\infty: an exponentially small residue, with the localization and overlap mechanism of domain-wall fermions (Kaplan 1992; Shamir 1993). With a boundary Yukawa matrix, X=0X=0 gives exact zero modes, and for flavour-degenerate bulk masses XX preserves exactly {(VQ,VU):VQX=XVU}\{(V_Q,V_U):V_QX=XV_U\}, so that XX is a spurion on the wall and the symmetry is exact at finite ll when X=0X=0. This boundary-coupled operator is a different construction from the tensor-product truncation above, and identifying them at finite ll would need a proof.
