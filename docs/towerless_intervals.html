Finite spectral triples as towerless intervals

Draft, 3 November 2026

Abstract

We test the reading of the finite factor of almost-commutative spectral triples as the remnant of a fifth dimension with its Kaluza-Klein tower removed. The Connes-Lott two-point space is exactly the minimal spectral truncation of a chiral interval of length ll, with mass π/(2l)\pi/(2l), and with a bulk mass the interval carries a light pair that truncates to Connes-Lott at any length. As l→∞l\to\infty at fixed bulk mass, the pair becomes chiral wall modes and a one-dimensional Weyl law appears above the gap. As l→0l\to0, a light sector of fixed mass survives along a tuned bulk mass or along a scaled boundary coupling; the latter gives the Dirac Yukawa block ΦQŶΦU\Phi_Q\hat Y\Phi_U. The counts 9 and 11 are KO-degrees of the two odd factorizations of a KO-6 triple. In the reduced Standard Model representation, charged paired sectors admit both, one or three unpaired neutrinos admit only KO-degree 7, and the full chiral representation admits neither. We propose reading these as labels of limits, with the physical point in the even interior; that identification is not constructed here. For the heat cutoff, the Taylor mass of the W obeys a weighted sum rule whose small-mass form is the relation 8MW2=∑Nfmf28M_W^2=\sum N_fm_f^2 of the asymptotic spectral action: a top alone above a fixed cutoff gives MW∼Λ2/mt→0M_W\sim\Lambda^2/m_t\to0, and mt/MW→8/3m_t/M_W\to\sqrt{8/3} needs mt/Λ→0m_t/\Lambda\to0. At leading order, in the cases computed, matching the finite spectral action to the tower requires a tower-scale fermion. In the spectral propinquity, the finite limits of connected commutative metric triples are points carrying a finite Dirac block, while compressions at fixed truncation generate finite algebras whose structure is set by the symmetries of the spectral window. A two-sheet parent reproduces the algebra, bimodule and real structure of the Chamseddine-Connes classification, with stated inputs. A nonzero wall coupling on a chosen channel between the sheets gives a rank-one block whose commutation algebra is ℂ⊕ℍ⊕M3(ℂ)\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb H\oplus M_3(\mathbb C), with the fibres, the grading and the constant matrix multipliers as inputs.

1 Introduction

Between 1979 and 1987 the Kaluza-Klein programme sought the Standard Model in the geometry of extra dimensions. The Higgs field appeared as the internal components of a higher-dimensional gauge field (Manton 1979; Fairlie 1979; Forgács and Manton 1980; Chapline and Manton 1981), and Witten’s search for a realistic Kaluza-Klein theory found that SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1)SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1) can arise from isometries of the internal space while the fermion quantum numbers are difficult to achieve (Witten 1981). In 1983 he made the difficulty precise (Witten 1985): for a ground state M4×BM^4\times B with BB compact and all gauge fields coming from isometries of BB, a theorem of Atiyah and Hirzebruch makes the index of the internal Dirac operator vanish as a character of the isometry group, so the four-dimensional fermion quantum numbers are vectorlike. The result is true in that scope, and the same text names its escapes: elementary gauge fields in the higher dimension, and a unified theory, perhaps a supergravity or superstring theory, that fixes the gauge group. From 1984 attention moved to supersymmetry and superstrings, and the heterotic string took both escapes, with elementary gauge fields whose group the theory fixes. The reviews of 1986 and 1987 record that move and close the programme (Duff, Nilsson, and Pope 1986; Bailin and Love 1987); we read their open-problem sections only through abstracts and later retrospectives (Duff 1994; Duff, Nilsson, and Pope 2025), the second of which counts Witten’s chirality result as one of three reasons the coset programme lost ground, with the large negative cosmological constant and the string models.

Noncommutative geometry entered particle physics with a Kaluza-Klein reading from the start. In 1988 Madore generalized Kaluza-Klein theory by replacing the functions on the internal space with a matrix algebra, for which the internal Laplacian has a finite spectrum (Madore 1989). Connes and Lott wrote the Standard Model as a gauge theory on a spacetime “of Kaluza-Klein type, where the fiber is formed of a two-point set”, with the Higgs field as the connection along the discrete direction (Connes and Lott 1991). In its simplest form the fibre has algebra ℂ⊕ℂ\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C and Dirac operator mσ1m\sigma_1, and its two points lie at Connes distance 1/|m|1/|m|. Coquereaux, Esposito-Farèse and Vaillant called their gauging of discrete directions “in a sense, a discrete analog” of the Kaluza-Klein approach (Coquereaux, Esposito-Farèse, and Vaillant 1991), and in 1994 Viet and Wali discretized the fifth dimension (Viet and Wali 1996). In the texts and reference lists we read, none of these founding papers cites the chirality obstruction; the advantage they claim for the discrete direction is finiteness, and chirality is supplied by the grading of the finite Hilbert space.

The two-point space is the oldest layer of the present theory, which adds a real structure, a KO-dimension and a first-order condition (Connes 2006; Chamseddine, Connes, and Marcolli 2007; Chamseddine and Connes 2008); the finite space of the noncommutative Standard Model has KO-dimension 6, so that its product with four-dimensional spacetime has total KO-dimension 10≡2(mod8)10\equiv2\pmod 8 (Connes 2006; Barrett 2007; Chamseddine, Connes, and Marcolli 2007). We read the two-point space as the minimal spectral truncation of a chiral interval, whose walls carry the chirality that the obstruction excludes from a closed internal manifold, so that the obstruction and the discrete direction are two statements about the same limit. Within the searches recorded in the Supplemental Material, part H (the history of the field, Section 1.4), this link has not been made in print before.

KO-dimension and metric dimension differ: the finite space has metric dimension 0 and KO-dimension 6. So the numbers 9, 10 and 11 below are KO-degrees of factorizations, while the emergence or loss of metric dimension is measured by Weyl laws and requires a singular limit. We make the discrete-dimension reading precise and test it against a proposal suggested by the Kaluza-Klein picture: with massless weak bosons a dimension should open (10 to 11), with infinitely heavy weak bosons and top a dimension should close (10 to 9), and the physical point, with its finite Fermi constant and measured weak angle, lies between these limits. Below we give exact results for an interval model. The maps from KO signs to interval geometries, and from the length ll to the measured parameters, are part of the proposal and are not constructed here.

9 Discussion

What the towerless picture gives. The finite Dirac operator of an almost-commutative geometry behaves as the remnant of internal momentum on an interval: its excited levels combine quantized momenta with bulk masses, its light masses are set by localization and boundary couplings, its texture is localization data together with the matrix Ŷ\hat Y, and its chirality is carried by boundary conditions, with an ordinary index that is stable under boundary couplings and an equivariant index read on the stabilizer of the coupling. In the families treated, the emergence limit needs the tower, and the collapse limit keeps a finite light sector along a tuned bulk mass or a scaled coupling. In the reduced representation the labels 9 and 11 attach to sectors: charged paired sectors carry both, and an odd number of unpaired neutrinos carries only 11.

What remains open. The results are spectral and kinematic. The length, the bulk masses and the boundary data are free, and chirality is an input through boundary conditions, as in orbifold constructions. The results reach the Connes-Lott layer and the Yukawa block as compressions. Beyond that layer, Proposition H gives finite algebras at fixed truncation, with order zero and order one on a restricted doubled parent. Proposition I supplies an operator connecting the sheets in a parent with chosen fibres. The derivation of those fibres, the selection of a particular finite algebra, the KO-6 sign and the grading from a commutative parent, and any dynamical origin remain open. Proposition G decides the whole-triple limit in one metric. Proposition E.1 concerns one classical action at quadratic order about a prescribed background. It shows that, for a top alone, the heavy limit of the 10 to 9 proposal keeps the CCM ratio only as mt/Λ→0m_t/\Lambda\to0, hence jointly with Λ→∞\Lambda\to\infty; at fixed cutoff, with a light sector retained, the vectors end up light in the families treated. The link between the KO labels and these limits, and between ll and measured parameters, remains a proposal. A companion note (“The Finite Dirac Operator as a Boundary Condition on an Interval” 2026) carries Theorem C to the whole finite Dirac operator, as the relative position of two gradings at the walls, with the compression as an exact average, for a matrix-valued parent. There the sum rule of Proposition E has the coefficient (3−d)/24(3-d)/24, with dd the spectral dimension of the interval at the cutoff, so the relation 8MW2=∑Nfmf28M_W^2=\sum N_fm_f^2 is its value at d=0d=0.

No-go results and their escapes. Each negative statement above holds in a stated family, at a stated order, and we state it together with its hypotheses and known escapes, as the Coleman-Mandula theorem is stated together with supersymmetry. The list below collects them. We read them as guides to where to go next; none of them closes the question it bears on, and other escapes are possible.

  • No net chiral spectrum from an odd bulk: smooth closed product, standard reduction, exact isometries. Escapes: Boundaries and defects (Hořava and Witten 1996); massive non-Killing gauge fields (Baptista 2026); as our proposal, an even interior with odd limits.

  • No odd factorization: full chiral Standard Model representation, involution of Theorem D. Escapes: Reduced algebra and representation, with vectorlike sectors (Theorem D).

  • Only KO-7: scalar-character neutral sector, a singular value of odd multiplicity, zero included. Escapes: Even multiplicity; νR\nu_R with Dirac pairing (Theorem D).

  • Empty light sector in collapse: massless type-(c) interval, fixed spectral cutoff (Proposition A.1). Escapes: Type-(a) zero modes; tuned bulk mass (Theorem B); scaled boundary coupling (Theorem C).

  • Fixed compression not gauge covariant: local gauge transformations, fixed light projection (Proposition F). Escapes: A projection invariant under the transformation; covariant projection.

  • First order fails: two-point compression, inherited JJ (Theorem A). Escapes: Fluctuations without order one (Chamseddine, Connes, and Suijlekom 2013b); doubled parent (Proposition H).

  • Order zero fails: noncommutative AFA_F, J0J_0-invariant window, inherited pointwise reality (Proposition H). Escapes: Doubled or two-sheet parent; another opposite action.

  • No matching with bounded fermions: leading action, positive kinetic form, stated trace, common profiles (Proposition F). Escapes: Tower-scale fermion, which removes the bound, while full matching needs more; other order or action.

  • MW→0M_W\to0 at fixed cutoff: massless complete doublet, stated heavy family, Taylor mass, f(0)>0f(0)>0, classical quadratic action (Proposition E.1). Escapes: Joint limit Λ→∞\Lambda\to\infty; every doublet heavy; a doublet of fixed nonzero mass; f(0)=0f(0)=0; quantum effective action.

  • No finite nonzero radial vacuum: ray V=cV*V=cV_*, flat background, nonnegative cutoff of unbounded support, no added term (Proposition E.1). Escapes: Compact support (a plateau); sign-changing cutoffs; a potential or constraint; a compact radial direction on the interval, with its tower (“The Finite Dirac Operator as a Boundary Condition on an Interval” 2026).

  • Proposed Γ5\Gamma_5 not inherited: even observables, g=PγPg=P\gamma P, g2=1g^2=1, coordinates retained (Proposition H). Escapes: Graded prealgebra; added finite data with a restricted even algebra.

  • Scalar gluing selects no algebra: closed sets with empty interior, independent functions off them, unchanged separate windows (Proposition I). Escapes: Coupled window; boundary degrees of freedom; restricted multipliers; changed representation.

  • Finite limits are points with a Dirac block: connected scalar algebras, this propinquity class (Proposition G). Escapes: Truncation; matrix-valued parent algebras; other notions of convergence.
