Ten and twenty-six from volume-normalized curvature residues

OpenAI Codex

27 November 2026

Abstract

We give a geometric characterization of the dimensions in a unit-curvature criterion proposed by Rivero and in its supersymmetric extension. On every closed Riemannian manifold of dimension d>2d>2, the volume-normalized residue of the Hodge Laplacian on all forms has curvature coefficient −(d−2)/24-(d-2)/24. Subtracting the scalar-sector residue gives −(d−2)/8-(d-2)/8. Equality in magnitude with the scalar curvature measured by small geodesic balls holds at d=26d=26 and d=10d=10, respectively, whenever curvature is detectable. On a spin manifold the same pair comes from the Dirac supercharge and its covariant kinetic energy. The ratio of two unit-sphere areas supplies (d−2)/2(d-2)/2. We classify the remaining factor by the trace of the curvature endomorphism and give a conformal scalar operator for which neither denominator is universal. The geometric equalities require no worldsheet. Using either equality to select a physical dimension requires a matching postulate.

1 The coefficient fixed in 2004

How does spin change the propagation of a particle on a curved manifold? This question gives a way to vary the observable in the d=26d=26 proposal while keeping its unit of volume. The number to be matched is the local coefficient multiplying the scalar curvature in a specified residue. We vary the dimension d>2d>2, fix the metric normalization of the principal symbol, and use the same prescription in each dimension.

Four dimensions give a familiar geometric example. For a massless scalar with conformal coupling, the operator is the positive scalar Laplacian plus ξdR\xi_d R, where ξd=(d−2)/[4(d−1)]\xi_d=(d-2)/[4(d-1)]. Its first local heat coefficient after the volume term is (Physres1 research project 2026) (16−ξd)R=4−d12(d−1)R.\left(\tfrac16-\xi_d\right)R =\frac{4-d}{12(d-1)}\,R . (1) At d=4d=4 the geometric +R/6+R/6 from the heat kernel and the −ξ4R=−R/6-\xi_4 R=-R/6 from the conformal coupling cancel at every point, even on a round sphere. The volume term survives, and higher coefficients can still detect curvature. Four is distinguished here by a zero. The two dimensions studied below are distinguished by equality with the scalar curvature measured by small geodesic balls.

Let MdM^d be a closed Riemannian spin manifold. Write RR for its scalar curvature, positive on a round sphere, and DD for the self-adjoint Dirac operator of its Levi-Civita spin connection. On the classical triple (C∞(M),L2(M,S),D)(C^\infty(M),L^2(M,S),D), the normalized residue of |D|2−d\lvert D\rvert^{2-d} gives the Einstein–Hilbert integral. The 2004 note proposes that its dimensionless coefficient, with the Euclidean minus sign taken out, equal one. The note also leaves open what quantization would enforce that condition (Rivero 2004; Kalau and Walze 1995).

Here is the normalization explicitly. Let P=∇*∇+VP=\nabla^*\nabla+V be a nonnegative Laplace-type operator on a Hermitian bundle of complex rank rr, let ff be a smooth test function, and put p=d/2−1p=d/2-1. The identical prefactor at the volume pole z=d/2z=d/2 gives ∫Mfdvolg\int_M f\,d\mathrm{vol}_g, for every PP above. Equivalently, the residue in Eq. (2) is one half the Wodzicki residue of fP−pfP^{-p}, since PP has order two. Inserting ff tests the density even on metrics with zero total scalar curvature.

Write ZP(t;f)=(4πt)d/2Tr⁡(fe−tP)/rZ_P(t;f)=(4\pi t)^{d/2}\operatorname{Tr}(f e^{-tP})/r. The local heat expansion through its first curvature term gives (McKean and Singer 1967; Kalau and Walze 1995) ZP(t;f)=∫Mf[1+tbP+O(t2)]dvolg,bP=R/6−tr⁡(V)/r.\begin{aligned} Z_P(t;f)&=\int_M f\,[1+t b_P+O(t^2)]\,d\mathrm{vol}_g,\\ b_P&=R/6-\operatorname{tr}(V)/r . \end{aligned} (3) Taking the Mellin residue multiplies bPb_P by Γ(d/2)/Γ(p)=p\Gamma(d/2)/\Gamma(p)=p. (4) Unit matching gives d=26d=26. It fixes a dimension only when the coefficient is matched before introducing an adjustable gravitational coupling.

2 The spinning particle

Take the worldline supercharge Q=DQ=D and normalize its Hamiltonian by H=Q2H=Q^2, so the principal symbol is |ξ|2\lvert\xi\rvert^2. In even dimension QQ is odd under spinor chirality. For a graded realization in odd dimension one may double the spinors and put DD in the two off-diagonal blocks. Division by the fibre rank gives the same local calculation. Set K=∇S*∇SK=\nabla^{S*}\nabla^S, the positive kinetic operator built from that same connection. The supersymmetry algebra and the Lichnerowicz identity give H=K+14R𝟏S.H=K+\tfrac14 R\,\mathbf 1_S . (5) We propose to measure the gravitational response of this spin coupling by subtracting the kinetic residue. This uses one bundle, one volume trace and one Laplace symbol. KK is a reference Hamiltonian. A second physical particle with Hamiltonian KK is unnecessary.

Theorem 1. For the classical spin triple with the above connection and normalization, ℰd(H;f)−ℰd(K;f)=−d−28∫MfRdvolg.\mathcal E_d(H;f)-\mathcal E_d(K;f) =-\frac{d-2}{8}\int_M fR\,d\mathrm{vol}_g. (6) If this relative coefficient must equal −1-1 as an identity of local densities, its unique dimension is ten.

Proof. The two heat traces have the same volume and the same R/6R/6 term. Equation (5) leaves −R/4-R/4 in their difference. Multiplication by pp gives Eq. (6). ◻

There is also a direct symbol calculation. The resolvent identity puts one insertion of R/4R/4 between the resolvents of KK and HH. In the complex-power formula the leading symbol of the difference is therefore the derivative of x−px^{-p} at x=|ξ|2x=\lvert\xi\rvert^2 times that insertion. Symbol derivatives and further insertions have lower order. Thus H−p−K−pH^{-p}-K^{-p} has order at most −d-d, with symbol of degree −d-d equal to −pR|ξ|−d𝟏S/4-p R\lvert\xi\rvert^{-d}\mathbf 1_S/4. Integration on the unit cosphere in the volume normalization gives the same answer for nonconstant RR.

At d=10d=10 the spin and kinetic coefficients are −1/3-1/3 and +2/3+2/3. Their difference is −1-1. At d=26d=26 they are −1-1 and +2+2. These values specify which observable distinguishes the two dimensions. If the reference is allowed its own curvature coupling Kξ=K+ξRK_\xi=K+\xi R, its subtraction gives −p(1/4−ξ)∫fRdvolg-p(1/4-\xi)\int fR\,d\mathrm{vol}_g. This follows locally from Eq. (3) and also holds on a round sphere for the nonnegative examples ξ=0,1/6\xi=0,1/6. The latter choice returns the d=26d=26 coefficient. The proposed reference is fixed by using covariant kinetic energy alone.

3 Two traces of the de Rham particle

The supersymmetric quantum mechanics of differential forms gives another way to make the subtraction with a single Hamiltonian (Witten 1982). Its complex supercharges are the exterior derivative d\mathrm d and its adjoint d*\mathrm d^*. The real supercharge QH=d+d*Q_{\mathrm H}=\mathrm d+\mathrm d^* squares to the Hodge Laplacian Δ\Delta. Form degree kk counts occupied fermion modes. The degree-zero sector contains all scalar wavefunctions, including their excited eigenstates.

Proposition 1. The heat trace of Δ\Delta over all forms, divided by the fibre rank 2d2^d, minus the scalar heat trace has vanishing leading volume term and local curvature coefficient −R/4-R/4. Its volume-normalized Mellin residue therefore gives Eq. (6).

Proof. On kk-forms write Δk=∇*∇+Vk\Delta_k=\nabla^*\nabla+V_k. The fibre trace of VkV_k is an orthogonal invariant linear in curvature, hence a multiple of RR. On a metric of constant sectional curvature κ\kappa, the Weitzenböck endomorphism is k(d−k)κ𝟏k(d-k)\kappa\,\mathbf 1. Each of the kk indices pairs with d−kd-k complementary directions in the curvature action. Since R=d(d−1)κR=d(d-1)\kappa, for every metric tr⁡Vk(dk)=k(d−k)d(d−1)R.\frac{\operatorname{tr} V_k}{\binom{d}{k}} =\frac{k(d-k)}{d(d-1)}\,R . (7) Under the ordinary trace each of the 2d2^d occupation states has equal weight. An ordered pair of distinct modes is occupied and empty, respectively, with probability 1/41/4. Hence the mean of k(d−k)k(d-k) is d(d−1)/4d(d-1)/4. The all-form trace has coefficient R/6−R/4=−R/12R/6-R/4=-R/12, whereas k=0k=0 has R/6R/6. ◻

The dependence on the choice of trace can be computed. Weight each state by yky^k, y>0y>0, and divide by (1+y)d(1+y)^d to preserve the volume. Each mode is occupied with probability y/(1+y)y/(1+y). After subtracting the scalar sector the magnitude of the curvature coefficient is Cd(y)=d−22y(1+y)2≤d−28.C_d(y)=\frac{d-2}{2}\frac{y}{(1+y)^2} \ \leq\ \frac{d-2}{8}. (8) Within these positive factorized weights, invariance of the state weights under Hodge duality k↔d−kk\leftrightarrow d-k fixes y=1y=1. Ten is the lowest dimension in this family that can reach unit magnitude, and only the ordinary trace reaches it at ten.

The supersymmetric index measures a different quantity. In the alternating trace Tr⁡((−1)ke−tΔ)\operatorname{Tr}((-1)^k e^{-t\Delta}), QHQ_{\mathrm H} pairs the two parities at every positive eigenvalue. The global trace equals the Euler characteristic for all tt (Witten 1982). It supplies no integrated curvature residue at p>0p>0. The degree-zero projector used in Proposition 1 commutes with Δ\Delta and generally fails to commute with QHQ_{\mathrm H}. The postulate specifies which spectral sectors the gravitational observable compares.

4 A volume comparison in every dimension

Both factors in the calculation have a geometric meaning. Write ωd=Vol(B1d)\omega_d=\mathrm{Vol}(B^d_1) and sj=Vol(S1j)s_j=\mathrm{Vol}(S^j_1) for the Euclidean unit ball and sphere volumes. The elementary recursion ωd=(2π/d)ωd−2\omega_d=(2\pi/d)\omega_{d-2}, or the Gaussian integral for sjs_j, gives πsd−3sd−1=Γ(d/2)Γ((d−2)/2)=d−22.\pi\,\frac{s_{d-3}}{s_{d-1}} =\frac{\Gamma(d/2)}{\Gamma((d-2)/2)} =\frac{d-2}{2}. (9) It is the ratio that converts the first curvature heat coefficient to a residue when the leading residue measures Riemannian volume. For the curvature itself there is a measurement using only distances and volumes. At any x∈Mx\in M set Rball(x)=limb↓06(d+2)b2(1−Volg(Bg(x,b))ωdbd).R_{\mathrm{ball}}(x)= \lim_{b\downarrow0}\frac{6(d+2)}{b^2} \left(1-\frac{\mathrm{Vol}_g(B_g(x,b))}{\omega_d b^d}\right). (10) This limit equals R(x)R(x) (Gray and Vanhecke 1979). Indeed, in normal coordinates the volume density is 1−Ricij(x)uiuj/6+O(|u|3)1-\mathrm{Ric}_{ij}(x)u^iu^j/6+O(|u|^3). The average of uiuju^iu^j on a Euclidean ball of radius bb is δijb2/(d+2)\delta_{ij}b^2/(d+2). These two volume formulas fix the comparison without choosing a length scale.

Theorem 2. Let MdM^d be any smooth closed Riemannian manifold, d>2d>2. Let Δall\Delta_{\mathrm{all}} be the Hodge Laplacian on the full complex exterior algebra and Δ0\Delta_0 the scalar Laplacian. Use Eq. (2) on each bundle with its own rank. As identities for every smooth ff, ℰd(Δall;f)=−d−224∫MfRballdvolg,ℰd(Δall;f)−ℰd(Δ0;f)=−d−28∫MfRballdvolg.\begin{aligned} \mathcal E_d(\Delta_{\mathrm{all}};f) &=-\frac{d-2}{24}\int_M fR_{\mathrm{ball}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_g,\\ \mathcal E_d(\Delta_{\mathrm{all}};f)-\mathcal E_d(\Delta_0;f) &=-\frac{d-2}{8}\int_M fR_{\mathrm{ball}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_g. \end{aligned} (11) If RR is nonzero somewhere, equality of the negative of the first functional with ∫fRballdvolg\int fR_{\mathrm{ball}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_g for all ff holds exactly at d=26d=26. For the second functional it holds exactly at d=10d=10. The statements apply to nonorientable manifolds with the Riemannian volume density and the L2L^2 adjoint of the exterior derivative.

Proof. The orthogonal trace calculation in Proposition 1 is local and uses the exterior algebra of each tangent space. It gives −R/12-R/12 and −R/4-R/4 for the two heat densities. Multiply by the area ratio in Eq. (9) and insert Eq. (10). A nonnegative test function supported where RR has a strict sign then proves both necessity statements. ◻

This is one precise sense in which the dimensions are critical in pure geometry. The positive ratio of the negative spectral density to the ball curvature, wherever R≠0R\ne0, crosses one with slope 1/241/24 or 1/81/8 in the two dimension formulas. At the crossing two independently defined curvature measurements agree pointwise. Scalar-flat metrics give zero in all three measurements, and a single integral with f=1f=1 may lose the signal through sign cancellation. The comparison concerns the usual scalar curvature and the residue calibrated at the volume pole. Allowing an arbitrary prefactor for either observable would move the crossing. In d=2d=2 the power P0P^0 is the identity up to a smoothing projection and has zero Wodzicki residue. In d=1d=1 scalar curvature vanishes identically.

4.1 Which geometric operators have these denominators?

There is a necessary and sufficient local test. For any Laplace-type operator P=∇*∇+VP=\nabla^*\nabla+V put v=tr⁡V/rv=\operatorname{tr}V/r. Its ordinary residue density and its density relative to ∇*∇\nabla^*\nabla, with respect to Riemannian volume, are eord=p(R/6−v),erel=−pv.e_{\mathrm{ord}}=p(R/6-v),\qquad e_{\mathrm{rel}}=-p\,v . (12) Equation (3) proves both identities. If PP is only bounded below, discarding its finitely many nonpositive eigenvalues leaves these positive-pole residues unchanged. For a specified positive kk, the ordinary density is −(d−2)R/k-(d-2)R/k everywhere exactly when v=(1/6+2/k)Rv=(1/6+2/k)R. The relative density takes that value exactly when v=2R/kv=2R/k. These tests include points where R=0R=0. If only magnitudes are compared, one takes the absolute values in Eq. (12). In particular the signed pair k=24k=24 ordinary and k=8k=8 relative is equivalent to the single condition v=R/4v=R/4.

Every full spin Dirac operator coupled to a unitary auxiliary connection by Clifford multiplication passes this test. In its square the additional endomorphism is a sum of c(ei)c(ej)c(e_i)c(e_j) times the auxiliary curvature for i<ji<j. Each Clifford product has zero spin trace, so the rank mean of the endomorphism remains R/4R/4. The same argument holds locally for the full spinc^{c} Dirac operator with its determinant-line connection. It thus applies, for example, to the canonical spinc^{c} operator 2(∂‾+∂‾*)\sqrt{2}(\bar\partial+\bar\partial^*) on complex projective space with its Kähler metric in every complex dimension mm, with d=2md=2m. The real dimensions ten and twenty-six correspond to m=5m=5 and m=13m=13. The all-form version of Theorem 2 requires neither a spin nor a spinc^{c} structure.

Other canonical choices on the very same metric answer the family question differently. A minimal scalar has v=0v=0, ordinary coefficient +(d−2)/12+(d-2)/12, and zero relative coefficient. The conformal scalar Laplacian has v=(d−2)R/[4(d−1)]v=(d-2)R/[4(d-1)]. Substitution gives eordconf=−(d−2)(d−4)24(d−1)R,erelconf=−(d−2)28(d−1)R.\begin{aligned} e_{\mathrm{ord}}^{\mathrm{conf}} &= -\frac{(d-2)(d-4)}{24(d-1)}R ,\\ e_{\mathrm{rel}}^{\mathrm{conf}} &= -\frac{(d-2)^2}{8(d-1)}R . \end{aligned} (13) Neither is a dimension-independent 1/81/8 or 1/241/24 times −(d−2)R-(d-2)R. The ordinary expression carries the zero of Eq. (1) through volume normalization. Projection to a fixed form degree provides another geometric choice with vv given by Eq. (7). Thus membership in a family of manifolds alone cannot decide which denominator a probe measures.

4.2 Families available in every real dimension

For round SbdS^d_b and for a closed hyperbolic quotient of curvature −1/b2-1/b^2, the ball curvatures are +d(d−1)/b2+d(d-1)/b^2 and −d(d−1)/b2-d(d-1)/b^2, respectively. Closed hyperbolic examples exist in every dimension under consideration (Millson 1976). The two signs give opposite measured densities and the same two ratios. Real projective ℝℙbd\mathbb{RP}^d_b, with the antipodal quotient metric, has the same local densities as the sphere and half its integrated residues. This also tests the bundle hypothesis. With aa the degree-one mod-two generator, Tℝℙd⊕𝟏=(d+1)γT\mathbb{RP}^d\oplus\mathbf 1=(d+1)\gamma for the tautological line γ\gamma, so w(T)=(1+a)d+1w(T)=(1+a)^{d+1}. For d>2d>2 there are ordinary spinors only for d=3mod⁡4d=3\bmod 4. At both ten and twenty-six the form residues still realize the corresponding equalities.

An algebraic group family holds curvature fixed as dimension varies. Take Gd=SU(2)×Td−3G_d=SU(2)\times T^{d-3} with the round bi-invariant metric of radius bb on SU(2)SU(2) and flat torus factors. Then R=6/b2R=6/b^2 for every d≥3d\ge3. More generally, multiplying any closed seed with detectable scalar curvature by flat circles leaves its pointwise ball curvature fixed. For tests pulled back from the seed, divide integrated quantities by the flat volume. Adding one circle increases the two dimensionless coefficients in magnitude by exactly 1/241/24 and 1/81/8. A family starting at seed dimension mm realizes each crossing for which the corresponding dimension is at least mm.

Flat tori give zero readings in all dimensions. One can keep the topology and vary the metric. For g=e2ug0g=e^{2u}g_0 on TdT^d with g0g_0 flat, integration of the conformal scalar-curvature formula by parts gives ∫TdRgdvolg=(d−1)(d−2)∫Tde(d−2)u|∇0u|2dvol0.\int_{T^d} R_g\,d\mathrm{vol}_g =(d-1)(d-2)\int_{T^d}e^{(d-2)u} |\nabla_0 u|^2\,d\mathrm{vol}_0 . (14) Every smooth nonconstant uu makes this quantity positive and exposes both coefficients on the same torus. Nonflat scalar-flat examples also occur. A round two-sphere times a closed hyperbolic surface of the opposite sectional curvature, times Td−4T^{d-4}, has R=0R=0 for all d≥4d\ge4 although its sectional curvatures are nonzero. These metrics give a test of exactly what the first curvature residue detects.

5 Checks on curved products

An exact heat trace checks the sign and the normalization without using a local heat coefficient for forms. Take M=Sb2×Td−2M=S^2_b\times T^{d-2} with a round sphere of radius bb and a flat torus. Write Z0S(t)Z_0^S(t) and Z0T(t)Z_0^T(t) for their scalar heat traces including zero modes. Every nonconstant scalar spherical harmonic uu gives the one-forms du\mathrm du and *du*\mathrm du at the same eigenvalue. They span the one-form spectrum by Hodge decomposition and H1(S2)=0H^1(S^2)=0. Hodge duality identifies the two-form and scalar spectra. The sum over sphere form degrees is consequently 4Z0S−24Z_0^S-2. Each torus form component has the scalar spectrum, so factorization gives, for every t>0t>0, 2−dTr⁡(e−tΔM)−Tr⁡(e−tΔ0,M)=−12Z0T(t).2^{-d}\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-t\Delta_M})-\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-t\Delta_{0,M}}) =-\tfrac12 Z_0^T(t). (15) The right side starts with −Vol(Td−2)/[2(4πt)(d−2)/2]-\mathrm{Vol}(T^{d-2})/ [2(4\pi t)^{(d-2)/2}]. Since ∫Sb2Rdvolg=8π\int_{S^2_b}R\,d\mathrm{vol}_g=8\pi, this is precisely the −R/4-R/4 term in Eq. (3), independently of bb.

For a ten-to-nine comparison take Y=T4×SB5Y=T^4\times S^5_B and M=Y×Sρ1M=Y\times S^1_\rho, where BB and ρ\rho are radii. This is a smooth circle version of the round-sphere and interval example in the Kaluza–Klein construction (GPT-6.2-Astra max 2026). Use the periodic spin structure on the circle and a test function pulled back from YY. Both HH and KK split into the corresponding operator on YY plus the circle Laplacian. The restriction of the ten-dimensional spin bundle consists of two copies of the nine-dimensional spinors, so division by the respective ranks gives the same circle factor for both traces, Θρ(t)=∑m∈ℤe−tm2/ρ2.\Theta_\rho(t)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}e^{-tm^2/\rho^2}.(16) For fixed ρ>0\rho>0, Poisson summation gives Θρ(t)∼2πρ/4πt\Theta_\rho(t)\sim 2\pi\rho/\sqrt{4\pi t} as t→0t\to0. The ten-dimensional relative residue has density −R-R, with R=20/B2R=20/B^2. Taking the circle zero mode first gives Θρ→1\Theta_\rho\to1 as t/ρ2→∞t/\rho^2\to\infty. The resulting nine-dimensional operator has its own residue at p=7/2p=7/2, with density −7R/8-7R/8. Thus the unit condition applies to the resolved smooth parent in this example. Selecting a circle radius or a compactification path requires dynamics beyond the local coefficient.

6 Relation to the superstring

There is a published string calculation of the same local curvature difference. In the spectral action of Chamseddine, Eqs. (50)–(52) give equal leading volume terms for the Neveu–Schwarz and Ramond zero-mode Hamiltonians. With three-form torsion set to zero, their next densities carry +R/6+R/6 and −R/12-R/12, in the common factor used there. Equation (53) combines them in the NS-minus-R orientation and obtains +R/4+R/4 (Chamseddine 1997). That calculation has already set the target dimension to ten before the heat expansion. Theorem 1 starts with variable dd, uses the opposite orientation to retain the Euclidean sign of Eq. (4), and applies the unit criterion. Agreement concerns the curvature term. The full string action also carries oscillator, projection and ghost contributions.

For comparison the flat Ramond–Neveu–Schwarz string with no extra matter has, per chiral sector, one real scalar and one real Majorana fermion per target direction. Their central charges are 11 and 1/21/2. The reparametrization ghosts contribute −26-26 and the commuting superconformal ghosts contribute +11+11. Cancellation of the conformal anomaly therefore gives (Friedan, Martinec, and Shenker 1986) ctot=32d−26+11=0,d=10.c_{\mathrm{tot}}=\tfrac32 d-26+11=0,\qquad d=10 .(17) In the effective-string calculation the factor (d−2)/24(d-2)/24 also occurs in the vacuum energy of transverse oscillators on a cylinder (QCDString research project 2026). That observable is an energy shift. In the critical string, anomaly cancellation supplies the selection rule. In the commutative spectral triple, supersymmetry fixes R/4R/4, volume calibration fixes the normalization of the residue, and unit matching for the relative action supplies the remaining premise. A derivation from a quantized parent would have to fix the subtraction and its gravitational normalization independently.
