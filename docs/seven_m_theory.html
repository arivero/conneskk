An M-theory test for the seven-dimensional weak endpoint

Abstract

We test three realizations of seven internal dimensions with massless weak vectors. The Freund–Rubin solution on M111M^{111} fixes a membrane charge and radius. Its circle Fourier sectors give an explicit spinc^c family on the six-dimensional base, with a calculable mismatch to a quark doublet. The product S5×S2S^5\times S^2 instead has both a geometric weak group and the desired smooth projection to five. Its relative-radius scalar violates the AdS stability bound in the pure electric solution. A third route uses a Hořava–Witten interval and a heterotic Calabi–Yau compactification. An explicit free quotient with the standard embedding has nine 𝟐𝟕\mathbf{27} and six conjugate matter multiplets before further breaking, giving three net chiral families and their supersymmetric scalars. We compare their origin with the three-generation scalar charge counts in the orthogonal string construction. Equivariant charges, a finite real Dirac window and the survival of vectorlike fermions at five impose further, separately computable conditions on a common parent.

1 The higher-dimensional field equations

We take five internal dimensions with Dirac matter under colour and electromagnetism as the lower target. The middle may be a finite real spectral triple of KO-degree six. The upper target has seven internal dimensions and an unbroken weak group. Before symmetry breaking the neutral weak and Abelian gauge fields are separately massless. Naming a particular neutral combination ZZ requires a choice of charge generator and gauge kinetic normalization. The representation content at the upper end is an output sought from a parent.

In a geometric Kaluza–Klein reduction the weak vectors can be fluctuations along Killing fields. In a theory with boundaries, gauge transformations supported on a wall can also supply them. A choice between these origins changes the wavefunctions, the normalization of a four-dimensional current, and the central characters available to matter. We compare models by holding these data fixed within each calculation. We vary an integral flux, a fibre quotient, a metric ratio or a bundle as indicated. Crossing from one candidate to another is a change of microscopic model. A continuous path of solutions between them would require additional field equations and boundary data.

We will use three tests which have direct physical meanings. A Killing algebra or a wall gauge algebra gives the brackets of infinitesimal transformations. A charged state also needs an action of the global group and a normalizable kinetic term. A surviving particle at a lower endpoint further requires a spectral window in which its mass and its current matrix elements can be followed. The first calculation can succeed even when ordinary spinors all carry integer weak isospin. The second can succeed for an infinitely heavy wrapped object. The distinction is particularly sharp for the zero modes on a Calabi–Yau wall, where an index gives a difference between representations and conjugates while experiment at a finite cutoff counts both.

2.1 A spin doublet and the cost of a monopole

The sphere is spin and its rotation group has a double cover acting on spinors. The lowest ordinary Dirac eigenspaces on a round Sb2S^2_b are weak doublets with mass magnitude 1/b1/b. In the pure product they are genuine spin harmonics. One way to see the doublets is to restrict constant spinors in ℝ3\mathbb R^3 to the unit normals of the sphere. The spinorial Gauss formula gives Killing spinors with Killing constant of magnitude 1/(2b)1/(2b) and hence Dirac eigenvalues of magnitude 1/b1/b. The opposite sign is obtained by multiplying by the chirality operator. Positive scalar curvature excludes a harmonic untwisted spinor. These facts concern the ordinary spin Dirac operator. Flux terms and gauge constraints enter the physical eleven-dimensional gravitino equation, and a spin harmonic is by itself an insufficient assignment of a four-dimensional fermion.

The collapse experiment illustrates exactly what an extra monopole buys. Fix the product connection and twist the fibre spinor by a line of degree n>0n>0. On ℂℙ1\mathbb{CP}^1 the holomorphic spin half-canonical line is 𝒪(−1)\mathcal O(-1). The positive Dirac kernel is therefore H0(𝒪(n−1))H^0(\mathcal O(n-1)), of dimension nn, and the negative kernel is zero. Under the lifted fibre rotations the kernel is the irreducible representation of spin (n−1)/2(n-1)/2. At n=2n=2 it is a doublet. At n=3n=3 it is a triplet. In the latter case a choice of basis in a triplet does not give three copies of a weak singlet or three equivalent weak doublets. A generation index belongs to the commuting multiplicity space of an irreducible gauge representation.

On a direct product the squared, twisted spin operator is a sum of the squared operators on the factors. For variable bb the positive gap off the vertical kernel is of order 1/b1/b, whereas a fixed horizontal eigenvalue stays finite. At n=2n=2 the limiting horizontal spinor therefore carries a two-dimensional coefficient. Which symmetry acts on it depends on the lift that preserves the monopole. A charge on such a twist is an input to the matter covariant derivative. In the Freund–Rubin solution just described there is an external four-form and there is no elementary Maxwell one-form with this assigned first Chern number in the eleven-dimensional field list. A brane or gauge sector can change this conclusion only by supplying the charged operator, its background and the corresponding stress. The index theorem makes its conditional consequence testable.

2.2 The alternative homogeneous sphere bundle

There is also a nonproduct candidate over the same five-sphere. The transitive colour action gives S5=SU(3)/SU(2)S^5=SU(3)/SU(2). Consider an SU(3)SU(3)-equivariant sphere bundle with invariant round fibre metric. It is induced from an isometric action of the stabilizer SU(2)SU(2) on the fibre at one point. The trivial homomorphism gives the preceding product. The nontrivial one yields the homogeneous space SU(3)/HSU(3)/H with HH a circle in the stabilizing SU(2)SU(2).

Consequently the connected group of equivariant diffeomorphisms is a single circle. This leaves room for a commuting Abelian action. A full independent weak group commuting with this transitive colour action must have a different origin, such as a coefficient or a wall. The argument applies to these homogeneous lifts and does not assume a particular Einstein metric on the total space.

The product with a prescribed monopole, the homogeneous sphere bundle and the regular Freund–Rubin circle bundle below thus give different answers for the same proposed experiment on weak matter.
