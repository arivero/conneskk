An M-theory test for the seven-dimensional weak endpoint

GPT-6.2-Astra max

Draft, 18 November 2026

Abstract

Can a seven-dimensional parent with massless weak gauge vectors be the internal space of an M-theory compactification? We compare the five-sphere with two intervals to the Freund–Rubin solution on the regular circle bundle M111M^{111}. The first background is supported by a five-form in its own action. The second solves eleven-dimensional supergravity and has the required weak Lie algebra. Its quotient circle, the weak centre and a gauge field from the three-form give separate tests of electric charges. We normalize the harmonic two-form to one unit of wrapped membrane charge, compute the four-dimensional Maxwell coefficient, and bound the classical mass of a charged membrane. A free circle reduction produces a six-dimensional base with a spin obstruction to neutral fermion descent. Integral cohomology also distinguishes this total space from a smooth two-sphere bundle over the five-sphere. These results specify what an M-theory origin supplies and which transition and fermion data remain to be constructed.

1 The higher-dimensional field equations

We take five internal dimensions with Dirac matter under colour and electromagnetism as the lower target. The middle may be a finite real spectral triple of KO-degree six. The upper target has seven internal dimensions and an unbroken weak group. Before symmetry breaking the neutral weak and Abelian gauge fields are separately massless. Naming a particular neutral combination ZZ requires a choice of charge generator and gauge kinetic normalization. The representation content at the upper end is an output sought from a parent.

Eleven-dimensional supergravity gives a concrete test of the M-theory proposal at leading order in the eleven-dimensional Planck length ℓp\ell_p. Its bosonic fields are a metric and a three-form potential C3C_3 with field strength G4G_4. Warping, sources, additional flux components with external AdS curvature, and quantum corrections require different field equations or different solutions. A new bulk construction is needed to promote this interval example to an M-theory compactification.

2 A Freund–Rubin upper endpoint

The coset, its ordinary harmonics and its supermultiplets have been worked out explicitly (Witten 1981; Fabbri et al. 1999). The weak centre already constrains the answer. The central element −1-1 on the second sphere is trivial on the quotient. Its spin lift is also +1+1 for the primitive class (3,2)(3,2). To see the sign, follow the weak path diag(eit,e−it)\mathrm{diag}(e^{it},e^{-it}) to t=πt=\pi and compensate by the quotient phases. The closed ambient path has winding −1-1 in each of the three colour complex planes and windings 2,12,1 in the two weak planes. Their sum is zero. The radial normals and the quotient generator provide the three stable trivial directions, so the ambient spin sign descends to the tangent spin lift. Ordinary bulk tensors, spinors and tensor-spinors therefore carry integer weak isospin in this smooth compactification. The weak adjoint itself has precisely this parity. A doublet would require further degrees of freedom or a different lift. The full smooth harmonic spectrum is a known spectrum of this candidate. Its identification with the matter of the proposed upper endpoint remains open (Fabbri et al. 1999).

3 An Abelian vector and its membrane charge

4 A controlled circle reduction and its spinors

A nontrivial route from seven toward six uses the free Reeb circle. Quotient its fibres by ℤj\mathbb Z_j. The local Einstein equations and weak Killing fields persist. The principal circle bundle Yj→BY_j\to B now has Euler class j(3h+2k)j(3h+2k), unit-metric volume Vol⁡(Y)/j\operatorname{Vol}(Y)/j and physical fibre radius R11=L/(4j)R_{11}=L/(4j). Holding the quotient flux NN fixed replaces NN by NjNj in the formula for L6L^6 in Eq. (4). Reduction gives the two-form Kaluza–Klein flux on the base with integral class j(3h+2k)j(3h+2k).

Use ℓs2=ℓp3/R11\ell_s^2=\ell_p^3/R_{11} for the string length. The circle relation for the coupling is gs=(R11/ℓp)3/2g_s=(R_{11}/\ell_p)^{3/2} (Witten 1995). It gives gsg_s of order (N/j5)1/4(N/j^5)^{1/4} and L2/ℓs2L^2/\ell_s^2 of order (N/j)1/2(N/j)^{1/2}. For N1/5≪j≪NN^{1/5}\ll j\ll N there is a small circle and a weakly curved base in these units. This is a bosonic scale separation. Global spin data decide which fermions have neutral zero modes.

Proposition 2. YjY_j is spin precisely when jj is odd. For odd jj its spin structure is unique and the fibre rotation has a double-cover lift on ordinary spinors. It gives no invariant spinor for the infinitesimal circle action.

Proof. Stably TYj=π*TB⊕ℝTY_j=\pi^*TB\oplus\mathbb R, and w2(B)=hmod⁡2w_2(B)=h\bmod2. The degree-two mod-two Gysin sequence gives ker⁡π*=⟨j(3h+2k)mod⁡2⟩\ker\pi^*=\langle j(3h+2k)\bmod2\rangle. If the principal circle action lifted with its original period, reducing spin frames along the invariant unit vertical vector and taking the quotient would define a spin structure on BB. This contradicts w2(B)≠0w_2(B)\ne0. The lifted 2π2\pi turn therefore acts as −1-1 on spinors. Its weights in principal-circle units are half-integral. ◻

This explains why the bosonic limit alone cannot supply an ordinary neutral type-IIA gravitino or the fermions of a finite KO-six geometry. Charged spinc^c sectors, alternative circle actions or localized fields must be tracked with their respective operators. The spin calculation concerns circle weights. It does not give the eigenvalues of a flux-coupled Dirac or Rarita–Schwinger operator by itself.

5 What a route to five would have to change

For the unquotiented YY, multiplication by 3h+2k3h+2k from H2(B)H^2(B) to H4(B)H^4(B) has matrix (3023)\begin{pmatrix}3&0\\2&3\end{pmatrix}(7) in the bases (h,k)(h,k) and (h2,hk)(h^2,hk). The Gysin sequence gives H4(Y;ℤ)=ℤ/9H^4(Y;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/9. Every smooth S2S^2 bundle over S5S^5 has H4=0H^4=0, since the only nonzero entries in its integral cohomology spectral sequence have base degrees zero or five and fibre degrees zero or two. Thus this regular M111M^{111} cannot be the total space of a smooth sphere fibration over the round five-dimensional target. This statement leaves open a singular transition, a change of orbit structure, a dual description or a different upper space. For comparison, the distinct bundle SU(3)/U(1)1,0→S5SU(3)/U(1)_{1,0}\to S^5 in Part 53 has an actual sphere collapse with a specified monopole. Its bounded fermionic spectrum converges to a five-sphere Dirac operator on a rank-two bundle. In that construction the monopole charge and the origin of its weak multiplicities must be supplied.

A concrete next calculation is to choose an upper defect or gauge sector with a half-integral weak multiplet, specify its coupling to the Reeb and Betti vectors, and follow its normalizable modes through a degeneration. Its light spectrum should reproduce the charged currents at the middle and the vectorlike colour and electric charges at five. The smooth Freund–Rubin solution supplies genuine eleven-dimensional field equations, a quantized radius and massless weak vectors. Those facts, the spin calculation and the membrane bound give independently testable requirements for this proposed extension.
