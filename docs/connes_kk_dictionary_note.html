A dictionary between finite spectral triples and Kaluza-Klein data

Draft, 4 November 2026

Abstract

We collect, with the status of each, the statements that translate Connes’ finite spectral triple into Kaluza-Klein data. The grading of the two-point space is the reflection of an antiperiodic circle. The finite Dirac operator is a wall angle and a flat connection. The finite Higgs one-form is the compression of the gauge field along the internal direction, and the Higgs modulus is the angle between the wall gradings. The real structure decides whether a massless block has orthogonal or symplectic symmetry, which fixes the antisymmetric or symmetric diquark block. Noncommutative algebras arise as windows of commutative parents. The translation has limits, which we state: the finite data and the chirality remain inputs on the interval; the window algebras concern compression maps; no action in the flat family selects a path on which a dimension opens, and with the top alone it has no vacuum in the window. A reading through the heterotic and type I′' web fails in three specific ways: the angle-position map is nonlinear, the length of the interval is T-dual to the type I′' length, and the relevant configuration has a Romans mass, with no eleven-dimensional regime.

1 Introduction

Connes’ finite spectral triple packages the masses, the Higgs field and the gauge group of the Standard Model into finite-dimensional data. A Kaluza-Klein theory packages them into the geometry of internal dimensions. The proposal of (“Finite Spectral Triples as Towerless Intervals” 2026) is that the first is a towerless window of the second, with 9 and 11 as limits of an even interior. This note asks what the word “is” can mean here, entry by entry. We separate what is proved from what is a reading, and we say where the translation stops. It brings together the two companion notes (“The Finite Dirac Operator as a Boundary Condition on an Interval” 2026; “Finite Spectral Windows with an Opening Tower in Seven-Dimensional Parents” 2026) and a line of work on eleven-dimensional completions. It contains few new statements of its own; those are marked.

2 The grading is a reflection

Let Dl(L,R)=(R′,−L′)D_l(L,R)=(R',-L') on L2([0,l],ℂ2)L^2([0,l],\mathbb C^2), with R(0)=0R(0)=0 and L(l)=0L(l)=0, grading Γ=diag(1,−1)\Gamma=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1) and scalar functions on [0,l][0,l]: the chiral interval whose minimal truncation is the Connes-Lott two-point space (Connes and Lott 1991). Then (W(L,R))(x)=12(L(|x|)+isgn(x)R(|x|))(W(L,R))(x)=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\bigl(L(|x|)+i\,\mathrm{sgn}(x)R(|x|)\bigr)(1) is a unitary onto the antiperiodic functions on a circle of length 2l2l, with WDlW*=−id/dxWD_lW^*=-i\,d/dx, WΓW*W\Gamma W^* the reflection x→−xx\to-x, and the algebra going to the reflection-even functions (Supplemental Material, part 31). So the two sheets of the finite geometry are the two fixed points of a reflection, and the grading of the finite geometry is that reflection. The Connes-Lott mass is m=π/(2l)=1/(2R)m=\pi/(2l)=1/(2R), the lowest momentum of an antiperiodic spinor. The two-point distance 1/m1/m is a statement about this circle.

3 The finite Dirac operator is a boundary condition and a holonomy

Every DFD_F with the signs of KO-dimension six is exactly the lowest window of the pure derivative on the interval, for l∥DF∥<π/2l\|D_F\|<\pi/2, with the wall data X=tan⁡(lDF)X=\tan(lD_F), and with JFJ_F and γF\gamma_F exact. There is no bulk mass, and the masses are momenta. After a unitary change of domain, DFD_F is a constant connection, odd for the grading (“The Finite Dirac Operator as a Boundary Condition on an Interval” 2026). On the periodic circle of Supplemental Material, part 25 it is a Wilson line, and the angle θ=lσ\theta=l\sigma of a channel lives on the fundamental domain [0,π/2][0,\pi/2], whose ends are the periodic and the antiperiodic spectra (Supplemental Material, part 40).

The holonomy form has a consequence for dynamics. A flat connection cannot be seen by a local operator in the bulk. So the dependence of an action on DFD_F at large ll is nonlocal, of the Casimir kind, and it falls with ll; this is the point of Section 7.

4 The Higgs field is the gauge field along the interval

In Connes’ geometry the Higgs field comes from inner fluctuations ∑ai[DF,bi]\sum a_i[D_F,b_i]. In a Kaluza-Klein theory a scalar in the gauge sector comes from the component of a connection along the internal direction. That the Higgs is a connection in the discrete internal direction is the original reading of (Connes and Lott 1991). With a parent, it becomes a statement relating an operator on the interval to an operator on the window, in three precise senses.

First, for ff in the parent algebra, the compression QlQ_l to the window satisfies Ql([DX,f])=[DF,Ql(f)]Q_l([D_X,f])=[D_F,Q_l(f)] (Supplemental Material, part 22). More generally Ql(ET)=−l−1∫0lsin⁡(xadDF)(T(x))dxQ_l(ET)=-l^{-1}\int_0^l\sin(x\,\mathrm{ad}_{D_F})(T(x))\,dx, which is −l2[DF,T]-\tfrac l2[D_F,T] to leading order for constant TT. So a connection along the interval compresses to a finite one-form.

Second, a connection hh along the interval is equivalent to a rotation of the second wall: D(Γ0,Γ1)+Eh=uD(Γ0,w*Γ1w)u*D(\Gamma_0,\Gamma_1)+Eh=uD(\Gamma_0,w^*\Gamma_1w)u^*, with ww its holonomy (Supplemental Material, part 23). The fluctuations odd for the grading change the principal angles between the two wall gradings, which are the masses. So the Higgs modulus is the angle between the walls. For M2(ℍ)M_2(\mathbb H) the field space is S4S^4, and the modulus is half the geodesic distance between the points chosen by the walls. The even fluctuations, by contrast, leave all the singular values fixed (Supplemental Material, part 22, No-go theorem 22.7).

Third, which of these are physical is decided at the walls. With wall algebras of skew parts k0,klk_0,k_l and a five-dimensional Yang-Mills action, the massless scalars from the interval component are the orthogonal complement of k0+klk_0+k_l. A generator in k0∩klk_0\cap k_l has a massless vector and no massless scalar; a generator in the complement has a massless scalar and no massless vector; all others have neither (Supplemental Material, part 38). So in the parent of lemma 22, whose algebra is free at one wall, the interval component has no zero mode, and the Higgs is the wall datum XX. In the two-grading parent of lemma 23, it is a modulus. The rule “vector or scalar, never both” is the reason a gauge-Higgs scalar in a chosen representation forces the walls.

6 The algebra

A function algebra is commutative; a window of it need not be. Three exact examples, in increasing strength. The two lowest levels of the chiral interval give a four-dimensional operator system generating M2(ℂ)M_2(\mathbb C) on each chiral block (“Finite Spectral Triples as Towerless Intervals” 2026). The lowest Landau level of a torus with flux pp, doubled, has the signs of KO-dimension six and, with the unital datum Mp(ℂ)⊕ℂM_p(\mathbb C)\oplus\mathbb C of Supplemental Material, part 16, Corollary 16.8, window Mp(ℂ)M_p(\mathbb C), with the Landau gap ∼(p/A)1/2\sim(p/A)^{1/2} sending the tower to infinity as the area shrinks at fixed flux (Supplemental Material, part 40); at p=3p=3 this has the form of the colour sector, with the opposite algebra acting on the antiquarks. On the disc bundle of Λ−2T*S4\Lambda^2_-T^*S^4, with a Callias potential and a local wall, there are exactly eight light states, a copy of the 𝟒\mathbf 4 of Sp(2)Sp(2) near each end, and real functions compress to maps generating M2(ℍ)⊕M4(ℂ)M_2(\mathbb H)\oplus M_4(\mathbb C) (“Finite Spectral Windows with an Opening Tower in Seven-Dimensional Parents” 2026).

Two cautions come with these. Generated algebras need not converge when compression maps do: an unrestricted generated algebra can retain extra directions, and the quaternionic statement holds for bounded expressions, up to a defect (Supplemental Material, part 30). Second, order zero with the inherited JJ forces a commutative algebra under pointwise reality (Supplemental Material, part 16, Proposition 16.1), so a noncommutative window needs a doubling, a second sheet, or a loss of the inherited symmetry; on the disc bundle, the wall breaks charge conjugation, and this is what allows M4(ℂ)M_4(\mathbb C) there.

7 The massless limit and what selects the path

A path to MW=MZ=0M_W=M_Z=0 opens a dimension only if the first discarded level also falls, more slowly than the light masses, and the count at fixed energy diverges (“Finite Spectral Windows with an Opening Tower in Seven-Dimensional Parents” 2026). Such families exist on the interval and on the disc bundle, where the count has seven-dimensional growth. No action has selected one.

A one-loop potential with ll and the wall angles as its only scales has the form l−4G(θ)l^{-4}G(\theta). With θ\theta free the length runs away, and with θ=lm\theta=lm an isolated stationary angle fixes lmlm; a flat direction would select nothing (Supplemental Material, part 31). In the normalization of Supplemental Material, part 24, the angle-independent part is new here: 8π2l4V(0)=3ζ(5)16(4nf−3(ni−1516nh)−ns−5ng),8\pi^2l^4V(0)=\frac{3\zeta(5)}{16}\bigl(4n_f-3\bigl(n_i-\tfrac{15}{16}n_h\bigr)-n_s-5n_g\bigr),(3) with nfn_f twice the Dirac species, nin_i and nhn_h the gauge generators with integer and half-integer towers at θ=0\theta=0, nsn_s the real scalars with integer towers and ng=1n_g=1 with a five-dimensional graviton, assuming the bulk vacuum energy and the wall tensions tuned away (Supplemental Material, part 37). For three generations and 𝔰𝔭(2)⊕𝔰𝔲(4)\mathfrak{sp}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(4), with ni=16n_i=16 and nh=9n_h=9, the bracket is 192−22.7=169.3192-22.7=169.3, so at v=0v=0 the length runs to infinity, in the direction of the proposal.

With only the top at the wall angle, G>0G>0 on [0,π/2][0,\pi/2], and the length grows until the top leaves the window (Python). With a universal mass, GG changes sign and there is a stable point, at θ*=1.009\theta_*=1.009 for three generations and θ*=1.031\theta_*=1.031 for one (Python). These angles are of order one, so these vacua do not separate the window. A scale-free action can separate it, with a fixed small ratio, if the light mass is exponentially small in a stabilized shape parameter, as on the disc bundle; this is a separated window at each radius, not the parametric hierarchy of the joint limit, where the ratio tends to zero. A ratio that tends to zero needs a second scale, and a term in vv falling more slowly than m2/l2m^2/l^2. Since DFD_F is a holonomy, the flat family has exactly m2/l2m^2/l^2 at large ll, so that term must come from elsewhere, naturally a local bulk energy depending on the electroweak order parameter, which goes beyond DFD_F as a pure boundary condition.

8 What the string dualities give

The shape of the heterotic web, with a non-chiral theory in nine dimensions, chiral theories in ten and walls in eleven, suggests a dictionary: the two sheets as two orientifold walls, DFD_F as sixteen D8-brane positions, SO(30)×SO(2)SO(30)\times SO(2) as fifteen branes and one displaced for the top (“Finite Spectral Triples as Towerless Intervals” 2026; Polchinski and Witten 1996). In this web chirality distinguishes dimensions rather than groups: both heterotic strings are chiral in ten dimensions and are one non-chiral theory in nine, and the two groups differ by the open-string description of SO(32)SO(32) (Polchinski and Witten 1996) and the eleven-dimensional end of E8×E8E_8\times E_8 (Hořava and Witten 1996). At the level of free spectra the top channel is an SO(32)SO(32) Wilson-line channel, mt=a/Rm_t=a/R, which is a form of the Hosotani mechanism (Hosotani 1983). Against the sources, the rest needs three corrections (Supplemental Material, part 37).

First, the angle is nonlinear in the position: θk=π2W(xk)/W(L)\theta_k=\tfrac\pi2W(x_k)/W(L) with W=∫z−1/3dxW=\int z^{-1/3}dx, from the BPS masses (Polchinski and Witten 1996), so the dilaton is a trace of a function of |DF||D_F| only implicitly. Second, the tower is a winding tower of type I′', so the length ll and the type I′' length LpL_p are T-dual, with lLp=π2α′lL_p=\pi^2\alpha' at constant dilaton in the type I′' metric, and an extra factor λh1/2\lambda_h^{1/2} for the heterotic radius in the heterotic metric. The limit l→∞l\to\infty at fixed heterotic coupling shrinks the type I′' interval, and it ends at ten-dimensional SO(32)SO(32). Third, the configuration 15+115+1 has a Romans mass in the bulk. Its proper length is linear in the inverse coupling, so in the classical profile no region has both strong coupling and a length resolved in eleven-dimensional units, in line with the remark of (Bergman, Gaberdiel, and Lifschytz 1998) that only eight D8-branes with an orientifold plane are known to arise in M-theory.

The charged states massless at the far wall at infinite coupling are D0-branes (Bergman, Gaberdiel, and Lifschytz 1998), heterotic winding states: they are massless at the string-scale radius and grow heavier with RR, the opposite of l∼1/MWl\sim1/M_W. A neutral ZZ cannot be of this kind, since the rank is fixed. The dilaton gradient falls as 6/R26/R^2 along an opening path, and it can act as the second scale only if the coefficient in Eq. (3) vanishes. These are negative statements in the scope of that dictionary. They point to the other chambers, without the Romans mass, and to other completions.

One comparison with an eleven-dimensional geometry survives as an observation. Witten’s space with Killing spinors, M111M^{111}, is the hypercharge quotient of the 5‾\bar5 of SU(5)SU(5), since the weights 2,−32,-3 of its Kähler quotient are 6Y6Y on dcd^c and LL; the cone is Calabi-Yau because trY=0\mathrm{tr}\,Y=0, and 11=4+(2⋅5−3)11=4+(2\cdot5-3) (Supplemental Material, part 32, (Witten 1981; Fabbri et al. 1999)). The Z6Z_6 of the Standard Model is then the kernel of SU(3)×SU(2)SU(3)\times SU(2) on it, so no bosonic Kaluza-Klein mode is a triplet or a doublet. The weights are in (Fabbri et al. 1999) and the sources it builds on; a targeted search found no statement of the hypercharge reading, and that search was not exhaustive.

9 Where this leaves the question

Three things seem settled, in their scopes. The finite Dirac operator, grading and real structure are boundary data of a parent, exactly. The Higgs field is the interval component of a connection, seen in the window. Noncommutative finite algebras arise from commutative parents as limits of compression maps, and a local operator can produce the window.

Three things stay open, in order of consequence. The first is the vector sector on a seven-dimensional parent: nothing yet identifies the tunnelling scale with MWM_W, and the coupling g4−2=Vol/g2g_4^{-2}=\mathrm{Vol}/g^2 falls with the size. The second is the selection of the family, by a term in the action with a local dependence on the order parameter, or by a stabilized shape. The third is the part of the finite data that remains input (the number of generations among them; the obstructions to an exchange of the top with the neutrinos, and the four-family count, are collected in (“The Two Families of 84 and the Number of Generations” 2026)): on the interval the chirality comes from the choice of walls, and on the disc bundle the 𝟒\mathbf 4 is pseudoreal, so the chiral spectrum of the Standard Model is not obtained. Equivariant cobordism invariance of the index (Witten 1985) gives total index zero on the boundary, so a chiral spectrum needs another source: walls with data beyond the extension of a bundle, or fluxes on an even base.
