The real structure of a massless block: SO(30)SO(30) in the finite geometry

Draft, 4 November 2026

Abstract

The five light quarks in three colours with their conjugates span the vector of SO(30)SO(30), whose adjoint contains a colour antitriplet with the charge counts (6,6,3)(6,6,3) used in a composite reading of the antisquarks. We place this group in the finite geometry as the commutant of the real structure jj, with j2=+1j^2=+1, on the massless light block. Finite fluctuations compatible with the real structure have a symmetric block within one multiplet, with the counts (3,6,1)(3,6,1). The connection along the interval of the parent of the finite Dirac operator has the antisymmetric block, and its compression to the finite window is symmetric.

1 Introduction

A paper of 2024 (Rivero 2024) classifies the scalars of a supersymmetric Standard Model with three generations as composites in the adjoint of SO(32)SO(32). The vector of its SO(30)SO(30) is formed by the five light quarks u,c,d,s,bu,c,d,s,b in three colours with their conjugates, and the top stays apart. Under SU(5)×SU(3)SU(5)\times SU(3) it keeps the mesons in (24,1)(24,1) and the diquarks in (15,3‾)(15,\bar3) and its conjugate, and the antitriplet has the charge counts (−23,+13,+43):(6,6,3).\bigl(-\tfrac23,\,+\tfrac13,\,+\tfrac43\bigr):\qquad(6,\,6,\,3).(1) The counting goes back to (Rivero 2005), which finds neither SO(30)SO(30) nor a role for SO(32)SO(32) (Supplemental Material, part 21).

A reading of the LaTeX source of (Rivero 2024) (Supplemental Material, part 36, section 1) records the following. The paper reaches SO(30)SO(30) and SO(32)SO(32) from SU(16)×U(1)⊃SU(15)⊃SU(5)×SU(3)SU(16)\times U(1)\supset SU(15)\supset SU(5)\times SU(3) by adding singlets. Its postulates force the quark that does not bind to be up-type, and the top is favoured with the remark “we have not a concrete argument”. So the binding mechanism, the identity of the top as the quark that stays out, and the fermion partner of the three scalars of charge +4/3+4/3 are open there.

Independently, in the exact quadratic spectral action of Supplemental Material, part 18, among single charged species only an up-type quark can be removed while keeping cos2θNC=5/8\cos^2\theta_{\mathrm{NC}}=5/8 and the ratio 8/38/3 of the photon and gluon coefficients (Supplemental Material, part 21, for the classical bosonic trace with three generations). Removing the top leaves 30=(5,3)⊕(5‾,3‾)30=(5,3)\oplus(\bar5,\bar3), with Λ2(30)=(24,8)+(24,1)+(1,8)+(1,1)+(10,6)+(15,3‾)+conjugates of the last two,\begin{split} \Lambda^2(30)={}&(24,8)+(24,1)+(1,8)+(1,1)\\ &+(10,6)+(15,\bar3)+\text{conjugates of the last two}, \end{split}(2) and 496→(435,1)+(1,1)+(30,2)496\to(435,1)+(1,1)+(30,2) under SO(30)×SO(2)⊂SO(32)SO(30)\times SO(2)\subset SO(32). The two selections of an up-type quark are independent arguments with the same outcome (Supplemental Material, part 36).

We ask where SO(30)SO(30) lives in the finite geometry. Section 2 places it as the symmetry of the massless light block under the real structure for s=+1s=+1. Section 3 excludes the counts (1) from finite fluctuations within one multiplet. Sections 4 and 5 find them in the connection along the interval of the parent of the finite Dirac operator DFD_F, and (3,6,1)(3,6,1) in its compression to the window. Results carry the numbers of the parts that prove them.

3 JJ-compatible operators and gauge generators

Proposition 9.3 (Supplemental Material, part 36). Let JJ be an antiunitary on V⊕V‾V\oplus\bar V exchanging the summands, with J2=ϵJ^2=\epsilon, let XX be Hermitian with JX=ϵ′XJJX=\epsilon'XJ, and let B∈V⊗VB\in V\otimes V be its block from V‾\bar V to VV. So, within one multiplet VV and in the labels 9 to 11, the particle-antiparticle block of a finite Hermitian operator compatible with JJ, such as an inner fluctuation, cannot have the counts (1). This is the finite form of the symmetry of a Majorana mass matrix, and of the Fermi-statistics constraint on a Lorentz-scalar bilinear of one Weyl multiplet without derivatives (recalled in Supplemental Material, part 36). The known escapes are three: a connection along a circle or an interval, as in the last statement above; blocks between two distinct multiplets, which JJ pairs with their transposes and otherwise leaves free; and derivative couplings. The first is taken up next; the second is open (Section 7).

6 The charge-4/34/3 antitriplets

The counts rest on one premise: five light quarks generate every squark and slepton as a composite in the adjoint of SO(30)SO(30) (Supplemental Material, part 21), scalars that the finite geometry of Connes et al. does not contain. The premise fixes the top’s absence. For nun_u up-type and ndn_d down-type light flavours the symmetric diquark antitriplets number nd(nd+1)/2n_d(n_d+1)/2, nundn_un_d and nu(nu+1)/2n_u(n_u+1)/2 at charges −2/3-2/3, 1/31/3 and 4/34/3; three generations of antisquarks need 66 and 66, whose only solution is (nu,nd)=(2,3)(n_u,n_d)=(2,3), and the charge-4/34/3 count is then 33 (Supplemental Material, part 21, Proposition 21.4). With the top, (3,3)(3,3) gives (6,9,6)(6,9,6), and the top would need 36=30+636=30+6 real dimensions, while the plane that completes SO(30)SO(30) to SO(32)SO(32) is colour-neutral.

So the three antitriplets of charge 4/34/3 are half of a chiral-multiplet pairing: a Dirac quark of that charge needs two complex scalars per generation. Read as superpartners, their fermions are three left-handed Weyl singlets in (3‾,4/3)(\bar3,4/3), anomalous on their own, since the SU(3)3SU(3)^3, SU(3)2U(1)SU(3)^2U(1), U(1)3U(1)^3 and gravitational traces are −3-3, 22, 64/364/3 and 1212, and the meson sector, colour singlets of charges 0,±10,\pm1, cancels none of them for any chirality of its fermions (Supplemental Material, part 21, Corollary 21.5); the SU(3)3SU(3)^3 trace is odd for every chirality assignment of the three multiplets. Two readings survive the counting: the three scalars have no fermion partner, as the Higgs doublet has none in the Standard Model, so that supersymmetry is a property of the (6,6)(6,6) part alone and the exotics are diquark scalars; or the top is in the block, which completes the exotic and breaks the 1/31/3 count. In the window, the shadow (6) removes the same-flavour diquarks for distinct masses: the counts become (3,6,1)(3,6,1) in the first reading, with the one state u∧cu\wedge c of charge 4/34/3, and (3,9,3)(3,9,3) in the second, with ucuc, utut, ctct; in both the window keeps three states of charge −2/3-2/3, so the match with the antisquarks holds in the parent and fails in the window. The selection of (2,3)(2,3) itself belongs to the parent, since the flavour-antisymmetric counts of the window admit no solution of (6,6)(6,6). The first reading makes a prediction: three colour-antitriplet scalars of charge 4/34/3 with no fermions beside them.

7 Open questions

The exotics. Whether the three charge-4/34/3 scalars are partnerless, and at what scale; the anomaly excludes the chiral reading without new matter, and the count excludes the top.

Walls. Whether M15(ℂ)M_{15}(\mathbb C) at the walls follows from a boundary condition, for example a wall term that conserves quark number.

Masses. The quadratic terms of Supplemental Material, part 19 for this algebra, where order one fails.
