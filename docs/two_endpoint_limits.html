From a finite KO-six geometry toward five and seven dimensions

GPT-6.2-Astra max

Draft, 18 November 2026

Abstract

We follow both limits of a finite real fermion geometry in a family on a five-sphere and two intervals. Two windings fix a boundary angle as a function of area. At small area the surviving operator is a five-dimensional Dirac operator with vectorlike charges. At large area the weak vectors become massless and two towers can be resolved. The fermion limit alone leaves the current response undetermined. We compute the complete tree-level transverse charged-vector exchange for a specified product gauge action. Constant kinetic densities give a finite weak pole and growing contact interaction at the five-dimensional end. A smooth dependence on the same winding density gives a finite electromagnetic coupling, vanishing weak exchange and weak poles separated from the first axial excitations. Two allowed fermion completions have different massless content at seven and identical bounded-energy data near five. The construction specifies the gauge representation and the constitutive functions as microscopic inputs.

1 What the three labels measure

At five we ask for surviving four-dimensional Dirac fermions with the same colour and electric charge on their two chiralities. At seven we require a parent with resolvable internal modes and massless weak gauge vectors. Restoring the weak group specifies its gauge fields. Its representation on light fermions is a separate part of the problem. At six the light data may be a finite algebra acting on a graded fermion space with a real structure and a Dirac operator. Its KO-degree is six. The heat trace of that finite space is bounded at short time. A six-dimensional manifold is one possible model for an intervening energy band; the finite geometry needs no such band (Connes 2006; “Finite Spectral Triples as Towerless Intervals” 2026; “Finite Spectral Windows with an Opening Tower in Seven-Dimensional Parents” 2026).

These distinctions suggest two measurements on the same family. We follow the fermion operator to find the states that remain at a fixed energy. We then couple a weak current to a gauge field and follow its exchange amplitude. A mass assigned in a finite fermion matrix answers the first question only when the matrix has been obtained as an isolated spectral window. A statement about beta decay also needs the second measurement.

3 The charged current through the tower

Use a specified product Yang–Mills and scalar action on this background. The gauge fields propagate on the product, have Neumann spacetime-vector profiles at the interval ends, and act through fixed colour, weak and hypercharge matrices on the retained coefficients. In particular the neutral coefficient Q=T3+YQ=T_3+Y agrees with the electric charges displayed above. This choice supplies the gauge sector. This example exhibits a choice of local operators that achieves both current limits. For KK proportional to bb asymptotically, any HH growing as bpb^p with 1<p<21<p<2 gives the same ordering on the square path. The endpoints therefore leave a continuous freedom in the functions and in the finite value of the charge. The argument treats these functions as an exact classical constitutive law. Obtaining a Thomson charge or a QCD observable requires the charged and coloured thresholds, a quantum matching scheme and the corresponding matter action. A strong scale has not entered the construction.

5 Why the upper fermions remain undetermined

There is a direct way to vary the upper matter at fixed bosonic background. Add a fermion sector and its mirror, with the gauge representations of the reflected model, a vanishing original Yukawa link, and no bilinear mixing with the old fermions. On the reflected cover write the doubled kinetic operator as ρ3D\rho_3 D, with Pauli matrices ρi\rho_i in copy space. Take opposite outer reflection parities. A gauge-invariant mass mρ1m\rho_1 exchanges these parities in the kinetic pairing. In a constant vacuum its square adds m2m^2 to D2D^2. The two copies supply opposite four-dimensional chiralities. One may restrict attention to the additional gauge-singlet sector or keep the whole vectorlike pair.

Consider the two fixed mass laws ma=y|ϕ|2(1+b2/b02),mb=M+mam_a=y\,|\phi|^2(1+b^2/b_0^2),\qquad m_b=M+m_a(6) with y,M>0y,M>0 of mass dimension one. At five both masses diverge. At a chosen broken middle with s>0s>0, choose yy such that both exceed the stated fermion cutoff. The bounded lower spectrum and the finite middle window then agree between the two completions. At seven ma=0m_a=0 and mb=Mm_b=M. A zero mode and its mirror occur in the first completion; the second has a gap at least MM in this sector. The statement at finite unbroken area concerns exact zero modes. The gap of the second persists on decompactification. With zero classical fermion background the bosonic equations and vector masses do not change. Virtual matter affects quantum thresholds, which have been excluded in this comparison.

This explains the status of the one-generation example. Its wall domain, twist and representation determine the observed modes once they are specified. The same two endpoints of the bosonic action permit the two different matter spectra above. To infer an upper particle content requires a parent that fixes those data, including their quantum consistency. To infer the physical electromagnetic, weak and strong scales further requires a principle selecting the kinetic functions and matching scales. The two limits and their current matrix elements provide distinct tests of such a parent.
