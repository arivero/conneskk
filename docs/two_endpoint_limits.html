From a finite KO-six geometry toward five and seven dimensions

GPT-6.2-Astra max

Revised draft, 19 November 2026

Abstract

We follow a finite real fermion geometry between a five-dimensional Dirac limit and a seven-dimensional parent with restored weak symmetry. Two windings determine the wall angle. We construct three chiral generations as the periodic channels of four bulk families subject to a single flavour line at one wall. We give their Yukawa operators, mixing matrices and charged-current amplitudes through weak breaking. The bulk family number and the wall representation are inputs. On twelve retained colour and flavour coefficients the compressed four-family exchange has rank nine, with the three lepton coefficients in its kernel. A three-family CP invariant distinguishes the resulting finite geometry from any two-family quark sector with the same charged-current prescription. The complete transverse vector resolvent shows when an interaction disappears at five, how a current on one face measures the unfixed aspect ratio, and why a current on a corner needs a short-distance prescription. Gauge matching leaves continuous scale data. We also compare the resulting particle count with the proposed scalar partners of the five light quarks. The composite charge count and the required physical boson spectrum impose different tests on a parent.

1 What the three labels measure

At five we ask for surviving four-dimensional Dirac fermions with the same colour and electric charge on their two chiralities. At seven we require a parent with resolvable internal modes and massless weak gauge vectors. Restoring the weak group specifies its gauge fields. Its representation on light fermions is a separate part of the problem. At six the light data may be a finite algebra acting on a graded fermion space with a real structure and a Dirac operator. Its KO-degree is six. The heat trace of that finite space is bounded at short time. A six-dimensional manifold is one possible model for an intervening energy band. The finite geometry needs no such band (Connes 2006; “Finite Spectral Triples as Towerless Intervals” 2026; “Finite Spectral Windows with an Opening Tower in Seven-Dimensional Parents” 2026).

These distinctions suggest two measurements on the same family. We follow the fermion operator to find the states that remain at a fixed energy. We then couple a weak current to a gauge field and follow its exchange amplitude. A mass assigned in a finite fermion matrix answers the first question only when the matrix has been obtained as an isolated spectral window. A statement about beta decay also needs the second measurement.

There are two meanings of counting a family in this problem. An independent left-handed quark doublet includes three colour components. These colour components constitute one copy of a gauge representation. A family index labels equivalent copies on which all the gauge generators act identically. A four-dimensional Weyl field and the conjugate written in its real spectral triple also constitute a single independent field. We use the complex dimension of the finite Hilbert space when describing the triple, and remove this conjugate bookkeeping when counting particles. A set of light copies with the same colour and electric charge can still be distinguished by its masses or by a weak mixing experiment at a nearby broken vacuum.

The question about three families can accordingly be posed in a form admitting a calculation. Fix the bosonic action, the winding sector and the relative gauge normalization. Allow the representations and the reflections of the fermion bundle to be specified at the microscopic scale. Which of those data determine a multiplicity, and which observables would test it at the finite middle or either end? This extends the question about the two 84-dimensional subspaces studied previously (“The Two Families of 84 and the Number of Generations” 2026). There a count of 96 finite states assumed three complete families. Here we compute the rank of a wall kernel before forming the finite triple, and follow its mixing through the collapse. The gauge fields, the two windings and the four-dimensional frame are the same when these fermionic boundary data are compared.

4 Three light families from four bulk channels

Let FF be a Hermitian flavour space of dimension gg. First tensor the particle coefficients with FF, the conjugate coefficients with F‾\bar F, and leave the algebra and the sphere and xx operators diagonal in flavour. The real structure exchanges the two spaces antiunitarily. With the original outer reflection the finite window now has 32g32g complex dimensions. For each colour and electric representative at five there are gg Dirac copies. At a compact restored member the chosen wall module has gg left weak quark doublets in colour 𝟑\mathbf3, gg left lepton doublets, and their right singlets. This operation is possible for every gg. It gives a control against which to compare a mechanism that actually removes a channel.

A correspondence of dimensions with an exceptional-group decomposition would leave these tasks open.

At a broken middle choose a fixed energy cutoff with all three light Yukawa norms below it and a margin to the first omitted level. This is one way for information about the fourth family to survive in seven while three generations describe a middle experiment.
