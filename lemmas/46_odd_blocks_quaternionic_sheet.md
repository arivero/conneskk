## Statements

**Proposition 46.1 (the odd Hermitian blocks for the axis p).** Let Odd_p = {Y in End_C(W) : Y^* = Y, Gamma_p Y = -Y Gamma_p}. Then:

1. Odd_p = {[[0, X^*], [X, 0]] : X in Hom_C(L_p, L_p^perp)}, of real dimension 8; as a representation of K_p it is Hom_C(L_p, L_p^perp) = (1, 2)^* (x) (2, 1) = (2, 2) (x)_R C, two real copies of the bidoublet.
2. The j-linear part Odd_p cap M_2(H) is the real span of the Gamma_a with a orthogonal to p, the (2, 2) inside the 5: the maps with X in Hom_H(L_p, L_p^perp) = T_p S^4. By Proposition 10.1 it is the odd part of the compression of the coordinates of S^4, Gamma_a = 5 Q(x_a).
3. The j-antilinear part, {Y in Odd_p : j Y j^{-1} = -Y}, is the real span of the i Gamma_a Gamma_p with a orthogonal to p, which is i times the (2, 2) inside the 10 = sp(2). It lies outside M_2(H), hence outside the compression of the real functions of S^4, and it is the compression of the real functions on CP^3 that are odd under the fibre involution [w] -> [j w]: since j P_w j^{-1} = P_{jw} and the involution preserves the Fubini-Study measure, Q(f o j) = j Q(f) j^{-1}, so the j-odd real functions compress to j-antilinear Hermitian matrices and the j-even ones to M_2(H); for example i Gamma_a Gamma_p = Q(5 w^* i Gamma_a Gamma_p w / w^* w).
4. Under G, End_C(W) = 1 + 5 + 10, and under K_p: 1 -> (1, 1); 5 -> (1, 1) + (2, 2), spanned by Gamma_p and by the Gamma_a with a orthogonal to p; 10 -> (3, 1) + (1, 3) + (2, 2), spanned by the Gamma_a Gamma_b with a, b orthogonal to p, which form sp(1)_a + sp(1)_b, and by the Gamma_a Gamma_p. The even part End(L_p) + End(L_p^perp) is the complex span of 1, Gamma_p and the Gamma_a Gamma_b with a, b orthogonal to p; the odd part is the complex span of the two (2, 2).

*Proof.* (1) A Hermitian Y anticommuting with Gamma_p exchanges its eigenspaces, so Y = [[0, X^*], [X, 0]] with X : L_p -> L_p^perp arbitrary, and conversely; the real dimension is 2 dim_C Hom(C^2, C^2) = 8. Under K_p, Hom_C(L_p, L_p^perp) = L_p^* (x) L_p^perp = (1, 2) (x) (2, 1) = (2, 2) as a complex representation, the complexification of the real bidoublet. (2) j commutes with every Gamma_a, so Gamma_a lies in M_2(H); for a orthogonal to p, Gamma_a is Hermitian and anticommutes with Gamma_p; the four such Gamma_a are linearly independent, so they span a four-dimensional real subspace of the j-linear part of Odd_p, which is {[[0, X^*], [X, 0]] : X in Hom_H(L_p, L_p^perp)}, of real dimension 4, since the H-linear maps between two quaternionic lines form a copy of H. So the two spaces coincide, and Proposition 10.1 identifies them with the odd parts of the compressed coordinates. (3) Y -> j Y j^{-1} is a real-linear involution of Odd_p: (j Y j^{-1})^* = j Y^* j^{-1} for antiunitary j, and j commutes with Gamma_p. So Odd_p is the direct sum of its j-linear and j-antilinear parts, and the latter has real dimension 8 - 4 = 4. For a orthogonal to p, i Gamma_a Gamma_p is Hermitian, since (i Gamma_a Gamma_p)^* = -i Gamma_p Gamma_a = i Gamma_a Gamma_p; it anticommutes with Gamma_p, since Gamma_p Gamma_a Gamma_p = -Gamma_a; and it is j-antilinear, since j commutes with Gamma_a Gamma_p and anticommutes with i. The four are linearly independent, so they span the j-antilinear part. A nonzero j-antilinear element is outside End_H(W) = M_2(H), the real algebra generated by the compressed functions of S^4 (Example 16.9, item 1). For a real function f on CP^3, the covariance j P_w j^{-1} = P_{jw} and the invariance of the Fubini-Study measure under [w] -> [jw] give Q(f o j) = j Q(f) j^{-1}, so f odd under the involution has j Q(f) j^{-1} = -Q(f), and the formula of Example 16.9, item 2, applied to f = 5 w^* B w / w^* w with B = i Gamma_a Gamma_p, which is odd, gives Q(f) = B. (4) The 4 of Sp(2) = Spin(5) is symplectic, so End_C(W) = 4 (x) 4 = Lambda^2(4) + S^2(4) = (1 + 5) + 10, with the 5 the vector of SO(5) spanned by the Gamma_a and the 10 the adjoint spanned by the Gamma_a Gamma_b. Under K_p = Spin(4) the vector of SO(5) is 1 + (2, 2), the trivial summand being Gamma_p, and so(5) = so(4) + R^4 is (3, 1) + (1, 3) + (2, 2), the last spanned by the Gamma_a Gamma_p, which transform as the vector; Gamma_p commutes with 1, Gamma_p and the Gamma_a Gamma_b with a, b orthogonal to p and anticommutes with the Gamma_a and the Gamma_a Gamma_p with a orthogonal to p, which gives the even and odd parts, of complex dimensions 1 + 1 + 3 + 3 = 8 and 4 + 4 = 8. ∎

**Proposition 46.2 (the masses of a doublet).** Identify L_p and L_p^perp with H = C^2 through j, so that an element X of Hom_C(L_p, L_p^perp) is X = L_q + i L_{q'} with q, q' in H acting by left multiplication, L_q the j-linear and i L_{q'} the j-antilinear part. Then the two singular values of X, the masses of the two members of the doublet under the Yukawa block [[0, X^*], [X, 0]] (x) 1_V, are

    m_+-^2 = |q|^2 + |q'|^2 +- 2 |Im(conj(q) q')|,

with Im the imaginary part of the quaternion conj(q) q'. Consequently:

1. m_+ = m_- exactly when conj(q) q' is real, that is when X = z L_q for a complex number z and a quaternion q. In particular the odd part of the compression of any real function on S^4 is j-linear, q' = 0, and gives equal masses to the two members of every doublet.
2. m_- = 0 with m_+ > 0 exactly when q' = q u for a unit imaginary quaternion u (equivalently q' = u' q with u' = q u q^{-1}), that is X = L_q (1 + i L_u) = 2 L_q P_u with P_u = (1 + i L_u)/2 a projection of rank one: the pattern in which one member of the doublet is massless and the other is not is a block of rank one whose j-linear and j-antilinear halves have equal norm, in the Hilbert-Schmidt and in the operator norm. Tensored with 1_V it gives the up-type quark and the Dirac neutrino of the generation the mass m_+ and the down-type quark and the charged lepton the mass zero; the top-only family of lemma 21, with the leptons massless, needs a lepton block different from the quark block, which a sheet-diagonal operator of Corollary 16.8 with V ungraded does not provide.
3. For q, q' nonzero, with |q'| = t |q| and the quaternionic angle phi in [0, pi] given by Re(conj(q) q') = |q| |q'| cos phi, m_+-^2 = |q|^2 (1 + t^2 +- 2 t sin phi): the ratio of the two masses depends on the relative size of the two halves and on the angle between them, and on nothing else.

*Proof.* Write H = C + j C with C acting on the right, x = x_1 + j x_2. For q = a + j b with a, b in C, left multiplication is the matrix [[a, -conj(b)], [b, conj(a)]]; q -> L_q is a homomorphism of real algebras with L_{conj(q)} = L_q^*; L_q commutes with j (x_1, x_2) = (-conj(x_2), conj(x_1)), the right multiplication by j, and i L_{q'} anticommutes with it; the L_q and the i L_{q'} together span the eight real dimensions of M_2(C). Then X^* X = (L_{conj(q)} - i L_{conj(q')}) (L_q + i L_{q'}) = |q|^2 + |q'|^2 + i (L_{conj(q) q'} - L_{conj(q') q}) = |q|^2 + |q'|^2 + 2 i L_w, with w = Im(conj(q) q'), since conj(q') q = conj(conj(q) q') and the real parts cancel. Left multiplication by an imaginary quaternion w is a traceless anti-Hermitian matrix with eigenvalues +- i |w|, so 2 i L_w has eigenvalues -+ 2 |w| and the eigenvalues of X^* X are as displayed. Equivalently, det X = |q|^2 - |q'|^2 + 2 i q . q' with q . q' = Re(conj(q) q'), the complex-bilinear norm of q + i q' in H (x) C, so m_+ m_- = |det X| and m_+^2 + m_-^2 = 2 (|q|^2 + |q'|^2). (1) The masses coincide exactly when w = 0. If q = 0, X = i L_{q'}. If q != 0, conj(q) q' = s real gives q' = (s/|q|^2) q and X = (1 + i s/|q|^2) L_q; conversely X = z L_q has X^* X = |z|^2 |q|^2. The compression of a real function on S^4 is a Hermitian element of M_2(H) (Example 16.9, item 1), and Gamma_p is j-linear, so the odd part is j-linear. (2) m_- = 0 exactly when |q|^2 + |q'|^2 = 2 |w|; since |w| <= |conj(q) q'| = |q| |q'| <= (|q|^2 + |q'|^2)/2, this forces |q| = |q'| and Re(conj(q) q') = 0, so conj(q) q' = w with |w| = |q|^2 and q' = q w/|q|^2 = q u with u = w/|q|^2 a unit imaginary quaternion; then X = L_q + i L_q L_u = L_q (1 + i L_u), and i L_u is Hermitian with L_u^2 = -1, so (1 + i L_u)/2 is a projection of rank one. Conversely such an X has rank one. (3) Substitution, with |w|^2 = |q|^2 |q'|^2 - Re(conj(q) q')^2. ∎

**Corollary 46.4 (the hypercharge direction and the connector's line).** Let X be the Yukawa block of a doublet with m_+ != m_-. The elements of Sp(1)_b = Sp(L_p) commuting with X^* X form a circle U(1)_X, the maximal torus of Sp(1)_b whose weight lines are the two mass eigenlines of X^* X in L_p; it is a maximal torus of the kind lemma 42 marked as an input, and it equals the U(1)_b of lemma 42, the stabilizer in Sp(1)_b of the connector's line Cw, exactly when Cw is one of its weight lines; under it T_p S^4 = (2, 2) restricts to (2)_{+-1}, one complex doublet of hypercharge 1/2, and the twistor fibre K_p/(Sp(1)_a x U(1)_b) = S^2 is the set of complex lines in L_p. For the connector of lemma 20 (Theorem 20.2), with the axis p taken as the point of S^4 under the quaternionic line of its marked line w, so that w lies in L_p = W_+ and carries the Majorana block, the Majorana state is a Dirac-mass eigenstate exactly when w is one of the two eigenlines of X^* X, that is a fixed point of U(1)_X on the fibre S^2. The stabilizer of X in K_p is the circle {(X b X^{-1}, b) : b in U(1)_X} for invertible X with m_+ != m_-, again a circle for a rank-one X, and the diagonal {(q b q^{-1}, b)} = Sp(1), the custodial SU(2)_{L+R}, for X = z L_q; the stabilizer of X and of the connector's line together contains a circle exactly when that line is an eigenline of X^* X, so the eigenline condition is exactly the condition for the Yukawa block and the connector to leave a common circle unbroken, the analogue of the electromagnetic U(1). For a j-linear Yukawa block, m_+ = m_-, every circle in Sp(1)_b commutes with X^* X and every line is an eigenline, so neither the torus nor the position of w on the fibre is determined by the masses.

*Proof.* X^* X is a Hermitian operator on L_p = C^2 with two distinct eigenvalues, so its commutant in U(L_p) = U(2) is the torus of its eigenbasis, whose intersection with Sp(1)_b = SU(2) is the circle U(1)_b with those weight lines; Proposition 42.2 gives the restriction of (2, 2) and the fibre. The Majorana block of lemma 20 sits on the state w (x) conj(v), on which the Dirac block acts as X^* X (x) 1 on the right-handed factor, so it is a mass eigenstate exactly when w is invariant under X^* X. For the stabilizers: (a, b) in K_p fixes X exactly when a X = X b, so b commutes with X^* X and a = X b X^{-1}; for split masses b runs over U(1)_X and a lies in SU(L_p^perp), while for X = z L_q the condition is a = q b q^{-1} for every b in Sp(1); the V-phase does not enter, since the stabilizer of T_kappa contains the diagonal circle of Theorem 20.2 and X (x) 1_V is invariant under every unitary of V. The last statement is Proposition 46.2 (1). ∎

2. the Yukawa block Y, odd for Gamma_p, in Hom_C(L_p, L_p^perp) = (2, 2) (x) C: its j-linear half is the direction of S^4 at p, the odd part of the compressed base coordinates; its j-antilinear half is the part no real function on the base supplies through the odd part of its compression (complex functions with the projections of the grading supply it), which splits the two masses of a doublet and thereby fixes a maximal torus of Sp(1)_b, the U(1)_b of the connector when the connector's line is one of its weight lines; the stabilizer of the block itself is the diagonal Sp(1) of K_p for equal masses and a circle for split ones (Propositions 46.1 and 46.2, Corollary 46.4);
3. the connector of lemma 20, the rank-one block marking a complex line w in L_p and a line v in V, with stabilizer Sp(1)_a x U(1)_b in K_p: it reduces A_PS to C + H + M_3(C) (Theorem 20.2), the orbit of its line Cw is CP^3 = G/H (Proposition 42.2), and the orbits of the connector have the moduli of Proposition 42.3.

The Higgs doublet of the corpus note is the tangent of the first orbit at the second block's foot: the variation of the axis, T_p S^4 = (2, 2) restricted by the U(1)_b of Corollary 46.4 to one complex doublet of hypercharge 1/2 (lemma 42). The heavy top of lemma 21 is the extreme case of the second block, Proposition 46.2 (2): the third generation, whose block in this scope is shared by the quark and the lepton doublets, has a Yukawa block close to rank one; with r = m_b/m_t, Proposition 46.2 gives ((1 - t^2)/(1 + t^2))^2 + (2 t cos phi/(1 + t^2))^2 = (2r/(1 + r^2))^2, so the two halves are equal in norm and in quadrature up to terms of order r, about 0.02 at the top scale; the odd part of the compression of a real function on the base would give m_t = m_b. Every G-breaking cross block of the window operator from a bounded multiplication operator, in the 5 or in the 10, is bounded by the exponential that bounds lambda_M (Proposition 46.3), and the block of a coordinate of S^4 is c_M Gamma_a, with c_M not shown to be nonzero; the Yukawa block of the bimodule is not a cross block of the window (Proposition 28.20 shows that the doubled window operator has zero compression to the bimodule, P_- DD P_- = 0): the two sheets supply the representation types, the quaternionic half from the real functions on S^4 and the full bidoublet from those on CP^3, and the block itself is an input of Corollary 16.8.

## Scope

Finite linear algebra on the quaternionic sheet W = H^2 with the Clifford structure of Example 16.9; one generation, the Yukawa block tensored with the identity of V (the same block for the lepton and the three colours, as in a sheet-diagonal operator of Corollary 16.8); the grading axis as an input (Proposition 28.19); for Proposition 46.3, the window of Theorem 29.7 with the weighted estimates of Proposition 29.8. Three generations replace q and q' by 3 x 3 quaternionic matrices; if q^* q' is quaternion-Hermitian, X^* X is j-linear and each singular value has even multiplicity, the analogue of Proposition 46.2 (1) in one direction; the closed formula and the rank statement have no direct analogue and are not proved here. Nothing here derives the Yukawa block, the Majorana line or a potential; the j-antilinear half is identified by representation type with the compression of functions on CP^3, not derived from one. The direction the result points to: a parent in which the Yukawa block is the compression of a function on the wall sheet, odd under the fibre involution, so that the up-down splitting, and with it the top's mass, is the fibre dependence of that function, against the alternative of complex functions on the base with the projections of the grading, which reach the same bidoublet and should be compared with it before either is adopted; and a potential that drives the block of the third generation to rank one. Leads: (1) a rank-one block X = lambda mu^* marks the line C mu in P(L_p) = S^2, a point of the twistor fibre over p, the same kind of datum as the connector's line, and in the C-action of Theorem 20.2 the states on the connector's line are the up-type right-handed ones, so with the eigenline condition and the up-type assignment of the top, C mu = Cw: one marked point of CP^3 over p would carry both the Majorana line and the third-generation Yukawa block, a concrete target for a parent and for T12; (2) the custodial breaking is measured by |Im(conj(q) q')| = (m_+^2 - m_-^2)/4, a row for the dictionary; (3) by Theorem 29.9 and Proposition 28.8 the core compression of a real function on X has a j-antilinear half of norm at most C ||a||_{C^1} M^{-1/2}, so a block taken from a core compression has a bounded relative splitting unless its j-linear half is of the same order, and a heavy top at large M needs the wall sheet or the finite-M terms, whose j-breaking the numerics of lemma 28 showed; (4) at mu = lambda the window block of Proposition 46.3 is 2 lambda P_{L_n} with exact zero modes on the two copies of L_n^perp, so a G-breaking block in the 5 marks the quaternionic line that Proposition 28.19 leaves to be chosen, a lead for the axis as an input, with no dynamics behind it here; (5) the CKM matrix in the variables (q, q') with q^* q' quaternion-Hermitian as the custodial case.
