## Setting

Let W = C^4 with the Hermitian Clifford generators Gamma_1, ..., Gamma_5 of Cl(5), Gamma_a Gamma_b + Gamma_b Gamma_a = 2 delta_ab, and the antiunitary j with j^2 = -1 commuting with every Gamma_a, so that W = H^2 (Example 16.9: W is the kernel H^0(CP^3, O(1)) with the quaternionic structure of the twistor space, and Proposition 10.1 gives the compression Q(x_a) = Gamma_a/5 of the coordinates of S^4). The real algebra End_H(W) = M_2(H) is the real span of 1, the Gamma_a and the Gamma_a Gamma_b (a < b), of dimension 1 + 5 + 10 = 16; its unitary group is G = Sp(2), acting on W by the 4; under G the Gamma_a span the 5 (the traceless Hermitian elements of M_2(H)) and the Gamma_a Gamma_b the 10 = sp(2) (the anti-Hermitian elements). For p in S^4 put Gamma_p = p^a Gamma_a; Gamma_p^2 = 1, its eigenspaces L_p (+1) and L_p^perp (-1) are j-invariant, hence quaternionic lines, and p -> L_p identifies S^4 with HP^1. The stabilizer of p is K_p = Sp(1)_a x Sp(1)_b with Sp(1)_b = Sp(L_p) and Sp(1)_a = Sp(L_p^perp), in the notation of lemma 42, so that the bidoublet (2, 2) of K_p is Hom_H(L_p, L_p^perp) = T_p S^4 (the corpus note, section 6, and Proposition 42.2).

The second sheet V = C^4 carries the compression of the functions on CP^3, which generate M_4(C) (Example 16.9, item 2), and is identified with W as a G-representation (Proposition 28.19). The bimodule H_F = Hom(V, W) + Hom(W, V) = W (x) conj(V) + V (x) conj(W) is the sixteen states of one generation with their conjugates: W the two doublets of a lepton or a quark, conj(V) the lepton line and the three colours (Example 16.9). The axis p is the quaternionic grading of Proposition 28.19 and Example 16.9: Gamma_F acts as Gamma_p on the W factor, and the even algebra is H + H + M_4(C) = A_PS, acting on L_p, L_p^perp and V, the Pati-Salam algebra of lemma 20, with H_R on L_p and H_L on L_p^perp (the corpus note, section 5). A sheet-diagonal Dirac operator of Corollary 16.8 acts on H_F by a Hermitian block Y on the W factor, odd for Gamma_p, tensored with the identity of V: the Yukawa block, the same for the lepton and the three colours.

## Statements

**Proposition 46.1 (the odd Hermitian blocks for the axis p).** Let Odd_p = {Y in End_C(W) : Y^* = Y, Gamma_p Y = -Y Gamma_p}. Then:

1. Odd_p = {[[0, X^*], [X, 0]] : X in Hom_C(L_p, L_p^perp)}, of real dimension 8; as a representation of K_p it is Hom_C(L_p, L_p^perp) = (1, 2)^* (x) (2, 1) = (2, 2) (x)_R C, two real copies of the bidoublet.
2. The j-linear part Odd_p cap M_2(H) is the real span of the Gamma_a with a orthogonal to p, the (2, 2) inside the 5: the maps with X in Hom_H(L_p, L_p^perp) = T_p S^4. By Proposition 10.1 it is the odd part of the compression of the coordinates of S^4, Gamma_a = 5 Q(x_a).
3. The j-antilinear part, {Y in Odd_p : j Y j^{-1} = -Y}, is the real span of the i Gamma_a Gamma_p with a orthogonal to p, which is i times the (2, 2) inside the 10 = sp(2). It lies outside M_2(H), hence outside the compression of the real functions of S^4, and inside the compression of the real functions of CP^3, which is onto the Hermitian matrices (Example 16.9, item 2).
4. Under G, End_C(W) = 1 + 5 + 10, and under K_p: 1 -> (1, 1); 5 -> (1, 1) + (2, 2), spanned by Gamma_p and by the Gamma_a with a orthogonal to p; 10 -> (3, 1) + (1, 3) + (2, 2), spanned by the Gamma_a Gamma_b with a, b orthogonal to p, which form sp(1)_a + sp(1)_b, and by the Gamma_a Gamma_p. The even part End(L_p) + End(L_p^perp) is the complex span of 1, Gamma_p and the Gamma_a Gamma_b with a, b orthogonal to p; the odd part is the complex span of the two (2, 2).

*Proof.* (1) A Hermitian Y anticommuting with Gamma_p exchanges its eigenspaces, so Y = [[0, X^*], [X, 0]] with X : L_p -> L_p^perp arbitrary, and conversely; the real dimension is 2 dim_C Hom(C^2, C^2) = 8. Under K_p, Hom_C(L_p, L_p^perp) = L_p^* (x) L_p^perp = (1, 2) (x) (2, 1) = (2, 2) as a complex representation, the complexification of the real bidoublet. (2) j commutes with every Gamma_a, so Gamma_a lies in M_2(H); for a orthogonal to p, Gamma_a is Hermitian and anticommutes with Gamma_p; the four such Gamma_a are linearly independent, so they span a four-dimensional real subspace of the j-linear part of Odd_p, which is {[[0, X^*], [X, 0]] : X in Hom_H(L_p, L_p^perp)}, of real dimension 4, since the H-linear maps between two quaternionic lines form a copy of H. So the two spaces coincide, and Proposition 10.1 identifies them with the odd parts of the compressed coordinates. (3) Y -> j Y j^{-1} is a real-linear involution of Odd_p: (j Y j^{-1})^* = j Y^* j^{-1} for antiunitary j, and j commutes with Gamma_p. So Odd_p is the direct sum of its j-linear and j-antilinear parts, and the latter has real dimension 8 - 4 = 4. For a orthogonal to p, i Gamma_a Gamma_p is Hermitian, since (i Gamma_a Gamma_p)^* = -i Gamma_p Gamma_a = i Gamma_a Gamma_p; it anticommutes with Gamma_p, since Gamma_p Gamma_a Gamma_p = -Gamma_a; and it is j-antilinear, since j commutes with Gamma_a Gamma_p and anticommutes with i. The four are linearly independent, so they span the j-antilinear part. A nonzero j-antilinear element is outside End_H(W) = M_2(H), the real algebra generated by the compressed functions of S^4 (Example 16.9, item 1), and the compression of the real functions of CP^3 is onto the Hermitian 4 x 4 matrices, which contain Odd_p. (4) The 4 of Sp(2) = Spin(5) is symplectic, so End_C(W) = 4 (x) 4 = Lambda^2(4) + S^2(4) = (1 + 5) + 10, with the 5 the vector of SO(5) spanned by the Gamma_a and the 10 the adjoint spanned by the Gamma_a Gamma_b. Under K_p = Spin(4) the vector of SO(5) is 1 + (2, 2), the trivial summand being Gamma_p, and so(5) = so(4) + R^4 is (3, 1) + (1, 3) + (2, 2), the last spanned by the Gamma_a Gamma_p, which transform as the vector; Gamma_p commutes with 1, Gamma_p and the Gamma_a Gamma_b with a, b orthogonal to p and anticommutes with the Gamma_a and the Gamma_a Gamma_p with a orthogonal to p, which gives the even and odd parts, of complex dimensions 1 + 1 + 3 + 3 = 8 and 4 + 4 = 8. ∎

**Proposition 46.2 (the masses of a doublet).** Identify L_p and L_p^perp with H = C^2 through j, so that an element X of Hom_C(L_p, L_p^perp) is X = L_q + i L_{q'} with q, q' in H acting by left multiplication, L_q the j-linear and i L_{q'} the j-antilinear part. Then the two singular values of X, the masses of the two members of the doublet under the Yukawa block [[0, X^*], [X, 0]] (x) 1_V, are

    m_+-^2 = |q|^2 + |q'|^2 +- 2 |Im(conj(q) q')|,

with Im the imaginary part of the quaternion conj(q) q'. Consequently:

1. m_+ = m_- exactly when conj(q) q' is real, that is when X = z L_q for a complex number z and a quaternion q. In particular the odd part of the compression of any real function on S^4 is j-linear, q' = 0, and gives equal masses to the two members of every doublet.
2. m_- = 0 with m_+ > 0 exactly when q' = q u for a unit imaginary quaternion u, that is X = L_q (1 + i L_u) = 2 L_q P_u with P_u = (1 + i L_u)/2 a projection of rank one: the pattern in which one member of the doublet is massless and the other is not, the heavy-top-only pattern of lemma 21, is a block of rank one whose j-linear and j-antilinear halves have equal norm.
3. With |q'| = t |q| and the quaternionic angle given by Re(conj(q) q') = |q| |q'| cos phi, m_+-^2 = |q|^2 (1 + t^2 +- 2 t sin phi): the ratio of the two masses depends on the relative size of the two halves and on the angle between them, and on nothing else.

*Proof.* For q = a + b j, left multiplication on H = C^2 is the matrix [[a, -conj(b)], [b, conj(a)]], which commutes with j, and i L_{q'} anticommutes with j. Then X^* X = (L_{conj(q)} - i L_{conj(q')}) (L_q + i L_{q'}) = |q|^2 + |q'|^2 + i (L_{conj(q) q'} - L_{conj(q') q}) = |q|^2 + |q'|^2 + 2 i L_w, with w = Im(conj(q) q'), since conj(q') q = conj(conj(q) q') and the real parts cancel. Left multiplication by an imaginary quaternion w is a traceless anti-Hermitian matrix with eigenvalues +- i |w|, so 2 i L_w has eigenvalues -+ 2 |w| and the eigenvalues of X^* X are as displayed. (1) The masses coincide exactly when w = 0, that is conj(q) q' = s real, q' = s q, X = (1 + i s) L_q; conversely X = z L_q has X^* X = |z|^2 |q|^2. The compression of a real function on S^4 is a Hermitian element of M_2(H) (Example 16.9, item 1), and Gamma_p is j-linear, so the odd part is j-linear. (2) m_- = 0 exactly when |q|^2 + |q'|^2 = 2 |w|; since |w| <= |conj(q) q'| = |q| |q'| <= (|q|^2 + |q'|^2)/2, this forces |q| = |q'| and Re(conj(q) q') = 0, so conj(q) q' = w with |w| = |q|^2 and q' = q w/|q|^2 = q u with u = w/|q|^2 a unit imaginary quaternion; then X = L_q + i L_q L_u = L_q (1 + i L_u), and i L_u is Hermitian with L_u^2 = -1, so (1 + i L_u)/2 is a projection of rank one. Conversely such an X has rank one. (3) Substitution, with |w|^2 = |q|^2 |q'|^2 - Re(conj(q) q')^2. ∎

Under the weighted estimates of the proof of Proposition 29.8 (with the cut-offs of its second reading), for every eta in (0, 1) there are M_eta and C_eta with

    |<v, B u>| <= C_eta ||B|| exp(-(1 - eta)(M/2) int_e^{l - a} f dr),    M >= M_eta.

So every off-diagonal block of the compression of a bounded local operator to the eight states is of the exponential order of lambda_M. For B the multiplication by a coordinate x_a of S^4 pulled back to X, the cross block is P_V x_a P_W = c_M Gamma_a under the identification W_M = V_M = 4 of Proposition 28.19, with |c_M| bounded as above; a window operator with off-diagonal block d = lambda + mu n^a Gamma_a (lambda >= 0 after a phase, mu complex, n a unit vector) has the eight eigenvalues +- (lambda^2 + |mu|^2 +- 2 lambda Re(mu))^{1/2}, each with multiplicity two, on the quaternionic lines L_n and L_n^perp of the two copies of the 4.

The window projections P_W and P_V are G-equivariant, since T_M is, and the coordinates x_a span the 5; so a -> P_V x_a P_W is a G-map from the 5 to Hom(W_M, V_M) = End_C(4) = 1 + 5 + 10, and by Schur's lemma it is a multiple c_M of the unique equivariant embedding, a -> Gamma_a. **Corollary 46.4 (the hypercharge direction and the connector's line).** Let X be the Yukawa block of a doublet with m_+ != m_-. The elements of Sp(1)_b = Sp(L_p) commuting with X^* X form a circle U(1)_b, the maximal torus of Sp(1)_b whose weight lines are the two mass eigenlines of X^* X in L_p; it is the U(1)_b that lemma 42 marked as an input, under which T_p S^4 = (2, 2) restricts to (2)_{+-1}, one complex doublet of hypercharge 1/2, and the twistor fibre K_p/(Sp(1)_a x U(1)_b) = S^2 is the set of complex lines in L_p. For the connector of lemma 20 (Theorem 20.2), whose marked line w in L_p carries the Majorana block, the Majorana state is a Dirac-mass eigenstate exactly when w is one of the two eigenlines of X^* X, that is a fixed point of U(1)_b on the fibre S^2. For a j-linear Yukawa block, m_+ = m_-, every circle in Sp(1)_b commutes with X^* X and every line is an eigenline, so neither U(1)_b nor the position of w on the fibre is determined by the masses.

*Proof.* X^* X is a Hermitian operator on L_p = C^2 with two distinct eigenvalues, so its commutant in U(L_p) = U(2) is the torus of its eigenbasis, whose intersection with Sp(1)_b = SU(2) is the circle U(1)_b with those weight lines; Proposition 42.2 gives the restriction of (2, 2) and the fibre. The Majorana block of lemma 20 sits on the line w; it is a mass eigenstate of the Dirac block exactly when w is invariant under X^* X. The last statement is Proposition 46.2 (1). ∎

## Reading

The finite data of the twistor window carry three blocks, and the three are stabilizers in one chain inside G = Sp(2), the unitary group of the base algebra M_2(H):

1. the axis Gamma_p, a Hermitian involution in the 5, with stabilizer K_p = Sp(1)_a x Sp(1)_b: it reduces M_2(H) + M_4(C) to its commutant H + H + M_4(C) = A_PS, and its orbit is S^4 = G/K_p, whose tangent at p is the bidoublet (2, 2) (Example 16.9; Proposition 46.1);
2. the Yukawa block Y, odd for Gamma_p, in Hom_C(L_p, L_p^perp) = (2, 2) (x) C: its j-linear half is the direction of S^4 at p, the odd part of the compressed base coordinates; its j-antilinear half is the part no function on the base supplies, which splits the two masses of a doublet and thereby fixes U(1)_b inside Sp(1)_b (Propositions 46.1 and 46.2, Corollary 46.4);
3. the connector of lemma 20, the rank-one block marking a complex line w in L_p and a line v in V, with stabilizer Sp(1)_a x U(1)_b in K_p: it reduces A_PS to C + H + M_3(C) (Theorem 20.2), its orbit is CP^3 = G/H (Proposition 42.2), and its moduli are those of Proposition 42.3.

The Higgs doublet of the corpus note is the tangent of the first orbit at the second block's foot: the variation of the axis, T_p S^4 = (2, 2) restricted by the U(1)_b of Corollary 46.4 to one complex doublet of hypercharge 1/2 (lemma 42). The heavy top of lemma 21 is the extreme case of the second block, Proposition 46.2 (2): the quark doublet of the third generation has a Yukawa block close to rank one, whose two halves are nearly equal in norm and in quadrature; a geometric Yukawa, compressed from the base, would give m_t = m_b. The window operator of lemma 29 can carry G-breaking blocks in the 5 and in the 10 (Proposition 46.3), but only at the exponential order of the tunnelling mass, so the Yukawa block of the bimodule is not a cross block of the window (Proposition 28.20 says the same from the other side): the two sheets supply the representation types, the quaternionic half from S^4 and the full bidoublet from CP^3, and the block itself is an input of Corollary 16.8.

## Scope

Finite linear algebra on the quaternionic sheet W = H^2 with the Clifford structure of Example 16.9; one generation, the Yukawa block tensored with the identity of V (the same block for the lepton and the three colours, as in a sheet-diagonal operator of Corollary 16.8); the grading axis as an input (Proposition 28.19); for Proposition 46.3, the window of Theorem 29.7 with the weighted estimates of Proposition 29.8. Three generations replace q and q' by 3 x 3 quaternionic matrices, and the statements on masses become statements on singular values of q + i q' with the same structure. Nothing here derives the Yukawa block, the Majorana line or a potential; the j-antilinear half is identified by representation type with the compression of functions on CP^3, not derived from one. The direction the result points to: a parent in which the Yukawa block is the compression of a function on the wall sheet, so that the up-down splitting, and with it the top's mass, is the fibre dependence of that function, and a potential that drives the block of the third generation to rank one.
