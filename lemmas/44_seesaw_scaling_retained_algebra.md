# The retained algebra along the seesaw scaling

For STRATEGY T2c (CORPUS_SALVAGE items 3 and 7), following [lemma 20](20_connecting_operator.md) (Theorem 20.2: the centralizer of the Majorana block is C + H + M_3(C)), [lemma 06](06_vectorlike_odd_factorization.md) (the representation of Result 5 and the criterion of Lemma 4) and the multiscale collapse of the corpus (historical/corpus/solidification.md, section 2: for F_t = tT + t^alpha V + S the bounded-commutator algebra is the commutant of T and of V). Written by Claude Fable 5.1 on 4 November 2026, by hand.

**Outcome.** The heavy Majorana block T_R selects the Standard Model algebra out of Pati-Salam (Theorem 20.2). A neutrino Dirac block V_nu that grows with the Majorana scale, as it must for a seesaw mass that stays finite (V_nu ~ t^{1/2}), is a second heavy operator, and the algebra retained by bounded commutators is its commutant inside C + H + M_3(C), which is C + M_3(C): the algebra of colour and electromagnetism, the same as after the heavy top. So, under the bounded-commutator criterion, the finite seesaw at fixed light mass is a limit in the broken algebra; the Standard Model algebra survives the heavy Majorana limit only with a bounded Dirac coupling, along which the light neutrino mass vanishes as 1/t. Under the order-one criterion of Theorem 20.2 the conclusion is different: V_nu is part of the Standard Model Dirac operator and satisfies order one on A_SM, so A_SM stays admissible at every alpha; the two criteria agree for T_R (both give A_SM) and differ for V_nu. Read physically, an unbounded commutator is a gauge direction whose mass grows (lemma 20, Result 9), so the proposition says that a Dirac neutrino mass growing without bound breaks SU(2). In nine dimensions the Majorana block cannot be a bulk mass (a single Majorana field in dimension 8k + 1 has no scalar mass term), so in the interval parent it is wall data, and the statement above is about the wall operator.

## Setting

One generation in the representation of lemma 06, Result 5, with H_p = (C^2 (x) F)_L + (C^2 (x) F)_R, F = C + C^3, the left action q (x) I_4 on p_L, Q_lambda (x) I_4 = diag(lambda, conj lambda) (x) I_4 on p_R, I_2 (x) diag(lambda, m) on the antiparticles, and J(p, pbar) = (K pbar, K p). The neutrino states are nu_L (p_L, upper weak component, lepton line), nu_R (p_R, upper component, lepton line) and their conjugates. Let

    T_R = |nu_R><J nu_R| + |J nu_R><nu_R|   (the Majorana block, rank two, J-real, odd),
    V_nu = y (|nu_L><nu_R| + |nu_R><nu_L|) + conjugate   (the neutrino Dirac block),

and S the remaining Yukawa blocks. The family of finite operators is D_t = t T_R + t^alpha V_nu + S with t -> infinity and 0 <= alpha < 1.

## Statements

**Proposition 44.1 (the retained algebra).** The algebra of a in A_PS = H_R + H_L + M_4(C) with sup_t ||[D_t, rho(a)]|| < infinity is

    A_PS cap {T_R}' cap {V_nu}'   for alpha > 0,   and   A_PS cap {T_R}' = C + H + M_3(C)   for alpha = 0.

For alpha > 0 it equals {(lambda, Q_lambda, m)} = C + M_3(C): the centralizer of the top block of lemma 06 and the commutant C_F of lemma 22 for the Standard Model data.

*Proof.* Boundedness of [t T_R + t^alpha V_nu + S, rho(a)] for all t forces [T_R, rho(a)] = 0 and, when 0 < alpha < 1, [V_nu, rho(a)] = 0 (compare the coefficients of t and t^alpha; at alpha = 1 only the commutant of T_R + V_nu would be forced). The first commutant is C + H + M_3(C) by Theorem 20.2 (rank-one block, kappa != 0), realized as a = (lambda, q, m) acting by lambda on nu_R. For the second, with y != 0, rho(a) V_nu nu_R = y rho(a) nu_L = y (q_11 nu_L + q_21 e_L) and V_nu rho(a) nu_R = lambda y nu_L, so q_11 = lambda and q_21 = 0; for q in H, q = [[alpha', beta], [-conj beta, conj alpha']] gives q = diag(lambda, conj lambda) = Q_lambda. The conjugate states give no condition: J nu_L and J nu_R both carry lambda, so the conjugate part of V_nu commutes with all of A_SM; and m is unconstrained. So a = (lambda, Q_lambda, m). ∎

**Proposition 44.2 (the light mass along the scaling).** On the neutral block spanned by nu_L and nu_R with their conjugates, the spectrum of t T_R + t^alpha V_nu is plus and minus the roots mu_+- of mu^2 - t mu - t^{2 alpha} |y|^2 = 0 (the symmetric mass matrix [[0, s], [s, t]] of lemma 06, Proposition 6, with s = t^alpha y, whose characteristic polynomial on the four states is lambda^4 - (t^2 + 2|s|^2) lambda^2 + |s|^4), so the light mass is the singular value |mu_-| with

    mu_-(t) = - 2 t^{2 alpha} |y|^2 / (t + sqrt(t^2 + 4 t^{2 alpha} |y|^2)) ~ - |y|^2 t^{2 alpha - 1}.

It tends to zero for alpha < 1/2, to the finite value -|y|^2 for alpha = 1/2 (the seesaw mass, the Schur complement -V_nu T_R^{-1} V_nu of the corpus), and diverges for alpha > 1/2. In one generation the other Yukawa blocks act on other species and leave the neutral block alone.

*Proof.* The 2 x 2 block [[0, s], [s, t]] has the displayed characteristic polynomial; the cancellation-free form of the small root is the corpus formula with m = t^alpha y (research_checks, section 2.6), and the three regimes are read off. ∎

**Corollary 44.3 (the seesaw and the algebra).** Under the bounded-commutator criterion, a finite light neutrino mass at the heavy Majorana limit (alpha = 1/2) and the Standard Model algebra are obtained in two different limits of the same family: the algebra C + H + M_3(C) needs alpha = 0, along which the light mass is -|y|^2/t -> 0, and the finite mass needs alpha = 1/2, along which the retained algebra is C + M_3(C). The two coincide only at finite t, where no limit has been taken and, for a in A_SM, the commutators are bounded by t^{1/2} |y| up to a constant and the bounded term ||[S, rho(a)]||; physically that bound is the Dirac neutrino mass, of the order of the electroweak scale, so the statement concerns the strict limit and says that the seesaw limit at fixed light mass is a limit of the broken theory, in the sense of lemma 06's Proposition 5. Under the order-one criterion of Theorem 20.2 the Standard Model algebra is admissible at every alpha, and the corollary does not apply.

In the interval parent of lemma 22 every block of D_F, Yukawa and Majorana alike, is wall data, X = tan(l D_F), and that parent is five-dimensional; the nine-dimensional statement adds that in a nine-dimensional bulk the Majorana block could not be a bulk mass either, as lemma 20, item 1, already reads it (the connector is the Majorana block), and it agrees with lemma 06, Proposition 6, where a nonzero Majorana block obstructs the KO-5 reduction that carries the label 4 + 5. The scaling of Proposition 44.1 is then a scaling of the wall coupling, and Theorem 22.1 keeps the light pair in the window as long as l |D_F| < pi/2, which the growing Majorana entry violates first: the seesaw scale is the first datum that leaves the window.
