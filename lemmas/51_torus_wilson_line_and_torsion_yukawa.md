## Setting

Let A = R^4/Lambda be the flat four-torus of lemma 48, A = a (x) R^2 with a the standard representation of S_3 and R^2 the tangent space of the elliptic curve E, with the periodic spin structure, the one invariant under S_3; its spinor bundle is A x Sigma_4, Sigma_4 = C^4 = Sigma_+ + Sigma_- with the Clifford action c(v) of v in R^4 (Hermitian, c(v)^2 = |v|^2), the chirality Gamma = c(e_1) c(e_2) c(e_3) c(e_4) for an oriented orthonormal basis, and the quaternionic structure j commuting with Spin(4), c and Gamma (lemma 49, Setting). By Proposition 49.1 (Sigma_4, c(e_1), ..., c(e_4), Gamma, j) is the quaternionic sheet W = H^2 of Example 16.9 with axis p at Gamma, L_p = Sigma_+, L_p^perp = Sigma_-, and T A = T_p S^4 through v -> c(v). The Dirac operator is D = -i sum_a c(e_a) partial_a on L^2(A, Sigma_4), self-adjoint on H^1, with kernel the constant spinors; its spectrum is the union over the dual lattice of the eigenvalues +-|k| of c(k) on the sectors e^{i k . z} Sigma_4, k in 2 pi Lambda^*. Let 2 pi/l be the length of the shortest nonzero vector of 2 pi Lambda^* (l the side for a cubic torus).

The data: a constant real one-form A = A_a e^a, the connection d + i A of the trivial line bundle, flat, with holonomy e^{i A(lambda)} around lambda in Lambda, a Wilson line, defined modulo 2 pi Lambda^* by the gauge transformations e^{i k . z}, which shift the momentum; and a constant real three-form H. The Dirac operator with these data is

    D_{A,H} = D + c(A) + s i c(H),

where c(H) = sum_{a<b<c} H_{abc} c(e_a) c(e_b) c(e_c) is anti-Hermitian, so that s i c(H) is Hermitian for real s, and s is the constant of the chosen normalization of the three-form term. In Agricola's lectures (arXiv:math/0606705, section "The square of the Dirac operator and parallel spinors", read in the e-print by the referee) the spinor connection nabla^{s'}_X psi = nabla^g_X psi + s' (the contraction of T with X) . psi has torsion 4 s' T and Dirac operator D^{s'} = D^g + 3 s' T. In the Hermitian convention used here this is s = 3 s'. So s = 3/4 for the Dirac operator of the connection with torsion H (s' = 1/4), and s = 1/4 for the connection with torsion H/3 (s' = 1/12). The second is the rescaling Bismut used for his local index theorem, and on naturally reductive spaces it is Kostant's cubic Dirac operator. The coupling of a three-form flux to spinors gives another constant. The value of s enters none of the statements, provided s != 0. In four dimensions c(e_a) c(e_b) c(e_c) = +- Gamma c(e_d) for {a, b, c, d} = {1, 2, 3, 4} (for instance c(e_1) c(e_2) c(e_3) = Gamma c(e_4)), so for every constant three-form H there is a unique eta in R^4, eta = +- s *H with the sign fixed by the orientation conventions, such that

    s i c(H) = i c(eta) Gamma,

and, for s != 0, every eta arises from a unique H. All statements below are in terms of eta. By Proposition 46.1, i c(eta) Gamma is the general j-antilinear odd Hermitian block and c(A) the general j-linear one.

The Weyl group S_3 acts on A by the permutations of the coordinates (z_1, z_2, z_3) of E^3 restricted to z_1 + z_2 + z_3 = 0; a transposition tau acts on T A = a (x) R^2 by rho(tau) (x) 1, the reflection of a in the hyperplane orthogonal to its normal n_tau, so d tau is the rotation by pi in the plane n_tau (x) R^2 with fixed plane P_tau = n_tau^perp (x) R^2, the tangent plane of the mirror {z_i = z_j}, which is a complex line for the complex structure J = 1 (x) J_E that A inherits from E; the lift of tau to Spin(4) is x = +- c(f_1) c(f_2) for an orthonormal basis of n_tau (x) R^2, of order four, acting on both chiralities with eigenvalues +-i (lemma 48, Proposition 48.1; lemma 49, Proposition 49.2), and tau-tilde psi(z) = x psi(tau^{-1} z) is its action on spinor fields, which is well defined for the periodic spin structure. A three-cycle acts by a rotation of order three of a tensored with the identity of R^2.

## Statements

**Proposition 51.1 (the lowest window of the torus with a Wilson line and a torsion).** D_{A,H} commutes with the translations, and on the sector e^{i k . z} Sigma_4 it is the constant matrix

    Y_k = c(k + A) + i c(eta) Gamma,

an odd Hermitian block of lemma 46 with j-linear half c(k + A) and j-antilinear half i c(eta) Gamma, whose eigenvalues are +-m_+(k) and +-m_-(k), each once, with

    m_+-(k)^2 = |k + A|^2 + |eta|^2 +- 2 |(k + A) wedge eta|,

|v wedge eta| = (|v|^2 |eta|^2 - (v . eta)^2)^{1/2}. If |A| + |eta| < pi/l, the constant spinors are the lowest spectral window of D_{A,H}: the four eigenvalues +-m_+(0), +-m_-(0) lie in [-(|A| + |eta|), |A| + |eta|] and every other eigenvalue has absolute value at least 2 pi/l - |A| - |eta|, which is larger. The compression of D_{A,H} to that window is exact and equals

    Y = c(A) + i c(eta) Gamma,

the Yukawa block of lemma 46 on the sheet Sigma_4 = W of Proposition 49.1, with q <-> A and q' <-> eta in the variables of Proposition 46.2 and |Im(conj(q) q')| = |A wedge eta|. The tower above the window has the masses m_+-(k), k != 0, the Wilson line shifting the momenta and the torsion splitting each level into two.

*Proof.* The coefficients of D_{A,H} are constant, so it commutes with the translations z -> z + z_0 and preserves each sector e^{i k . z} Sigma_4, where -i partial_a acts as k_a; so D_{A,H} e^{i k . z} psi = e^{i k . z} (c(k + A) + s i c(H)) psi = e^{i k . z} Y_k psi. Y_k is Hermitian, odd for Gamma (c(v) anticommutes with Gamma, and i c(eta) Gamma anticommutes with Gamma), c(k + A) commutes with j and i c(eta) Gamma anticommutes with it. For the spectrum put v = k + A and X = Y_k restricted to L_p = Sigma_+, X : L_p -> L_p^perp; on L_p, Gamma = 1, and

    X^* X = (c(v) - i Gamma c(eta)) (c(v) + i c(eta) Gamma) = c(v)^2 + c(eta)^2 + i (c(v) c(eta) - c(eta) c(v)) Gamma = |v|^2 + |eta|^2 + 2 i c(v wedge eta),

restricted to L_p, where c(v wedge eta) = (c(v) c(eta) - c(eta) c(v))/2 is the Clifford action of the two-form v wedge eta; we used Gamma c(eta) = -c(eta) Gamma and Gamma = 1 on L_p. The two-form v wedge eta is simple of norm |v wedge eta|, so c(v wedge eta) squares to -|v wedge eta|^2 and is traceless on each chirality (it is a multiple of c(f_1) c(f_2) for an orthonormal pair, which commutes with Gamma and has trace zero on each chirality), with eigenvalues +-i |v wedge eta| on L_p; hence X^* X has the eigenvalues m_+-^2 as displayed, and Y_k, with X^* X on L_p and X X^* on L_p^perp of the same spectrum, has the eigenvalues +-m_+, +-m_-, each once. For the window: m_+(0) <= |A| + |eta| by the triangle inequality for the operator norm of Y_0 = c(A) + i c(eta) Gamma, and for k != 0, m_-(k) >= |k + A| - |eta| >= 2 pi/l - |A| - |eta|, since m_-(k)^2 >= (|k + A| - |eta|)^2 (the smaller eigenvalue of X^* X is at least (|v| - |eta|)^2 because |v wedge eta| <= |v| |eta|) and |k| >= 2 pi/l; the inequality |A| + |eta| < 2 pi/l - |A| - |eta| is the hypothesis. The compression to the invariant subspace of the constant spinors is the restriction, Y_0 = Y. The identification with lemma 46 is Proposition 49.1, with the j-linear and j-antilinear halves matched by Proposition 46.1, and Proposition 46.2 gives the same masses. The map v -> c(v)|_{L_p} identifies R^4 isometrically with Hom_H(L_p, L_p^perp) = {L_q}, so q = q(A) and q' = q(eta) for one real-linear isometry q : R^4 -> H. For quaternions, Re(conj(q) q') is the Euclidean inner product and |conj(q) q'| = |q| |q'|. Hence |Im(conj(q) q')|^2 = |q|^2 |q'|^2 - (q . q')^2 = |A|^2 |eta|^2 - (A . eta)^2 = |A wedge eta|^2. ∎

**Proposition 51.2 (the photon condition is the invariance under the transposition).** Let tau be a transposition with mirror plane P_tau and lift x, and tau-tilde its action on spinor fields. Then

    tau-tilde D_{A,H} tau-tilde^{-1} = D + c(d tau A) + i c(d tau eta) Gamma,

so D_{A,H} commutes with tau-tilde exactly when d tau A = A and d tau eta = eta, that is exactly when A and eta lie in P_tau. Since A is defined modulo 2 pi Lambda^*, D_{A,H} commutes with tau-tilde up to a gauge transformation e^{i k . z} exactly when d tau A - A lies in 2 pi Lambda^* and d tau eta = eta. Under the window hypothesis |A| < pi/l of Proposition 51.1, |d tau A - A| <= 2 |A| < 2 pi/l, which is below the length of every nonzero vector of 2 pi Lambda^*. So d tau A = A, and the two conditions agree. In that case the window block Y = c(A) + i c(eta) Gamma commutes with x on both chiralities, that is with the algebra C = span_R(1, x) of the mirror stratum (Proposition 49.4), which is the restriction to the first sheet of the subalgebra C_F of Chamseddine, Connes and Marcolli, so that [D_F, C_F] = 0, the condition that keeps the photon massless (Proposition 49.3), holds; conversely the blocks of Proposition 49.3 commuting with x are exactly the Y with A, eta in P_tau, a real four-dimensional family (two complex Yukawa couplings). A nonzero A, or a nonzero eta, in P_tau is fixed by tau and by no other element of S_3, since P_tau meets P_tau' in 0 for tau' != tau and a three-cycle fixes no nonzero vector of a (x) R^2; so a nonzero Wilson line or torsion breaks S_3 to the stabilizer of one mirror, and D_{A,H} commutes with the lift of the whole of S_3 exactly when A = eta = 0. So a first sheet at a symmetric point, whose algebra C + H requires invariance under the whole lifted stabilizer, carries no Yukawa block from these data, as Proposition 49.3 found for the invariant blocks. For v in the normal plane P_tau^perp = n_tau (x) R^2, x c(v) x^{-1} = -c(v): these are the components of the Higgs directions T A = T_p S^4 that anticommute with x, the charged ones of lemma 49.

*Proof.* tau is a linear isometry of R^4 preserving Lambda, so it acts on A, and tau-tilde preserves the periodic spin structure. The Dirac operator D commutes with every isometry lifted to the spinors, since c(d tau v) = x c(v) x^{-1} and partial transforms by d tau: tau-tilde D tau-tilde^{-1} = D. For the constant terms, tau-tilde c(A) tau-tilde^{-1} = x c(A) x^{-1} = c(d tau A), and tau-tilde i c(eta) Gamma tau-tilde^{-1} = i c(d tau eta) x Gamma x^{-1} = i c(d tau eta) Gamma, since x lies in Spin(4) and d tau has determinant one, so that x commutes with Gamma. The difference D_{A,H} - tau-tilde D_{A,H} tau-tilde^{-1} = c(A - d tau A) + i c(eta - d tau eta) Gamma vanishes exactly when both terms vanish, the j-linear and j-antilinear parts being independent (Proposition 46.1), that is when A and eta are fixed by d tau, whose fixed space is P_tau. The window block is the restriction of D_{A,H} to the constant spinors, on which tau-tilde acts as x; so it commutes with x. The real span of x on Sigma_4 with the identity is the algebra C of Proposition 49.4, and Proposition 49.3 identifies the commutation with x on both chiralities with the condition [D_F, C_F] = 0. For the converse count, the blocks commuting with x are X = diag(y_1, y_2) of complex dimension two (Proposition 49.3), real dimension four, and the Y with A, eta in P_tau form a real four-dimensional space of such blocks (A and eta in a two-plane; the map (A, eta) -> c(A) + i c(eta) Gamma is injective, by the injectivity of c and Proposition 46.1), so the two spaces coincide. P_tau = n_tau^perp (x) R^2 and P_tau' = n_tau'^perp (x) R^2 with n_tau, n_tau' linearly independent in the two-dimensional a, so n_tau^perp and n_tau'^perp are distinct lines of a and the planes meet in 0; a three-cycle acts on a by a rotation of order three, with no fixed vector, so its action on a (x) R^2 has none. For v in n_tau (x) R^2, d tau v = -v, and x c(v) x^{-1} = c(d tau v) = -c(v). ∎

**Proposition 51.3 (the rank-one pattern: the torsion as the quarter turn of the Wilson line).** Let A and eta lie in the mirror plane P_tau, a complex line for the complex structure J of A inherited from E, and let phi in [0, pi] be the angle between them. Then m_+-^2 = |A|^2 + |eta|^2 +- 2 |A| |eta| sin phi, and:

1. m_+ = m_- exactly when eta is parallel to A (sin phi = 0), the custodial case of Proposition 46.2 (1): a torsion along the Wilson line gives equal masses to the two members of the doublet.
2. m_+ = |A| + |eta| and m_- = ||A| - |eta|| exactly when eta is perpendicular to A, that is eta in R J A. Then (m_+ + m_-)/2 = max(|A|, |eta|) and (m_+ - m_-)/2 = min(|A|, |eta|): for |eta| <= |A| the Wilson line is the mean mass of the doublet and the torsion half its splitting. Put the up-type member on the Majorana line w, a weight line of x (Proposition 49.2 (iii); the up-type assignment of Proposition 50.1 (3) is an input). Then the up-type mass is |A + J_w eta| (item 4). So m_up = |A| + |eta| and m_down = |A| - |eta| when eta is a positive multiple of -J_w A, and the two masses are exchanged when eta is a positive multiple of J_w A. Which member is heavier is the sign of <J_w A, eta>, the oriented area of (A, eta) in the complex line (P_tau, J_w), a datum of the pair (A, eta).
3. m_- = 0 exactly when eta = +- J A: the heaviest quark, with its massless partner in this block, is the generation whose torsion dual is the quarter turn of its Wilson line in the complex structure of the elliptic curve; then m_+ = 2 |A| = 2 |eta|, and Y = {c(2A), P_mu} with mu the weight line of x whose complex structure is J_mu = -+J, so that eta = -J_mu A, lemma 50's delta' = -J_mu h/2 with h = 2A. It is the block c(h) P_w of lemma 50 exactly when the Majorana line w is mu, that is J_w = -+J for eta = +-J A.
4. For a weight line mu of x in Sigma_R, with complex structure J_mu = +-J, the coupling of Proposition 49.3 on mu has modulus |A + J_mu eta|. With the up-type member on the Majorana line w, y_u = A + J_w eta and y_d = A - J_w eta in the complex line (P_tau, J_w), up to a common phase and the conjugation of Proposition 49.3. In a complex coordinate z of P_tau in which J_w acts as i, y_u = z(A) + i z(eta) and conj(y_d) = z(A) - i z(eta). Hence |y_u|^2 = |A|^2 + |eta|^2 - 2 <J_w A, eta> and |y_d|^2 = |A|^2 + |eta|^2 + 2 <J_w A, eta>, with <J_w A, eta> = +-|A| |eta| sin phi. In the coordinate given by J these formulas hold when J_w = J, and they hold with i replaced by -i when J_w = -J. So the sign is fixed by which weight line of x is the Majorana line, an input, and no other convention enters.

The finite data of Corollary 16.8 with V_X = Sigma_4, A_{F,X} = C = span_R(1, x) (the mirror stratum, Proposition 49.4), d_X = Y with A, eta in P_tau, and V_Y = C^3 with M_3(C), d_Y = 0, are a 24-state bimodule over C + M_3(C) with the KO-6 signs, order zero and order one; every one-form vanishes, since C commutes with Y, so the masses are explicit (Proposition 17.3) and the algebra is the centralizer of the block (the centralizer of Y in C + H is span_R(1, x) for generic, rank-one and parallel data; checked by the referee), the algebra of the label 9 of lemma 50, Proposition 50.4; the orientability relation fails there: on the first summand every sum of products pi(a) b° lies in span_R(1, x) (x) M_3(C), which acts on the +i weight lines of x in Sigma_R and in Sigma_L by the same matrix, while Gamma_F = Gamma (x) 1 is 1 on the first and -1 on the second (referee).

*Proof.* In P_tau, |A wedge eta| = |A| |eta| sin phi, and the formula is Proposition 51.1. (1) sin phi = 0. (2) sin phi = 1 gives m_+-^2 = (|A| +- |eta|)^2; the perpendicular of A in the two-plane P_tau is R J A, J being orthogonal on P_tau; conversely m_+ = |A| + |eta| forces sin phi = 1 when A, eta are nonzero. The mass on the line w is |A + J_w eta| by item 4, which gives the assignment for each sign of <J_w A, eta>. (3) m_- = 0 exactly when |A|^2 + |eta|^2 = 2 |A| |eta| sin phi, which forces |A| = |eta| and sin phi = 1; then eta = +- J A, and Y = c(A) + i c(+- J A) Gamma; in lemma 50, Proposition 50.2 (3), the rank-one block c(h) P_w has j-linear half c(h)/2 and j-antilinear half i c(delta') Gamma with delta' = -J_w h/2, so A = h/2 and eta = delta' = -J_w A, that is J_w = -+ J, the sign according to which weight line of x carries the heavy member, since J_{jw} = -J_w (lemma 50, Proposition 50.2 (3)), and the two weight lines of x in Sigma_R are the two points of the twistor fibre with J_mu = J and J_mu = -J, since x|_{Sigma_R} = +-c(omega_J)/2 with omega_J the Kähler form of J (lemma 49, Reading; checked by the referee). (4) By Proposition 49.4 the Clifford element c(A) of a vector of the mirror plane is a block L_a commuting with x, with a complex, and A -> a(A) is a real-linear isometry P_tau -> C, complex linear for J_mu with mu the line on which x acts as i in the identification of Proposition 49.3, and i c(eta) Gamma = i L_c with c the image of eta; Proposition 49.3 gives y_1 = a + i c and conj(y_2) = a - i c, with |y_1|^2 and |y_2|^2 as displayed, Im(conj(a) c) being +- the area |A| |eta| sin phi. For the finite data, Theorem 16.7 applies formally to the real subalgebra C of End_C(Sigma_4) (Remark 16.5); the one-forms [D_F, pi(a)] are [Y, a_X] (x) 1 on Hom(V_Y, V_X) and vanish for a_X in span(1, x) by Proposition 51.2; Proposition 17.3 then gives the explicit masses; the centralizer statement is Proposition 50.4 with the summand C of A_SM embedded along the weight lines of x; Gamma acts as (1, -1) on the chiralities while x acts with eigenvalues +-i on both, so Gamma lies outside span_R(1, x), the hypothesis gamma_{V_X} in A_{F,X} of Corollary 16.8 fails, and the relation itself fails, since the products pi(a) b° act on the two +i weight lines by one matrix and Gamma_F by opposite signs. ∎

## Reading

Lemma 22 and lemma 25 read every finite Dirac operator as the lowest window of a derivative on an interval, unfolded to a circle with a constant connection; the present lemma is the four-torus form of that reading with the sheet of lemma 49 as the fibre: the custodial half of the Yukawa block is a Wilson line, the mass of a doublet without torsion is |A|, the internal momentum shifted by the holonomy, and a torsion along the Wilson line gives both members the mass (|A|^2 + |eta|^2)^{1/2} (the mechanism of Hosotani, recorded for the interval in lemma 40 and lemma 20, reading 3), and the up-down splitting, which no Wilson line and no displacement supplies, is a constant three-form, the torsion or flux term of the Dirac operator. Along a mirror of the Weyl group the two data make the two complex Yukawa couplings of a generation, y_u = A + i eta and conj(y_d) = A - i eta in the complex coordinate of the mirror, and the photon condition of Chamseddine, Connes and Marcolli, imposed there on D_F by hand, is the invariance of the Wilson line and the torsion under the transposition whose mirror carries them. The heaviest quark is the extreme in which the torsion is the quarter turn of the Wilson line; for the third generation in the perpendicular case the ratio |eta|/|A| = (m_t - m_b)/(m_t + m_b) is about 0.97 with the running masses at the scale m_t (m_t about 163 GeV, m_b about 2.8 GeV; 0.95 with m_t = 173 GeV and m_b = 4.2 GeV). For the second generation (m_c - m_s)/(m_c + m_s) is about 0.84 at a common scale (m_c/m_s about 11.8; 0.86 with m_c(m_c) = 1.27 GeV and m_s(2 GeV) = 0.093 GeV). The first generation has m_d > m_u, so in the perpendicular case its torsion has the opposite orientation relative to J_w A, with |eta|/|A| = (m_d - m_u)/(m_d + m_u) about 0.37. The lighter generations are further from the rank-one pattern. (Running values recalled, not computed here; ratios at a common scale are independent of the QCD running.) The symmetric points of A, where the algebra is C + H, carry no Yukawa block and the quark sector is massless with the full algebra; the mirror, where the algebra is C, carries the block with explicit masses, no one-forms and the algebra C + M_3(C) of the label 9: the two strata are the two ends of the 10 -> 9 reduction of the quark sector, as a reading, with the choice of mirror the choice of the photon circle among three (lemma 49). ## Scope

The flat four-torus with the periodic spin structure and constant data A, H; the window statement needs |A| + |eta| < pi/l, and the compression is exact because the window is a reducing subspace, so no estimate is involved; the Wilson line is a gauge class modulo 2 pi Lambda^*, which under the window hypothesis changes nothing in Proposition 51.2; the constant s != 0 of the three-form term (verified in Agricola's lectures) and the sign of eta depend on conventions and enter no statement; the sign that does enter, which weight line of x is the Majorana line, is an input (Proposition 51.3); the equivariance statement uses the lift of the transposition as an isometry of the whole torus, and the finite data at the mirror stratum are those of Corollary 16.8 with the algebra of Proposition 49.4, as an identification of data, the crossed product of lemma 48 seeing the orbit types as quotients; the one-generation form with one Wilson line and one three-form, the three-generation form being left as a direction; the E factor of T^6, whose spinors double the fibre and whose own Wilson line c(A_E) is even for the chirality of A, is outside the statements; the reading of the two strata as the ends of the 10 -> 9 reduction and the moduli-space lead are readings. Nothing here derives A or H from an action, and no potential selects eta = +- J A. Two remarks of the referee, in their scope: in heterotic flux compactifications the Dirac operator of the gauge fermions uses the connection with torsion one third (Kimura and Yi, arXiv:hep-th/0605247, abstract), the value s = 1/4 here, which would enter a flux-quantization condition relating |eta| to the integer fluxes of H over the three-cycles of A; and on the compact flat torus a constant H != 0 solves no leading-order NS-NS equation, since the trace of R_ij + 2 nabla_i nabla_j phi - (1/4) H_ikl H_j^kl = 0 integrates to (1/4) int |H|^2 = 0, so the direction below needs a source or another background (Lie groups with parallel torsion, where nabla^{+-1/4} are flat; brane or orientifold sources; higher orders), a remark not checked by a second model and leaving the statements, which take A and H as data, unchanged. The direction the result points to: a potential or a quantization condition on the torus that relates the three-form to the Wilson line (a flux quantization of H over three-cycles of A, with the Wilson line on the dual cycle); the E factor, where a Wilson line gives an even block, the candidate for the connecting operator of the lepton generation; and the identification of the point of A, a flat SU(3)-connection on E, with the Wilson line on A.
