## Proposition 5.1 (the two families of 84 are not isometric)

**Statement.** Let H_F be the 96 roots of three families, the top block T the 12 roots of the up-type quark of one family f_t with its conjugate, and the neutrino block N the 12 roots of the neutrinos of the three families with their conjugates; F1 = H_F − T and F2 = H_F − N. Then F1 and F2 have the same numbers of pairs at each inner product (42 at −2, 816 at −1, 1812 at 0, 816 at +1), but no isometry of R^8 maps F1 onto F2. In particular no element of the Weyl group of E8 does, and no isometry of H_F maps T onto N.

**Proof.** For a root v of a set S let d_S(v) be the number of roots of S at inner product +1 with v; it is preserved by isometries. Two half-integer roots have inner product +1 exactly when their signs agree in six of the eight places.

1. d_{H_F} = 22 for every root. H_F is a single orbit of the group generated by the Weyl group of SO(10) (transitive on the 16, a minuscule representation), the permutations of the three families and the negation. For ν^c of family 0, (𝟙 | ++ | 𝟙), the roots of H_F with exactly two minus signs are: two in the first five places with family 𝟙 (10), two among the family places (2, the patterns of families 1 and 2 in H_F), and antiparticles with one minus sign in each block (5 x 2). The total is 22.
2. Removing N. For u_L = (c | −+ | f): the inner product with ν_L(g) is (1 + f·g)/4 and with ν^c(g) is (1 + f·g)/4, where f·g = 3 for g = f and −1 otherwise; so u_L has exactly 2 roots of N at +1 (its own family's ν_L and ν^c), and the same holds for u^c and the conjugates. For d_L = (c | +− | f): the inner product with ν_L(g) is (−3 + f·g)/4, which is −1 for g ≠ f, so the two roots −ν_L(g), g ≠ f, are at +1, and ν^c(f) is at +1: 3 in all; the same count holds for d^c, e_L, e^c and the conjugates. So in F2, d = 20 on the 36 up-type quark roots and d = 19 on the 48 others.
3. Removing T. For a neutrino of the top's family, ν_L(f_t) = (−𝟙 | −+ | f_t): its inner products with t_L(c) = (c | −+ | f_t) and with t^c = (−c | ++ | f_t) are (−1 + 2 + 3)/4 = 1 and (1 + 0 + 3)/4 = 1, six roots at +1; for g ≠ f_t, ν_L(g) has inner product (−1 + 2 − 1)/4 = 0 with t_L and (1 + 0 − 1)/4 = 0 with t^c. The same holds for ν^c. So in F1, d = 16 on the 4 roots of the neutrinos of the top's family and d = 22 on the 8 roots of the other neutrinos (the up-type quarks of the other families have 2 roots of T at +1 and the down-type quarks and charged leptons 3, by inner products of the same kind as in item 2, so d = 20 and d = 19).

F1 has roots with d = 16 and d = 22, and F2 has none. The sums of d agree, 4·16 + 48·19 + 24·20 + 8·22 = 48·19 + 36·20 = 1632 = 2 x 816, which is the equality of the pair counts at +1. Both sets are closed under negation, so the pairs at −1 are as many as those at +1 and the pairs at −2 are the 42 antipodal pairs; the 1812 pairs at 0 are the remainder of the 3486 pairs. ∎

**Scope.** The roots of H_F in E8, through the embedding above, which is unique up to W(E8) for the aligned (16, 3) + (16bar, 3bar); isometries of R^8 acting on the roots. Escapes: maps that are not linear on the roots (bijections of states with no geometric meaning), other realizations of H_F in a larger group, and a fourth family (Proposition 5.2). The tau neutrino carries the obstruction: in F1 the neutrino of the top's own family is the only state whose neighbourhood changes by six.

## Proposition 5.2 (four families: the transposition of colour and family)

**Referee note (GPT-6.1 Terra, 4 Nov 2026).** The Weyl map and block exchange are independently checked. Family-zero lepton species blocks are fixed setwise; \(\nu^c\) and \(e^c\) individually go to their negatives. See the [referee report](../historical/reviews/2026-11-04_terra_check_11d.md#proposition-52-four-family-colour-family-transposition).

**Statement.** Let P be the permutation of coordinates that exchanges the three colour coordinates with the three family coordinates and the two weak coordinates with each other, and M = −P. Then M is an involution in the Weyl group of E8 that preserves the 128 roots of the four families and SU(2)_L, and acts on the up-type and down-type states as the transposition of the four Pati-Salam colours (r, g, b, lepton) with the four families (1, 2, 3, 0), with the lepton paired with family 0 and the colour c paired with the family −c. In particular:

1. the up-type quark of family 0 (t_L and t^c, three colours, with conjugates) goes onto the neutrinos of families 1, 2, 3 (ν_L and the conjugate of ν^c), and back;
2. the down-type quark of family 0 goes onto the charged leptons of families 1, 2, 3;
3. nu_L and e_L of family 0 are fixed, nu^c and e^c of family 0 go to their negatives, u^c and d^c go to conjugate roots, and the quarks of families 1, 2, 3 go to quarks of families 1, 2, 3 with colour and family transposed.

So the four-family sets 128 − T(0) and 128 − N(1, 2, 3), each of 116 roots, are exchanged.

**Proof.** P is an even permutation of coordinates and −1 is in the Weyl group of E8, so M is in it; M^2 = P^2 = 1. Both preserve the set of half-integer vectors with an even number of minus signs. P maps (χ | w_1 w_2 | φ) to (φ | w_2 w_1 | χ), so M maps it to (−φ | −w_2 −w_1 | −χ). The weak part (−+) goes to (−+) and (++) to (−−), so T_3 of SU(2)_L, (w_2 − w_1)/2, is preserved and T_3 of SU(2)_R is reversed. Then t_L(c) = (c | −+ | 𝟙) goes to (−𝟙 | −+ | −c) = ν_L(−c); t^c = (−c | ++ | 𝟙) goes to (−𝟙 | −− | c) = −ν^c(−c); b_L(c) = (c | +− | 𝟙) goes to (−𝟙 | +− | −c) = e_L(−c); ν_L(0) = (−𝟙 | −+ | 𝟙) and e_L(0) are fixed; u_L(c, f) = (c | −+ | f) goes to u_L(−f, −c), with −f a quark colour and −c a family pattern. ∎

The search found further signed permutations with the same effect on the two blocks; P itself maps T(0) onto N(1, 2, 3) up to conjugation, and M = J ∘ P, with J = −1 the real structure of Proposition 4.6.

## Proposition 5.3 (the flip in a four-family finite geometry)

Then:

1. F is unitary, F^2 = 1, and F commutes with γ and J.
2. For the up-type Dirac blocks T_top = Y ⊗ P_q ⊗ P_0 and T_ν = Y ⊗ P_ℓ ⊗ (1 − P_0), where Y maps the up state of C^2_L to the up state of C^2_R (and Y^* back), P_ℓ and P_q = 1 − P_ℓ are the lepton and quark projectors of C^4_c and P_0 the projector on e_0, one has F T_top F^{-1} = T_ν, and likewise for the down-type blocks of family 0 and the charged leptons of families 1, 2, 3.
3. So the heavy limits D + t T_top and D + t T_ν, for D commuting with F, are conjugate by F: their light spaces, of 116 states each, correspond. The light algebras A ∩ {T}' correspond when the algebra is invariant under F; the Pati-Salam and Standard Model algebras act on C^4_c alone and are not.

**Proof.** F acts on the factor C^4_c ⊗ C^4_f only, where it conjugates a ⊗ b to ι^{-1} b ι ⊗ ι a ι^{-1}, so P_q ⊗ P_0 goes to P_ℓ ⊗ (1 − P_0) by the choice of ι. γ and Y act on the other factor, and J F J^{-1} on conj(H_f) is the conjugate flip. ∎

F equals M on the left-handed states and M followed by the particle-antiparticle exchange on the right-handed ones (second check): F exchanges T_top and T_nu inside H_f, while M sends right-handed states to antiparticle roots and so flips gamma on 64 states; F lies outside W(E8). ## Proposal 5.5 (four slots, three zero modes)

**Referee note (GPT-6.1 Terra, 4 Nov 2026).** General bundle zero modes are outside the root-slot hypotheses. The index gives net chirality, and split bundles do not automatically reduce to three distinct root slots; the Weyl action on a specified bundle and its zero modes remains open. See the [referee report](../historical/reviews/2026-11-04_terra_check_11d.md#proposal-55-four-slot-bundles-and-three-families).

In heterotic compactifications on a Calabi-Yau threefold with E8 ⊃ SO(10) × SU(4), 248 = (45, 1) + (1, 15) + (16, 4) + (16bar, 4bar) + (10, 6), the SU(4) is the structure group of a rank-four bundle V, and the families are the zero modes, the 16s coming from H^1(X, V); their net number is the index |c_3(V)|/2, which is 3 in the heterotic Standard Models built on SU(4) bundles (Braun, He, Ovrut and Pantev, hep-th/0501070; recalled without rereading). In that reading the four slots of this file are the fibre of V, and each family is a zero mode, a section of V that in general involves all four slots. Propositions 5.1 and 5.4 do not apply to it, since they assume one slot per family.

The transposition M of Proposition 5.2 then exchanges the Pati-Salam SU(4), a gauge group, with the structure group SU(4) of V: colour and holonomy trade places, and SO(10) goes to the commutant SO(10)' of the other SU(4). The top-neutrino exchange becomes a statement about the fibre, which an index map must carry to the zero modes. The test is an explicit bundle. For a split bundle V = L_0 + L_1 + L_2 + L_3 (line-bundle models), slot i carries n_i = −χ(L_i) families with n_0 + ... + n_3 = 3; such models fall under Proposition 5.1 only for n = (1, 1, 1, 0) up to order; for every n_i >= 0 with sum 3 the degree multisets still differ (second check, 40 cases), so the obstruction persists for aligned split bundles. A non-split V, whose zero modes are spread over the slots, is the case to compute.

## Proposition 5.6 (the so(9) of the Chevalley involution is conjugate to the Spin(9) of Reading 3)

Let h be a Cartan subalgebra of e8 contained in the regular sl(9) of lemma 33, section 3, and let theta be the Chevalley involution of e8 for h: in a Chevalley basis, theta acts as -1 on h and maps e_alpha to -e_{-alpha}. Let sigma be the involution of the root table of this lemma, +1 on h and on the integer roots and -1 on the half-integer roots; its fixed algebra is so(16)_sigma and its -1 eigenspace is the 128 of the four families. Then:

1. theta preserves sl(9) and acts on it by an outer involution, whose fixed algebra so(9)_theta is conjugate in SL(9) to the so(9) of lemma 33.
2. The fixed algebra k_theta of theta on e8 is isomorphic to so(16). As so(9)_theta-modules, k_theta = 36 + 84 and p_theta = 44 + 84, where p_theta is the -1 eigenspace. The 44 equals p_theta ∩ sl(9) and lies in the gravity sector. The two 84 are the graphs {x + theta x} and {x - theta x}, x in Λ^3 C^9, and each meets the C_3 and C_6 sectors equally.
3. so(9)_theta acts on the 16-dimensional vector of k_theta by the spinor.
4. so(9)_theta is conjugate in E8 to the Spin(9) of Reading 3, the image of Spin(9) under the spinor 16 inside so(16)_sigma, in the class with 128 -> 44 + 84. Under both, 248 = 36 + 44 + 84 + 84.
5. In E8 both connected subgroups are isomorphic to SO(9). The other spinor class, with an irreducible 128 and 248 = 36 + 84 + 128, is isomorphic to Spin(9).

**Proof.** (1) theta maps the root system A_8 to itself, since it acts as -1 on h, so it preserves sl(9). On sl(9) it acts as -1 on a Cartan subalgebra, and -1 lies outside W(A_8), so the involution is outer. Since 9 is odd, every outer involution of sl(9) has the form X -> -B^{-1} X^T B with B symmetric; its fixed algebra so(B) has dimension 36 and is conjugate in SL(9) to the so(9) of lemma 33. The conjugating element lies in SL(9), which commutes with the centre of SU(9), so it preserves the three sectors.

(2) The fixed algebra of theta is spanned by the e_alpha - e_{-alpha}, so it has dimension 120, the number of positive roots; it is the complexified maximal compact subalgebra of the split form e8(8), which is so(16) (Cartan's classification; Helgason, chapter X). On sl(9), theta has fixed part so(9)_theta (36) and -1 part 44, which is h plus the span of the e_alpha + e_{-alpha} for alpha a positive root of A_8. theta maps Λ^3 to Λ^6. For Y in so(9)_theta, theta [Y, x] = [Y, theta x], so the maps x -> x ± theta x from Λ^3 are so(9)_theta-equivariant and injective, and their images are the fixed and -1 parts of Λ^3 + Λ^6, each isomorphic to Λ^3 C^9 = 84.

(3) As k_theta-modules, k_theta = Λ^2 W with W the vector 16, so Λ^2(W restricted to so(9)_theta) = 36 + 84. The 16-dimensional representations of so(9) are 16, 9 + 7·1 and 16·1, with exterior squares 36 + 84, 36 + 7·9 + 21·1 and 120·1. Only the spinor fits.

(4) Complex e8 has two conjugacy classes of involutions, with fixed algebras so(16) (dimension 120) and e7 + sl(2) (dimension 136) (Cartan; Kac's classification of automorphisms of finite order). So there is g in E8 with g theta g^{-1} = sigma; g maps k_theta to so(16)_sigma and p_theta to the 128, and g(so(9)_theta) is a spin(9) in so(16)_sigma, acting on the 16 by the spinor by (3) and on the 128 as 44 + 84 by (2). Let rho_1 and rho_2 be two embeddings of spin(9) in so(16)_sigma through the spinor, both with 128 -> 44 + 84. The spinor is the only 16-dimensional irreducible representation and its invariant symmetric form is unique up to scale, so rho_2 = Ad(A) rho_1, up to an inner automorphism of so(9), for some A in O(16). If det A = -1, then Ad(A) exchanges the two half-spinors, and rho_2 would act on the 128 through the other half-spinor of rho_1, which is the irreducible 128. So A lies in SO(16), and the image in E8 of either lift of A to Spin(16) conjugates rho_1 to rho_2. Hence the class with 128 -> 44 + 84 is a single class under the subgroup Spin(16)/Z_2 of E8, and g(so(9)_theta) lies in it.

(5) The modules 36, 44 and 84 are tensor representations, on which -1 of Spin(9) acts trivially, so in the class with 248 = 36 + 44 + 84 + 84 the connected subgroup of E8 is SO(9), of trivial centre. The 128 has half-integer weights, on which -1 acts as -1, so in the other class the subgroup is Spin(9). ∎

The statement is the one recorded by Claude Sonnet 5.5 on 4 November 2026 in Reading 3b (c); the proof is by Claude Opus 5.5 as referee (5 November 2026, historical/reviews/2026-11-05_opus_check_05_21_34.md), who also computed, from weight multisets with exact fractions, the restrictions 80 = 36 + 44 and Λ^3 = 84 for the so(9) of sl(9), and 120 = 36 + 84 with the two half-spinors 44 + 84 and 128 for the spinor Spin(9) in SO(16); both classes have Dynkin embedding index 2, and the vector class so(9) ⊂ so(16) through 9 + 7·1 has index 1 and 248 = 36 + 21·1 + 7·9 + 8·16. The proof needs no claim that the decomposition 36 + 44 + 84 + 84 by itself is a single conjugacy class.

## Readings (proposals)

1. **Three families are the obstruction.** The exchange of lemma 21 is a symmetry of the E8 geometry exactly in the four-family completion, where the colour-family square is 4 x 4 and the top belongs to the family paired with the lepton colour. With three families in single slots the square is 4 x 3 and the exchange is not an isometry (Propositions 5.1 and 5.4, in their scope of one root per state). Lemma 21's 96 = 84 + 12 in two ways is the three-family shadow of 128 = 116 + 12 in two ways, exchanged by M.
2. **The two heavy directions of the algebra chain.** Canonical §2.2 and §3 obtain the chain Pati-Salam -> Standard Model -> colour plus electromagnetism from two heavy operators: the Majorana direction, which selects the lepton among the four Pati-Salam colours and ν_R among the SU(2)_R states, and the top Yukawa coupling, which selects the heavy family and the up-type state. M exchanges the lepton colour with family 0 and keeps the up-type direction (F, its finite-geometry counterpart, differs from it on right-handed states): it exchanges the direction selected by the first heavy operator with the direction selected by the second. If the 11 label is the grand algebra M_2(H) + M_4(C) (lemma 28) and the 9 label colour plus electromagnetism, M exchanges the two steps of 11 -> 10 -> 9. This needs a finite geometry in which the generation space is C^4 with an algebra exchangeable with M_4(C), and a Dirac operator compatible with the exchange.
3. **Four families and eleven dimensions.** The 128 of the four families is the chiral spinor of SO(16) ⊂ E8; under Spin(9) ⊂ SO(16), embedded through its spinor, it decomposes as 44 + 84 in the class with 128 -> 44 + 84 (the other chirality of the spinor embedding gives an irreducible 128), the graviton and the three-form of eleven-dimensional supergravity (Pengpan and Ramond, hep-th/9808190; Kostant's cubic Dirac operator on F4/Spin(9); recalled). The two families of 116 are not Spin(9) subrepresentations; whether the exchange M has a Spin(9) meaning is open.

3b. **The arithmetic 44 = 32 + 12 and the two placements of the top (observation of the user, 4 Nov 2026).** Observation with an open question; nothing beyond (a) to (d) is claimed.

(a) With four families, 128 = 44 + 84 under the Spin(9) of Reading 3, and 128 − 84 = 44 = 32 + 12: removing F1 (three families minus the top, 84 states) leaves the fourth family plus the top block, and removing F2 (three families minus the neutrinos) leaves it plus the neutrino block. In this count the top goes with the fourth family into the graviton number 44, and dually the neutrinos do: the four-family completion of the 96 = 84 + 12 of lemma 21 in two ways. M of Proposition 5.2 exchanges the two blocks of 12 that fill the 44, T(0) and N(1, 2, 3); it sends each family to the states of one colour (family 0 to the leptons), so it does not carry one set of 44 onto the other.

(b) The fit is numerical in the present scope. Proposition 3.8 excludes a colour-equivariant identification of Λ^3 R^9 with either family of 84 for any linear action of colour on the 9. The colour SU(3) lies in no such Spin(9) (Claude Opus 5.5, 4 Nov 2026; checked by Claude Sonnet 5.5): the vector 16 of SO(16) is 3 + 3bar + 10 (1) under colour, while the spinor 16 of Spin(9) restricted to an SU(3) inside Spin(9) is 16 (1), 2 (3 + 3bar) + 4 (1) or 8 + 8, by the three real nine-dimensional representations of Proposition 3.8. The stabilizer in so(16) of the split 44 + 84 is spin(9) itself (the intermediate subalgebras would be submodules of 36 + 84, and so(16) is irreducible on the 128), so the split is not colour-covariant; and no set of 84 roots spans the 84, since a root partition has the rank-8 Cartan subalgebra in its stabilizer. Also, colour has 248 = 8 + 78 (1) + 27 (3 + 3bar), while the 44 of any so(9) of e8 with 248 = 36 + 44 + 84 + 84 contains a 6 or a 27, so colour lies in no such so(9), including that of file 04. **Independent check of Proposition 5.1 (the neighbour table).**
*From the referee report on the 11D line, section 'Proposition 5.1: aligned three-family root configurations', transferred verbatim in substance on 4 Nov 2026.*

Write a half-integer root as (s | f)/2 with five colour and weak signs s and a family sign pattern f. Distinct family patterns have inner product -1 on the family part and identical ones have 3. For two particle roots in the same slot, inner product +1 means that the five-sign patterns have Hamming distance two. For distinct slots, a particle root is a +1 neighbour exactly when the five-sign patterns are equal, and an antiparticle root is a +1 neighbour exactly when the five-sign patterns have distance four; no antiparticle in the same slot is a +1 neighbour. Let U, D, E, N denote the up-quark, down-quark, charged-lepton and neutrino blocks of one slot, each with its antiparticles. The number of +1 neighbours of one root of the row block among all roots of a column block, for the same slot and for a different slot, is

| source | U | D | E | N |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| U | 4 / 2 | 3 / 3 | 1 / 1 | 2 / 0 |
| D | 3 / 3 | 4 / 2 | 2 / 0 | 1 / 1 |
| E | 3 / 3 | 6 / 0 | 0 / 2 | 1 / 1 |
| N | 6 / 0 | 3 / 3 | 1 / 1 | 0 / 2 |

The entries follow from the root types and colour choices of the Setting and the Hamming criterion; for instance a neutrino has six same-slot up-quark neighbours and none in another slot, and a charged lepton has six same-slot down-quark neighbours and none in another slot. In three aligned slots each row sums to 10 + 2 x 6 = 22, the degree in H_F. Removing all three neutrino blocks gives degree 20 on the 36 up-quark roots and 19 on the 48 down-quark and charged-lepton roots. Removing the top block instead gives degree 22 - 6 = 16 to the neutrinos of its slot, 22 to the other eight neutrino roots, 20 to the 24 remaining up-quark roots and 19 to the 48 others. So F1 has degrees 16 and 22 that F2 lacks; both sets are closed under negation with centroid zero, so an affine Euclidean isometry between them would fix the origin and preserve degrees, and none exists. The degree sums 4(16) + 48(19) + 24(20) + 8(22) = 48(19) + 36(20) = 1632 give 816 unordered pairs at inner product +1 in each set, 816 at -1 by negation, 42 antipodal pairs at -2, and the remaining C(84, 2) - 42 - 2(816) = 1812 pairs at inner product zero. Scope: the indicated half-root embedding, three aligned slots, complete species blocks and Euclidean isometries.

**Independent check of Proposition 5.2.**
*From the section 'Proposition 5.2: four-family colour-family transposition'.*

P exchanges three pairs of colour and family coordinates and the two weak coordinates; negating all eight coordinates is an even sign change, and signed permutations with an even number of sign changes preserve the D_8 roots and lie in W(D_8), a subgroup of W(E8). So M = -P is in W(E8), M^2 = 1, and M preserves the half-integer roots with an even number of minus signs. For a quark colour pattern c and the all-plus family pattern 1,

    M(c | -+ | 1) = (-1 | -+ | -c) = nu_L(-c),     M(-c | ++ | 1) = (-1 | -- | c) = -nu^c(-c),

and the three patterns -c are the other three family patterns; with negatives this is the whole neutrino block, and M^2 = 1 gives the reverse map. **Counterexample (referee) to Proposition 5.3 without unitarity.**

**Independent proof of the index statement in Proposal 5.5, with the net-chirality caution.**
*From the referee report on the 11D line, section 'Proposal 5.5: four-slot bundles and three families', transferred verbatim in substance on 4 Nov 2026.*

Verdict of the referee: correct with amendment as a possible escape. General bundle zero modes are sections, and the isometry hypotheses of Propositions 5.1 and 5.4, which concern individual fibre roots, do not automatically apply to them. The index counts net chirality, and the proposed Weyl map on zero modes remains to be constructed.

*Index.* On a Calabi-Yau threefold X, for a rank-four SU(4) bundle V, c_1(V) = 0 gives ch(V) = 4 - c_2(V) + c_3(V)/2, and c_1(X) = 0 gives Td(X) = 1 + c_2(X)/12. Riemann-Roch gives

    chi(V) = int_X ch(V) Td(X) = (1/2) int_X c_3(V).

When H^0(V) = H^3(V) = 0, this is -h^1(V) + h^2(V), with h^2(V) = h^1(V^*) by Serre duality, since K_X is trivial. Thus index three gives three net chiral families. Exactly three 16 zero modes additionally requires the opposite-chirality cohomology H^1(V^*) to vanish.

*Split bundles.* For a split bundle V = L_0 + L_1 + L_2 + L_3, the integers n_i = -chi(L_i) are signed indices; their sum being three does not force one zero mode in each of three distinct slots. Even in that special distribution, applying Proposition 5.1 to normalized global sections requires an identification of the physical zero-mode metric with the root configuration.

*The Weyl map.* The fibre Weyl transposition M can exchange the abstract subgroups, but a symmetry of a particular compactification must also act compatibly on the bundle, the connection, the zero-mode spaces, their inner products and any Wilson-line data. Establishing such an action is an open construction; the escape from the root no-go alone does not establish it.
