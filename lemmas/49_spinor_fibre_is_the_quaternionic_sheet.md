Along the orbit types of A the lifted stabilizer algebras are C + H at the nine S_3-fixed points, C on the mirror planes of the transpositions, where the generated algebra is the circle of the lifted transposition acting on both chiralities, the C_F of Chamseddine, Connes and Marcolli restricted to the first sheet and the unbroken circle of Corollary 46.4, and R elsewhere: unitary groups U(1) x SU(2), U(1) and {+-1} (Proposition 49.4). With the whole fibre as the first sheet, Corollary 16.8 gives a 24-state bimodule over C + H + M_3(C), the quark summand H_F^(3) of Chamseddine, Connes and Marcolli for one generation, with its grading the chirality of A and their printed pairing (Proposition 49.5).

## Setting

Let A = a (x) R^2 be the flat four-torus of lemma 48, with tangent space R^4 = a (x) R^2 at every point, a the standard representation of S_3, and the periodic spin structure, the only one invariant under S_3 (lemma 48, Setting). Its spinor fibre is Sigma_4 = C^4 = Sigma_+ + Sigma_-, the two half-spin representations of Spin(4) = Sp(1)_+ x Sp(1)_-, each a quaternionic line H on which its Sp(1) acts by left multiplication and the other trivially; the quaternionic structure j, the right multiplication by the unit quaternion j on each line, commutes with Spin(4), with the Clifford multiplication c(v) by vectors v of R^4, and with the chirality Gamma = c(e_1) c(e_2) c(e_3) c(e_4), which is +1 on Sigma_+ and -1 on Sigma_-. We orient A so that the lifts of order three of the three-cycles act trivially on Sigma_+; for the complex orientation of A inherited from E, with Gamma = c(e_1) c(e_2) c(e_3) c(e_4) in an oriented orthonormal basis, they act trivially on Sigma_- (the generator of the rotation of a three-cycle is an anti-self-dual two-form for that orientation), so the orientation used here is the opposite one, and with the complex orientation the axis is p = -e_5; with this convention Sigma_R = Sigma_+ = L_p, which carries Sp(1)_b and H_R as in lemmas 42 and 46 (referee). The real structure of the spin geometry of A, with J_M^2 = -1, J_M D = D J_M and J_M Gamma = Gamma J_M, anticommutes with the Hermitian c(v) and equals j Gamma up to a phase; it agrees with j up to sign on each chirality, so H_R + H_L and C + H are the same for both, while on the odd blocks the roles are exchanged, c(v) being J_M-antilinear and i c(v) Gamma J_M-linear; the words quaternionic and anti-quaternionic below refer to j, the structure of Example 16.9 (referee). The circle of the summand C below is the T_3R circle; lemma 42 calls it the hypercharge with Y = charge/2, which is exact on the Higgs, where B - L = 0, and the hypercharge of the spectral Standard Model is its unimodular combination with the U(1) of M_3(C). Put Gamma_a = c(e_a) for a = 1, ..., 4 and Gamma_5 = Gamma: five Hermitian operators with Gamma_a Gamma_b + Gamma_b Gamma_a = 2 delta_ab, commuting with j, whose real span together with the Gamma_a Gamma_b is End_H(Sigma_4) = M_2(H). The unitary group of End_H(Sigma_4) is Sp(2), containing Spin(4) as the stabilizer of Gamma_5; under Spin(4) the vector R^4 is the (2, 2) and acts, through c, as the odd Hermitian j-linear maps Sigma_+ -> Sigma_- and back. The lifted stabilizer of the orbit type (a, a, a) is Dic_3 = <a, x> in Spin(4), x the lift of a transposition, of order four, and c-tilde = -a the lift of order three of a three-cycle (lemma 48, Proposition 48.1). Sigma_6 restricted to Dic_3 is twice Sigma_4 (lemma 48), so the whole fibre of T^6 carries two copies of everything below, with the spinors of E as multiplicity space, on which T E and the inversion act; c(e_5) and c(e_6) anticommute with the c(e_a) and commute with the chirality of A, outside the Cl(5) below.

## Statements

**Proposition 49.1 (the sheet as the spinor fibre).** (Sigma_4, Gamma_1, ..., Gamma_5, j) is the quaternionic sheet W = H^2 of Example 16.9 and lemma 46 with the axis p = e_5: Gamma_p is the chirality, L_p = Sigma_+ and L_p^perp = Sigma_-, the stabilizer K_p = Sp(1)_b x Sp(1)_a is Spin(4) = Sp(1)_+ x Sp(1)_-, and the bidoublet Hom_H(L_p, L_p^perp) = T_p S^4 of lemma 42 is the Clifford action of the tangent space of A: v -> c(v) restricted to Sigma_+ is a Spin(4)-equivariant isomorphism R^4 -> Hom_H(Sigma_+, Sigma_-). The even algebra H + H of Example 16.9 for this axis is End_H(Sigma_+) + End_H(Sigma_-), the Pati-Salam pair acting on the two chiralities, and the odd Hermitian blocks of lemma 46 are the j-linear c(v) and the j-antilinear i c(v) Gamma.

*Proof.* Cl(4) acts on Sigma_4 irreducibly with c(v)^2 = |v|^2 and c(v)^* = c(v) for the Hermitian metric of the spinors, and the chirality Gamma anticommutes with every c(v), is Hermitian and squares to one; so Gamma_1, ..., Gamma_5 generate Cl(5) on C^4, and Example 16.9 and lemma 46 apply with these generators. The real algebra End_H(Sigma_4) has real dimension 16 and contains the 16 linearly independent elements 1, Gamma_a, Gamma_a Gamma_b, so it is their real span, M_2(H); Spin(4) is the subgroup of Sp(2) = U(End_H(Sigma_4)) that commutes with Gamma_5, since the even Clifford algebra of R^4 is Spin(4)'s and the stabilizer of Gamma_5 in Sp(2) is the unitary group of the even algebra H + H, which is Sp(1) x Sp(1). The map v -> c(v)|_{Sigma_+} is Spin(4)-equivariant by the covariance of Clifford multiplication, injective, and both sides have real dimension four, Hom_H between two quaternionic lines being a copy of H. The rest is lemma 46, Proposition 46.1, with Gamma_p = Gamma. ∎

The identification is the isomorphism of Cl(5)-modules with quaternionic structure that matches c(e_a) with Gamma_a for a = 1, ..., 4 and Gamma with Gamma_5, unique up to a phase by Schur's lemma. Composing it with an element of Sp(2) carries the axis to any point of S^4: the axis is fixed by the four-torus, whose symmetry at a point is Spin(4), and it remains an input of the twistor window, whose symmetry is Sp(2) (referee).

**Proposition 49.2 (the lifted stabilizer inside the sheet).** (i) The lifts of order three of the three-cycles act trivially on one chirality, which we call Sigma_R, and by rotations of order three on the other, Sigma_L; the lifts of order six act as -1 on Sigma_R and with order six on Sigma_L; the lift x of a transposition acts on both with eigenvalues +-i, and the three transpositions give the same circle on Sigma_R up to sign. So Dic_3 -> Sp(1)_R has image the cyclic group Z_4 generated by x|_{Sigma_R}, and Dic_3 -> Sp(1)_L is the faithful embedding of the binary dihedral group in SU(2). (ii) The real algebra generated by Dic_3 on Sigma_4 is C + H, with C = R[x|_{Sigma_R}] = span_R(1, x|_{Sigma_R}) acting on Sigma_R as (lambda, conj(lambda)) in the eigenbasis of x, and H = End_H(Sigma_L) the real span of the faithful representation: the part acting on the first sheet of the Standard Model subalgebra A_F = {(lambda, q_L, lambda, m)} of Chamseddine, Connes and Marcolli (arXiv:hep-th/0610241, Proposition 2.11 and Definition 2.14, where every embedding of C in H is conjugate to this one), inside the Pati-Salam H_R + H_L of Example 16.9, with C the Cartan subalgebra of H_R generated by the lifted transposition; in their model the same lambda also acts on the lepton line of the second sheet. (iii) Consequently the circle generated by the lifted transposition x is a maximal torus of Sp(1)_R, the same for the three transpositions. It is the U(1)_b of lemma 42 exactly when the connector's line Cw is one of its two weight lines, and it is the torus U(1)_X of Corollary 46.4 exactly when X^* X has distinct eigenvalues with the weight lines of x as eigenlines, as for the blocks of Proposition 49.3 with |y_1| != |y_2|; its two weight lines in Sigma_R are the two right-handed singlets on which C acts by lambda and conj(lambda).

*Proof.* (i) Spin(4) = Sp(1)_+ x Sp(1)_- acts on R^4 = H by v -> p v conj(q). Identify a with C by a complex structure, so that R^4 = a (x) R^2 = C (x) R^2 = C^2 = H; the differential of a three-cycle, the rotation by 2 pi/3 of a tensored with the identity of R^2, is the complex scalar e^{2 pi i/3} on C^2, a rotation by the same angle and in the same sense in two orthogonal planes, which is left (or right) multiplication by a unit complex number: an element of one Sp(1) factor only. Its lift c-tilde of order three therefore acts trivially on the chirality of the other factor, Sigma_R, and on Sigma_L by a rotation of order three; the lift -c-tilde acts as -1 on Sigma_R and with order six on Sigma_L. The differential of a transposition is the rotation by pi in the two-plane n (x) R^2 (lemma 48, Proposition 48.1), with eigenvalues (-1, -1, 1, 1). The lift x has x^2 = -1 (lemma 48), and -1 = (-1, -1) in Sp(1)_+ x Sp(1)_-, so each component u of x satisfies u^2 = -1: both components are unit imaginary quaternions, with eigenvalues +-i on their chiralities. As a cross-check, an element of one factor alone acts on H by left or right multiplication by a unit quaternion, an isoclinic rotation with the same angle in two orthogonal planes, while d tau has the angles pi and 0 (for instance (i, i) acts as v -> i v i^{-1}, the identity on the complex line of 1 and i and -1 on that of j and k, a rotation by pi in one plane). (ii) The representation of Dic_3 on Sigma_R + Sigma_L is spinorial, -1 acting as -1, and faithful on the spinorial part C + H of R[Dic_3] (lemma 48, Proposition 48.2, where Sigma_R carries the character pair chi_1 + chi_3, since x acts as (i, -i) and a as -1, and Sigma_L carries the faithful representation V_f); so the image of R[Dic_3] is C + H, with the C acting on Sigma_R through span_R(1, x) = C as (lambda, conj(lambda)) in the eigenbasis of x, and the H acting on Sigma_L as the real span of the finite subgroup Dic_3 of Sp(1), which is all of H since Dic_3 contains 1, the imaginary unit u and an element outside the complex line of u. The even algebra of Example 16.9 for the axis Gamma is End_H(Sigma_R) + End_H(Sigma_L) = H_R + H_L (Proposition 49.1), and span_R(1, u') is a maximal commutative subalgebra of H_R, a Cartan subalgebra. (iii) The unitary group of the C of (ii) is the circle {cos t + u' sin t} in Sp(1)_R, the maximal torus with weight lines the eigenlines of u' = x|_{Sigma_R}; lemma 42 marks such a torus by hand, and Corollary 46.4 identifies it from a split Yukawa block when the connector's line is one of its weight lines. ∎

**Proposition 49.3 (the invariant Yukawa blocks).** Identify Sigma_R and Sigma_L with H = C + j C so that x acts on both as left multiplication by i (possible by conjugation in Sp(1)_R and Sp(1)_L). The odd Hermitian blocks [[0, X^*], [X, 0]] with X in Hom_C(Sigma_R, Sigma_L) that commute with x are exactly those with X = diag(y_1, y_2) in the bases (1, j) of the two lines, a complex two-dimensional space: the blocks Y_u, Y_d of the spectral Standard Model. Commuting with x is commuting with the circle of x acting on both chiralities, whose real span on Sigma_4 is the image of the subalgebra C_F = {(lambda, lambda, 0)} with which Chamseddine, Connes and Marcolli require the finite Dirac operator to commute (arXiv:hep-th/0610241, Definition 2.20 and Remark 2.19: the photon remains massless). No nonzero odd block is invariant under the circle of x on Sigma_R alone, the circle of the summand C. In the variables of lemma 46, X = L_a + i L_c with a = (y_1 + conj(y_2))/2 and c = (y_1 - conj(y_2))/(2i), both complex numbers, and the mass formula of Proposition 46.2 reads |a|^2 + |c|^2 +- 2 |Im(conj(a) c)| = |y_1|^2, |y_2|^2; the quaternionic blocks commuting with x are those with y_2 = conj(y_1), equal masses with conjugate couplings. No nonzero odd block commutes with the whole of Dic_3, nor with the lift of order three of a three-cycle alone: a Yukawa block breaks the S_3 of the symmetric point, and on the mirror of tau, where the lifted stabilizer is <x>, the blocks commuting with it are those above; the escapes are a fibre larger than Sigma_4 (the E factor) or another grading.

*Proof.* With x acting as L_i on both lines, X L_i = L_i X means that X preserves the eigenlines of L_i, which are C and j C, so X is diagonal in the basis (1, j); conversely every diagonal X commutes with L_i. For a, c complex, L_a = diag(a, conj(a)) and i L_c = diag(i c, i conj(c)) in that basis, so X = diag(a + i c, conj(a) + i conj(c)) and y_1 = a + i c, y_2 = conj(a) + i conj(c), which inverts as stated; the singular values of a diagonal matrix are |y_1|, |y_2|, and the formula of Proposition 46.2 with q = a, q' = c complex gives |a|^2 + |c|^2 +- 2 |Im(conj(a) c)| = |a +- i c|^2 in the appropriate order. The quaternionic case is c = 0, that is y_2 = conj(y_1). If X commutes with the lift c-tilde of a three-cycle, which acts as the identity on Sigma_R and without fixed vectors on Sigma_L, then X = c-tilde X maps into the fixed space of c-tilde on Sigma_L, which is zero. ∎

**Proposition 49.4 (the orbit types of A and the unbroken groups).** The stabilizer in S_3 of a point of A is S_3 at the nine points (a, a, a) with 3a = 0; the group <tau> generated by one transposition on the mirror z_i = z_j of that transposition, off the nine points; and trivial elsewhere. The real algebras generated on Sigma_4 by the lifted stabilizers are C + H, C and R, with C = span_R(1, x) acting on both chiralities as (lambda, conj(lambda)) in the weight bases of x; their unitary groups are U(1) x SU(2), U(1) and {+-1}, the image of the kernel of Spin(4) -> SO(4). The algebra of the middle type is the restriction to Sigma_4 of the subalgebra C_F of Chamseddine, Connes and Marcolli (Proposition 49.3), and its circle is the common unbroken circle of Corollary 46.4: it is the stabilizer, in K_p = Spin(4), of every diagonal block diag(y_1, y_2) with |y_1| != |y_2| together with a weight line of x in Sigma_R. For a displacement delta of the point along the mirror plane of tau, the Clifford element c(delta) is a quaternionic odd block commuting with x, of the form L_a with a complex, that is y_2 = conj(y_1).

*Proof.* A permutation fixes (z_1, z_2, z_3) exactly when it permutes equal coordinates; with z_1 + z_2 + z_3 = 0, the three coordinates are equal exactly when 3 z_1 = 0, nine points of the 3-torsion of E, and two are equal on the mirror z_i = z_j. The lifted stabilizers are Dic_3, <x> = Z_4 and the trivial group, whose spinorial real algebras on Sigma_4 are C + H (Proposition 49.2), span_R(1, x) = C acting on both chiralities by x = (i, -i) in its weight bases, and R; the unitary groups follow. The stabilizer in Sp(1)_R x Sp(1)_L of a diagonal block X = diag(y_1, y_2) with distinct moduli is the circle {(X b X^{-1}, b)} with b in the torus of x (Corollary 46.4), and X b X^{-1} = b for diagonal X and b in that torus, so the circle is {(b, b)}, the circle of x acting on both chiralities, which also fixes the weight lines. For delta in the fixed plane of d tau, x c(delta) x^{-1} = c(d tau delta) = c(delta), so c(delta) is a j-linear odd block commuting with x: by Proposition 49.3, L_a with c = 0. ∎

**Proposition 49.5 (the quark generation on the whole fibre).** Apply the finite content of Corollary 16.8 with V_X = Sigma_4, A_{F,X} = C + H acting as in Proposition 49.2, gamma_X = Gamma (the chirality of A, +1 on Sigma_R and -1 on Sigma_L, with the orientation of the Setting; for the complex orientation gamma_X = -Gamma), d_X a nonzero odd Hermitian block of Proposition 49.3, and V_Y = C^3 with A_{F,Y} = M_3(C), gamma_Y = 1, d_Y = 0. *Proof.* As in Theorem 48.3 (2): the data are a finite even representation of the real algebra C + H, which is a real *-subalgebra of End_C(Sigma_4), with the central grading Gamma (1 on Sigma_R, where C acts, and -1 on Sigma_L, where H acts), so Theorem 16.7 and Corollary 16.8 apply formally (Remark 16.5), and their conclusions hold for the real algebra as for its complex span. ## Reading

In the four-torus model the three inputs that the twistor picture left as data, the grading axis (Proposition 28.19, Example 16.9), the marked torus U(1)_b in Sp(1)_b (lemma 42) and the real form of the second summand (lemma 45), are supplied by the spinor fibre of A with its Weyl stabilizer: the axis is the chirality of A, fixed up to the orientation; the torus is the circle of the lifted transposition, which is lemma 42's U(1)_b when the connector's line is one of its two weight lines; and the quaternions are the real span of the faithful representation on the other chirality. The twistor window receives them through the isomorphism of Proposition 49.1, which exists for every axis, so there the axis remains an input. The Higgs directions, Hom_H(L_p, L_p^perp) = T_p S^4 in lemmas 42 and 46, are the tangent directions of A, and the stabilizer algebras along the orbit types of A are C + H at the S_3-fixed points, with unitary group U(1) x SU(2); on the mirror planes span_R(1, x), the subalgebra C_F of Chamseddine, Connes and Marcolli on the first sheet, with which their finite Dirac operator must commute, whose unitary group is the circle that Corollary 46.4 found as the common stabilizer of a split Yukawa block and the connector's line; and R, with unitary group {+-1}, at generic points. As a reading of the electroweak pattern this differs from the spectral Standard Model in one respect: there A_F is fixed and C_F appears as a condition on D_F, while here the algebra changes along the strata. A displacement along a mirror gives a quaternionic, custodial block, equal masses with conjugate couplings; the anti-quaternionic half that splits the masses is i c(delta') Gamma, which is +- i times the Clifford multiplication by the three-form dual to delta'; every c(v) is j-linear, so no displacement of the point supplies it, and a constant three-form term of the Dirac operator on A, of the kind torsion or a flux adds, has exactly this form (referee). For delta, delta' in the mirror plane the general x-invariant block is c(delta) + i c(delta') Gamma, with m_+^2 + m_-^2 = 2 (|delta|^2 + |delta'|^2) and m_+^2 - m_-^2 = 4 |det(delta, delta')|: the up-down splitting is four times the area of the parallelogram spanned by the displacement and the dual of the three-form, and the masses are equal exactly when the two are parallel; the mirror directions are the neutral components of the bidoublet and the normal directions, whose blocks anticommute with x, the charged ones (referee). The crossed product of lemma 48 sees these orbit types as different quotients, so the motion of the point is a reading and the proved content is the list of stabilizer algebras. The whole fibre carries one quark generation (Proposition 49.5), and the lepton generation needs more than a sheet whose stabilizer algebra is C on a single line: with V_Y = C + C^3 Corollary 16.8 gives 32 states over the four-summand algebra C + H + C + M_3(C), and the identification of the two copies of C that gives A_F = {(lambda, q_L, lambda, m)} is, in Chamseddine, Connes and Marcolli, the order-one condition for an operator between particles and antiparticles (Proposition 2.11), a connecting operator that the sheet-diagonal D_F of Corollary 16.8 cannot supply; in the project that is the role of the connector of lemma 20, whose centralizer is C + H + M_3(C) (referee). The circle of C is also the complex structure of A: for each transposition the lift agrees on Sigma_R, up to sign, with half the Clifford action of the Kähler form of the complex structure that A inherits from E, so with Sigma_4 = Lambda^{0,*} (x) K^{1/2} the two right-handed singlets are K^{+-1/2}, the left doublet is Lambda^{0,1} (x) K^{1/2}, and C + H is the real span of the spin lift of the unitary group U(2) of that complex structure, of which Dic_3 is a finite subgroup spanning the same algebra; the Weyl action preserves the complex structure of E, and the two weight lines are exchanged by the lift of a reflection of T E (referee).

## Scope

Finite linear algebra on the spinor fibre of the flat four-torus A with the periodic spin structure; the lifted stabilizer Dic_3 of lemma 48; the convention that Sigma_R is the chirality on which the three-cycles act trivially (which of the two half-spin representations this is depends on the orientation of A only, since each three-cycle has a single lift of order three; for the complex orientation of A inherited from E it is the chirality Gamma = -1, on which the Kähler form acts). The E factor of T^6 = A x E is outside the statements: its spinors double the fibre, Sigma_6 = Sigma_4 (x) Sigma_2, the inversion acts on Sigma_2 and is absent from the stabilizer at the orbit type (a, a, a), and the Clifford action of T E adds two generators outside the Cl(5) of Proposition 49.1. Proposition 49.4 proves stabilizer algebras, and the reading of a displacement as a Higgs field is a reading. The direction: the Dirac operator of the torus restricted to a point of a mirror, item (b) of Remark 45.3, with a constant three-form term whose dual lies in the mirror plane, as the candidate that supplies both halves of an x-invariant Yukawa block and derives the condition [D_F, C_F] = 0 from the geometry, the three mirrors being three candidates for the photon circle on Sigma_L; and the lepton generation through a connecting operator that identifies the two copies of C.
