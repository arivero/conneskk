## Setting

Let A = a (x) R^2 be the flat four-torus of lemma 48, with tangent space R^4 = a (x) R^2 at every point, a the standard representation of S_3, and the periodic spin structure, the only one invariant under S_3 (lemma 48, Setting). Its spinor fibre is Sigma_4 = C^4 = Sigma_+ + Sigma_-, the two half-spin representations of Spin(4) = Sp(1)_+ x Sp(1)_-, each a quaternionic line H on which its Sp(1) acts by left multiplication and the other trivially; the quaternionic structure j, the right multiplication by the unit quaternion j on each line, commutes with Spin(4), with the Clifford multiplication c(v) by vectors v of R^4, and with the chirality Gamma = c(e_1) c(e_2) c(e_3) c(e_4), which is +1 on Sigma_+ and -1 on Sigma_-. Put Gamma_a = c(e_a) for a = 1, ..., 4 and Gamma_5 = Gamma: five Hermitian operators with Gamma_a Gamma_b + Gamma_b Gamma_a = 2 delta_ab, commuting with j, whose real span together with the Gamma_a Gamma_b is End_H(Sigma_4) = M_2(H). The unitary group of End_H(Sigma_4) is Sp(2), containing Spin(4) as the stabilizer of Gamma_5; under Spin(4) the vector R^4 is the (2, 2) and acts, through c, as the odd Hermitian j-linear maps Sigma_+ -> Sigma_- and back. The lifted stabilizer of the orbit type (a, a, a) is Dic_3 = <a, x> in Spin(4), x the lift of a transposition, of order four, and c-tilde = -a the lift of order three of a three-cycle (lemma 48, Proposition 48.1).

## Statements

**Proposition 49.1 (the sheet as the spinor fibre).** (Sigma_4, Gamma_1, ..., Gamma_5, j) is the quaternionic sheet W = H^2 of Example 16.9 and lemma 46 with the axis p = e_5: Gamma_p is the chirality, L_p = Sigma_+ and L_p^perp = Sigma_-, the stabilizer K_p = Sp(1)_b x Sp(1)_a is Spin(4) = Sp(1)_+ x Sp(1)_-, and the bidoublet Hom_H(L_p, L_p^perp) = T_p S^4 of lemma 42 is the Clifford action of the tangent space of A: v -> c(v) restricted to Sigma_+ is a Spin(4)-equivariant isomorphism R^4 -> Hom_H(Sigma_+, Sigma_-). The even algebra H + H of Example 16.9 for this axis is End_H(Sigma_+) + End_H(Sigma_-), the Pati-Salam pair acting on the two chiralities, and the odd Hermitian blocks of lemma 46 are the j-linear c(v) and the j-antilinear i c(v) Gamma.

*Proof.* Cl(4) acts on Sigma_4 irreducibly with c(v)^2 = |v|^2 and c(v)^* = c(v) for the Hermitian metric of the spinors, and the chirality Gamma anticommutes with every c(v), is Hermitian and squares to one; so Gamma_1, ..., Gamma_5 generate Cl(5) on C^4, and Example 16.9 and lemma 46 apply with these generators. The real algebra End_H(Sigma_4) has real dimension 16 and contains the 16 linearly independent elements 1, Gamma_a, Gamma_a Gamma_b, so it is their real span, M_2(H); Spin(4) is the subgroup of Sp(2) = U(End_H(Sigma_4)) that commutes with Gamma_5, since the even Clifford algebra of R^4 is Spin(4)'s and the stabilizer of Gamma_5 in Sp(2) is the unitary group of the even algebra H + H, which is Sp(1) x Sp(1). The map v -> c(v)|_{Sigma_+} is Spin(4)-equivariant by the covariance of Clifford multiplication, injective, and both sides have real dimension four, Hom_H between two quaternionic lines being a copy of H. The rest is lemma 46, Proposition 46.1, with Gamma_p = Gamma. ∎

**Proposition 49.2 (the lifted stabilizer inside the sheet).** (i) The lifts of the three-cycles act trivially on one chirality, which we call Sigma_R, and by rotations of order three on the other, Sigma_L; the lift x of a transposition acts on both with eigenvalues +-i. So Dic_3 -> Sp(1)_R has image the cyclic group Z_4 generated by x|_{Sigma_R}, and Dic_3 -> Sp(1)_L is the faithful embedding of the binary dihedral group in SU(2). (ii) The real algebra generated by Dic_3 on Sigma_4 is C + H, with C = R[x|_{Sigma_R}] = span_R(1, x|_{Sigma_R}) acting on Sigma_R as (lambda, conj(lambda)) in the eigenbasis of x, and H = End_H(Sigma_L) the real span of the faithful representation: the Standard Model subalgebra C + H_L of the Pati-Salam H_R + H_L of Example 16.9, with C the Cartan subalgebra of H_R generated by the lifted transposition. (iii) Consequently the maximal torus U(1)_b of Sp(1)_R that lemma 42 marked as an input, and the torus of Corollary 46.4 when the connector's line is one of its weight lines, is the circle generated by the lifted transposition x, and its two weight lines in Sigma_R are the two right-handed singlets on which C acts by lambda and conj(lambda).

*Proof.* (i) Spin(4) = Sp(1)_+ x Sp(1)_- acts on R^4 = H by v -> p v conj(q). Identify a with C by a complex structure, so that R^4 = a (x) R^2 = C (x) R^2 = C^2 = H; the differential of a three-cycle, the rotation by 2 pi/3 of a tensored with the identity of R^2, is the complex scalar e^{2 pi i/3} on C^2, a rotation by the same angle and in the same sense in two orthogonal planes, which is left (or right) multiplication by a unit complex number: an element of one Sp(1) factor only. Its lifts therefore act trivially on the chirality of the other factor, Sigma_R, and on Sigma_L by the rotation of order three (for the lift c-tilde of order three) or six (for -c-tilde). The differential of a transposition is the rotation by pi in the two-plane n (x) R^2 (lemma 48, Proposition 48.1), with eigenvalues (-1, -1, 1, 1). The lift x has x^2 = -1 (lemma 48), so each of its two components in Sp(1)_+ x Sp(1)_- is +-1 or a unit imaginary quaternion. An element of one factor alone acts on H by left or right multiplication by a unit quaternion, an isoclinic rotation, with the same angle in two orthogonal planes; d tau has the angles pi and 0, so neither component of x is +-1, and both are unit imaginary quaternions u, u', of square -1, with eigenvalues +-i on each chirality (for instance (i, i) acts as v -> i v i^{-1}, the identity on the complex line of 1 and i and -1 on that of j and k, a rotation by pi in one plane). (ii) The representation of Dic_3 on Sigma_R + Sigma_L is spinorial, -1 acting as -1, and faithful on the spinorial part C + H of R[Dic_3] (lemma 48, Proposition 48.2, where Sigma_R carries the character pair chi_1 + chi_3, since x acts as (i, -i) and a as -1, and Sigma_L carries the faithful representation V_f); so the image of R[Dic_3] is C + H, with the C acting on Sigma_R through span_R(1, x) = C as (lambda, conj(lambda)) in the eigenbasis of x, and the H acting on Sigma_L as the real span of the finite subgroup Dic_3 of Sp(1), which is all of H since Dic_3 contains 1, the imaginary unit u and an element outside the complex line of u. The even algebra of Example 16.9 for the axis Gamma is End_H(Sigma_R) + End_H(Sigma_L) = H_R + H_L (Proposition 49.1), and span_R(1, u') is a maximal commutative subalgebra of H_R, a Cartan subalgebra. (iii) The unitary group of the C of (ii) is the circle {cos t + u' sin t} in Sp(1)_R, the maximal torus with weight lines the eigenlines of u' = x|_{Sigma_R}; lemma 42 marks such a torus by hand, and Corollary 46.4 identifies it from a split Yukawa block when the connector's line is one of its weight lines. ∎

**Proposition 49.3 (the invariant Yukawa blocks).** Identify Sigma_R and Sigma_L with H = C + j C so that x acts on both as left multiplication by i (possible by conjugation in Sp(1)_R and Sp(1)_L). The odd Hermitian blocks [[0, X^*], [X, 0]] with X in Hom_C(Sigma_R, Sigma_L) that commute with x are exactly those with X = diag(y_1, y_2) in the bases (1, j) of the two lines, a complex two-dimensional space: the blocks Y_u, Y_d of the spectral Standard Model, invariant under the hypercharge circle. In the variables of lemma 46, X = L_a + i L_c with a = (y_1 + conj(y_2))/2 and c = (y_1 - conj(y_2))/(2i), both complex numbers, and the mass formula of Proposition 46.2 reads |a|^2 + |c|^2 +- 2 |Im(conj(a) c)| = |y_1|^2, |y_2|^2; the quaternionic blocks commuting with x are those with y_2 = conj(y_1), equal masses with conjugate couplings. No nonzero odd block commutes with the whole of Dic_3.

*Proof.* With x acting as L_i on both lines, X L_i = L_i X means that X preserves the eigenlines of L_i, which are C and j C, so X is diagonal in the basis (1, j); conversely every diagonal X commutes with L_i. For a, c complex, L_a = diag(a, conj(a)) and i L_c = diag(i c, i conj(c)) in that basis, so X = diag(a + i c, conj(a) + i conj(c)) and y_1 = a + i c, y_2 = conj(a) + i conj(c), which inverts as stated; the singular values of a diagonal matrix are |y_1|, |y_2|, and the formula of Proposition 46.2 with q = a, q' = c complex gives |a|^2 + |c|^2 +- 2 |Im(conj(a) c)| = |a +- i c|^2 in the appropriate order. The quaternionic case is c = 0, that is y_2 = conj(y_1). If X commutes with the lift c-tilde of a three-cycle, which acts as the identity on Sigma_R and without fixed vectors on Sigma_L, then X = c-tilde X maps into the fixed space of c-tilde on Sigma_L, which is zero. ∎

**Proposition 49.4 (the orbit types of A and the unbroken groups).** The stabilizer in S_3 of a point of A is S_3 at the nine points (a, a, a) with 3a = 0; the group <tau> generated by one transposition on the mirror z_i = z_j of that transposition, off the nine points; and trivial elsewhere. The real algebras generated on Sigma_4 by the lifted stabilizers are C + H, C and R, with C = span_R(1, x) acting on both chiralities as (lambda, conj(lambda)) in the weight bases of x; their unitary groups are U(1) x SU(2), U(1) and the trivial group. The circle of the middle type is the common unbroken circle of Corollary 46.4: it is the stabilizer, in K_p = Spin(4), of every diagonal block diag(y_1, y_2) with |y_1| != |y_2| together with a weight line of x in Sigma_R. For a displacement delta of the point along the mirror plane of tau, the Clifford element c(delta) is a quaternionic odd block commuting with x, of the form L_a with a complex, that is y_2 = conj(y_1).

*Proof.* A permutation fixes (z_1, z_2, z_3) exactly when it permutes equal coordinates; with z_1 + z_2 + z_3 = 0, the three coordinates are equal exactly when 3 z_1 = 0, nine points of the 3-torsion of E, and two are equal on the mirror z_i = z_j. The lifted stabilizers are Dic_3, <x> = Z_4 and the trivial group, whose spinorial real algebras on Sigma_4 are C + H (Proposition 49.2), span_R(1, x) = C acting on both chiralities by x = (i, -i) in its weight bases, and R; the unitary groups follow. The stabilizer in Sp(1)_R x Sp(1)_L of a diagonal block X = diag(y_1, y_2) with distinct moduli is the circle {(X b X^{-1}, b)} with b in the torus of x (Corollary 46.4), and X b X^{-1} = b for diagonal X and b in that torus, so the circle is {(b, b)}, the circle of x acting on both chiralities, which also fixes the weight lines. For delta in the fixed plane of d tau, x c(delta) x^{-1} = c(d tau delta) = c(delta), so c(delta) is a j-linear odd block commuting with x: by Proposition 49.3, L_a with c = 0. ∎

**Proposition 49.5 (the quark generation on the whole fibre).** Apply the finite content of Corollary 16.8 with V_X = Sigma_4, A_{F,X} = C + H acting as in Proposition 49.2, gamma_X = Gamma (the chirality of A, +1 on Sigma_R and -1 on Sigma_L), d_X an odd Hermitian block of Proposition 49.3, and V_Y = C^3 with A_{F,Y} = M_3(C), gamma_Y = 1, d_Y = 0. Then H_F = Hom(V_Y, V_X) + Hom(V_X, V_Y) has complex dimension 24, carries the KO-6 signs, order zero, order one, nonzero one-forms and the real orientability relation of Corollary 16.8, over the real algebra C + H + M_3(C); it is the quark sector of one generation of the spectral Standard Model, C acting on the two right-handed singlets by (lambda, conj(lambda)), H on the left-handed doublet, M_3(C) on colour, with the grading the chirality of A. Its intersection form on the minimal projections 1_C, 1_H, e_11 is [[0, 0, 2], [0, 0, -2], [-2, 2, 0]], skew of rank 2. The 18-state bimodule of Theorem 48.3 is its restriction to one of the two characters of Sigma_R.

*Proof.* As in Theorem 48.3 (2): the data are a finite even representation of the real algebra C + H, which is a real *-subalgebra of End_C(Sigma_4), with the central grading Gamma (1 on Sigma_R, where C acts, and -1 on Sigma_L, where H acts), so Theorem 16.7 and Corollary 16.8 apply formally (Remark 16.5), and their conclusions hold for the real algebra as for its complex span. The dimension is 2 x 4 x 3 = 24. The action is that of the spectral Standard Model on the quark sector: the bimodule Hom(C^3, Sigma_4) = Sigma_4 (x) conj(C^3) is a right-handed up-type and down-type singlet (the two weight lines of x, with lambda and conj(lambda)) and a left-handed doublet, each in three colours, with the conjugates in the other summand. The intersection form is <e, f> = tr(Gamma a_e) tr(b_f) - tr(b_e) tr(Gamma a_f) as in Theorem 48.3, with tr(Gamma 1_C) = 2, tr(Gamma 1_H) = -2, tr(b_{e_11}) = 1. Restricting Sigma_R to one weight line gives V_X = C + C^2 and Theorem 48.3. ∎

## Reading

The three inputs that the twistor picture left as data, the grading axis (Proposition 28.19, Example 16.9), the marked torus U(1)_b in Sp(1)_b (lemma 42) and the real form of the second summand (lemma 45), are all supplied by the spinor fibre of the four-torus with its Weyl stabilizer: the axis is the chirality of A, the torus is the lifted transposition, and the quaternions are the real span of the faithful representation on the other chirality. The Higgs directions, Hom_H(L_p, L_p^perp) = T_p S^4 in lemmas 42 and 46, are the tangent directions of A, and the orbit-type stratification of A reads as the electroweak pattern: at the S_3-fixed points the unbroken group is U(1)_Y x SU(2)_L, on the mirror planes the circle of the lifted transposition acting on both chiralities, the U(1) that Corollary 46.4 found as the common stabilizer of a split Yukawa block and the connector's line, and at generic points nothing. A displacement along a mirror gives a quaternionic, custodial block, equal masses with conjugate couplings; the anti-quaternionic half that splits the masses, i c(delta') Gamma, is the Clifford element of a displacement twisted by the chirality. The crossed product of lemma 48 sees these orbit types as different quotients, so the motion of the point is a reading and the proved content is the list of stabilizer algebras. The whole fibre carries one quark generation (Proposition 49.5), and the lepton generation would need a sheet whose stabilizer algebra is C acting on a single line, which the referee of lemma 48 suggested at the orbit types with stabilizer a transposition or the inversion, untested.

## Scope

Finite linear algebra on the spinor fibre of the flat four-torus A with the periodic spin structure; the lifted stabilizer Dic_3 of lemma 48; the convention that Sigma_R is the chirality on which the three-cycles act trivially (which of the two half-spin representations this is depends on the orientation of A and on the choice of lift). The E factor of T^6 = A x E is outside the statements: its spinors double the fibre, Sigma_6 = Sigma_4 (x) Sigma_2, the inversion acts on Sigma_2 and is absent from the stabilizer at the orbit type (a, a, a), and the Clifford action of T E adds two generators outside the Cl(5) of Proposition 49.1. Proposition 49.4 proves stabilizer algebras, and the reading of a displacement as a Higgs field is a reading. The direction: the Dirac operator of the torus restricted to the orbit, item (b) of Remark 45.3, now has a candidate form for its Yukawa part, the Clifford action of a displacement along a mirror twisted by the chirality; and the lepton sheet.
