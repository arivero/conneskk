# The Weyl crossed product and the two-sheet bimodule

For STRATEGY T16 (CORPUS_SALVAGE items 4 and 10), following [lemma 16](16_coarse_truncation.md) (Theorem 16.7 and Corollary 16.8, the two-sheet bimodule) and the corpus constructions recorded as rs_N18 (the finite quotient of the Weyl crossed product) and rs_N6 (the 18-state orientable bimodule), historical/corpus/connes_kk_research_state.md, sections 6.5 and 6.13. Written by Claude Fable 5.1 on 4 November 2026, by hand; the crossed-product facts used are standard (evaluation at a fixed point, Green's imprimitivity for a finite orbit). Referee pending.

**Outcome.** The two constructions the corpus left separate are one construction. The 18-state orientable KO-6 bimodule over C + M_2(C) + M_3(C) of section 6.5 is the two-sheet bimodule of Corollary 16.8 with the two sheets taken as the two orbit types of the Weyl group W = S_3 x Z_2 of SU(3) x SU(2) acting on T^6 = A x E: the fixed point, whose fibre of the crossed product is the group algebra and supplies C + M_2(C) and the grading, and a three-point orbit, whose fibre supplies M_3(C). So the first half of T16 is answered by what is already proved: the finite algebra with its real structure, its grading, order zero, order one and orientability come from the crossed product and lemma 16. What remains open is the second half: the quaternionic real form H in place of M_2(C), which needs the binary dihedral lift Dic_3 of the corpus or a chosen real form, since the plain spin lift of the Weyl action fails at the transposition fixed point (rs_N20), and a Dirac operator from the geometry in place of the inserted D_v.

## Setting

Let E be an elliptic curve, A = {(z_1, z_2, z_3) in E^3 : z_1 + z_2 + z_3 = 0} = E^2, and X = A x E = T^6, with W = S_3 x Z_2 acting by permutations of the z_i and by z -> -z on E; X/W = CP^2 x CP^1 (the corpus, section 2.5). Let B = C(X) x W be the crossed product, with the covariance relations u_g f u_g^{-1} = f o g^{-1}. Two orbit types enter: the fixed point x_0 = (0, 0) of W, and the three-point orbit O of x_1 = ((z, z, -2z), 0) with 3z != 0, whose stabilizer is H = S_2 x Z_2.

## Statements

**Proposition 45.1 (the two fibres of the crossed product).** (i) Evaluation at the fixed point, f u_g -> f(x_0) u_g, is a surjective *-homomorphism B -> C*(W) = C^4 + M_2(C) + M_2(C). (ii) Restriction to the orbit O is a surjective *-homomorphism B -> C(O) x W = M_3(C*(H)) = M_3(C)^4, and composing with a character of H = S_2 x Z_2 gives B -> M_3(C). (iii) Selecting the trivial character and the standard two-dimensional representation of S_3 in (i), and one character in (ii), gives the surjection

    B -> (C + M_2(C)) + M_3(C) = A_F^C,

the complexification of the finite algebra of the classification, with the fixed point carrying C + M_2(C) and the three-point orbit carrying M_3(C).

*Proof.* (i) The evaluation map is well defined because W fixes x_0, so the covariance relation is preserved: u_g f u_g^{-1} -> f(g^{-1} x_0) = f(x_0). Its image is spanned by the u_g, hence is C*(W); for W = S_3 x Z_2, C*(S_3) = C + C + M_2(C) (the trivial, sign and standard representations) and C*(Z_2) = C + C. (ii) Restriction to the invariant closed set O is a *-homomorphism onto C(O) x W, and for a transitive action on a finite set G/H, Green's imprimitivity theorem gives C(G/H) x G = M_{|G/H|}(C*(H)) (here with finite groups it is the statement that the induced representation from H is a Morita equivalence, concretely: functions on O act by diagonal matrices and u_g by permutation matrices twisted by H, and the algebra generated is M_3 (x) C*(H)); C*(H) = C^4 for H = Z_2 x Z_2. A character of H gives the quotient M_3(C). (iii) is the direct sum of the two selected quotients; the two orbits are separated by an invariant function, so the sum map is surjective. ∎

The corpus proved (iii) by a finite-matrix computation (rs_N18, generated dimension 1 + 4 + 9 = 14); the argument above is the structural one. The selection in (iii) is a choice among the irreducible representations of C*(W) and of C*(H): the sign character of S_3, the other Z_2 characters and the other three characters of H are discarded.

**Theorem 45.2 (the two-sheet bimodule over the two orbits).** Apply Corollary 16.8 with the sheets X and Y both equal to T^6 and with

    V_X = C + C^2,  A_{F,X} = C + M_2(C) acting on it,  gamma_{V_X} = G = diag(1, -1, -1),  d_X = D_v = [[0, v^dagger], [v, 0]]  (v in C^2 nonzero);
    V_Y = C^3,      A_{F,Y} = M_3(C),                      gamma_{V_Y} = 1,                   d_Y = 0.

Then H_F = Hom(V_Y, V_X) + Hom(V_X, V_Y), of complex dimension 18, with J_F(x, y) = (y^dagger, x^dagger), Gamma_F(x, y) = (G x, -y G), and D_F(x, y) = (D_v x, y D_v), has the KO-6 signs J^2 = 1, J D = D J, J Gamma = -Gamma J, order zero, order one, nonzero one-forms, and the real orientability relation Gamma_F = pi(z c) c° with c = (G, 1) and z = (1, -1), since G lies in C + M_2(C) and 1 in M_3(C). This is the bimodule of the corpus, section 6.5 (rs_N6), whose intersection form on minimal projections is [[0, 0, 1], [0, 0, -2], [-1, 2, 0]], of rank 2.

*Proof.* The data satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem 16.7 and Corollary 16.8: V_X is graded by G, which is even for the algebra C + M_2(C) (it is the central projection difference e_1 - e_2 of that algebra), D_v is odd for G and self-adjoint, V_Y is ungraded with d_Y = 0. Corollary 16.8 gives the signs, the order conditions and the orientability relation; its D_F is d_X (x) 1 + gamma (x) d_Y^c = D_v on the first factor, which is the operator of section 6.5 in the identification Hom(V_Y, V_X) = V_X (x) conj(V_Y). The matrix of the intersection form is the one computed in the corpus; it is skew of odd rank, as Lemma 41.1 requires, with kernel (2, 1, 0). ∎

**Remark 45.3 (what the identification settles, and what it leaves).** Settled: the complex finite algebra, its 18-state bimodule with the right real structure and grading, order zero, order one, orientability, all as the two-sheet construction of lemma 16 over the two orbit types of the Weyl action, with the sheets separated by an invariant function. The grading Gamma_F comes from the sheet sign z = (1, -1) and from the central difference G inside C + M_2(C) of the fixed-point fibre, so no spin lift of the Weyl action is used; the obstruction rs_N20 (no equivariant ordinary spin lift at the transposition fixed point) concerns the construction of H from a spin lift and is bypassed here at the price of leaving M_2(C) complex. Left open, in order: (a) the quaternionic real form, H = R + su(2) inside M_2(C), which the corpus obtained from the binary dihedral group Dic_3 in the spin sector (canonical section 10) and which here is a chosen real form, with Poincaré duality failing in either case (rank 2); (b) a Dirac operator from the geometry: D_v is inserted, and a crossed-product Dirac operator (the torus Dirac operator with the W-action) would have to be shown to compress to it on the selected orbit representations, which is the coupled-window problem of lemma 20, Result 7, in a new setting; (c) the selection of the two representations in Proposition 45.1(iii) among the eight irreducible representations of C*(W) and the four characters of H, for which the corpus has no criterion and this lemma adds none.

## Scope

The complex finite algebra and its bimodule; finite groups and finite orbits, where the crossed-product facts are elementary; the Dirac operator as an input. The direction: T16 reduces to the two questions (a) and (b), and (b) is of the same kind as the connector problem of lemmas 20 and 42, now with a discrete group in place of the twistor fibration.
