# The Weyl crossed product and the two-sheet bimodule

For STRATEGY T16 (CORPUS_SALVAGE items 4 and 10), following [lemma 16](16_coarse_truncation.md) (Theorem 16.7 and Corollary 16.8, the two-sheet bimodule) and the corpus constructions recorded as rs_N18 (the finite quotient of the Weyl crossed product) and rs_N6 (the 18-state orientable bimodule), historical/corpus/connes_kk_research_state.md, sections 6.5 and 6.13. Written by Claude Fable 5.1 on 4 November 2026, by hand; the crossed-product facts used are standard (evaluation at a fixed point, Green's imprimitivity for a finite orbit). Refereed by Claude Opus 5.5 (effort max, 4 November 2026; report in historical/reviews/2026-11-04_opus_check_41_45.md): Proposition 45.1 and Theorem 45.2 correct with amendments (the word "complexification", the characters of Z_2, the intersection form of the complex algebra, the formal use of Corollary 16.8, the grading as a choice); two errors in Remark 45.3 corrected below (the number of irreducible representations; the route to H, which the corpus obtains at another orbit type).

**Outcome.** The two constructions the corpus left separate are one construction. The 18-state orientable KO-6 bimodule over C + M_2(C) + M_3(C) of section 6.5 is the two-sheet bimodule of Corollary 16.8 with the two sheets taken as the two orbit types of the Weyl group W = S_3 x Z_2 of SU(3) x SU(2) acting on T^6 = A x E: the fixed point, whose fibre of the crossed product is the group algebra and supplies C + M_2(C) and the grading, and a three-point orbit, whose fibre supplies M_3(C). So the first half of T16 is answered by what is already proved: the complex finite algebra with its real structure, a grading, order zero, order one and orientability come from the crossed product and the finite content of lemma 16. What remains open is the second half: the quaternionic real form H in place of M_2(C), which the corpus obtains from the binary dihedral lift Dic_3 at a different orbit type (Remark 45.3), and a Dirac operator from the geometry in place of the inserted D_v, for which the spin lift of the Weyl action and its obstruction rs_N20 return.

## Setting

Let E be an elliptic curve, A = {(z_1, z_2, z_3) in E^3 : z_1 + z_2 + z_3 = 0} = E^2, and X = A x E = T^6, with W = S_3 x Z_2 acting by permutations of the z_i and by z -> -z on E; X/W = CP^2 x CP^1 (the corpus, section 2.5). Let B = C(X) x W be the crossed product, with the covariance relations u_g f u_g^{-1} = f o g^{-1}. Two orbit types enter: the fixed point x_0 = (0, 0) of W, and the three-point orbit O of x_1 = ((z, z, -2z), 0) with 3z != 0, whose stabilizer is H = S_2 x Z_2.

## Statements

**Proposition 45.1 (the two fibres of the crossed product).** (i) Evaluation at the fixed point, f u_g -> f(x_0) u_g, is a surjective *-homomorphism B -> C*(W) = C^4 + M_2(C) + M_2(C). (ii) Restriction to the orbit O is a surjective *-homomorphism B -> C(O) x W = M_3(C*(H)) = M_3(C)^4, and composing with a character of H = S_2 x Z_2 gives B -> M_3(C). (iii) Selecting the trivial character and the standard two-dimensional representation of S_3 in (i), and one character in (ii), gives the surjection

    B -> (C + M_2(C)) + M_3(C),

the complex algebra with M_2(C) in place of H (the quotient of the corpus, section 6.13; the complexification of the real A_F would be C^2 + M_2(C) + M_3(C)^2), with the fixed point carrying C + M_2(C) and the three-point orbit carrying M_3(C). Each selected representation of W = S_3 x Z_2 includes a character of the Z_2 factor.

*Proof.* (i) The evaluation map is well defined because W fixes x_0, so the covariance relation is preserved: u_g f u_g^{-1} -> f(g^{-1} x_0) = f(x_0). Its image is spanned by the u_g, hence is C*(W); for W = S_3 x Z_2, C*(S_3) = C + C + M_2(C) (the trivial, sign and standard representations) and C*(Z_2) = C + C, so C*(W) has six irreducible representations: four characters and the standard representation tensored with each character of Z_2. (ii) Restriction to the invariant closed set O is a *-homomorphism onto C(O) x W, and for a transitive action on a finite set G/H, Green's imprimitivity theorem gives C(G/H) x G = M_{|G/H|}(C*(H)) (here with finite groups it is the statement that the induced representation from H is a Morita equivalence, concretely: functions on O act by diagonal matrices and u_g by permutation matrices twisted by H, and the algebra generated is M_3 (x) C*(H)); C*(H) = C^4 for H = Z_2 x Z_2. A character of H gives the quotient M_3(C). (iii) is the direct sum of the two selected quotients; the two orbits are separated by an invariant function, so the sum map is surjective. ∎

The corpus proved (iii) by a finite-matrix computation (rs_N18, generated dimension 1 + 4 + 9 = 14); the argument above is the structural one. The selection in (iii) is a choice among the six irreducible representations of C*(W) and the four characters of H; the others are discarded, and this lemma adds no criterion for the choice.

**Theorem 45.2 (the two-sheet bimodule over the two orbits).** Apply the finite content of Corollary 16.8 (Theorem 16.7 on finite even representations, which Remark 16.5 shows to be formal for any such pair) with the two selected representations of the crossed product as the two sheets, labelled by the two orbit types, and with

    V_X = C + C^2,  A_{F,X} = C + M_2(C) acting on it,  gamma_{V_X} = G = diag(1, -1, -1),  d_X = D_v = [[0, v^dagger], [v, 0]]  (v in C^2 nonzero);
    V_Y = C^3,      A_{F,Y} = M_3(C),                      gamma_{V_Y} = 1,                   d_Y = 0.

Then H_F = Hom(V_Y, V_X) + Hom(V_X, V_Y), of complex dimension 18, with J_F(x, y) = (y^dagger, x^dagger), Gamma_F(x, y) = (G x, -y G), and D_F(x, y) = (D_v x, y D_v), has the KO-6 signs J^2 = 1, J D = D J, J Gamma = -Gamma J, order zero, order one, nonzero one-forms, and the real orientability relation Gamma_F = pi(z c) c° with c = (G, 1) and z = (1, -1), since G lies in C + M_2(C) and 1 in M_3(C). This is, in Hilbert space, D, J and grading, the bimodule of the corpus, section 6.5 (rs_N6), stated there over the real algebra C + H + M_3(C). On minimal projections of the complex algebra C + M_2(C) + M_3(C) the intersection form is [[0, 0, 1], [0, 0, -1], [-1, 1, 0]], skew of rank 2 on a K_0 of rank 3, with kernel (1, 1, 0); the corpus form [[0, 0, 1], [0, 0, -2], [-1, 2, 0]] is the one of the real algebra, where the minimal projection of H is 1_H, of rank two (referee).

*Proof.* The data satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem 16.7 and Corollary 16.8: V_X is graded by G, which is even for the algebra C + M_2(C) (it is the central projection difference e_1 - e_2 of that algebra), D_v is odd for G and self-adjoint, V_Y is ungraded with d_Y = 0. Corollary 16.8 gives the signs, the order conditions and the orientability relation; its D_F is d_X (x) 1 + gamma (x) d_Y^c = D_v on the first factor, which is the operator of section 6.5 in the identification Hom(V_Y, V_X) = V_X (x) conj(V_Y). The grading G is a chosen central element: -G, or other sign assignments on the selected representations, are equally admissible. The intersection forms are computed on minimal projections as in Theorem 41.2. ∎

**Remark 45.3 (what the identification settles, and what it leaves).** Settled: the complex finite algebra, its 18-state bimodule with the real structure, a grading that the fixed-point fibre allows, order zero, order one and orientability, all as the two-sheet construction of lemma 16 over the two orbit types of the Weyl action, with the sheets separated by an invariant function. The grading Gamma_F comes from the sheet sign z = (1, -1) and from the central element G of C + M_2(C); no spinor bundle on T^6 enters, so no spin lift of the Weyl action is used, and the obstruction rs_N20 (no equivariant ordinary spin lift at the transposition fixed point) is deferred to item (b), where a crossed-product Dirac operator on the spinors of T^6 needs W, or a cover of it, to act on the spinor bundle. Left open, in order: (a) the quaternionic real form H inside M_2(C). The corpus does not obtain it at the fixed point x_0 used here: at full fixed isotropy the lift of the inversion of E is central and complexifies the spin-sector algebra to C + C + M_2(C); the corpus obtains C + H at the orbit type (a, a, a), 3a = 0, with a point of E that is not 2-torsion (stabilizer S_3, orbit of size two), as a corner of M_2(C + H) (canonical section 10; referee). So the route to H needs a first sheet other than the one of Theorem 45.2, and the two halves of T16 as framed here use different first sheets; in either case Poincaré duality fails in the trace form at rank 2. (b) A Dirac operator from the geometry: D_v is inserted, and a crossed-product Dirac operator (the torus Dirac operator with the W-action on spinors) would have to be shown to compress to it on the selected orbit representations, which is the coupled-window problem of lemma 20, Result 7, in a new setting; the corpus (section 6.13) proves that no finite-rank projection gives a multiplicative compression of C(T^6), so the selected representations are quotients of the crossed product, as used here, and never spectral windows of multiplication operators. (c) The selection of the two representations in Proposition 45.1(iii) among the six irreducible representations of C*(W) and the four characters of H.

## Scope

The complex finite algebra and its bimodule; finite groups and finite orbits, where the crossed-product facts are elementary; the finite content of Corollary 16.8, applied to quotient representations rather than to spectral windows; the Dirac operator and the grading as inputs. The direction: T16 reduces to the questions (a), (b) and (c) of Remark 45.3, with (a) pointing to the orbit type (a, a, a) as the first sheet and (b) of the same kind as the connector problem of lemmas 20 and 42, now with a discrete group in place of the twistor fibration.
