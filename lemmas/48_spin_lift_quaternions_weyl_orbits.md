**Outcome.** The Weyl group W = S_3 x Z_2 of SU(3) x SU(2) acts on T^6 = A x E by isometries, and its lift to the spinors is the group W-tilde = (Dic_3 x Z_4)/Z_2 of order 24, isomorphic to S_3 x Z_4: the transpositions lift to elements of order four, which is the obstruction rs_N20 of the corpus, the inversion of E lifts to a central element of square -1, and the twisted copy of S_3 that pairs each odd permutation with the inversion lifts homomorphically (Proposition 48.1). The algebra that acts on the spinors at an orbit is the spinorial part of the real group algebra of the lifted stabilizer, the part where -1 acts as -1, each of whose irreducible representations occurs in the spinor fibre. At the orbit type (a, a, a), 3a = 0, with a point of E that is not 2-torsion, the stabilizer is S_3, its lift is Dic_3, and the spinorial part of R[Dic_3] = R + R + M_2(R) + C + H is C + H: the pair of characters in which the lifted transposition acts as +-i, and the faithful two-dimensional representation, which is quaternionic (Proposition 48.2). At the fixed point the central lift of the inversion acts as +-i on every spinorial representation, so the spinorial part is C + C + M_2(C), with no quaternionic summand even over R (lemma 45 used the same point with the complex untwisted group algebra, from which it selected C + M_2(C)); at the three-point orbit the spinorial part is C + C, two conjugate pairs of characters, each pair a copy of M_3(C) after Green's imprimitivity. The spinorial condition fixes the algebra on each of the six orbit types, and the orbit type (a, a, a) is the only one whose stabilizer algebra has a quaternionic summand, as the three-point orbit is the only one with matrix size three. So the two-sheet bimodule of Theorem 45.2, with its first sheet moved from the fixed point to the orbit type (a, a, a), carries the real algebra C + H + M_3(C), the Standard Model algebra, the two orbit types being forced by that target (Theorem 48.3). What remains of T16: a Dirac operator from the geometry (item (b) of Remark 45.3), a real structure from the geometry in place of the adjoint flip of Corollary 16.8, and the choices listed in Remark 48.4.

## Setting

Let E be an elliptic curve with its flat metric, A = {(z_1, z_2, z_3) in E^3 : z_1 + z_2 + z_3 = 0}, isometric to a flat four-torus with the metric induced from E^3, and X = A x E. The Weyl group W = S_3 x Z_2 acts by isometries: S_3 permutes the z_i, and the generator iota of Z_2 acts on E by z -> -z. The tangent space of A at any point is a (x) R^2, with a = {x in R^3 : sum x_i = 0} the standard real representation rho of S_3 and R^2 the tangent space of E; the tangent space of E is R^2. Fix the spin structure of X induced from the trivial spin structures of the factors (periodic spinors); the periodic spin structure is the only one on A invariant under S_3 (S_3 acts on A_2/2A_2 = F_2^2 as GL_2(F_2), with no nonzero invariant functional), and the four spin structures of E are invariant under the inversion and give the same lifted stabilizers (referee). The spinor bundle is X x Sigma_6 with Sigma_6 = C^8, and an affine isometry g with constant differential dg in SO(6) acts on spinors through either of the two lifts of dg to Spin(6) in Cl(6), which differ by the sign. Write B-tilde = C(X, R) x W-tilde for the real crossed product by the lift group W-tilde, the preimage of dW in Spin(6), acting on L^2(X, Sigma_6) with -1 in W-tilde acting as -1 on spinors; let B_spin be its quotient by the ideal generated by u_{-1} + 1, the part that acts on the spinors; since -1 is central in W-tilde and acts trivially on X, u_{-1} is central, B_spin is the direct summand B-tilde (1 - u_{-1})/2, and it is the twisted crossed product of C(X, R) by W for the cocycle of the extension W-tilde -> W (referee). For a finite group S-tilde with a central element -1 of order two, the spinorial part of its real group algebra R[S-tilde] is the quotient by u_{-1} + 1, the sum of the simple summands on which -1 acts as -1. The orbit types of W on X and the fibres of the complex crossed product are those of lemma 45, Proposition 45.1.

## Statements

**Proposition 48.1 (the lift of the Weyl action).** (i) For a transposition tau in S_3, d tau = rho(tau) (x) 1 on a (x) R^2 is the rotation by pi in the two-plane n (x) R^2, with n the normal of the reflection rho(tau); both of its lifts to Spin(4) are the products +- f_1 f_2 of an orthonormal basis of that plane, of order four. For a three-cycle c, d c is the rotation by 2 pi/3 in two orthogonal two-planes, and one of its lifts has order three. The lift of S_3 to Spin(4) is the binary dihedral group Dic_3 = <a, x | a^6 = 1, x^2 = a^3, x a x^{-1} = a^{-1}> of order 12, and no homomorphism S_3 -> Spin(4) lifts d: this is the corpus statement rs_N20, either preimage of a transposition squares to -1. (ii) d iota = -1 on the tangent space of E lifts to iota-tilde = +- e_5 e_6 in Spin(2), with iota-tilde^2 = -1, which commutes with the lift of S_3. (iii) The lift group is W-tilde = (Dic_3 x Z_4)/Z_2, of order 24, with Z_4 generated by iota-tilde and the Z_2 identifying the two elements -1. It is isomorphic to S_3 x Z_4: the elements a^k x iota-tilde are involutions, since (x iota-tilde)^2 = x^2 iota-tilde^2 = 1, so the twisted copy {(sigma, iota^{sgn sigma})} of S_3 in W lifts homomorphically (tau iota is the rotation by pi of the four-dimensional subspace (n (x) R^2) + T E, whose lift squares to +1, as in the T^4/Z_2 orbifold), and W-tilde is the product of that lift with <iota-tilde>; so rs_N20 holds for S_3 and for every subgroup of W that contains a transposition without the inversion (referee). The lifted stabilizers are Dic_3 at an orbit of stabilizer S_3, W-tilde at the fixed point, and (Z_4 x Z_4)/Z_2, of order 8, generated by the lifts of a transposition and of iota, at the three-point orbit of lemma 45.

*Proof.* (i) rho(tau) is a reflection of a, rho(tau) = 1 - 2 n n^T, so rho(tau) (x) 1 = 1 - 2 P with P the orthogonal projection onto the two-plane n (x) R^2: the identity on the orthogonal complement and -1 on the plane, the rotation by pi in it. In Cl(4) the rotation by pi in the plane of the orthonormal vectors f_1, f_2 lifts to +- f_1 f_2, and (f_1 f_2)^2 = -f_1^2 f_2^2 = -1. rho(c) is the rotation by 2 pi/3 of a, so rho(c) (x) 1 is that rotation in each of the two planes a (x) e_1 and a (x) e_2; with orthonormal bases g_1, g_2 and h_1, h_2 of the two planes, the lift exp((pi/3)(g_1 g_2 + h_1 h_2)) has cube exp(pi g_1 g_2) exp(pi h_1 h_2) = (-1)(-1) = 1, of order three. The preimage of rho(S_3) (x) 1 in Spin(4) is a group of order 12 containing a cyclic subgroup of order 6 generated by a = (lift of c of order 3) times (-1), and an element x of order four with x^2 = -1 = a^3; x c-tilde x^{-1} has order three and lies above tau c tau^{-1} = c^{-1}, so it is the order-three lift c-tilde^{-1}, hence x a x^{-1} = x (-c-tilde) x^{-1} = -c-tilde^{-1} = a^{-1}; this is the presentation of Dic_3, which has a single involution, -1, and so differs from Z_2 x S_3 and from the abelian groups of order 12. A lift S_3 -> Spin(4) would send tau to an element of order two above d tau, and both candidates have order four. (ii) The rotation by pi of the plane of E lifts to +- e_5 e_6, of square -1; e_5 e_6 is an even element in the Clifford algebra of the E-directions and commutes with the even elements in the Clifford algebra of the A-directions. (iii) The lifts of S_3 and of Z_2 commute and share the element -1, which gives the central product; the stabilizers are read off from lemma 45 and from the fact that -1 lies in every lifted stabilizer. ∎

**Proposition 48.2 (the spinorial parts of the real group algebras).** (i) The irreducible representations of Dic_3 are the four characters of its abelianization Z_4, x -> i^k and a -> (-1)^k for k = 0, 1, 2, 3, and two representations of dimension two, a -> diag(zeta, conj(zeta)) and x -> [[0, zeta^3], [1, 0]], that is [[0, -1], [1, 0]] for zeta a primitive sixth root of unity (faithful) and [[0, 1], [1, 0]] for zeta a primitive third root (the standard representation of S_3). Their Frobenius-Schur types are real for k = 0 and k = 2 and for the standard representation, complex for the conjugate pair k = 1, 3, and quaternionic for the faithful representation, so

    R[Dic_3] = R + R + M_2(R) + C + H,    of real dimension 1 + 1 + 4 + 2 + 4 = 12.

The spinorial part, where -1 = x^2 = a^3 acts as -1, is C + H: the pair k = 1, 3, on which the lifted transposition x acts as +-i, and the faithful representation. Each spinorial irreducible representation of Dic_3, of W-tilde and of the lift of <tau, iota> occurs in Sigma_6 (Sigma_6 restricted to Dic_3 is 2(chi_1 + chi_3) + 2 V_f, with chi_1 + chi_3 and V_f in opposite chiralities of the A-directions), so the spinorial part is the algebra generated on the spinor fibres over the orbit (referee). (ii) At the fixed point, the spinorial irreducible representations of W-tilde are the spinorial representations of Dic_3 tensored with the two characters iota-tilde -> +-i, all of complex type, and the spinorial part of R[W-tilde] is C + C + M_2(C), with no quaternionic summand: in the description W-tilde = S_3 x Z_4 with -1 = iota-tilde^2, it is R[S_3] (x)_R C. (iii) At the three-point orbit, the lifted stabilizer (Z_4 x Z_4)/Z_2 has four spinorial characters, x -> +-i and iota-tilde -> +-i, forming two conjugate pairs, and the spinorial part of its real group algebra is C + C.

*Proof.* (i) The commutator subgroup of Dic_3 is <a^2>, of order three, since x a x^{-1} a^{-1} = a^{-2}, so the abelianization has order four and is generated by the image of x, with a mapping to x^2 = a^3; this gives the four characters, with a -> (-1)^k. The two-dimensional representations are induced from the characters a -> zeta of <a> = Z_6 with zeta != +-1; zeta and conj(zeta) give the same representation, so there are two, and the sum of squares 4 x 1 + 2 x 4 = 12 shows the list is complete. The character k = 2 is the sign of S_3 and k = 0 the trivial one; k = 1, 3 are conjugate and take the value +-i, so their type is complex. The faithful representation lies in SU(2), with x^2 = -1, so its Frobenius-Schur indicator is -1 and its type is quaternionic; the standard representation of S_3 is real. The real group algebra is the sum over the irreducible real representations of the matrix algebras over their commutants, R, C or H, which gives the display; -1 acts as -1 exactly on the characters with k odd and on the faithful representation. (ii) W-tilde is a central product, so an irreducible representation of W-tilde is an irreducible representation of Dic_3 tensored with a character of <iota-tilde> = Z_4 agreeing on -1; the spinorial ones pair the spinorial representations of Dic_3 with iota-tilde -> +-i. A representation on which a central element acts as i or -i is of complex type, since a real or quaternionic structure would commute with the central element and conjugate i to -i. So the characters give C + C and the faithful representation tensored with +-i gives M_2(C). (iii) The group is abelian, generated by x and iota-tilde with x^2 = iota-tilde^2 = -1; its spinorial characters are the four sign choices, in the two conjugate pairs (i, i), (-i, -i) and (i, -i), (-i, i), each pair a copy of C in the real group algebra. ∎

**Theorem 48.3 (the real two-sheet bimodule over the two orbit types).** Let O_1 be an orbit of type (a, a, a), 3a = 0, with a point of E that is not 2-torsion, of size two with stabilizer S_3, and O_2 the three-point orbit of lemma 45. Then:

1. The fibre of B_spin at O_1 is M_2(C + H), and its corner at one point of the orbit is the real algebra C + H, with C the character pair of Proposition 48.2 (i) and H the faithful representation; the fibre at O_2 is M_3(C + C), and one conjugate pair of characters of the stabilizer gives the real algebra M_3(C). The first sheet uses the corner at one point of O_1 and the second the full quotient at O_2; this is a choice, like the choice of characters (with corners on both sheets the algebra would be C + H + C, with full fibres on both M_2(C) + M_2(H) + M_3(C)).
2. The data of Theorem 45.2 with V_X = C + C^2, the real algebra A_{F,X} = C + H acting by the character on C and by left multiplication on C^2 = H, the grading G = (1, -1), central in C + H, and d_X = D_v; V_Y = C^3 with A_{F,Y} = M_3(C), gamma_Y = 1, d_Y = 0, give through the finite content of Corollary 16.8 the 18-state bimodule H_F = Hom(V_Y, V_X) + Hom(V_X, V_Y) with the KO-6 signs, order zero, order one, nonzero one-forms and the real orientability relation, now over the real algebra

    A_F = C + H + M_3(C),

   the Standard Model algebra, with the intersection form on minimal projections [[0, 0, 1], [0, 0, -2], [-1, 2, 0]] of the corpus (rs_N6), skew of rank 2.
3. The spinorial condition fixes the algebra on each orbit type. The six orbit types of W on X, with the spinorial parts of the lifted stabilizer algebras and the fibres, are: trivial stabilizer, orbit of size 12, R, M_12(R); a transposition, size 6, C, M_6(C); the inversion, size 6, C, M_6(C); S_2 x Z_2 (O_2), size 3, C + C, M_3(C + C); S_3 (O_1), size 2, C + H, M_2(C + H); S_3 x Z_2 (the fixed points), size 1, C + C + M_2(C); there is no stabilizer of type Z_3 alone, since a three-cycle fixes only the diagonal points, which S_3 fixes (referee). So the orbit type (a, a, a) is the only one whose stabilizer algebra has a quaternionic summand, and there the spinorial part is exactly C + H; the three-point orbit is the only one with matrix size three. Given the target C + H + M_3(C), the two orbit types are forced. The module V_X = C + C^2 keeps one of the two conjugate characters, a choice of submodule of the spinor fibre chi_1 + chi_3 + V_f; on the whole fibre C acts by (lambda, conj(lambda)) and no choice is made. At O_2 the choice is between the two conjugate pairs of Proposition 48.2 (iii). At the fixed point the spinorial part is C + C + M_2(C), of complex type in every summand; lemma 45 used the same point with the untwisted complex group algebra, from which it selected C + M_2(C).

*Proof.* (1) Green's imprimitivity for the finite orbit, as in Proposition 45.1 (ii), holds with real coefficients and with the twisted action: C(O, R) x_{lift} W is M_{|O|} of the spinorial part of R[S-tilde] for the lifted stabilizer S-tilde, the functions on the orbit acting by diagonal matrices and the lifted group elements by permutation matrices twisted by S-tilde; the corner at a point is the stabilizer algebra. Proposition 48.2 gives the stabilizer algebras. (2) C + H is a real *-subalgebra of the complex algebra C + M_2(C) of Theorem 45.2, acting on the same V_X, whose complex span is C + M_2(C) (a real form of C + M_2(C) would have real dimension 5; C + H has 6); the grading G = (1, -1) lies in it and is central; Theorem 16.7 and Corollary 16.8 are formal for any finite even representation (Remark 16.5), and their conclusions, the signs, the order conditions, the one-forms and the orientability relation, hold for the real algebra as for its complexification, since they are stated for the represented operators. The intersection form over the real algebra, with the minimal projection of H of rank two, is the corpus form, as the referee of lemma 45 computed. (3) Proposition 48.2. ∎

## Scope

Flat T^6 with the periodic spin structure and the isometric action of W; real crossed products by the lift group, with the spinorial quotient; finite groups and finite orbits, where Green's imprimitivity is elementary; the Dirac operator D_v and the grading as inputs, as in lemma 45. The direction: with the lift group acting on the spinors of T^6, the compression of the torus Dirac operator to the two selected sheets is the next computation of T16, and the Frobenius-Schur type of a lifted stabilizer's spinorial representations is the rule that decides the real form of each summand, the rule that also decides the orthogonal, symplectic and unitary factors of orientifold gauge groups (Blum and Intriligator 1997, section 5, where the representations of Dic_3 are sorted by type with the spinorial ones pseudo-real or complex; paper/LITERATURE.md, section N).
