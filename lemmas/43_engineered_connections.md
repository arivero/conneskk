# Which connections on a vertical kernel are Berry connections

For STRATEGY T15 (from CORPUS_SALVAGE items 1 and 2), following [lemma 10](10_toeplitz_twistor.md) (Proposition 10.1 and the second-moment identity), [lemma 29](29_window_on_the_disc_bundle.md) (the collar connection of case (b)) and [lemma 38](38_so30_fluctuations.md). Written by Claude Fable 5.1 on 4 November 2026, by hand. Refereed by Claude Opus 5.5 (effort max, 4 November 2026; report in historical/reviews/2026-11-04_opus_check_41_45.md): Proposition 43.1 and Theorem 43.2 correct with amendments (the mean-curvature term, the transposed adjoint, the exclusion of vertical components), the two computations of Corollary 43.3 correct, and its last clause, which read lemma 29's collar connection as an instance, an error, withdrawn below. The construction was used in the corpus (historical/corpus/canonical_research.md, section 9, on N over S^5 with the flux (1, 1, 2)); this lemma supplies the general statement, and leaves the question of lemma 29's collar connection open.

**Outcome.** Let a fibrewise Dirac family on a bundle of compact fibres F_b have kernels K_b of constant rank, forming a bundle K over the base B, with the Berry connection induced from a unitary connection on the coefficient bundle E. Changing the coefficient connection by a horizontal one-form alpha changes the Berry connection by the fibrewise compression P alpha P. Every connection on K is a Berry connection of some coefficient connection exactly when the fibrewise compression Gamma(F_b, u(E)) -> u(K_b) is surjective at every point of B, and that holds exactly when the pairwise products s_i s_j^* of a frame of kernel sections are linearly independent as sections of End(E) over the fibre. For scalar changes of the connection the same criterion reads: the pointwise inner products <s_i, s_j> are linearly independent functions on the fibre. On CP^3 with the kernel H^0(O(1)) this holds (sixteen independent functions z-bar_i z_j/|z|^2), so every connection on the rank-four kernel bundle is a Berry connection of an abelian change; for functions pulled back from the base S^4 the compressed image has real dimension six out of sixteen (Example 16.9 with Proposition 10.1), so scalar changes along the base reach six of the sixteen directions. The collar connection of lemma 29 is outside this statement: it acts at the wall, on the CP^3 copy, and it changes the fibre operator and moves the kernel, the effect excluded below (referee).

## Setting

Let pi: P -> B be a fibre bundle with compact fibres F_b and a connection (a horizontal distribution), E -> P a Hermitian vector bundle with a unitary connection nabla, and D = (D_b) a smooth family of fibrewise elliptic self-adjoint operators on L^2(F_b, E|_{F_b}) whose kernels K_b have constant dimension k; P_b denotes the orthogonal projection onto K_b. The Berry connection on the bundle K -> B is nabla^K_X s = P_b nabla_{X^h} s for X in T_b B, with X^h the horizontal lift and s a section of K read as a family of sections of E over the fibres; this is unitary for the fibrewise L^2 product when horizontal transport preserves the fibre measure (Riemannian submersions with totally geodesic fibres, as in the homogeneous examples), and in general the unitary connection adds (1/2) k(X) with k the mean-curvature form of the fibres (Bismut; Berline-Getzler-Vergne, chapter 9), a term common to all the connections compared here, so that the statements below hold with that term included or with volume-preserving transport assumed (referee). For a fibre F_b, the compression map is

    C_b : Gamma(F_b, End(E)) -> End(K_b),   C_b(X) = P_b X P_b,

and in a unitary frame s_1, ..., s_k of K_b its matrix elements are C_b(X)_{ij} = int_{F_b} <s_i(y), X(y) s_j(y)> dy. Its adjoint for the L^2 pairing on Gamma(F_b, End(E)) with the Hilbert-Schmidt pairing on End(K_b) is C_b^*(Y) = rho_Y = sum_{ij} Y_{ij} s_i s_j^*, a smooth section of End(E) over F_b (the density of Y; the first version had the indices transposed, which changes nothing below since the two families of products coincide as sets).

## Statements

**Proposition 43.1 (the change of the Berry connection).** Let nabla' = nabla + alpha with alpha in Omega^1(P, u(E)) vanishing on vertical vectors. Then the Berry connection of nabla' is nabla^K + beta, where beta_b(X) = C_b(alpha(X^h)|_{F_b}): the compression of the horizontal component of alpha, fibre by fibre, exactly. A fibrewise twisted Dirac operator uses only the vertical part of the coefficient connection, so such an alpha leaves the family D_b and its kernels unchanged. A vertical component of alpha changes D_b and moves the kernels, and a first-order motion of the kernels changes the Berry connection at first order through the second fundamental form (for a kernel line n(b) in C^2 over S^2 moved along the flow of a vector field xi, the Berry curvature changes by the Lie derivative along xi); such components are excluded here, and this is the effect at work in the collar connection of lemma 29 (referee).

*Proof.* With the kernels unchanged, nabla'_{X^h} s = nabla_{X^h} s + alpha(X^h) s, and P_b of the second term is C_b(alpha(X^h)) s. ∎

**Theorem 43.2 (which connections are Berry connections).** The following are equivalent for the family (P, E, D):

1. Every unitary connection on K -> B is the Berry connection of nabla + alpha for some alpha in Omega^1(P, u(E)) vanishing on vertical vectors.
2. C_b : Gamma(F_b, u(E)) -> u(K_b) is surjective for every b in B.
3. For every b and every (one, hence every) unitary frame s_1, ..., s_k of K_b, the k^2 sections s_j s_i^* of End(E) over F_b are linearly independent.

When these hold, a preimage can be chosen smoothly: alpha(X^h) = C_b^*(C_b C_b^*)^{-1} beta_b(X) is a smooth u(E)-valued form on the horizontal distribution, extended by zero on vertical vectors.

*Proof.* (1) implies (2): a connection whose difference from nabla^K at b is a prescribed Y in u(K_b) must have Y in the image of C_b. (2) implies (3): C_b is surjective iff C_b^* is injective on u(K_b); C_b^* is complex linear with C_b^*(Y^*) = C_b^*(Y)^*, so its kernel in End(K_b) is closed under the adjoint and meets u(K_b) trivially exactly when it is zero, and C_b^*(Y) = sum Y_{ij} s_i s_j^* vanishes for a nonzero Y exactly when the sections s_i s_j^* are dependent. (3) implies (1): with C_b surjective, C_b C_b^* is a positive invertible operator on u(K_b) depending smoothly on b, so for a given beta in Omega^1(B, u(K)) the form alpha(X^h) = C_b^*(C_b C_b^*)^{-1} beta_b(X) is smooth, u(E)-valued, and C_b(alpha(X^h)) = beta_b(X); by Proposition 43.1 the Berry connection of nabla + alpha is nabla^K + beta. ∎

**Corollary 43.3 (scalar changes).** Restricting alpha to scalar forms (alpha = i a (x) 1_E, a real), the attainable changes are the compressions of real functions, and every connection on K is attained by a scalar change iff the k^2 functions <s_i, s_j> on F_b are linearly independent. For the kernel H^0(CP^3, O(1)) of the Dolbeault-Dirac operator twisted by O(1), with s_i = z_i/|z| in the unitary frame, <s_i, s_j> = z-bar_i z_j/|z|^2, sixteen linearly independent functions, so the criterion holds and every connection on the rank-four kernel bundle is the Berry connection of an abelian change of the coefficient connection; in matrix form, Q(<w, B w>/<w, w>) = (B + tr B)/5 (lemma 10, the second-moment identity), a bijection of Hermitian matrices. For functions pulled back from the base S^4 of the twistor fibration, the compressed image is the real span of 1 and Gamma_1, ..., Gamma_5, of dimension six (a real function from S^4 compresses to a Hermitian element commuting with j, Example 16.9, item 1, and Proposition 10.1 shows the span is attained), so scalar changes along the base attain only the connections whose difference from nabla^K lies in that span. Whether the complementary ten directions are reached by matrix-valued alpha of a restricted class, such as components between the summands of E, is a further application of criterion 3 to the core modes that has not been made; the first version of this corollary asserted it and read the collar connection of lemma 29 as an instance, which it is not (that connection acts at the wall, on the CP^3 copy, and moves the kernel).

*Proof.* The first sentence is Theorem 43.2 with the scalar subalgebra in place of Gamma(u(E)). The CP^3 statement is the second-moment identity Q(w^* B w/w^* w) = (B + tr B)/5 (Example 16.9, item 2, from the fourth-moment formula in the proof of Proposition 10.1), which maps Herm(4) onto itself with eigenvalues 1/5 and 1; the S^4 statement is Example 16.9, item 1, with Proposition 10.1. ∎

**Remark 43.4 (the test case of the corpus).** On the Berger family of S^5 with the kernel of flux (1, 1, 2) the corpus engineered a connection with a prescribed Berry connection and found that all three coefficient lines keep a circle zero mode exactly when eta^2 is an integer; that calculation rests on stated spin lifts and was checked for its phase arithmetic only (historical/corpus/canonical_research.md, section 9, and research_checks, section 4.7). Theorem 43.2 says when such engineering is possible; it does not redo that calculation, and the integrality statement stays a record.

## Scope

Constant-rank kernels, compact fibres, unitary connections with volume-preserving horizontal transport or the mean-curvature term; the Berry connection as the compressed horizontal derivative; changes of the connection with no vertical component, which leave the kernels fixed. The theorem is linear algebra fibre by fibre and a smoothness remark; its content for the project is the criterion of item 3, which separates the two sheets of lemma 29 for scalar changes: the wall copy (functions on CP^3) attains every connection, the core copy (functions on S^4) six directions of sixteen. The collar connection of lemma 29 is a vertical change at the wall and moves the kernel, so the question of T15, whether it is a choice or a construction, stays open; a theorem that covers it must include the motion of the kernels (referee).
