## Outcome

1. **In the scope stated below, the dilaton gradient does not supply the second scale.** Along any path that opens the circle, the relative variation of the profile is bounded by 6/R^2 (Proposition 10.5, an upper bound; it is zero in the chamber 8 + 8). Under the hypothesis that the effective potential has a regular joint expansion in theta and alpha'/l^2 around the flat family, the gradient enters as a power of alpha'/l^2, and it can matter only if the leading Casimir coefficient a_0 vanishes and, when a_1 also vanishes, only if the coefficient b of theta^2 vanishes too (Proposition 10.6, with the counterexample at b = 0).
2. **a_0 is now computed**, which was the open item of [01](31_interval_as_antiperiodic_circle.md): 8 pi^2 a_0 = (3 zeta(5)/16)(4 n_D' - 3 (n_i - (15/16) n_h) - n_s - 5 n_g), with n_i and n_h the generators with integer and half-integer towers at theta = 0. For the corpus content it is positive, so at v = 0 the interval runs to large length, in the direction H wants.
3. **The dictionary of 06 to 09 needs three corrections** (Propositions 10.1 to 10.4). The map from angles to positions is nonlinear, and linear as a function of every position exactly when the profile is constant. If the tower of Proposition 6.1 is identified with the type I' winding states, which needs the proposed string embedding and holds in the source at constant dilaton, then the length l of H and the type I' length are T-dual, and l -> infinity shrinks the type I' interval at fixed heterotic coupling. And the configuration 15 + 1 has a Romans mass in the bulk, which bounds length times coupling, so the classical profile has no regime resolved at the formal local scale g^{1/3} l_s.
4. **A scale-free action can still separate the window**, with a fixed small ratio, if the light mass is exponentially small in a stabilized shape parameter (Remark 10.7), which amends the last sentence of Proposition 1.5. This is a separated window at each radius; it is not the parametric hierarchy of Theorem 30.5 of lemma 30, where the ratio tends to zero.

The direction: the second scale must enter through a bulk energy depending on the electroweak order parameter, through a cancellation a_0 = 0, or through a fixed shape with an exponentially small ratio, as in [lemma 29](29_window_on_the_disc_bundle.md). These targets are stated in Proposition 10.6 and Remark 10.7. None is derived.

## The dictionary against the source

Polchinski and Witten give, in conformal gauge, g = Omega^2(x) eta with Omega = C z^{-1/6}, e^phi = z^{-5/6}, and z = 3 C (B mu_8 - nu_0 x)/sqrt 2, with nu_0 = (N(x) - 8) mu_8 piecewise constant. This confirms the slope rule recalled in [08](35_orientifold_sign.md) and [09](36_dilaton_spectral_and_so32.md). We write z for this function up to its constant, normalized so that its slope is exactly 8 - N(x), which fixes the normalization in the formula for R below, with x in [0, L].

**Proposition 10.1 (the map from angles to positions).** Put W(x) = int_0^x z^{-1/3} dy. By Polchinski-Witten, equations (mass) and (wilmap), the Wilson-line eigenvalue of a brane at x_k is lambda_k = W(x_k) / (2 W(L)), that is

    theta_k = pi lambda_k = (pi/2) W(x_k) / W(L),

and with L = 2 pi, in their units alpha' = 2, R^2 = (3/4) int z^{1/3} dx / (int z^{-1/3} dx)^2. So the linear dictionary x_k = (2L/pi) theta_k of Proposition 9.1 holds as a function of every position exactly when z is constant (8 + 8), and asymptotically for large R; a particular configuration can satisfy it at its brane positions with z nonconstant, as fifteen branes at 0 and one at L do, with angles 0 and pi/2 and slope -7 in the bulk. The normalization: the physical profile in e^phi = z_phys^{-5/6} is z_phys = (3 C mu_8/sqrt 2) h with h of slope 8 - N(x), and Polchinski and Witten set the heterotic alpha'_h = 2 and keep the type I' alpha' separate.

*Proof.* The string stretched from the wall to x_k has mass (1/(2 pi alpha')) int_0^{x_k} Omega^2 dx, since its area element is Omega^2 dx^0 dx^9, and Omega^2 is proportional to z^{-1/3}. A string winding from 0 to L and back has the mass 1/R. The ratio gives lambda_k; the formula follows from the brane-to-brane mass differences of their equations (14) and (15), since a string ending on the wall is not an available state in every configuration. The formula for R is their equation (16), reduced as in their equation for the dilaton. ∎

Consequence for Proposition 9.1: its items 1 and 2 hold as statements about z in terms of the positions x_k. Reading them as a trace of a function of |D_F| needs the map above, which depends on the whole configuration, so the coupling function is a trace only implicitly. Item 3 changes accordingly.

**Proposition 10.2 (15 + 1 with infinite coupling at the far wall).** Take fifteen branes at x = 0 and one at x_t, with z(L) = 0, and put rho = (z(x_t)/z(0))^{2/3} in [0, 1]. Then, with alpha' = 2,

    theta_t = (pi/2) 8 (1 - rho) / (8 - rho),        R_*^2 = 14 (8 - rho^2) / (8 - rho)^2.

So R_* runs from sqrt 2, for the top's brane on the stack, to sqrt 7 / 2, for the top's brane on the far wall, which are the values (1/2)|n - 8|^{1/2} of Polchinski and Witten at n = 16 and 15. In heterotic string units, R_*/sqrt(alpha'_h) lies in [sqrt(7/8), 1]. At each fixed Wilson angle, a nonnegative far-wall value of the normalized profile gives R_h >= R_*(theta): with A = h(0)^{2/3}, T = h(x_t)^{2/3}, E = h(L)^{2/3} and a = theta/pi, direct integration gives R_h^2 = 2 - a^2 + 28 E/(8A - T - 7E), and R_*^2 = 2 - (theta/pi)^2 is the value at E = 0. The critical point uses the classical profile at divergent coupling.

*Proof.* Integrate z^{1/3} and z^{-1/3} on the two segments, with slopes -7 and -8, and use z(x_t) = 8 (L - x_t). ∎ Python agrees at x_t/L = 0.5, with theta_t = 0.5859 and R^2 = 1.9652, against 0.7854 for the linear dictionary (Python, scipy: 0.585918469 and 1.965216392).

So infinite coupling sits at the *smallest* radius of the chamber, at the string scale, and large R is large B, the weakly coupled region. Polchinski and Witten say this directly: "When B is large, the coupling e^{phi(x^9)} is small everywhere and the Type I' theory is weakly coupled; in this range the heterotic radius R is large".

**Proposition 10.3 (T-dual lengths, conditional on the string identification).** Suppose the tower of Proposition 6.1, n/R, is identified with the type I' winding tower. The source identifies a particular heterotic BPS momentum state with a type I' winding state ("a heterotic Kaluza-Klein state which is dual to a Type I' winding state", Polchinski and Witten); identifying the interval tower with those string states needs the proposed string embedding, and Wilson-line charges shift the momentum-to-winding dictionary (Bergman, Gaberdiel and Lifschytz), so the identification is a hypothesis here. With constant dilaton, the proper length L_p of the type I' interval and the radius satisfy L_p R_I = pi alpha' on the type I side, with alpha' that of type I'. So with l = pi R_I, measured in the type I' metric, l L_p = pi^2 alpha'. For the heterotic radius in the heterotic metric, l L_p = pi^2 alpha' sqrt(lambda_h), by Bergman, Gaberdiel and Lifschytz, with lambda_h the heterotic coupling.

The flat relations L_p R_I = pi alpha' and l L_p = pi^2 alpha' sqrt(lambda_h) hold at constant dilaton with these frame identifications; the source restricts its simple equations (1.2)-(1.3) to eight D8-branes at each wall, and in the chamber 15 + 1 the metric and coupling vary, so the applicable relation carries the Weyl factor, 1/R_h = (D/(pi alpha'_{I'})) int_0^L Omega^2 dx with L_p = int_0^L Omega dx. Equality of two lengths at a self-dual value does not identify the walls of the two descriptions. Under the hypothesis, the walls of H, at distance l, and the O8 planes, at distance L_p, coincide only at the self-dual length. In particular, the reading "the interval opens" in section 6b of [07](../lemmas/40_dualities_9_11.md) has to be qualified: under the identification and at fixed heterotic coupling, l -> infinity shrinks the type I' interval and decompactifies the SO(32) circle; the large-R_h, fixed-lambda_h decompactification is a conditional reading, which the exact flat formulas do not prove throughout the chamber 15 + 1.

The endpoint of that limit is the ten-dimensional SO(32) theory (heterotic or type I), since at fixed lambda_h the type I' coupling falls as 1/(sqrt(lambda_h) R_h). *Recalled, and supported in the two sources only by remarks of Bergman, Gaberdiel and Lifschytz at the constant-dilaton point:* the eleven-dimensional limit of the rank-17 moduli space is M-theory on an interval times a circle, with E8 x E8 at the walls, which by [Remark 8.3](35_orientifold_sign.md) is out of reach with colour-respecting moves. If so, label 11 of section 6b is not reached by theta -> 0, l -> infinity in the chamber 15 + 1.

**Proposition 10.4 (Romans mass, length and coupling).** On a segment with N branes to the left, the proper distance is linear in the inverse coupling:

    L_p = (C_L l_s / |8 - N|) |e^{-phi_2} - e^{-phi_1}|,     C_L = 8 pi / 5,     l_s^2 = alpha'_{I'},

with C_L from the standard normalization of the Romans mass, |F_0| = |8 - N|/(2 pi l_s), whose sign depends on the orientation; from the source profile and its charge normalization, |d e^{-phi}/ds| = 5 |8 - N|/(8 pi l_s). For N = 8, F_0 = 0, the coupling is constant on the segment, and the division and the bound below do not apply. So in the configuration 15 + 1, g(0) L_p <= C_L l_s / 7 for the total length. Near an infinite-coupling wall the coupling is g = C_L l_s / (|8 - N| s) at proper distance s, and the region g >= g_0 has length (3/4) (C_L / |8 - N|) g_0^{-4/3} in the formal local scale l_11 = g^{1/3} l_s, which is 0.54 g_0^{-4/3} for N = 15. Therefore no region of the classical 15 + 1 background has both strong coupling and a length resolved at that scale. A standard eleven-dimensional lift has not been established for this massive background, and the strong-coupling end of the classical profile is uncontrolled; the source presents its broader M-theory conclusion as an expectation.

*Proof.* Omega dx = C z^{-1/6} dz / (a C (8 - N)) integrates to a multiple of z^{5/6} = e^{-phi}. ∎ This agrees with Bergman, Gaberdiel and Lifschytz, who conclude from the D0-brane bound states that they "expect neither to be applicable to the N != 0 backgrounds", meaning an M-theory interpretation or a matrix theory, and that "only the combination of eight D8-branes and an orientifold plane is known to arise in M-theory". Here their N is 8 - N. It holds wherever N != 8. The scope is the classical profile, which is not controlled where g is of order one, so this is a statement about the extrapolated background.

**The far-wall electroweak group.** Remark 8.3 places SU(2) x U(1) at the far wall at infinite coupling. By Bergman, Gaberdiel and Lifschytz, the states that become massless there are D0-branes at the wall, heterotic winding states. ## The test

**Proposition 10.5 (the gradient is a string-scale effect).** With L = 2 pi and alpha' = 2, let B = min h > 0 for the normalized profile h. For large R, R^2 = 3 B/(8 pi) + O(1), and the relative variation (max h - min h)/B across the interval is at most 16 pi / B ~ 6 / R^2, an upper bound, attained nowhere in the chamber 8 + 8, where the variation is zero. So along a joint family, R_eps ~ eps^{-alpha}, this variation falls as eps^{2 alpha}, the map of Proposition 10.1 linearizes, and the differences of the dilaton along the interval tend to zero; a finite limiting value of the coupling needs control of the constant C, since z_phys is proportional to C h, and a path can have R_h -> infinity with the coupling diverging uniformly.

*Proof.* h is B plus a function of size at most 8 L = 16 pi. Insert in the formula for R. ∎

If the effective potential has a regular, differentiable expansion in the relative variation, and with the frame identification relating l to R_h, the corrections from the gradient are powers of alpha'/l^2. Small variation alone gives no such expansion: a correction proportional to the square root of the variation has another power. So the regular expansion is a hypothesis of Proposition 10.6, which this estimate guides and does not establish. In the supersymmetric type I' background there is no potential at all: D8 positions, B and C are flat directions. A potential needs broken supersymmetry, as in the flat family of lemmas 23 and 24.

**The coefficient a_0.** Write 8 pi^2 l^4 V = A_0 + 3 F(2u) + (3/2) F((16/5) u) - n_f F(t), the potential of lemma 24 with its theta-independent part A_0 restored, where n_f counts twice the Dirac species at frequency one (lemma 26). The relative terms vanish at theta = 0, and each tower at theta = 0 is half of a circle tower, periodic or antiperiodic:

    A_0 = (3 zeta(5) / 16) (4 n_D' - 3 (n_i - (15/16) n_h) - n_s - 5 n_g),

with n_D' = 2 N_D counting twice all Dirac species, of any frequency (for frequency zero the tower is the theta = 0 tower), n_i and n_h the five-dimensional gauge generators with integer and half-integer towers at theta = 0, n_s the real bulk scalars with integer towers, and n_g = 1 if the five-dimensional graviton is included. This assumes that the bulk vacuum energy and the wall tensions are tuned away, since they carry the terms of order Lambda^5 l and Lambda^4.

*Proof.* A real degree of freedom on a periodic circle of length 2 l has V = -3 zeta(5)/(4 pi^2 (2 l)^4), and the interval tower n >= 0 gives half of this, -3 zeta(5)/(128 pi^2 l^4). In the normalization of lemma 24 this is -(3/16) zeta(5) per real degree of freedom. A half-integer tower has the factor -15/16 relative to an integer one, so a generator with mixed conditions contributes +3 (15/16) (3/16) zeta(5). For a fermion channel the sign is opposite, with four degrees of freedom per unit of n_f. The check: the shift to theta = pi/2 multiplies a tower by -15/16, so the difference is (31/16)(3/4) zeta(5) = 93 zeta(5)/64 = F(1), which is the value computed in Proposition 1.4. ∎

In lemmas 23, 24 and 26 the gauge field is valued in sp(2) (+) su(4), with 25 generators. At theta = 0, sixteen have integer towers: 8 gluons, 3 W_L, 1 hypercharge combination with Neumann conditions at both walls, and the 4 odd generators O with Dirichlet conditions at both, whose interval components are the Higgs quaternion. Nine have mixed conditions and half-integer towers: 2 R^+-, 6 X and 1 orthogonal neutral direction. So n_i - (15/16) n_h = 121/16. For three generations, with 24 Dirac species, the bracket is 192 - 22.6875 = 169.3125 before gravity and bulk scalars, and A_0 = 32.92. So a_0 > 0: at v = 0 the length runs to infinity, in the direction of H, and the first half of the modulus target of the audit, a r^{-p} with a > 0 and p = 4, is present. For one generation the bracket is 41.3125.

**Proposition 10.6 (when the gradient can be the second scale).** Let V = l^-4 [G_0(theta) + (alpha'/l^2) G_1(theta) + ...], theta = l m, with G_0(0) = a_0 and G_0 = a_0 + b theta^2 + ... near 0.

1. If a_0 != 0, and the joint expansion in (theta, s), s = alpha'/l^2, with its derivatives is controlled near (0, 0), then at small m the leading balance is the scale-free one of Proposition 1.5: alpha = 1 or a runaway, and there is no stationary joint branch with theta, s -> 0. The term in alpha'/l^2 changes neither.
2. If a_0 = 0, G_1(0) = a_1 > 0 and b < 0, the truncated potential b m^2/l^2 + a_1 alpha'/l^6 has a minimum at l^4 = 3 a_1 alpha' / (|b| m^2), so alpha = 1/2: 1/l is the geometric mean of m and the string scale, up to constants, and m << 1/l << 1/l_s; with regular higher terms this is the leading asymptotic of the minimum, and in general not an exact equality.
3. If supersymmetry is exact at theta = 0, then V = 0 at theta = 0 at all orders, a_1 = 0, and V = m^2 l^-2 H(theta, alpha'/l^2). If b != 0 and the higher terms are regular, l is fixed by H independently of m, or runs away, and no joint family with theta, s -> 0 results, unless the stationarity condition has a root interval (a flat direction), as in the counterexample to the universal form of Proposition 1.5. If b = 0 the higher terms decide, and a joint family can result: for G_0 = theta^6 and G_1 = theta^2, V = m^6 l^2 + alpha' m^2 / l^4 is even in theta, vanishes at theta = 0 for every l, and has a strict minimum at l^6 = 2 alpha'/m^4, with l ~ m^{-2/3} and l m -> 0 (the example constructs no field theory producing this potential).

So in the scope of a potential with a regular joint expansion in theta and alpha'/l^2 around the flat family, with corpus content, where a_0 > 0, the gradient is not the second scale; the case a_0 = a_1 = b = 0 is outside the statement. Item 2 would need a Bose-Fermi balance at theta = 0 that is not a supersymmetry at theta = 0, with about 169 further real bosonic degrees of freedom for three generations.

*Proof.* Items 1 and 2: differentiate; in item 2, V = b m^2/l^2 + a_1 alpha'/l^6 has V'' = 8 |b| m^2/l^4 > 0 at the stationary point. Item 3: V is even in theta and vanishes at theta = 0 for every l, so V = theta^2 l^-4 H = m^2 l^-2 H. Stationarity is -2 H + theta H_theta - 2 s H_s = 0 with s = alpha'/l^2. With H(0, 0) = b != 0 and regular higher terms, the equation at small theta and s has no solution with both tending to zero, so its roots are angles independent of m, or there are none. With b = 0 the equation can balance powers of theta against powers of s, as the counterexample shows. ∎

**The flat family with the angle tied.** For the top alone, n_f = 6: b is proportional to 54/5 - 6 > 0, and G > 0 on [0, pi/2] (Python: minimum 23.88, with A_0 = 32.92). So V has no stationary point with theta < pi/2, and l runs until theta leaves the fundamental domain of Proposition 7.1, where the top joins the tower; the periodically continued potential has its first stationary points far outside the window, near theta = 8.15 and 8.40, with further roots near 11.17, 11.71, 14.27, ... (grid-and-root scan in Python, which proves no global count; periodicity of G with an interval where G' > 0 gives further roots for all large theta). For a universal mass at three generations, n_f = 48, G changes sign and there is one stable stationary point, theta_* = 1.009, with M_W/m = 0.685. For a universal mass at one generation, n_f = 16, theta_* = 1.031, with M_W/m = 0.653 (Python). Top-only content therefore has no vacuum in the window in this scope, and adding a graviton does not change the classification. The stationary angles are of order one, so these vacua do not separate the window.

**A localized channel.** For a channel with bulk mass M = -mu and c = mu l, the spectrum of lemma 9 gives the one-loop term

    8 pi^2 l^4 V_loc = -2 int_0^infinity x^3 log(1 + ((w + c)/(w - c)) exp(-2 w)) dx,    w = sqrt(x^2 + c^2),

from the secular function cosh(w) - (c/w) sinh(w). At c = 0 it is -(45/64) zeta(5) = -0.729, and for large c it is -4 c^3 exp(-2 c) up to corrections. Corrections at large c are of relative order 1/c. In between it has its unique minimum at c = 1 exactly, since differentiating the logarithm of the integrand gives d_c log Q = 2 (1 - c)/w, of depth -1.3246 (Python, mpmath). This concerns the interaction term alone: the bulk term, -(4/15) c^5 in dimensional regularization, and the wall term are dropped as renormalizations of the bulk energy and wall tension, and with the bulk term included the minimum disappears. So the interaction term of one fermion channel prefers c = 1, where the light pair merges with the tower (lemma 9.7). A large c_*, which is what a separated window needs, must come from other fields, such as the bosonic tower of the scalar that gives mu.

## Other second scales

**Remark 10.7 (amendment of Proposition 1.5, last sentence).** On the scale-free path, m l is a fixed number. Proposition 1.5 says that on it the finite window never separates from the tower. That holds for theta_* of order one, which is what the flat family gives above. If m is exponentially small in a stabilized shape parameter, the ratio m/Delta is a small fixed number: a separated window at each radius, which is not the parametric hierarchy of Theorem 30.5, where the ratio tends to zero; and delta/R is a lower bound for the next level, not an established value of the gap. On the disc bundle of [lemma 29](29_window_on_the_disc_bundle.md), fix M and let R grow: m = lambda_M / R and Delta = delta / R both tend to zero, with lambda_M / delta exponentially small, and the count at fixed energy has seven-dimensional growth. So a potential R^-4 G(M), with M stabilized at a large M_* and G(M_*) > 0, gives the hierarchy of [lemma 30](30_massless_limit_as_a_joint_limit.md) with alpha = 1 and the fixed ratio lambda_{M_*}/delta, with no second scale; in lemma 30 the ratio tends to zero. The cost is that R then has no vacuum at finite value. The physical point at finite v needs one more term, and the stabilization at large M_* is a target, since the interaction term of one fermion channel prefers c = 1.

**Remark 10.8 (a bulk energy).** The ansatz V = a r^-p + b eps^2 r^q, with a > 0, has a minimum exactly when p + q > 0 and q b > 0, and then alpha = 2/(p + q), so alpha < 1 needs p + q > 2: for p = 4, q > -2 with q != 0. On the flat family the angle is a holonomy (Corollary 22.2), which no local bulk operator sees: at large l its effect is the Casimir term, q = -2, the borderline case alpha = 1. So a term falling more slowly than m^2/l^2 must come from elsewhere, and the natural place is a local bulk energy in the electroweak order parameter: for q = 1, V = a/l^4 + b v^2 M^3 l gives l^5 proportional to 1/v^2, so alpha = 2/5, if the bulk energy vanishes at v = 0. That requires the order parameter to be a bulk field with a bulk vacuum energy, and it departs from D_F as a pure boundary condition. Whether the spectral action provides this is open. In the heat cutoff, the dependence of the potential on D_F is exponentially small at large l Lambda, by Poisson summation over the tower, of relative size 4 exp(-(l Lambda)^2), so that action has no such term at this order. The factor (3 - d_eff)/2 of lemma 25 multiplies the term quadratic in the gauge field, a different term.

## Scope and escapes

Proposition 10.6 assumes a potential with a regular expansion in alpha'/l^2, around the flat family with its towers. It concerns the dilaton *gradient*. The following lie outside it: a dilaton potential from string tree level, in non-supersymmetric strings or with tadpoles, for which a term in positive powers of 1/g could grow with l at fixed v through the law of Proposition 10.4; a different frame relation between l and the four-dimensional Planck mass; wall-localized dynamics; and the other chambers of the moduli space, where 8 + 8 removes the Romans mass and E8 x E8 is available. The string-theory statements are read from the two sources for type I' and recalled for the moduli-space limits; only the first have been checked against the text. For three generations A_0 = 32.9184, with the positivity and the runaway direction depending on the displayed field content and on the tuning of the bulk energy and the wall tensions.
