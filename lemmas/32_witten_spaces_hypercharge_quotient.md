## Setting

Witten's spaces M^{pqr} = G/H, with G = SU(3) x SU(2) x U(1) and H = SU(2) x U(1) x U(1), are the seven-dimensional spaces of least dimension with the isometry group of the Standard Model, so 4 + 7 = 11 (Witten 1981; [paper/LITERATURE.md §F](../paper/LITERATURE.md#f-weinberg-angle-from-a-squashed-s3--lens-space-and-from-mpqr)). Castellani, D'Auria and Fré gave them Einstein metrics; Killing spinors exist only for p = q, where the AdS4 vacuum has N = 2 and the space is M^{111} (Fabbri, Fré, Gualtieri, Termonia, hep-th/9903036). Fabbri et al. (hep-th/9907219, §4) write the cone over M^{111} as the Kähler quotient of C^5 by a circle acting with weights 2 on a triplet U and -3 on a doublet V, with D-term 2|U|^2 = 3|V|^2, and M^{111} as a circle bundle over P^2 x P^1. In later notation it is M^{3,2}.

These weights are six times the hypercharges of the 5-bar of SU(5). The rest of the file develops that observation.

## Theorem 2.1 (M^{111} is the hypercharge quotient of the 5-bar)

**Statement.** Let 5bar = C^3 + C^2 carry the Standard Model action on the left-handed fields d^c in (3bar, 1)_{1/3} and L in (1, 2)_{-1/2}; write U for d^c and V for L, so 6Y = 2 on U and -3 on V. Let mu_Y = (2|U|^2 - 3|V|^2)/6 be the moment map of hypercharge, and

    Y_{3,2} = (mu_Y^{-1}(0) ∩ S^9) / U(1)_Y.

1. Y_{3,2} is a smooth, simply connected seven-manifold, the circle bundle of O(3, 2) over CP^2 x CP^1 (up to orientation), and its cone is the Kähler quotient C^5 // U(1)_Y of Fabbri et al.
2. The cone is Calabi-Yau (Gorenstein, sum of weights 0) because tr Y = 0 on the 5-bar, that is, because U(1)_Y lies in SU(5).
3. The group acting effectively on Y_{3,2} through the quotient is (U(3) x U(2))/U(1)_Y. Its subgroup S(U(3) x U(2))/U(1)_Y is PSU(3) x SO(3), and the overall phase of U(5) supplies one further circle, the Reeb (R-symmetry) direction.
4. The functions on Y_{3,2} that are polynomial in U, V and their conjugates have U(1)_Y-charge zero; the holomorphic ones are spanned by the monomials of degree (3k, 2k) in (U, V), whose span as tensors is Sym^{3k}(3bar) x Sym^{2k}(2) and as functions its dual Sym^{3k}(3) x Sym^{2k}(2): for k = 1 the functions form the (10, 3) of Fabbri et al.

**Proof.** On mu_Y^{-1}(0) ∩ S^9 we have |U|^2 = 3/5 and |V|^2 = 2/5, a product of round spheres S^5 x S^3. The circle acts by (lambda^2 U, lambda^{-3} V); a fixed point would need lambda^2 = lambda^3 = 1, so lambda = 1 and the action is free. The quotient of S^5 x S^3 by the full torus T^2 of phases of U and V is CP^2 x CP^1, and Y_{3,2} -> CP^2 x CP^1 is a principal bundle for T^2/U(1)_Y. A character lambda_U^m lambda_V^n of T^2 is trivial on U(1)_Y exactly when 2m - 3n = 0, so the characters of T^2/U(1)_Y are generated by (m, n) = (3, 2), whose line bundle is O(3) ⊠ O(2) up to the sign convention; this gives item 1 and item 4. Simple connectivity follows from the exact homotopy sequence of U(1) -> S^5 x S^3 -> Y_{3,2}. Item 2: the sum of weights is 3 * 2 + 2 * (-3) = 0. Item 3: an element (g, h) of U(3) x U(2) acts trivially on the quotient exactly when (g, h) = (lambda^2, lambda^{-3}), because mu_Y^{-1}(0) spans C^5. The intersection of SU(3) x SU(2) with U(1)_Y consists of lambda^6 = 1, generated by (e^{2 pi i/3} I_3, -I_2), the centre Z_3 x Z_2. ∎

*Scope.* The identification with M^{111} is conditional on the recalled description of M^{111} as the circle bundle of class 3 h_1 + 2 h_2; the quotient of the round metric becomes Sasaki-Einstein after the transverse rescaling by 5/4, and the holomorphic functions transform in the dual of the action on U (end of the lemma).

So the isometry group of the Witten space with Killing spinors, SU(3) x SU(2) x U(1) (Fabbri et al.), is the centralizer of hypercharge in U(5), divided by hypercharge. The SU(3) x SU(2) part is the Standard Model one; the remaining circle is the R-symmetry, and hypercharge itself has been quotiented. Witten assigned the U(1) of G to hypercharge; in this realization hypercharge is a different U(1) ([03](../lemmas/39_branes_hypercharge_and_the_84.md), Proposal 3.1).

## Proposition 2.2 (uniqueness of the count 7)

**Statement.** (a) Among the quotients Y_{a,b} = (S^5 x S^3)/U(1), with weights b on C^3 and -a on C^2 and gcd(a, b) = 1, a, b > 0, the cone is Calabi-Yau only for (a, b) = (3, 2). (b) For a complex representation of dimension n and a free circle action, (S^{2n-1} ∩ mu^{-1}(0))/U(1) has dimension 2n - 3; this is 7 only for n = 5. Among representations of SU(5), with hypercharge as the circle, only the 5 and the 5-bar give a seven-dimensional quotient.

**Proof.** (a) The Calabi-Yau condition is 3b - 2a = 0. (b) Dimension count: 2n - 1 for the sphere, minus one for the moment map, minus one for the circle. The irreducible representations of SU(5) of dimension 5 are the 5 and the 5-bar. ∎

Witten's count reads: 11 = 4 + (2 * 5 - 3). The seven internal dimensions come from the smallest representation of SU(5), with hypercharge as the circle. For the 10 the quotient has dimension 17, and for the 5 + 10 it has 27.

## Proposition 2.3 (topology of Y_{a,b})

**Statement.** Let Y_{a,b} -> B = CP^2 x CP^1 be the circle bundle with Euler class a h_1 + b h_2, a != 0. Then H^2(Y) = Z + Z/gcd(a, b), b_3(Y) = 0, H^4(Y) is a group of order a^2, cyclic when gcd(a, b) = 1, and Y is spin exactly when a is odd and b is even. For (a, b) = (3, 2): H^2 = Z, H^4 = Z/9, spin. The pullback H^2(B) = Z^2 -> H^2(Y) = Z is (x, y) -> 2x - 3y up to sign: the two divisor classes h_1 = [{l(U) = 0}] and h_2 = [{m(V) = 0}] go to 2 and -3, the values of 6Y on d^c and L.

**Proof.** The Gysin sequence H^{k-2}(B) -> H^k(B) -> H^k(Y) -> H^{k-1}(B) -> H^{k+1}(B), with the first map the cup product with c = a h_1 + b h_2 and H^*(B) = Z[h_1, h_2]/(h_1^3, h_2^2). In degree 2 the cokernel of 1 -> (a, b) is Z^2/(a, b). In degree 4 the map H^2(B) -> H^4(B) has matrix [[a, 0], [b, a]] in the bases (h_1, h_2), (h_1^2, h_1 h_2), with determinant a^2 and Smith invariants (gcd(a, b), a^2/gcd(a, b)); since H^3(B) = 0 this gives H^4(Y), and the same matrix is injective, so H^3(Y) = 0. For spin: the vertical tangent line is trivial, so w_2(Y) = pi^* w_2(B), with w_2(B) = h_1 mod 2, and the kernel of H^2(B; Z/2) -> H^2(Y; Z/2) is spanned by c mod 2; so w_2(Y) = 0 exactly when (1, 0) = (a, b) mod 2. For (3, 2), Z^2/(3, 2) -> Z by (x, y) -> 2x - 3y is an isomorphism. ∎ (Python: Smith invariants and the spin condition, section C.)

The product member CP^2 x S^2 x S^1 of Witten's family (a = b = 0) is not spin.

## Proposition 2.4 (the Z_6 of the Standard Model)

**Statement.** (a) Every monomial in U and V, and every state of the exterior algebra Λ^*(5bar), satisfies 2t + 3d + 6Y = 0 mod 6, with t the triality (1 for 3, -1 for 3bar) and d = 2j mod 2. (b) The U(1)_Y-neutral monomials have t = 0 and d = 0. (c) Every tensor field on Y_{3,2} carries a representation of (U(3) x U(2))/U(1)_Y, on which SU(3) x SU(2) acts through PSU(3) x SO(3). So no tensor Kaluza-Klein mode on Y_{3,2}, on a background invariant under the displayed action, is a colour triplet or a weak doublet; spinors are open.

**Proof.** (a) holds for U (t = -1, d = 0, 6Y = 2) and V (t = 0, d = 1, 6Y = -3) and is additive. (b) U^alpha V^beta is neutral when 2 alpha = 3 beta, so 3 | alpha and 2 | beta. (c) The effective group of Theorem 2.1 acts on the frame bundle and on every tensor bundle, and its intersection with SU(3) x SU(2) is the centre Z_6 (Theorem 2.1, item 3). ∎

(a) is the statement that the Standard Model group is (SU(3) x SU(2) x U(1))/Z_6 = S(U(3) x U(2)). In the quotient realization the Z_6 is the kernel of SU(3) x SU(2) -> Isom(Y_{3,2}). States with fractional hypercharge are those charged under the quotient circle, and they cannot be functions or tensors on Y_{3,2}. For spinors the isometry group may lift to a double cover; whether fermionic Kaluza-Klein modes on Y_{3,2} can carry half-integer weak isospin or triality is open (next job, item 2 of the README).

## Chiral zero modes on the base with abelian fluxes

Witten's argument makes Kaluza-Klein fermions on an odd-dimensional space vectorlike, for gauge fields acting by isometries. The even base B_6 = CP^2 x CP^1 escapes it: abelian fluxes on B_6 give chiral four-dimensional fermions through the index of the twisted Dirac operator. This section asks which fluxes give one generation.

**Setting.** Ten-dimensional Weyl fermions on M^4 x CP^2 x CP^1, each with definite charges under some U(1) factors with fluxes on B_6. Since CP^2 is not spin, a fermion is a spin^c spinor, Λ^{0,*} ⊗ K^{1/2} ⊗ N with N the flux line bundle of its charges, and the four-dimensional chiral content is the virtual representation H^*(O(alpha, beta)) with (alpha, beta) = (-3/2, -1) + pairing of the charges with the fluxes. The cohomology is H^0(O(alpha)) = Sym^alpha(3bar) for alpha >= 0, H^2(O(alpha)) = Sym^{-alpha-3}(3) for alpha <= -3, and zero otherwise on CP^2; on CP^1, H^0(O(beta)) = Sym^beta(2) for beta >= 0 and H^1(O(beta)) = Sym^{-beta-2}(2) for beta <= -2. Each Standard Model Weyl field must come from one ten-dimensional field, from the whole cohomology of one line bundle.

**Lemma (required bidegrees).** Writing P_s = (alpha + 3/2, beta + 1) for the pairing of the charges of field s (up to the common sign of charge and chirality):

| field | P_s | note |
|---|---|---|
| Q (3, 2) | (-5/2, 2) | H^2(O(-4)) x H^0(O(1)) |
| u^c, d^c (3bar, 1) | (5/2, 1) | H^0(O(1)) x H^0(O(0)) |
| L (1, 2) | (+-3/2, 2) | |
| e^c, nu^c (1, 1) | (+-3/2, 1) | |

The second entry is 2j + 1 and the first is +-5/2 on colour triplets and +-3/2 on singlets. (Python, section D, by enumeration of the line bundles with these cohomologies and the Riemann-Roch check.)

### Proposition 2.5 (no generation from fluxes along hypercharge and B - L)

*Scope.* The statement on equal images and the linearity of 2j + 1 assume a common ten-dimensional chirality for the fields. The conclusion holds without it: in the span of 6Y and 3(B - L) the charges of L are -3 times those of Q, so their second pairings are P_2(L) = -3 P_2(Q), against |P_2| = 2 on both doublets.

The bounded search at the end of the proof below has an analytic proof (end of the lemma), which covers any real third charge and independently chosen source chiralities (the vectors y, b, p, w have rank 4 for every sign pattern).

**Statement.** In the setting above, with the canonical spin^c root and fluxes only in U(1) directions spanned by Y and B - L (any flux quanta), no choice reproduces one generation, with or without nu^c. More generally, the pairing must give u^c and d^c the same image, and 2j + 1 = (2, 1, 1, 2, 1, 1) on (Q, u^c, d^c, L, e^c, nu^c) must be a linear function of the fluxed charges.

**Proof.** The pairing M is linear in the charges, and M c_s = P_s with c_s the charges of the left-handed field s. With only Y and B - L fluxed, c_u - c_d = (-6, 0) in the coordinates (6Y, 3(B - L)), so M vanishes on the hypercharge direction and P_s = 3(B - L)_s m for one vector m. The second entries 2 on Q and 1 on u^c would then need 3(B - L) to take values in the ratio 2 : 1 on (Q, u^c), against 1 : -1. In general, 2j + 1 is not in the span of 6Y = (1, -4, 2, -3, 6, 0) and 3(B - L) = (1, -1, -1, -3, 3, 3): from Q and u^c the coefficients are -1 and 3, and then d^c gives -5 against 1. ∎ An exhaustive search adding a third integer charge z in [-3, 3]^6 to Y and B - L finds no solution either (Python, section D).

**Scope and escapes.** Abelian fluxes on CP^2 x CP^1 with the Fubini-Study complex structure; one ten-dimensional Weyl field per Standard Model field; canonical spin^c root; Dolbeault index as the chiral content. Known escapes: nonabelian bundles (an SU(2)_R doublet bundle), several ten-dimensional fields per four-dimensional field, a changed common twist, orbifolds and walls, and non-Kaluza-Klein (brane) states. The result points to the next proposition.

### Proposition 2.6 (one generation from a universal charge, the grading and B - L)

*Conventions.* For the colour action of the quotient of Theorem 2.1, reverse the first-component coefficients to obtain the labelled colour representations (end of the lemma); global flux quantization remains to be specified.

**Statement.** Let the fluxed charges be F (one on every field: a change of the common spin^c twist), chi (+1 on Q and L, -1 on u^c, d^c, e^c, nu^c) and B - L. Then one generation with nu^c is the exact index content for the pairing

    P^{(1)} = c_0 chi + c_1 (B - L),   P^{(2)} = (3F + chi)/2,

with (c_0, c_1) = (-9/4, -3/4) or (-3/2, -3). The bidegrees are Q: (-4, 1); u^c, d^c: (1, 0); L: (-3, 1) or (0, 1); e^c, nu^c: (0, 0) or (-3, 0). Without chi, or without B - L, there is no solution.

**Reading.**

## What this file establishes, and the direction

The \(M^{111}\) identification is conditional on the recalled fibration and metric normalization. The flux examples establish a particular construction and scoped obstructions; a general necessity for the grading flux remains open.

The Witten space with Killing spinors is the hypercharge quotient of the 5-bar (Theorem 2.1), the count 7 is fixed by SU(5) (Proposition 2.2), and the Z_6 of the Standard Model is the kernel of the isometry action (Proposition 2.4). The price is that hypercharge is no longer an isometry, and no tensor Kaluza-Klein mode on an invariant background carries triality, weak isospin 1/2 or fractional hypercharge. Kaluza-Klein chirality on the base needs fluxes that see the grading and leave T_3R free (Propositions 2.5 and 2.6). Both results point to states outside the Kaluza-Klein spectrum of Y_{3,2} for the quarks and leptons: [03](../lemmas/39_branes_hypercharge_and_the_84.md) takes them to be wrapped branes.

**Proposition 2.5 without a common chirality.**

Hypotheses: one abelian line bundle per field, the canonical formal shift, fluxes in the span of Y and B - L. The exclusion holds even if the ten-dimensional Weyl fields have different chiralities.

Let M_2 be the component of the flux pairing M along CP^1, and use the charges c = (6Y, 3(B - L)). A weak doublet occurs for a line bundle O(beta) on CP^1 only when beta = 1 or beta = -3, which give H^0(O(1)) = 2 and H^1(O(-3)) = 2 respectively. The formal K^{1/2} shift on CP^1 is -1, so M_2(c) = beta + 1 and |M_2(c)| = 2 for each doublet. This remains true if a right-handed mode is conjugated to the left-handed convention, since conjugation reverses the sign of the pairing.

For the left-handed doublets,

    c_Q = (1, 1),   c_L = (-3, -3) = -3 c_Q.

Linearity gives M_2(c_L) = -3 M_2(c_Q), so |M_2(c_L)| = 3 |M_2(c_Q)|, whereas both absolute values must be 2. This contradiction excludes a generation with or without nu^c, for arbitrary flux quanta in the two specified directions. It uses neither the colour component nor a choice of signs for the individual doublets.

*Amendment to the required-bidegrees lemma.* On CP^2 x CP^1, K^{1/2} is a formal notation: K = O(-3, -2) has no line-bundle square root. A genuine spin^c structure and its charged twists must give integral final bidegrees. With a common ten-dimensional chirality, Serre duality relates a right-handed conjugate to the sign-reversed pairing, and the convention P^{(2)} = 2j + 1 of the table is consistent. If the ten-dimensional chiralities can be chosen independently, the table is incomplete. For example, O(-4, -3) has H^3 = H^2(O(-4)) ⊗ H^1(O(-3)) = (3, 2) and pairing (-5/2, -2); a suitable ten-dimensional chirality makes this a left-handed Q, and (-5/2, -2) is neither the table entry (-5/2, 2) nor its overall negative.

Likewise, with independent chiralities and independent flux directions, u^c can arise from H^0(O(1, 0)) = 3bar, while d^c arises by conjugating a right-handed H^2(O(-4, 0)) = 3 with opposite abelian charges. Their pairings in the left-handed charge convention are (5/2, 1) and (5/2, -1). The equality of their images, and the unsigned linear formula P^{(2)} = 2j + 1, are therefore conditional on the common-chirality convention; the direct doublet obstruction above uses neither.

*Scope and direction.* This concerns the Dolbeault cohomology of a single abelian line bundle for each field on CP^2 x CP^1, with the canonical formal shift and fluxes in the span of Y and B - L. Nonabelian bundles, additional charges or a common twist, multiple source fields, walls, orbifolds and brane states remain possible directions. It makes no claim about those alternatives.

**The third-charge exclusion in Proposition 2.5.** The argument covers the bounded search of the proof, a third integer charge in [-3, 3]^6 under the common-chirality convention, and also an arbitrary real third charge with independently chosen ten-dimensional chiralities.

Write, on (Q, u^c, d^c, L, e^c, nu^c),

    y = 6Y = (1, -4, 2, -3, 6, 0),    b = 3(B - L) = (1, -1, -1, -3, 3, 3).

(Here b is a charge vector, unrelated to the Euler-class coefficient b of Proposition 2.3.) For the stated colour convention the necessary CP^2 pairings are

    p = (-5/2, 5/2, 5/2, p_L, p_e, p_nu),   |p_L| = 3/2,

and the CP^1 pairings have the form

    w = (2 s_Q, s_u, s_d, 2 s_L, s_e, s_nu),   s_i in {+1, -1}.

The signs allow independently chosen source chiralities. Reversing the colour convention reverses p and leaves the argument unchanged.

Suppose that p and w both lie in the span of y, b and z, for some third charge z. The Q and L entries of y and of b have ratio -3, so every vector of span(y, b) has L entry equal to -3 times its Q entry, while |w_L/w_Q| = 1; hence w lies outside span(y, b). Then span(y, b, w) has dimension three and lies in span(y, b, z), so the two spans coincide, and

    p = a y + b_0 b + t w

for real a, b_0, t. Apply the combination 2Q + u^c + d^c, the linear form v -> 2 v_Q + v_u + v_d. It vanishes on y (2 - 4 + 2 = 0), on b (2 - 1 - 1 = 0) and on p (-5 + 5/2 + 5/2 = 0), and takes the value 4 s_Q + s_u + s_d on w. So

    0 = t (4 s_Q + s_u + s_d).

The factor in parentheses is nonzero, since |4 s_Q| = 4 > 2 >= |s_u + s_d|; hence t = 0. Equality of the u^c and d^c entries of p = a y + b_0 b, -4a - b_0 = 2a - b_0, gives a = 0, and their common value -b_0 = 5/2 gives b_0 = -5/2. The resulting L entry is -3 b_0 = 15/2, contradicting |p_L| = 3/2. The same four fields suffice without nu^c.

*Scope and direction.* This concerns the one-line-bundle-per-field Dolbeault construction, the specified Y and B - L charges and the canonical formal shift. **Theorem 2.1 and Propositions 2.2 and 2.4: freeness, the neutral-monomial congruence, the dual action and the Sasaki-Einstein normalization.**

Hypotheses: the identification with M^{111} is conditional on the recalled description of M^{111} as the circle bundle of class 3h_1 + 2h_2.

*Freeness and the bundle.* On the moment level in the unit sphere, |U|^2 = 3/5 and |V|^2 = 2/5. Both factors are nonzero, so the stabilizer of a point under (lambda^2 U, lambda^{-3} V) has lambda^2 = lambda^3 = 1, hence lambda = 1, and the action is free. The quotient has dimension 9 - 1 - 1 = 7 and is simply connected by the homotopy sequence of U(1) -> S^5 x S^3 -> Y_{3,2}. The residual character of the two Hopf phases has primitive weight (3, 2); its circle bundle over CP^2 x CP^1 therefore has Euler class ±(3h_1 + 2h_2). Principal circle bundles over this base are classified by their Euler classes, which gives the conditional identification with M^{111}. The zero sum 3(2) + 2(-3) = 0 gives the Gorenstein holomorphic cone with trivial canonical bundle; a Ricci-flat metric needs a separate argument.

*The congruence of Proposition 2.4.* The congruence 2t + 3d + 6Y ≡ 0 (mod 6) holds on U, where (t, d, 6Y) = (-1, 0, 2), and on V, where (t, d, 6Y) = (0, 1, -3), and extends additively to tensor products and to conjugates, which reverse all three quantities. For a monomial in U, V and their conjugates, let Delta_U be the number of factors U minus the number of factors Ubar, and Delta_V likewise for V. Neutrality under U(1)_Y reads

    2 Delta_U - 3 Delta_V = 0,

so 3 | Delta_U and 2 | Delta_V. The triality of the monomial is -Delta_U ≡ 0 (mod 3) and d ≡ Delta_V ≡ 0 (mod 2): zero triality and integer weak isospin. This extends item (b) of Proposition 2.4 from the holomorphic monomials U^alpha V^beta to all monomials in U, V and their conjugates.

*Dual action.* If U is the vector representation 3bar and projective space parametrizes its lines, the conventional pullback action on holomorphic coordinate functions is the dual 3: functions of degree 3k transform as Sym^{3k}(3). Treating U as formal field components gives Sym^{3k}(3bar) instead. The convention must be fixed consistently when identifying the 10 versus the 10bar and the later cohomology table.

*Sasaki-Einstein normalization.* With the standard unit-sphere quotient metric, the two transverse Fubini-Study factors have relative scales 3/5 and 2/5. CP^2 is the base of the Hopf circle on the sphere of radius sqrt(3/5), with Ricci constant 6/(3/5) = 10, and CP^1 is the base of the Hopf circle on the sphere of radius sqrt(2/5), a round sphere of radius sqrt(2/5)/2 with Ricci constant 4/(2/5) = 10; so each has Ricci constant 10. A seven-dimensional Sasaki-Einstein metric needs transverse Ricci constant 8. The Sasaki metric obtained by the standard transverse homothety of factor 5/4 has the same underlying circle bundle and the required Einstein normalization. Thus the existence of the Sasaki-Einstein metric on that manifold is compatible with the quotient, and the initial round-sphere quotient metric is identified with it only after the rescaling.

**Proposition 2.6, with the colour convention.** The statement is conditional on global flux quantization. The bidegrees and the two linear solutions are correct with the cohomology convention of the file. With the colour action on the quotient fixed as in Theorem 2.1, that convention is conjugated and the first-component coefficients must be reversed.

*The pairings.* The charges of the left-handed fields are F = 1 on all; chi = +1 on Q, L and -1 on u^c, d^c, e^c, nu^c; and B - L = 1/3, -1/3, -1/3, -1, 1, 1 on Q, u^c, d^c, L, e^c, nu^c. For (c_0, c_1) = (-9/4, -3/4), direct substitution in P^{(1)} = c_0 chi + c_1 (B - L) and P^{(2)} = (3F + chi)/2 gives

    P_Q = (-5/2, 2),   P_{u^c, d^c} = (5/2, 1),   P_L = (-3/2, 2),   P_{e^c, nu^c} = (3/2, 1);

for instance P_Q^{(1)} = -9/4 - 1/4 and P_L^{(1)} = -9/4 + 3/4. For (c_0, c_1) = (-3/2, -3), the quark pairings are unchanged (-3/2 - 1 = -5/2 and 3/2 + 1 = 5/2), and the first components for L and for e^c, nu^c are +3/2 and -3/2 respectively. Subtracting the formal shift (3/2, 1) yields exactly the listed integral bidegrees. On CP^2, the nonzero cohomology of a line bundle O(k) is H^0(O(k)) for k >= 0, or H^2(O(k)) ≅ H^0(O(-k - 3))^* for k <= -3; CP^1 has the analogous H^0/H^1 formula. So O(-4, 1) gives (3, 2), O(1, 0) gives 3bar, O(-3, 1) and O(0, 1) give (1, 2), and O(0, 0) and O(-3, 0) give a singlet, in the convention of the Setting. All listed choices have even total Dolbeault degree (0 or 2), so the common-chirality convention gives the indicated chiral index.

*The colour convention.* If the base is P(3bar), as in the quotient of Theorem 2.1, then H^0(O(1)) = 3 and H^2(O(-4)) = 3bar, and the listed quark bundles carry the conjugates of the labelled colour representations. Replacing (c_0, c_1) by (9/4, 3/4) or (3/2, 3), with the second component unchanged, reverses the first pairings and exchanges O(1) with O(-4) for the quarks; this gives the labelled Q and u^c, d^c with the colour action of the quotient. Alternatively, the original table is valid for P(3), with the corresponding conjugate colour convention.

*Without chi.* The required weak-doublet pairings have absolute value 2. Write P^{(2)} = alpha F + beta (B - L), so P_Q^{(2)} = alpha + beta/3 and P_L^{(2)} = alpha - beta. If the two signs for Q and L agree, then beta = 0 and |alpha| = 2, and u^c gets |P^{(2)}| = 2, the wrong absolute value for a weak singlet. If they disagree, say alpha + beta/3 = 2 and alpha - beta = -2, then beta = 3, alpha = 1, and u^c gets P^{(2)} = 1 - 1 = 0.

*Without B - L.* Q and L have identical charges F = 1, chi = +1, so their pairings coincide, while they require colour pairings of absolute values 5/2 and 3/2 respectively.

These obstructions use only absolute values, so they also allow independent source chiralities.

*Global data.* The final line-bundle degrees are integral, but K^{1/2} itself is formal and the displayed coefficients include fractional flux components. An actual background requires a specified global gauge group, charge lattice, spin^c determinant and compatible flux quantization. The index calculation alone establishes neither those data nor the physical interpretation of the grading as a gauge connection.
