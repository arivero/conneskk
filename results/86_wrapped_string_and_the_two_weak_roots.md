# A wrapped string coupled to the two weak roots

Keep the oriented polar weak sphere $\Sigma$, its unit monopole line $V_1$, the circle charges $n_\pm=\pm k/2$ and the two sections $H_\pm$ of 63. Vary the degree of a Euclidean string worldsheet wrapping $\Sigma$ and the orientations and phases of the root doublets. We compute the fermion insertion on that worldsheet. The resulting Fourier component of the four-dimensional action can distinguish the two physical angles in 85. We specify an effective string with chiral worldsheet matter. A critical or supersymmetric string embedding would also have to supply the remaining worldsheet fields and verify their zero modes.

## Worldsheet anomaly and the determinant

**Theorem 86.1.** Add a unit electric string of the charge-two axion's two-form, with the following complex Weyl fields on its Euclidean sheet. The columns are charge under $A_v$ and momentum charge under the period-$2\pi$ circle connection. A neutral spectator has no other background bundle in this calculation.

| Chirality | Fields $(N,n)$ |
|---|---|
| Left | $\lambda_+:(1,-n_+)$, $\lambda_-:(1,-n_-)$, $\lambda_0:(0,0)$ |
| Right | $\rho:(2,0)$, $\rho_+:(0,-n_+)$, $\rho_-:(0,-n_-)$ |

Use mass vertices $y_+\bar\rho H_+\lambda_+ +y_-\bar\rho H_-\lambda_-+\mathrm{h.c.}$ with nonzero constant $y_\pm$. The gauge anomaly polynomial on the worldsheet is $-c_v^2$ in the left-minus-right convention, and the gravitational component vanishes. The unit magnetic flux on the weak sphere then supplies the integer surface charge of magnitude two. For a holomorphic sphere cover of degree $w\ge1$ the leading fermion insertion is the homogeneous resultant of the two pulled-back sections, times $(y_+y_-)^w$ and nonsingular normalization factors. With the surface orientation specified below, the gauge-invariant phase is $w(\alpha-\theta_2)$. It is nonzero in the determinant calculation for every genuine cover if the root doublets are independent.

**Proof.** The signed squared vortex charges sum to $1+1-4=-2$, with a factor $1/2$ in the complex Weyl anomaly polynomial. The mixed circle charge trace vanishes since $n_++n_-=0$. The two circle-only right spectators cancel the pure circle trace. There are three fields of each chirality, so the two gravitational terms cancel. Each mass vertex is invariant under both gauge groups. In the family of worldsheets, integrating $-c_v^2$ over a sheet carrying $w$ units of flux gives $-2w c_v^{\rm ext}$. The conjugate determinant line therefore carries charge $+2w$ in the insertion convention. We use the surface factor $\exp(-iw\theta_2)$ of the transgression in 77.1. Reversing orientation gives the complex conjugate amplitude. This factor and the chiral determinant together have a well-defined gauge variation even when a gauge-fixed two-form potential needs patching.

The spin line of the worldsheet is $\mathcal O(-1)$. The two positive-chirality charge-one fields each have holomorphic kernel $H^0(\mathcal O(w-1))$ of dimension $w$. The right charge-two kinetic operator has cokernel dual Grassmann modes from $H^0(\mathcal O(2w-1))$, of dimension $2w$. Their complementary chiralities have no unpaired zeros. The circle pulls back trivially on the sheet fixed at a circle position. Every neutral or circle-only spectator consequently has spin line $\mathcal O(-1)$ with zero kernel and cokernel. The unpaired Grassmann variables are saturated by the determinant of
$$
 H^0(\mathcal O(w-1))^{\oplus2}\ \longrightarrow\
 H^0(\mathcal O(2w-1)),\qquad
 (s_+,s_-)\longmapsto y_+P\,s_+ + y_-Q\,s_- ,
$$
where $P,Q$ are the two degree-$w$ pullbacks. In monomial bases the determinant is the Sylvester resultant up to orientation. If the sections have no common zero, the pencil exact sequence has kernel $\mathcal O(-1)$ after this twist. Its $H^0$ and $H^1$ both vanish, so the map on sections is an isomorphism. A common zero forces every image section to vanish there, hence the determinant vanishes. Writing the degree-$w$ cover as a basepoint-free pair $(P_0,Q_0)$ and the root coefficient matrix as $D_H$, the two columns arise by a constant two-by-two transformation of the source. Thus the determinant is proportional to $(\det D_H)^w\operatorname{Res}(P_0,Q_0)$. The root determinant transforms by $e^{2i\lambda_v}$ and has phase $\alpha$ up to a fixed choice of basis. This proves the phase and the zero-mode selection. $\square$

On the unit cover the charge-one zero spinor $e_b$ of the finite matter calculation supplies the actual columns $H_+e_b,H_-e_b$ in the charge-two kernel. At the polar solution they are orthogonal by their distinct axial weights. Their norm product times $|y_+y_-|$ is the absolute determinant in orthonormal bases. At fixed near-round radii it is of order $\sqrt\epsilon$ and is invariant under the opposite amplitude change $H_\pm\mapsto e^{\pm\tau}H_\pm$. The leading insertion can exert a force on the product amplitude while leaving that relative rescaling free. On a sufficiently long neck the small-mass expansion of the nonzero-mode determinant also needs to be checked. ## Two independent contributions to the phase potential

**Proposition 86.2.** Suppose the unit charged-sheet saddle contributes a nonzero vacuum harmonic with coefficient $\kappa_2>0$. Add it to ordinary QCD with full-rank quark masses and the anomaly distribution of 85.1. Write the potential as
$$
 V=E_{\rm QCD}(\Theta_0+3u-t v)-\kappa_2\cos(u+\delta_2).
$$
For $t\ne0$, at simultaneous minima with $\chi=E_{\rm QCD}''(0)>0$ the unnormalized Hessian determinant is $\chi\kappa_2t^2$. If the QCD minimum is unique per $2\pi$, there are $|t|$ minima on the two-angle torus. In particular the allowed integer choice $t=1$ gives one gapped phase vacuum and a vanishing physical strong angle for any $\delta_2,\Theta_0$ in this two-term model.

**Proof.** On the torus the integer rows of the two terms are $(3,-t)$ and $(1,0)$. They define a map of degree $|t|$. The first equation fixes the strong angle at a minimum and the second fixes $u=-\delta_2\bmod 2\pi$. The Hessian is the sum of the outer products of the rows with coefficients $\chi,\kappa_2$, giving the determinant stated. With the positive kinetic metric $G$ in 85.2 the product of the two squared pole masses is $\chi\kappa_2t^2/\det G$ at leading two-derivative order below the other thresholds. In the strongly pinned-$u$ limit the QCD-sensitive direction approaches $v$ and its decay constant is that in 85.2. When $t=0$ both terms depend on $u$ alone and the translation of $v$ survives. $\square$

For a string of tension $T$ in the product metric the classical unit-cover action is $T\mathcal A_\Sigma$, where $\mathcal A_\Sigma=a^2\mathcal A_b$ is the physical weak area. The pullback of the weak volume form is closed and has comass one. Integration bounds the area of any smooth representative below by the absolute degree times $\mathcal A_\Sigma$, with equality for the product sphere and orientation-preserving conformal covers. The unit saddle has position moduli in the other factors. With a nonsingular nonzero-mode determinant, the appropriate collective-coordinate measure and no unsaturated extra fermionic modes, the leading dilute term has $\kappa_2$ proportional to $e^{-T\mathcal A_\Sigma}|\det(y H)|$. Its dimension-four normalization is additional information about the string. Quantum determinants, moduli integrations and short-distance corrections can change it. A perturbative treatment requires curvature small on the string scale as well as large classical action. Perturbing the modulus potential also calls for solving the resulting radius and root equations to obtain a quantum vacuum.

Two copies of the charged worldsheet system can instead cancel the $p=2$ inflow of a charge-four surface. The leading unit insertion then has phase $2\alpha-\theta_4=2u-v$ and root factor $(\det D_H)^2$. If a second independent surface generates $-\kappa_4\cos(2u-v+\delta_4)$, it changes the strong-CP conclusion of the two-term model. For $t=1$ the QCD phase is the *sum* of the two surface angles up to constants. At a stationary point the $v$ equation requires $|E'_{\rm QCD}(\Theta)|\le\kappa_4$; for a solution continuously near the CP-conserving minimum this bounds $|\Theta|\lesssim\kappa_4/\chi$ when that ratio is small. Arbitrary comparable surface potentials need their relative phases and sizes. Whether a wrapped M5 supplies the second harmonic requires its actual membrane boundaries and their couplings, tested in 87. Algebraic zero-mode saturation proves which harmonic is available. A full string path integral and the absolute mass scales remain open.
