# The charged-current residue when the fibration metric fluctuates

Keep the local horizontal root action of Theorem 71.1, the lifted fermion currents of result 63, the fluxes and the gauge norms fixed. Vary the common horizontal kinetic coefficient $Z_H=Z_{H,c}$ when comparing the fixed actions, or vary the compact geometry within one action. We determine which terms of the earlier charged probe survive elimination of the auxiliary metric variations. The observable is exchange between normalized left-handed lepton currents. Sources are taken on shell or as conserved transverse probes with the corresponding coordinate Ward identity.

## The positive root form in the full metric problem

**Proposition 73.1.** In the zero-relative-circle-frequency, colour-singlet charged spin-one sector, use solenoidal weak metric vectors $X$, a circle vector $v\partial_\chi$ and the weak Maxwell profile $z$ as in Proposition 63.4. In the full fibration of result 71 the root term in the mass form is
$$
 2CVZ_H\|P\mathcal U(X,v,z)\|^2,\qquad
 \mathcal U_\pm=D_XH_\pm+i(z+n_\pm v)H_\pm .
$$
Here $P$ removes the closure of the real gradients $D_{\nabla f}H_\pm$ of a common scalar $f$ on the weak sphere, as a subspace of the two-root profile space. The inner product uses weak area average. This modification leaves the round three-pole charged response, the leading near-round weak masses and the fixed-broken-shape five-dimensional limiting Green function of result 63. It changes the assertion about sending $Z_H$ to infinity at a *fixed* compact member.

**Proof.** A transverse displacement of the external coordinates depending on internal position shifts a mixed metric vector by an internal gradient. Its covariant metric, flux and vortex action is zero on this pure coordinate variation. In the additional fibration kinetic term it occurs through $D_{\nabla f}H_\pm$ and has no external kinetic term. Its stationary equation gives precisely the orthogonal projection, by the horizontal derivative calculation of Theorem 71.1. Here $f$ has zero relative circle frequency and colour. Exact weak gradients with no independent Maxwell profile belong to the scalar-harmonic gravitational polarizations in the covariant light-cone equations. The Hodge Laplacian preserves this exact subspace. The Maxwell mixing against $\nabla f$ cancels by the background Maxwell equation, including the charged vortex current when it is nonzero, and antisymmetry of $F$ kills the Hessian of $f$. The projected root form vanishes on those gradients. Removing this subspace gives the solenoidal spin-one representatives and the original covariant mass and kinetic forms on them. The circle-equivariant fermion action lives on the circle quotient. At frequency zero a pure external-coordinate variation depending on the weak position is a change of coordinates on that quotient. Its on-shell variation of the fermion action vanishes, including the longitudinal external metric term. The auxiliary gradient therefore carries no independent lepton vertex. This gives the claimed mass replacement for the transverse source.

At dissolution $H_\pm=0$. The massless weak lift and the $2/r^2,4/r^2$ angular poles and their listed lepton and quark residues consequently persist. Approaching dissolution, the pair of leading degree-one sections is the equally normalized complete round doublet. In a local frame it is proportional, up to the opposite circle phases, to the two components of the normalized Hopf spinor. The real inner product of its covariant derivative along $\nabla f$ with its infinitesimal lifted unitary rotation is proportional to $\langle\nabla f,K_i\rangle$ integrated over the round sphere: the horizontal Hopf derivative induces the Fubini–Study metric and the unitary action projects to its Killing field. This integral is zero. For the circle generator the matrix on the two components is diagonal with opposite entries, another infinitesimal unitary rotation; a common phase has zero real overlap with the horizontal derivative. Thus projection preserves the leading weak and neutral mass matrices on the light gauge modes. More explicitly, the leading weak-derivative norm of this normalized pair is a positive multiple of the round tangent norm. The gradient projection has a uniform elliptic estimate on mean-zero $f$, so the change of the profiles and metric does not introduce a singular projection error in this charged sector. The unbroken generator remains exactly $Q$ at finite breaking. At fixed nonround shape the root term is $O_b(B^{-2})$ in the scaled mass form, also after contractive projection. Therefore the fixed-shape limit uses the same positive $S_b$ and the same finite-momentum formula as in Proposition 63.4. $\square$

## A charged residual exchange at large root stiffness

**Proposition 73.2.** At compact broken members sufficiently close to dissolution the full fibration theory has a strictly positive lower bound on the leptonic charged contact, uniform over $Z_H>0$ at that member. At canonical round matching the trial bound satisfies
$$
 \liminf_{b\to\infty}\ \inf_{Z_H>0}
                       CV\, C_{\ell\ell}(0)\ \ge\ \frac{15}{2248} .
$$
This is the broken-side limit with two equal roots, finite round radii and the unit left $V-A$ convention. The unprojected effective vector action in Proposition 66.3 has a different large-stiffness limit.

**Proof.** Use $K_3=\partial_\varphi$, the weak height function $x_3$ equal to one at the north pole, and let $F$ have axial weight of absolute value one. Multiply the *unbroken* electric generator and its connection lift by $F$. The resulting metric and Maxwell profiles before projecting the metric part are
$$
         V=F K_3,\qquad v=F/k,\qquad z=F\mu_b,\qquad
         d\mu_b=-\iota_{K_3}F_A .
$$
Their root variation vanishes pointwise because the lifted charge $Q$ annihilates each root. Let $X$ be the solenoidal projection of $V$. The difference is a weak gradient. Consequently $(X,v,z)$ belongs to the kernel of the **projected** root mass for every $Z_H$. It is a charged massive trial field since $F$ varies. Its contribution to the variational lower bound is the squared source overlap divided by eight times its covariant mass norm.

For an explicit real polarization take $F=x_1$ at the round limit, continued smoothly on the axially identified spheres. The solenoidal projection is $X=(x_1K_3+x_3K_1)/2$. Indeed $x_1K_3-x_3K_1=r^2\nabla x_2$. This is a coexact degree-two harmonic of mean squared norm $r^2/10$. Also $z=-x_1x_3/2$ and $dz+\iota_XF_A=-d(x_1x_3)/4$. The metric part of the covariant mass divided by $CV$ is $(6-2)r^{-2}(r^2/10)=2/5$. The Maxwell part is $16a^2(6/r^2)(1/15)/16=1/5$. The circle part is $2L^2/(3k^2r^2)=40/27$. The vortex section vanishes in this limit. Thus the mass norm divided by $CV$ is $281/135$.

Only the circle component contributes to the round doublet matrix element: the metric and Maxwell profiles just obtained have angular degree two while a doublet bilinear contains angular momenta zero and one. The matrix element of the circle profile in the normalization of 63.3 is $-2Y_\ell/3=1/3$ for a lepton. Hence the lower bound tends to $(1/3)^2/[8(281/135)]=15/2248$. Smooth continuation gives nonzero overlap at the nearby broken members. This field is an internal harmonic whose current raises the electric weight. The photon itself is the neutral constant multiple of $Q$ and has no such raising matrix element. $\square$

At fixed finite $Z_H$ and sufficiently small breaking the light charged residue remains nonzero and $M_C^2=g_2^2 v_h^2/4+O(e^{-b})$, where $v_h^2=2CV Z_H\langle |H_+|^2+|H_-|^2\rangle$. The contact can therefore be varied at the same geometry and tree colour data. For general $Z_H$ the resolvent derivative in 66.3 is nonpositive with $P$ inserted in the root mass form; its large-$Z_H$ limit is the response surviving on that form's kernel. The new lower bound makes the difference between the two actions measurable.
