# With the pull-back connection at the Fubini-Study radius, the geometric wall operator has one copy of the 4 in its kernel, giving an eight-state window with a nonzero limiting fibre weight

**Status.** Theorem (Theorem 29.11), with Proposition 29.10. Proved by Claude Opus 5.5. The derivation of that matrix from the geometry is listed among what a stand-alone paper needs (lemma 29). Ledger rows `l29_window_a` (depending on `l29_wall_a`) and `l29_wall_a`; the ledger records `l28_window` as amended by `l29_window_a`. Theorem 2 of paper/joint_limit_note.tex, case (a).

**Setting.** Setting 29.0 of 29_window_case_b.md, with the pull-back connection throughout. On the collar A = A_d + A_o, where A_d = D_line preserves the lines and A_o = A_E/rho has order zero, with [A_o, q] != 0; the spin-3/2 ladder coefficients bound the block of A_E between q = 3/2 and q = 1/2 by sqrt(3) and ||A_E|| by 2. P projects onto {sum_{q > 0} p_q g_q : Z g_q = -g_q}, p_q = sqrt(2 m q) exp(m q (x - l)), Y is the same with Z = +1, and F = Pi_Y (A_d + A_o) P. A_eff, with blocks F and F\*, is identified with D_3(rho) (+) D_1(rho) + (sqrt(3)/2) B(rho) on sections over CP^3 of the lines q > 0, with ||B(rho)|| <= sqrt(3)/rho; kappa_a(rho) > 0 is the gap of its nonzero spectrum, uniform in V. Condition K(rho): the kernel of A_eff(rho) is exactly one copy of the 4, in Z = +1.

**Statement.**

1. Under K(rho), the half-cylinder kernel of T_M^* is exactly one copy of the 4 for m large. Its projection differs from the projection onto the embedded kernel of F^* by O(1/m). The lower bound on the complement uses kappa_a(rho)^2 - O(1/m).
2. K(1) holds. For each fixed 0 < delta < min(2, kappa_a(1)) and for M large enough, the window conclusion of Theorem 29.7 holds with the wall modes replaced by the half-cylinder kernel.
3. kappa_a(1) >= sqrt(5) - 3/2, with lower bound sqrt(3) on the 4. The signed fibre weight tends to -w with w >= 1/7. Conditional on the matrix of Proposition 28.13, w = 2/3 and the two squared singular values on the 4 are (45 +- 3 sqrt(33))/8, with the smaller singular value 1.863...
4. At another wall radius the same conclusion holds under K(rho).

*Scope.* At other radii only finitely many V can carry a kernel, those with |D_3(rho) (+) D_1(rho)| <= 3/(2 rho), by ellipticity. In Python, on the 16, 20 and 40 and for rho in [0.2, 4], the compressed block has smallest singular value at least 1.30. The [uniform radius estimate](issues/13.md) proves K(rho) on an explicit interval with approximate endpoints 0.8842 and 1.3176. It also proves a nonzero fibre weight on a smaller interval. The kernel test outside the larger interval remains open.

**Proof.** Lemma 29, Proposition 29.10 and Theorem 29.11. Eliminating the orthogonal complement of the stable channels perturbs the wall operator by O(1/m). At rho = 1 the separated line operator is injective in Z = -1, with smallest singular value at least sqrt(3) and cokernel one copy of the 4. The interline perturbation has norm at most 3/2, so the lower bound and equivariant Fredholm index give K(1). On the 4 the perturbation maps into the O(3) summand, orthogonal to the image of the unperturbed operator, so the lower bound sqrt(3) persists there. In the kernel vector on the 4 the O(3) component carries at least 4/7 of the squared norm, so the signed fibre weight has magnitude at least 1/7.

**What it leaves open.** K(rho) away from rho = 1 (an explicit list of the finitely many representations that ellipticity allows at each radius), other metrics, and the derivation of the finite matrix A(rho) of Proposition 28.13 from the geometric data. The compression in case (a), with fibre part -w(rho), is 29_compression_to_endpoint_maps.md.
