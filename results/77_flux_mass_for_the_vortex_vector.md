# Giving the vortex vector a flux mass while preserving the photon

The neutral-current test in result 75 singles out a specific problem: the independently gauged vortex charge acts twice as strongly on a left lepton as on a right one, and its vector becomes light along with the weak vectors. We keep the monopole flux, root fields, quark and lepton lifts and circle character fixed. We add one dynamical two-form with a quantized Abelian Chern–Simons coupling and compute its effect on the neutral and charged currents. This is a different fixed action, with an experimentally distinguishable charged spectrum. Flux-induced Stückelberg masses and massless combinations in heterotic line-bundle compactifications provide a precedent for the mechanism. ## The axion charge and the surviving geometric action

**Theorem 77.1.** Let $A$ be the unit vortex-line connection with $\int_\Sigma F_A=2\pi$. Add a two-form connection with three-form curvature satisfying
$$
 \frac{dH_3}{2\pi}=-p\left(\frac{F_A}{2\pi}\right)^2,\qquad
 {\cal L}_{B}/C=-Z_B |H_3|^2/2,\qquad Z_B=z_B e^{4s},\quad
 p\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\},\ z_B>0 .
$$
Here the Lagrangian expression denotes its term inside the covariant Einstein prefactor. Work in the fixed bundle sector with the standard Chern–Simons trivialization and do not give the fermions a minimal two-form charge. All the classical polar vortex and root backgrounds still solve the equations at $H_3=0$. The constant vortex gauge transformation acquires an axion of integer charge $2p$. Each round weak rotation and the broken diagonal $Q$ instead has a connection-preserving lift without an axion mass.

**Proof.** The background line and connection pull back from the weak surface. Its squared Chern class and its four-form curvature square vanish there; one can pull back a trivialization of this square from the surface and choose zero background three-form. The new quadratic energy and all its first variations then vanish. Locally $H_3=dB_2-pA\wedge F_A/(2\pi)$. Linearizing at fixed topology in the direction of a globally defined connection perturbation $a$, the transgression has the form $\delta H_3=d\widetilde b-(p/\pi)a\wedge F_A$, with the exact patch term absorbed in the two-form variable. The factor two can be checked without choosing a local potential: integration of the linearized Bianchi identity over $\Sigma$ gives $d\int_\Sigma\delta H_3=-2p\,dV_0$ for a constant-profile external vortex connection $V_0$. Thus its four-dimensional derivative is $d\theta-2pV_0$ for the axionic period variable. At a round member with physical area $\mathcal A=4\pi r^2$ the harmonic projection gives
$$
 f_\theta^2=CV Z_B/\mathcal A^2,\qquad M_{v,0}^2=(2p)^2 f_\theta^2/I_v,
 \qquad I_v=16CVa^2,\quad r^2=2a^2 .
$$
This is the mass before any root expectation value. It is positive at fixed radii.

For a weak lift with gauge profile $z$ the obstruction to solving the new internal compensator equation is $\int_\Sigma zF_A$. The constant profile has the flux just calculated. A round rotation has $z=-x_i/2$ with zero mean. At a nonround member the $Q$ lift has $z=\mu_b=A_\varphi-1/2$. Since $A_\varphi$ runs from zero to one, $\int_\Sigma\mu_b F_A=0$. An explicit smooth coexact primitive of $(p/\pi)\mu_b F_A$ is
$$
 \eta_Q=\frac{p}{2\pi}(\mu_b^2-1/4)d\varphi
             =-\frac{p}{16\pi} f_b^2\,d\varphi .
$$
It vanishes in regular polar norm at each pole. This follows from $\mu_b=-u_t/4$ and $u_t^2=4-2 f_b^2$. It adds to the electric kinetic norm, giving
$$
 I_Q=CV\left[\frac{L^2}{k^2}
     +a^2\left(\langle f_b^2+16\mu_b^2\rangle+
         \frac{z_Bp^2}{256\pi^2}\langle f_b^2\rangle\right)\right].
$$
A gluon has $z=0$ and the colour norm $I_3=CVB^2/4$ is unchanged in this action. The new effective shape coefficient is bounded because $f_b^2\le2$. Thus the same monotone radius matching and the same compensated powers at five and in the six-dimensional band apply. The scalar geometry of the upper point still has its weak rotations. $\square$

The extra neutral current is weakened by a flux period over the very sphere producing the weak doublets. Giving a spacetime mass to this vector still leaves its local gauge redundancy. The local gauge and chiral-parent gravitational anomalies of an unpaired family are those in Proposition 72.2. A Green–Schwarz anomaly cancellation without these mirrors would require the appropriately quantized axion couplings and cancellation of every irreducible term, including the parent gravitational term. The two-form kinetic and Bianchi terms above establish a mass mechanism. We keep the mirror cancellation and its thresholds throughout.

## A relation among three vector masses

**Proposition 77.2.** At round dissolution put $I_E=CV\,2r^2/3$ and $\gamma=p\sqrt{z_B}/(16\pi)$. The sourced rotation–vortex–two-form triplet has squared masses $0,2/r^2,(4+2\gamma^2)/r^2$. The separate circle harmonic also has $M_D^2=2/r^2$. The independent neutral vortex vector has $M_{v,0}^2=2\gamma^2/r^2$. Hence
$$
                M_{\rm high}^2-2 M_D^2=M_{v,0}^2 .
$$
This provides a second measurement of the coupling which lifts the neutral pole.

**Proof.** In one Cartesian triplet write the metric rotation as $uK_i$, the unit vortex Maxwell potential as $z x_i$, and the external two-form vector using its coexact weak one-form $b\,{*}dx_i$. Set the sign of $b$ so the residual square below is $b+p z/(4\pi)$. The monopole is $F_A=\mathrm{vol}_\Sigma/(2r^2)$ and $d{*}dx_i=-2x_i\mathrm{vol}_\Sigma/r^2$. The mixed three-form therefore gives the kinetic coefficient $I_b=2CVZ_B/(3r^2)$ and the second residual square in
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 K&=I_Eu^2+4I_Ez^2+I_b b^2,\\
 H&=\frac2{r^2}\,[4I_E(z+u/2)^2+I_b(b+pz/(4\pi))^2] .
 \end{aligned}
$$
Exact two-form polarizations are gauge, and the other internal form sectors have no direct fermion vertex or mixing with these triplets. In canonical coordinates the residual map has rows $(1,1,0)$ and $(0,\gamma,1)$. Its two nonzero squared singular values are $1$ and $2+\gamma^2$. The massless vector has direction $(1,-1,\gamma)$ and inverse weak coupling $I_W=(2+\gamma^2)I_E$. For comparison the normalized doublet source is proportional to $(1/3,-2/3,0)$; the lepton has no direct two-form vertex. In units of the massless residue the two nonzero triplet residues are
$$
 \frac{\gamma^2(2+\gamma^2)}{9(1+\gamma^2)},\qquad
 \frac{(1+2\gamma^2)^2}{9(1+\gamma^2)} .
$$
The independent $x_i\partial_\chi$ harmonic carries the charge-dependent residues of 63.3 if $I_Y=5I_W/3$ is imposed at this matching point. Its contribution adds to the residue at the degenerate pole. Substituting $Z_B=z_Br^4/4$ in 77.1 gives the neutral mass and the stated relation. $\square$

## The low-energy tests after the lift

**Corollary 77.3.** At the same compact broken geometry the tree charged contact on the prescribed lepton sources is unchanged by adding this two-form. Near restoration the leading neutrino–electron contact has $g_L=-1/2+s_w^2+o(1)$ and $g_R=s_w^2+o(1)$, with the *new* normalized $s_w^2$. The large, positive vector contribution of Proposition 75.1 has been removed at leading order.

**Proof.** A zero-circle-frequency raising profile $z$ has absolute axial weight one. Its integral against the axial $F_A$ vanishes. At zero spacelike momentum a two-form vector has no external kinetic cost, and on the closed surface there is a smooth solution of $d_\Sigma\eta=(p/\pi)zF_A$. Minimizing over it sets the new positive mass square to zero for any such $z$. Its external fermion source is zero. The inverse quadratic form on the original charged sources therefore gives exactly the same static exchange. This argument also applies after the horizontal root projection in 73.1. At positive momentum the additional kinetic contribution is measured by the changed poles and residues. The leading fixed-shape relation for $G_F M_g^2$ keeps its form wherever the identified colour threshold is $1/L$; the new term in $I_Q$ is subleading there.

At compact dissolution the forced axionic mass is finite. Approaching it from the broken side, the two root doublets still give $M_C^2\sim g_2^2v_h^2/4$ and $M_Z^2\sim(g_2^2+g_Y^2)v_h^2/4$, now using $g_2^{-2}=I_W$ from 77.2. Equating the root norms cancels leading cross terms with the constant vortex vector. Its normalized contact contribution is suppressed by $v_h^2/[(2p)^2 f_\theta^2+v_h^2]$ to leading order in the compact limit. Heavy neutral exchanges have finite contacts before division by $G_F$. A possible massless two-form vector on the circle has no minimal lepton vertex, and its uniform one-form is orthogonal to the weak coexact compensators. These facts give the stated low-energy coefficients. An infrared angle near $0.233$ would give $g_V$ near $-0.034$ in this approximation. The angle is an observable to be run and matched using 75–76. An unrun canonical $\sin^2\theta=3/8$ instead gives $g_V=1/4$. $\square$
