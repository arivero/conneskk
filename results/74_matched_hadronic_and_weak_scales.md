# A matched trajectory and its weak strength in hadronic units

We vary the weak radius and shape on the supported Kähler-flux fibration of result 71 and fix the action, the integer $k$, the quark and mirror Yukawas and the two tree gauge norms. We use the product frame throughout. The first extra coloured boson, the first coupled weak pole and the exchange on a lepton doublet are distinct measurements. We choose renormalization conditions for the four-dimensional effective theory and report separately the hadronic conversion. An absolute identification of the compactification or matching scale with the Planck scale is unnecessary.

## A common low-energy colour theory on a finite-neck trajectory

**Proposition 74.1.** There are fixed choices of the microscopic coefficients and integer $k$ such that the round member, a long finite six-dimensional scalar band, and the compensated five-dimensional collapse belong to a single linearly stable family of classical solutions with first extra coloured boson $M_g=1/L$. On a closed broken segment from the finite six-dimensional member through the collapse, one can choose six light ordinary quarks and place every mirror and every other coloured fermion above $M_g$. The mirrors return on approach to the round endpoint. The common electric generator in the broken phase is the lifted $Q=T_3+Y$.

**Proof.** Fix a round normalization and put $d=a_\circ P_\infty$. Follow $a=d/P_b$ to a finite long-neck shape $b_m$, then decrease $a$ at this shape. The matching equations are $CVB^2=4I_3$ and $L^2/k^2=(I_Q/4I_3)B^2-a^2 J_b$. The ratio $\rho=I_Q/I_3$ may be specified independently or set to $8/3$ for the canonical relative benchmark. In units of the starting colour radius the equations for $B/B_\circ$ and $L/(|k|B_\circ)$ are independent of $k$ when the round shape ratios other than the circle period are fixed. The common normalization of lengths can then be set by the Einstein coefficient. On the compact shape segment the ratios $L/(|k|B)$ and $\delta_b B^2$ of Theorem 71.1 are strictly positive and continuous. Their limits at the uniform collapse are $\sqrt\rho/2$ and $(1-1/\sqrt2)3$ respectively. Choose once a large enough multiple of six for $|k|$ so that $\delta_b L^2>1$ throughout. The full bosonic result now applies. At canonical ratios and $|k|=6$ the inequality already holds in the asymptotic windows and at the round point. The uniform existence argument allows larger $|k|$ for a prescribed finite route. [Supported roots](63_vortex_roots_and_diagonal_photon.md) · [Full colour gap](71_colour_gap_with_diagonal_roots.md)

At canonical round ratios the bosonic assertion in fact holds on the entire route already for $|k|=6$. Here $d=\pi r_\circ$, $B_\circ=4r_\circ/\sqrt3$, and $J_b=\langle f^2+u_t^2\rangle=2+\langle u_t^2\rangle/2\le4$. Write $X=B/B_\circ$, $Y=L/(|k|B)$ and $R=a^2\mathcal A_b/(a_\circ^2\mathcal A_\circ)$. Since $\mathcal A_b\le2\pi\sqrt2 P_b$ and $P_b\ge P_\infty=\pi\sqrt2$, we have $R\le\pi/2$ on the first segment and throughout the subsequent shrinkage. Then $X^7 YR=\sqrt{5/12}$ and $Y\le\sqrt{2/3}$ give $X\ge(\sqrt{5/2}/\pi)^{1/7}=0.906573\ldots$. Using $a^2J_b/B^2\le3/(8X^2)$ then gives $Y^2\ge2/3-3/(8X^2)\ge0.21039>1/5$. The weak metric has nonnegative curvature and physical diameter $aP_b\le\pi r_\circ$. The Zhong–Yang eigenvalue estimate yields $\mu\ge1/r_\circ^2$. Consequently $\delta_b L^2>(1-1/\sqrt2)3k^2/5>1$. The same inequalities hold for the example $\rho=10$ below with $I_W=I_3$: there $Y_\circ=\sqrt{(\rho-1)/4}$, $Y\le\sqrt{\rho/4}$ and the lower bound on $Y^2$ increases. [Sharp diameter bound](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/sharp-modulus-of-continuity-for-parabolic-equations-on-manifolds-/)

On any compact broken portion away from dissolution the quark kernel links are linear to first order in their Yukawas with strictly positive normalized overlaps, by Proposition 63.2. Their dimensionless masses $Bm_i$ continue to strictly positive limits on the fixed-shape collapse for nonzero Yukawas. The complementary weak and projective fermion gaps in units $1/B$ have a positive infimum there, by compactness and the fixed-shape limit. First choose small but nonzero mirror Yukawas well below the complement. Choose $|k|$ sufficiently large that $B/L$ is below those mirror masses on the chosen broken segment. Choose the six ordinary Yukawas smaller still, so their masses lie below $\mu_*=\xi/L$ for a fixed $0<\xi<1$. This is a choice made once, not a retuning at each radius. The dimensionless tangential spectral problem and these overlap estimates depend on the shape ratios $B/a$ and the dimensionless Yukawa inputs, while the equivariant matter carries no extra circle tower. A specified lepton mass may be nonzero to allow a Thomson prescription. At the round endpoint the links vanish even for the mirrors and the coloured field count becomes that of Proposition 68.3. The charge labels follow from the actual bundle lifts and current vertices. $\square$

One can cross a mirror threshold between the two portions. With perturbative continuous one-loop decoupling the appropriate three-light-flavour expression is
$$
 \Lambda_3=\mu_*\,e^{-8\pi^2 I_3(\mu_*)/9}
       \prod_{i}\min(1,m_i/\mu_*)^{2/27}.
$$
Here $i$ runs over the three ordinary heavy quarks and the six possible mirror Dirac flavours, all required to be above the strong decoupling region when used as heavy quarks. Each active one changes the leading colour coefficient by $2/3$. A mass above $\mu_*$ has already been included in the matching there. This product is not valid when the supposed heavy flavour becomes strongly coupled before decoupling. Near restoration use the twelve-flavour response, or the measured correlators of the actual intervening theory. A choice of Yukawa rank instead gives the separate five-flavour branch of result 67.

## The lower exchange and the first colour threshold

**Corollary 74.2.** Let $S_b$ be the dimensionless charged Green function on a fixed broken shape in 63.4 and 73.1. Use the hierarchy on the broken portion of Proposition 74.1. If the matched hadron ratio $h=\lim BH_N$ exists and lies in the four-dimensional regime, the leading tree-current formulas are
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 \lim G_FH_N^2 &=\frac{\sqrt2\,S_b h^2}{32I_3},\\
 \lim H_N^2 C_{\ell\ell}(x^2 H_N^2)&=\frac{S_b h^2}{32I_3}
                         \qquad(x>0),\\
 \lim G_F M_g^2 &=\frac{\sqrt2\,S_b}{8k^2 I_Q}.
 \end{aligned}
$$
The last equation eliminates the nucleon input. In it $I_Q$ is a matched kinetic coefficient in the stated leading prescription. Converting it to an infrared charge requires charged-current vacuum polarization in the electromagnetic channel.

**Proof.** The complete tree sum at the lower end has $C_{\ell\ell}(0)\sim S_b/(8CV)$ and its first pole with an unsuppressed sphere vertex has squared mass $\lambda_C(b)/a^2$. The full gap in the source sector is of order $1/a$ even if a circle-profile pole has a small residue. With $H_N$ of order $1/B$, the spacelike and contact limits thus agree. Insert $CVB^2=4I_3$, $G_F=\sqrt2 C_{\ell\ell}(0)$ and $I_Q\sim CVL^2/k^2$, and use the identified mass $M_g=1/L$. The same formulas with $H_N$ replaced by a consistently run $\Lambda_3$ give the strong-running comparison when the nucleon matrix element is unavailable. $\square$

At a finite middle member the actual measurements at $H=H_N$ or a specified comparison energy use $M_g/H=1/(LH)$, $M_C/H=B\sqrt{\lambda_C}/(ah)$ and the normalized spectral sum in result 66 with the projected root mass of 73.1. Its resolvent at $x>0$ and at zero must be taken on the whole sourced tower. If the positive fermion cutoff is lower than $M_g$ for different Yukawas, the first omitted coloured particle is that fermion. At round restoration $M_C=0$ before quantum strong feedback, the first bosonic colour threshold is $1/L$ at canonical matching, and the three-pole charged response at $x>0$ is given in 63.3. There the mirror quarks are active and the existence and size of a nucleon mass in that theory require an independent infrared test. On an indefinitely lengthening middle neck at fixed *nonzero* Yukawas the half-interval theorem gives a different active quark content. The distinction is measurable even though the scalar geometry resolves the same meridian.

**Weak matching beyond the quadratic theory.** For a pole with nonzero limiting scaled residue $j_C^2$, the effective charged vertex satisfies $g_{\ell C}^2\sim j_C^2/(CVa^2)=j_C^2 B^2/(4I_3a^2)$. Thus the strict fixed-shape collapse eventually has large coupling at the classical pole mass, even for a small value of $G_FH_N^2$. Low-energy insertions are controlled by $G_FE^2$; a perturbative match at the heavy pole would require control of its own loop parameter. Additional KK thresholds enter in the intervening range. This distinguishes the calculable quadratic contact from an all-orders microscopic determination. If the common-profile, vector-aligned pion effective action of 61.2 applies to $N_D$ light left-gauged quark doublets, its leading dressing is $\Xi=\Xi_0/[1+\sqrt2 N_D(F/H_N)^2\Xi_0]$, with $\Xi_0$ the tree contact above. The decay constant, actual active doublets and electroweak threshold corrections belong to the same infrared theory. ## Computing the shape Green function

**Numerical calculation (Python, SciPy).** On the weak metric $f^2(dt^2+d\varphi^2)$ write $p=e^u$, $g_s=\partial_s f=(1-p)u_t/2$ in meridian arclength, and $\beta=(1-p)/4$. Use a complex weight-one stream $F(t)e^{i\varphi}$, $\iota_X dV=d(Fe^{i\varphi})$, and Maxwell profile $Z(t)e^{i\varphi}$. Put $D=\int_\mathbb R f^2dt$ and $A=u_t/(2f^2)$. The doubled real-polarization mass form entering the Galerkin calculation is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 D\,\mathcal H={}&
 \int\frac{4(F'-g_s F)^2+(F''-2g_sF'+F)^2}{f^2}\,dt\\
 &+16\int[(Z'+\beta F')^2+(Z+\beta F)^2]\,dt\\
 &+4\int p f^2[(AF)^2+(2Z-AF')^2]\,dt .
\end{aligned}
$$
The kinetic form is $D^{-1}\int(F'^2+F^2+16 f^2Z^2)dt$. For equal-charge coloured or colourless weak doublet profiles the leading left transition is
$$
 J=\frac{\displaystyle\int f p\left[
   \frac{u_t-1-g_s}{2f^2}(F-F')
     +\frac{-F''+F}{4f^2}+2Z\right]dt}
      {\displaystyle\int f p e^{-t}dt} .
$$
Indeed the northern upper spinor has polar angular label $1/2$, logarithmic meridian derivative $(u_t-1-g_s)/2$ and the same coefficient multiplying $i$ in its covariant azimuthal derivative. The antisymmetric part of the spinorial Lie derivative contributes one quarter of the curl. The normalized cap product gives the weight in $J$. A complex raising profile has twice the kinetic norm of its real Cartesian component, giving $S_b=2J\mathcal H^{-1}J^\dagger$ with these radial conventions.

## A finite six-dimensional member with both current scales

For a finite example choose $r_\circ=1$ as a unit of length, $I_W=I_3$ at round, $\rho=I_Q/I_3=10$, $k=6$, $d=\pi$, and $2Z_H\zeta_0 B_0^2/B_\circ^2=0.02$. These are matching inputs; $\rho$ need not equal $8/3$. At $b_m=0.001$ the vortex quadrature and the two norm equations give $a=0.168544$, $B=2.67812$, $L=25.3635$, $J_b=2.15307$. Thus $a/L=0.00664516$. The scalar meridian spacing is of order $\pi/d$, and the maximum azimuthal radius is $\sqrt2 a$. The light matter and current can be examined at energies below this six-dimensional scalar band.

The finite vector problem adds a circle profile $w$ with metric vector $(a/L)w\partial_\chi$. Its uncoupled mass form is $\int(w'^2+w^2)dt/D$. In the root form set $b_\pm=u_t/4\mp1/2$, $n_\pm=\pm k/2$. Up to the common factor $2Z_H\zeta_0 B_0^2/(B^2D)$ the added projected quadratic coefficient is
$$
 \inf_l\sum_\pm\int f^2 e^{u/2\mp t}
 \left[
  \frac{b_\pm^2}{f^4}(F+l')^2+
  \left(Z+n_\pm(a/L)w-\frac{b_\pm}{f^2}(F'+l)\right)^2
 \right]dt .
$$
The auxiliary gradient is that of $l(t)\sin\varphi$ for the real polarization chosen above. Add $(a/L)n_\ell w$ in the source integrand. The spline calculation eliminates $l$ by its Schur complement. At step $1/8$ it gives the dimensionless full static sum $S_{\rm mid}=0.001042948$, first sourced squared eigenvalue $\lambda_C=0.5297132$, scaled residue $1.604\times10^{-6}$ and remainder $0.001039921$ after subtraction of that pole. Step $1/4$ gives $S_{\rm mid}=0.001042947$. The small first residue is a physical distinction between finding a charged mode and identifying the dominant weak exchange. The numbers use the left doublet zero-mode probe. For light nonzero lepton Yukawas its on-shell source approaches this value continuously.

With $H/M_g=0.322213/2000$ as the independently calibrated reference ratio, this member gives $M_C/H\simeq6.8\times10^5$, $M_g/H\simeq6.2\times10^3$ and
$$
 \frac{\sqrt2\,H^2 C_{\ell\ell}(0)}{e_{*,Q}^2}
  =\frac{\sqrt2}{8}(HL)^2
       [k^{-2}+(a/L)^2 J_b]S_{\rm mid}
  \simeq1.33\times10^{-13}.
$$
At $x=1$ the dimensionless squared momentum in the pole sum is $(aH)^2\simeq1.15\times10^{-12}$. Positivity bounds the fractional contact correction by $(aH)^2/\lambda_C$. Thus this number is also the finite-momentum answer to the quoted precision. By comparison the fixed-shape lower end gave $1.35\times10^{-13}$ in these units, with $M_C/H$ tending to infinity. At this illustrative subtraction point $e_{*,Q}^2=1/(10I_3)\simeq0.1121$; the corresponding middle contact is about $1.50\times10^{-14}$ in $\Xi$ units. This is a calculation at specified inputs. A physical electromagnetic comparison requires the independent charge matching in result 75.

For fixed constant Yukawas the fermion mass ratios can move between the two members. The positive squared singlet form provides a size check. Its first positive unperturbed squared level in unscaled units is at least $e^{-1/2}\pi^2/P_b^2$: after factoring out the holomorphic singlet the density differs from constant area density by at most $e^{1/2}$ and the unweighted scalar $\bar\partial$ square is the scalar Laplacian. With $V$ the positive Yukawa potential and $v_0=\langle V\rangle$ on the central singlet, the first squared eigenvalue lies between $v_0-\|V\|v_0/(\delta^2-v_0)$ and $v_0$ for $v_0<\delta^2$, by Temple's inequality. The unscaled $V$ contains $(a/B)^2$. For the top link in the above example the peak-to-singlet-average factor in $|r_\pm|^2$ is about $10.11$, and this bound changes its leading squared value by less than $0.02\%$ when partnered with the stated bottom mass. $L/B$ also changes by about $0.2\%$. Using identical hadronic masses at every geometry with constant Yukawas would need the nucleon mass function at the shifted light inputs.

There is an explicit calibration option if the same physical quark ratios are desired throughout a broken segment. Prescribe small positive target $m_iL$ for each ordinary flavour and keep the renormalized colour coupling fixed at $\mu_*L=\xi$. At each compact member the derivative of the light Dirac singular value with respect to a small real diagonal Yukawa is its positive cap overlap times the common scale; the linear maps for the two charges are diagonal with positive entries. Uniform gaps on the finite broken segment and the fixed-shape limit permit the inverse function theorem for the small vector of masses. Choose the corresponding Yukawas once as functions of the scale dilaton $s=\log a$, which parametrizes this trajectory, and continue them smoothly away from the prescribed broken segment, taking finite values near dissolution. A smooth interpolation of the geometry at the junction is allowed. These fixed local functions encode the calibration and change no bosonic vacuum equation. At a single broken point the same calibration uses constants. At a common set of physical light mass ratios the independent $H_N/\Lambda_3$ conversion can then be used on that segment, to the matching order employed. The contact on the geometry is still computed from the current. A dynamical explanation of the fitted Yukawas or the observed infrared ratio remains a further requirement.
