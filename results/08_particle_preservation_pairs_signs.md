# If a particle-number involution Q commutes with D, Gamma, the algebra and its opposite and anticommutes with J, particle-preserving doubling involutions come in pairs U, QU of opposite sign

**Status.** Theorem (Result 12 of lemma 08). Proved by Astra 6 (job 08). Ledger row `l08_pairing`. Item 5 of Theorem D of paper/towerless_intervals.tex.

**Setting.** A standard KO-6 triple and a self-adjoint involution Q, +1 on particles and -1 on antiparticles, with [Q, D] = [Q, Gamma] = [Q, a] = [Q, a°] = 0 and QJ = -JQ. Admissible involutions U as in the factorization criterion (06_doubling_criterion.md).

**Statement.**

1. For an interval product with Q = Q_0 (x) 1, the bare reflection satisfies [1 (x) sigma, Q] = 0.
2. If an admissible U preserves particle number, then QU is another particle-preserving admissible involution with the opposite sign: from UJ = sJU, (QU)J = sQJU = -sJ(QU), and [QU, Q] = 0.
3. So particle preservation selects no sign in a particle-number-conserving sector.
4. Conversely, particle-preserving doubling requires the particle spaces of opposite grading to be isomorphic as bimodules, since U restricts to such an isomorphism. An unpaired left-handed neutrino with no matching right-handed particle in its bimodule therefore obstructs particle-preserving doubling.

*Hypotheses.* The assumption [Q, D] = 0 is essential to the pairing argument, and Majorana terms can violate it. The formal interval product builds in the missing partner, so it cannot represent the strict light spectrum without changing its particle content.

**Proof.** Lemma 08, Result 12; the proof is there: [Q, U] = 0 gives (QU)^2 = 1, and the remaining properties follow from those of Q and U.

**What it leaves open.** With Majorana terms, the sign is controlled by the neutral-sector multiplicities of lemma 06 (06_neutral_sector_criterion.md).
