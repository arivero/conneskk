# The same-chirality analogue of the Horava-Witten walls is type (a) at both ends with opposite localization masses, and unbroken E_8 x E_8 allows no bilinear connector between (248, 1) and (1, 248)

**Status.** Proposition (Result 8 of lemma 20). Proved by Astra 6 (job 20). Ledger row `l20_hw`. Cited in the discussion after Theorem D of paper/towerless_intervals.tex ("\supp{20}").

**Setting.** One common ten-dimensional chirality convention; the unbroken boundary gauge group E_8 x E_8; the boundary types (a), (b), (c) of lemma 09 and the pair of lemma 12 at X = 0.

**Statement.**

1. The same-chirality interval analogue is type (a) at both ends, or type (b) at both ends after reversing the chirality convention; two separately localized multiplets are two fields of that type with opposite signs of their localization masses.
2. Lemma 12 at X = 0 contains one type-(a) and one type-(b) field, so its displayed pair has opposite chiralities; type (c) produces opposite-chirality wall profiles.
3. On a space containing only the two same-chirality modes, an operator odd for that chirality grading has zero matrix elements between them.
4. Hom_{E_8 x E_8}((248, 1), (1, 248)) = 0, since the first factor acts trivially on the target and has no invariant vector in its adjoint representation; so these boundary multiplets supply no gauge-invariant bilinear connector of the interval mass-block type.

*Scope.* Horava-Witten impose Gamma_11 chi = chi on the boundary gaugino, and the boundary vector multiplets are intrinsic boundary fields (arXiv:hep-th/9603142, section 2). Labels based on outward normals change sign between the endpoints, which does not change the comparison in a fixed ten-dimensional convention.

**Proof.** Lemma 20, Result 8; the proof is there.

**What it leaves open.** Bulk propagation can define effective boundary-to-boundary interactions; identifying one with the finite T requires a specified field, representation and grading, possibly after compactification or symmetry breaking (lemma 20, after Result 8).
