# A supported scale modulus and weak exchange between separated caps

We fix the magnetic fluxes, the microscopic Einstein coefficient and a local bosonic action. We vary two constant dilatons, the vortex shape and the circle radius. The first observable is the external curvature. The second is the exchange between the two normalized cap states on the charged monopole doublet. We use the polar metric as an exactly supported control and approach it by helices whose only weak-sector Killing field is the shared photon.

## An Einstein–matter solution at arbitrary vortex scale

**Theorem 62.1.** There is a fixed eleven-dimensional Einstein–Abelian-Higgs–dilaton action with positive kinetic coefficients and a product of four-dimensional Minkowski space, $\mathbb{CP}^2_B$, a circle of length $2\pi L$, and the weak two-vortex sphere $a^2h_b$ for every $a,b,B,L>0$. This realizes the radius choices and the longitudinal bands of result 59 at polar tilt. At a nonround member it has separate circle and axial metric symmetries. The nonpolar diagonal-photon deformation requires additional support.

**Proof.** In vortex units put the following terms inside the common Einstein prefactor, where form norms include the factorial:
$$
 \mathcal R-2U_0 e^{-2\sigma}
 -\tfrac12 Z_0e^{6\sigma}|G_4|^2
 -|\partial \sigma|^2-|\partial s|^2
 -2\left(|D\Phi|^2+e^{2s}|F|^2+
                   \tfrac14 e^{-2s}(1-|\Phi|^2)^2\right).
$$
Here $G_4$ is a closed magnetic form, $F$ is the real curvature of the vortex line with $D=d-iA$, and the fluxes are fixed. Let $G_4=q\,\mathrm{vol}_{\rm FS}$ in unit shape coordinates. Choose once $B_0>0$, $Z_0 q^2=12B_0^6$ and $U_0=9/B_0^2$. At constant $\sigma$ set $B=B_0 e^\sigma$. Then $f=q/B^4$ is the physical magnetic density. The trace-reversed four-form and potential contribute $(2U-3Z f^2/2)g/9=0$ outside the colour factor and add $Z f^2g/2=6g/B^2$ inside it. The $\sigma$ equation is $2U'+Z'f^2/2=0$. Maxwell's equation $d(Z*G_4)=0$ holds on the product.

Set $a=e^s$ and pull back the original two-vortex fields on $h_b$ without changing their flux. The physical magnetic density is $\mathcal B=e^{-2s}(1-p)/2$ with $p=|\Phi|^2$, and $(D_1+iD_2)\Phi=0$ in a positively oriented orthonormal frame. For constant $s$ the vortex energy completes into the two squared BPS equations plus the magnetic flux. Hence the gauge and scalar field equations hold. Also $e^{2s}\mathcal B^2=e^{-2s}(1-p)^2/4$, so the $s$ equation holds pointwise. The transverse vortex stress vanishes. In the nine longitudinal directions the stress in the Einstein equation is $-\mathcal E g$, where $\mathcal E$ is the expression in parentheses with spatial derivatives only. Trace reversal cancels it longitudinally and adds $\mathcal E g$ on the sphere. In the profile variables of result 55 this is
$$
 \mathcal E=a^{-2}\left[\frac{p\,u_t^2}{2f_b^2}
                              +\frac{(1-p)^2}{2}\right]
             =a^{-2}K(h_b).
$$
The gradient term follows from the holomorphic vortex equation and $p=e^u$. This is exactly the transverse Ricci tensor in orthonormal units. There is no remaining mixed Einstein equation on the direct product. The flat circle length is arbitrary. The solution includes the round dissolving limit. All fields used here are bosonic. The dilatons are moduli, and the dilaton couplings and potential belong to this specified action. It differs from pure eleven-dimensional supergravity. ## A charged-current limit for the spinorial probe

**Proposition 62.2.** Let $b\downarrow0$, $a=d/P_b$, at the colour and $Q$ Einstein norms of Proposition 59.2. For the left-component current between the two cap zero modes of a degree-two species with equal Fourier momentum $n_s$, the polar tree response obeys

 0\leq C_{\ell\ell}(t)\leq C_{\ell\ell}(0)
 \ \leq\ \frac{C_1 A_b^2}{C V}
    \left(1+\frac{n_s^2a^2}{L^2}\right)e^{-P_b/\sqrt2},

$$
for a constant $C_1$ at fixed probe normalization in vortex units. In particular the leptonic contact goes to zero. Charge-one *vector modes* of mass $k/L$ still exist at polar tilt. Their circle momentum forbids their coupling to this source. There are nonpolar, prescribed tilts tending to zero for which the same vanishing holds while only the diagonal photon is exactly Killing.

**Proof.** On the polar product both doublet components have identical circle Fourier index. Their transition on the sphere has axial weight of absolute value one. Thus only zero-circle-frequency, weight-one fields contribute. In the colour singlet these are the solenoidal weak-sphere fields and $v(y)\partial_\chi$. An axial Killing field times $e^{ik\chi}$ has charge one under $Q$ and squared mass $k^2/L^2$ by product separation. It has zero overlap here.

First, the unscaled symmetrized derivative has a uniform positive gap on the sphere vector fields with this axial weight. To see this, suppose unit fields have norm of the symmetrized derivative tending to zero. On a compact part of each pointed cap, local Korn and elliptic compactness give a limiting Killing field. Every such cap has only its axial Killing line, since its Gaussian curvature is nonconstant and has regular latitudes. Thus the mass vanishes on each compact cap. In the central neck choose a fixed small $\eta>0$ and $-u\le\eta$. Here $|f_s|$ and $K(h_b)$ are uniformly small by the vortex first integral as $\eta$ tends to zero, and $f_b\le\sqrt2$. Covariant angular differentiation in the polar orthonormal frame at weight $m$ has singular values $|m\pm f_s|/f_b$. For $|m|=1$ this bounds the gradient energy below by a positive constant times the norm on the neck. For fields compactly supported there, integration by parts gives

 \tfrac12\|\mathcal L_X h_b\|^2
     =\|\nabla X\|^2+\|\mathrm{div}\,X\|^2-\int K(h_b)|X|^2.

Cut off between the neck and fixed portions of the two caps. The cutoff derivatives are bounded, and their errors tend to zero because the fields tend to zero there. The neck estimate then rules out a residual unit norm. This establishes the uniform gap. The same identity and bounded curvature control $\|\nabla X\|_2$ by the symmetrized derivative in this sector.
