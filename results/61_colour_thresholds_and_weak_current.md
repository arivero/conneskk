$$
 I_3(A)=V_5K_0\sqrt{A^2+(C_w/b_0)^2},\qquad

## A strong vacuum can change the upper weak pole

**Reference.** Suppose that ordinary four-dimensional colour breaks the chiral symmetry of $2N_D$ massless fundamental flavours to its vector subgroup, with decay constant $F$ in the convention $F_\pi\simeq92$ MeV in physical two-light-flavour QCD. Gauge $N_D$ left doublets and right weak singlets. The aligned vacuum preserves electric charge. Its leading weak masses are $N_Dg_2^2 F^2/4$ and $N_D(g_2^2+g_Y^2)F^2/4$ if this condensate is the only source. **Proposition 61.2 (pion contribution for a KK tower).** Let $K(t)$ be a positive transverse inverse propagator at spacelike $t=p_E^2>0$ before this contribution. Use canonical vectors with coupling profiles $l$ and $u$ to a unit lepton and quark doublet current. Assume all condensing doublets see the same $u$, approximate their strong chiral action by its leading two-derivative term and fix any analytic gauge kinetic counterterms. With $D=N_DF^2/4$, set
$$
 A=l^\dagger K^{-1}l,\quad B=u^\dagger K^{-1}u,\quad
 E=l^\dagger K^{-1}u .
$$
The dressed leptonic response is
$$
 C_{\ell\ell}(t)=\frac18\left(A-\frac{D|E|^2}{1+D B}\right).
$$
If $l=u$ and $C_0=A/8$, then $C_{\ell\ell}^{-1}=C_0^{-1}+2N_D F^2$. This formula applies at low momenta where the chiral derivative expansion is valid. In a gapped tree sector with $G_{F,0}=1/(\sqrt2 v_0^2)$ it gives $G_F=1/[\sqrt2(v_0^2+N_DF^2)]$. With $C_0$ containing a massless pole with nonzero universal residue, the dressed contact is $1/(2N_DF^2)$.

**Proof.** Insert the quark connection in $(F^2/4)\operatorname{tr}(D_\mu U)^\dagger D^\mu U$ at $U=1$. The left weak generator acts as $\sigma_i/2$ on each doublet, so the trace of its square is $N_D/2$. The canonically normalized charged-vector mass form increases by $D uu^\dagger$. For $Q$ the two chiral charges agree, so the corresponding mass term vanishes. Multiply the rank-one inverse formula for $K+Duu^\dagger$ by the two lepton sources to obtain the assertion. For smooth sources the same formula follows in an infinite tower by the resolvent identity for the rank-one form, when the source matrix elements exist. At a massless pole take the limit from $t>0$. $\square$

There is a source-dependent correction one can actually evaluate on the round probe of result 60. Use its two charged triplets, and assume a separate chiral completion retains the stated left-doublet vertices, with no charged right-doublet vertex. Let $g=I_W^{-1/2}$, $l=(g,\ell_D)$, $u=(g,u_D)$, $m_D^2=2/r^2$ and $\ell_D=2n_\ell/(3\sqrt{I_D})$, $u_D=2n_q/(3\sqrt{I_D})$ in a common orientation. At zero geometric breaking, the bare inverse kernel is $\mathrm{diag}(t,t+m_D^2)$. Its dressed contact is
$$
 C_{\ell\ell}(0)=\frac{1}{2N_DF^2}
                  +\frac{(\ell_D-u_D)^2}{8m_D^2}.
$$
Indeed at zero momentum the two linear forms on the vector fields are the quark combination with coefficient $D$ and the heavy-vector mass with coefficient $m_D^2$. Expressing the lepton source as the quark source plus $(0,\ell_D-u_D)$ diagonalizes the contact calculation. The determinant of the dressed mass-squared matrix is $g^2 D m_D^2$ and its trace is $m_D^2+D(g^2+u_D^2)$. Its two poles are the roots of this quadratic mass problem as long as the assumed constant chiral stiffness is valid at the pole. For a heavy pole above the chiral regime use momentum-dependent strong polarization instead. The last contribution to the contact vanishes for identical lepton and quark profiles. At canonical round matching $(\ell_D-u_D)^2=16g^2/45$. Thus the threshold correction is fixed in this probe, while $F$ still carries strong dynamics. The vectorlike round Dirac model by itself gauges both chiralities in the same weak representation and has no such axial stiffness from a vector-aligned condensate. Implementing the chiral completion is a physical condition.
