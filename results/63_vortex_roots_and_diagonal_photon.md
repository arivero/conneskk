# A supported diagonal photon from two roots of a vortex section

A change from a helical metric to a polar metric removes the longitudinal curvature cost of result 59. The polar metric by itself has an extra neutral isometry. Here a matter order parameter breaks the two neutral rotations to their diagonal. We keep one fibration action, the magnetic fluxes, the root couplings, the matter characters and the charge lattice fixed. We vary its vacuum moduli. We distinguish a symmetry of the full background including the fields from an isometry of the metric alone.

## Root fields and support

**Theorem 63.1.** Extend the Einstein–vortex action of Theorem 62.1 as follows. Regard the degree-two vortex line as the square of its degree-one root $V_1$, with connection $A$ and vortex curvature $2F_A$. The vortex $\Phi$ has charge two under this connection. Add two scalar sections $H_+,H_-$ of $V_1$ of circle weights $n_\pm=\pm k/2$, with even $k$. Use conventional positive external kinetic terms, with coefficient $Z_H>0$ inside the common Einstein prefactor. In the internal energy include positive squares of the colour derivatives, weak covariant antiholomorphic derivatives, and $L^{-1}(D_\chi-i n_\pm)H_\pm$. Include
$$
 \lambda\,|H_+ H_- - \zeta_0 e^{-2\sigma}\Phi|^2,\qquad
 \lambda,\zeta_0>0 .
$$
The product is the line-bundle product. These are local operators on a specified fibration. The vertical and horizontal derivatives are distinguished by that fibration. In particular the circle shift can be written using the metric connection $\eta$ of the principal circle, $\eta(\partial_\chi)=1$, in $d-iA-in_\pm\eta$. The horizontal lift annihilates $\eta$. The four-dimensional kinetic term couples to the actual circle Fourier weight. This specifies the coupling of the roots to the KK connection, including under a metric variation. In the internal sphere directions the antiholomorphic square uses the orientation and the two-metric. This action has more structure than minimally coupled eleven-dimensional Einstein–Maxwell or eleven-dimensional supergravity.

For every member of Theorem 62.1 its two simple vortex zeros determine a continuous symmetric branch of exact Minkowski product solutions with these fields. At each nonround member the connected unbroken geometric weak-sector action on the full vacuum is the diagonal $Q=K_3+k^{-1}\partial_\chi$, with the monopole lift on fields. At dissolution the weak rotation and circle actions are separately unbroken.

**Proof.** The degree-one holomorphic sections with one zero at either pole have product proportional to $\Phi$. Normalize the two factors $r_\pm$ so that $r_+r_-=\Phi$, their squared $L^2$ norms are equal, and their axial weights are $\pm1/2$. These choices exist by the axial lift and the north–south symmetry. In the northern chart one can take the sections proportional to $1,z$ with $\Phi=z$ in its holomorphic frame. Set
$$
 H_+=\sqrt{\zeta_0}\,e^{-\sigma}e^{ik\chi/2}r_-,
 \qquad H_-=\sqrt{\zeta_0}\,e^{-\sigma}e^{-ik\chi/2}r_+ .
$$
Every added square and its first variation vanish, including variations of the metric, connection, dilatons and the vortex. All the Einstein and matter equations verified in Theorem 62.1 therefore continue to hold. In the limit $\epsilon=e^{-b}\to0$ the root amplitudes are of order $\epsilon^{1/4}$ when the round radii are finite: their squared norms at the equator are proportional to $\exp(-b/2)$ and the normalized profiles approach the smooth round doublet. The zero fields solve the equations at dissolution. At finite $b$ the metric permits $K_3,Y=k^{-1}\partial_\chi$ besides colour. Under their invariant bundle lifts the axial vortex is neutral. A further constant vortex-gauge transformation preserving its nonzero section has zero infinitesimal parameter. The root fields then impose the single equation that the coefficients of $K_3$ and $Y$ are equal. At dissolution there is also an independent unbroken vortex gauge field. Unsourced harmonic vectors of the four-form require separate counting in a full particle inventory. $\square$

The relative real normalization of the roots is a modulus of the product potential. The reflection-symmetric member fixes it in the calculation below. The continuous shape and dilaton moduli likewise remain. The projection onto the horizontal and complex derivatives is an explicit microscopic choice; a bare spatial gradient for both scalar sections without the completing terms changes the vacuum stress.

## The charged exchange at five
