# The colour scalar instability of the Hopf product and a stabilizing action

Fix the flux, spin lift and stationary round colour factor of 94, with the transverse couplings of 97. Vary the intrinsic colour-leaf spatial action that controls the metric and flux perturbations. We ask whether the first coloured pole can be used as a threshold in a positive theory. The calculation treats the scalar-derived sector at frozen weak metric and the rotationally invariant weak sector on the round weak sphere. For the first threshold we specify a projected round comparison. The nonround weak-dependent constraint problem is left separate.

## A nonhomogeneous colour instability with a stable homogeneous radius

**Proposition 98.1.** There is a subfamily of actions of 94.2 with sufficiently small internal volume-line penalties in which the magnetic top form gives a physical negative second variation in the colour octet. In the frozen colour-dilaton and weak-metric restriction without those penalties, the squared mass is
$$
 m^2B^2=\frac{4(29-4\sqrt{67})}{7}\simeq-2.138.
$$
The homogeneous breathing stability of 94.2 does not exclude this direction.

**Proof.** The weak curvature and vortex stress cancel in the frozen weak slice. The resulting problem is $d=4$ flat external directions and a $q=5$ compact round factor supported by a top form and positive potential. At fixed colour dilaton let $\lambda=4/B^2$, normalized flux density $Z f_5^2/2=\lambda$ and scalar harmonic eigenvalue $\nu$. Use the scalar metric and form ansatz of 64.1, with $d=4,q=5$. The $j=2$ scalar harmonics on the five-sphere include the trace-free $z^\dagger T z$ of Hopf charge zero and colour adjoint. Their eigenvalue is $\nu=12/B^2=3\lambda$. The scalar traceless constraint is nondegenerate here. The determinant for $x=(\nu-m^2)/\lambda$ is $x^2+(qD_0-2)x-2(q-1)D_0\nu/\lambda$, with $D_0=2(d-1)/(d+q-2)=6/7$, by the internal Einstein trace and the form divergence. Thus $7x^2+16x-144=0$. This is a physical mode of the time-symmetric constrained problem. Its coordinate flux and metric amplitudes $t,H$ have $t-5H\ne0$, so it changes the local magnetic density.

In this octet the metric perturbation can be taken to be conformal on the colour leaf and the volume-line backgrounds can be rescaled with it so that their pointwise norms and the root product stay at the prescribed values. Their Hopf weights and weak holomorphic profiles stay fixed because the harmonic has zero Hopf weight. The new spatial penalties which see the colour derivatives then contribute continuously in their coefficients, and have zero first variation on the background. Keeping them sufficiently small leaves the negative constrained potential. Their external kinetic stiffness can be kept fixed in this time-symmetric comparison. This concerns a subfamily of the fixed-actions admitted by 94.2. ## Two positive intrinsic colour terms

**Proposition 98.2.** Add the following spatial energy densities on the intrinsic colour leaves, in the frozen weak comparison:
$$
 \frac{\eta}{2}\,Z(\sigma) B_0^2 e^{2\sigma}
      |\nabla_5(*_5F_5)|^2
 \ +\ \varrho B_0^2 e^{2\sigma}\,
             |\operatorname{Ric}^{\rm TF}(g_5)|^2.
$$
They have zero background value and first variation. They leave the transverse weak pencil and the Killing-vector kinetic terms used in 94 and 97 unchanged. With $\eta=5/2$ and $\varrho=3/10$, the scalar-derived colour metric, five-form and colour dilaton sector with weak-rotation singlet profiles at the round member has a gap $m^2\ge8/B^2$ off the homogeneous modes. The same gap in the frozen weak comparison holds if the colour dilaton kinetic coefficient lies in $0<c\le1$. For the round coupled weak singlet one uses the conformally adjusted action specified below.

**Proof.** These are intrinsic spatial terms on the marked colour leaves, not spacetime curvature-squared terms. The flux density is constant and the metric is Einstein on each leaf of every product background in 94. A transverse metric vector or an external form vector has zero first-order variations of the intrinsic metric and purely internal flux on that leaf. Thus the two new squares have vanishing quadratic contributions on those vectors. Their first variations also leave the linear constraints unchanged.

Here are the scalar constraints in the frozen weak or equivalent nine-dimensional Einstein comparison. Use conformal exponents $A Y$ on the three external spatial directions and $H Y$ on the five colour directions in initial data, an active dilaton perturbation $S Y$, and coordinate magnetic density $f_5(1+tY)$ relative to the background colour volume. Normalize $\langle Y^2\rangle=1$, put $z=\nu/\lambda$ and $D=t-5H$. The linear Hamiltonian constraint gives $3A+4H=t/z$. Expand the spatial potential in units $\lambda$. The curvature part is
$$
 -\tfrac52(3A+3H)^2-z(12H^2+24AH+6A^2).
$$
The scalar potential contributes $2(3A+5H-2S)^2$. The flux contributes
$$
 \tfrac12(3A-5H+8S)^2+2t(3A-5H+8S)+t^2 .
$$
These formulas follow by conformal rescaling on the colour leaf, integrating its Laplacian term by parts, and using the fixed coordinate flux. The dilaton adds $cz S^2$. After imposing the constraint and the two added squares the constrained quadratic energy is
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 Q/\lambda&=\widetilde A_z H^2+B_z HD+
        (1+4/(3z)+4\eta z)D^2+16DS+(40+cz)S^2,\\
 A_z&=\frac{(4z-5)(7z-20)}{3z},\qquad B_z=-32/3+40/(3z),\\
 \widetilde A_z&=A_z+36\varrho z(4z/5-1).
 \end{aligned}
$$
The flux square uses the physical flux-density variation $D Y$. The Ricci term uses $\delta\operatorname{Ric}^{\rm TF}=-3H(\operatorname{Hess}Y)^{\rm TF}$ and $\|(\operatorname{Hess}Y)^{\rm TF}\|^2=\nu(4\nu/5-\lambda)$.

For the kinetic form use a scalar shift $v\nabla Y$ and the differentiated Hamiltonian constraint. The Einstein kinetic cross and shift-square terms are $-2\nu v(3\dot A+4\dot H)-\lambda\nu v^2$. The electric flux term is $\lambda\nu(v+\dot t/\nu)^2$. Thus their shift terms cancel and the physical kinetic form is
$$
 T=\frac{28}3\dot H^2-\frac{14}{3z}\dot H\dot t
      +(1/z-2/(3z^2))\dot t^2+c\dot S^2 .
$$
Its $(H,t)$ determinant is $7(4z-5)/(3z^2)$. The $j=1$ conformal harmonic has $z=5/4$ and the null direction is a diffeomorphism with $D=0$. On its quotient the $D,S$ form gives the stated bound directly. The $j=0$ homogeneous colour calculation is the positive transverse breathing combination of 94.2, with a scale modulus.

For $j\ge2$ one has $z\ge3$ and $A_z>0$. Writing $t=D+5H$, Young's inequality gives $T\le(97/9)\dot H^2+(11/9)\dot D^2+c\dot S^2$. Also $B_z^2/(4A_z)=16(4z-5)/(3z(7z-20))$ decreases from $112/9$. Use half of each $H^2$ and $S^2$ coefficient to bound the cross terms. The $H^2,D^2,S^2$ coefficients of $Q/\lambda$ after that bound are at least
$$
 (7/9+151.2\varrho)/2,\qquad
 1+12\eta-224/9-3.2,\qquad
 (40+3c)/2.
$$
At the stated $\eta,\varrho$ they dominate twice the respective kinetic coefficients for $0<c\le1$. With the same normalization of spatial energy and time kinetic term this gives $m^2\ge2\lambda=8/B^2$.

For the round weak singlet use the nine-dimensional Einstein frame $g_9^{(11)}=e^{-4\omega/7}g_9^{(E)}$, where the weak radius is rescaled by $e^\omega$. The colour terms depend on $\chi=\sigma+2\omega/7$ and the effective active kinetic coefficient is $c=63/65$ with the original unit dilaton kinetic coefficient and weak volume coefficient $18/7$. The orthogonal scalar directions have nonnegative weak potential Hessian and free colour-gradient mass at least $\nu$. One fixed fibration prescription for the extra terms is to set $\omega=-\tfrac12\log(|F_A|_\Sigma/F_{\rm ref})$ with a fixed constant $F_{\rm ref}$, use the intrinsic metric $\tilde g_5=e^{4\omega/7}g_5$ and $\chi$ in the two squares, and multiply their eleven-dimensional density by $e^{4\omega/7}$. In the round reduction this cancels the measure factor and gives precisely the displayed calculation in Einstein frame. The weak top-form density is positive on the finite broken shapes in use. On all those backgrounds $\omega$ is constant along each colour leaf, so both extra squares still vanish and leave the transverse pencils intact. This verifies the standing family and the round gap. Weak-dependent nonround scalar perturbations need the full constraint comparison. $\square$

## The projected round threshold and the change at five

**Proposition 98.3.** In a comparison retaining the weak-rotation singlet coloured bosons at the round member, use the two stabilizers above, the minimal five-form and metric kinetic terms, and retain only colour-invariant volume-line amplitudes for the extra scalar fields. The first positive coloured boson near the round fitted scales of 97 is then the lower broken-Killing pole of 94.5. Its identification does not extend unchanged to the uniform weak shrink.

**Proof.** For a transverse colour one-form $X$ of Hodge eigenvalue $\lambda_j=(j+1)(j+3)/B^2$, $j\ge1$, the minimal metric mass before flux is $\lambda_j-2\lambda$. The coexact potential $\beta=-f_5 *_5dX^\flat/\lambda_j$ has squared norm $f_5^2\|X\|^2/\lambda_j$. The mass square supplied by its mixed five-form derivative is $2\lambda$ on the metric diagonal and $\lambda_j$ on the form diagonal after normalization. The off-diagonal is $-\sqrt{2\lambda\lambda_j}$. Thus the minimal matrix has both diagonal entries $\lambda_j$. At $j=1$ it is the Killing block of 94.5 once its $S$ term is retained. For $j\ge2$ the lower eigenvalue is at least $(15-\sqrt{120})/B^2$. Extra positive $S$ terms can be included by the variational principle in this sector. External spin-two scalar harmonics have the scalar gap $5/B^2$. Internal transverse traceless metric tensors have squared masses at least $12/B^2$, from their rough eigenvalue $j(j+4)-2$ and the curvature shift two in sphere units. Remaining coexact form sectors are above $8/B^2$ by the sphere spectrum. The scalar-derived block is bounded by 98.2. Free scalar spectators in this comparison start at the scalar harmonic gap and the auxiliary Maxwell field has zero background curvature. The coloured scalar modes of the volume-line coefficients are excluded by the stated projection. At the fitted scale the lower positive broken-Killing eigenvalue has $M^2B^2\simeq0.52$, so it lies below all those other bounds. [Spherical form spectra](https://doi.org/10.18910/6956)

There is a useful separate level comparison along the broken path. One may use the volume-line projection for the external derivative of the two roots while keeping the full tensor derivative of $S$. The two roots then add zero to the broken-colour Killing mass because their broken rotation is perpendicular to the invariant volume line. The weak radial derivatives and their Abelian charges are unaffected. The weak-constant broken vector and its form remain a pair of the matrix in 94.5 as $B$ changes. Keeping $f_S$ fixed, their lower eigenvalue has $M^2B^2\to8$ as $a\downarrow0$. The external spin-two harmonic of degree one is still an exact coloured excitation with $M^2B^2=5$. It lies below that pair at five. These two modes already show a change of order. The first omitted colour level of the full nonround theory requires additional spectral estimates. $\square$

In this projected comparison the threshold near restoration is of order $4$ TeV at $f_S=5$ TeV, rather than the inverse Hopf radius itself. A four-dimensional strong match must either use the actual active mirror spectrum or prescribe an independently gapped mirror sector. At exact classical restoration the root-generated mirror masses vanish. Keeping three ordinary and three mirror families gives twelve active Dirac quarks below the projected bosonic threshold while their next free projective and weak shells are heavy: the quark projective gap is $4/B$ for the dual $O(-4)$ kernel and the round monopole gap is $\sqrt2/r$. The one-loop colour coefficient in that spectrum is three. The ordinary six-flavour value of the hadronic scale cannot be used for that endpoint without another determination. The constant-Yukawa and comoving input distinctions of 93 still apply along the broken spin trajectory.
