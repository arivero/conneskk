# The full form sector of a magnetic spin product

Fix the two-derivative, source-free eleven-dimensional supergravity action, the brane charges and an integral weak flux. Vary the topology of the four-dimensional factor and require its radii to satisfy the Einstein equations. The first observable is the mass and charge of the vector generated by the two-form period. The second is the action and matter insertion of a wrapped five-brane. The single-class projection of 84.1 is recovered by an explicit restriction of sources and fields.

## Keeping all harmonic vectors

**Proposition 87.1.** Let $h_i$ be an integral basis for the free two-cohomology of a closed oriented Riemannian four-manifold $X$, represented harmonically. Write $I_{ij}=\int h_i h_j$, $J_{ij}=\int h_i *h_j$ and use the mixed flux $T_2 G_4^{\rm phys}=\sum_i F^i\wedge h_i$, where $\int_\Sigma F^i/(2\pi)=n^i$. Periods in this section are integral in the chosen free lattice. Then the axion charge covector and the massive vector direction in connection space are
$$
 q=I n,\qquad A_{\rm massive}\ \parallel\ J^{-1}I n .
$$
The unbroken transformations in this block are $\ker q$. If one restricts all connections to the line $n$ before computing the mass, the squared mass obtained is the fraction
$$
 \gamma_{\rm line}^2=
 \frac{(n^T I n)^2}{(n^T J n)^2}
$$
of the squared mass in the full block. In particular the line contains the massive eigenvector precisely for a self-dual or anti-self-dual flux class. Additional charged axions or Higgs terms change these assertions.

**Proof.** Integration of the magnetic equation gives $dH_3/(2\pi)=-I_{ij}(F^i/2\pi)(F^j/2\pi)/2$. Integrating over $\Sigma$ yields $d\theta-q_i A^i$ in the local phase action. The vector metric is $K=C_{\rm ext}J/(T_2^2 V_X)$, with $C_{\rm ext}$ the reduced Einstein coefficient, and the mass form is $f_\theta^2 q q^T$. The nonzero generalized eigenvalue is $f_\theta^2 q^T K^{-1}q$. Restricting to $n$ gives $f_\theta^2(q^T n)^2/(n^TK n)$. On harmonic two-forms $*^2=1$ implies $I J^{-1} I=J$. This gives the fraction and the statement about the direction. For example a probe covector proportional to $Jn$ has fraction $\gamma_{\rm line}^2$ of its summed squared vertex on the massive direction and $1-\gamma_{\rm line}^2$ on massless directions. Other sources require their own projection. $\square$

For $X=C_1\times C_2$ with one factor a two-sphere and the other an oriented surface, take $h_1,h_2$ of unit periods and area ratio $u=\mathcal A_2/\mathcal A_1$. Then $I$ interchanges the basis directions and $J=\operatorname{diag}(u,u^{-1})$. With $n=(1,1)$, $q=(1,1)$ and $(1,-1)$ generates the additional massless direction. A wrapped membrane of integral charges $(w_1,w_2)$ that is neutral under this extra vector has $w_1=w_2$. It has even charge on the proposed diagonal generator $(1,1)$. A degree-one weak singlet spinor or a charge-one root of 63 therefore needs a source of additional charge, a mass for the unwanted vector or another embedding. The small kinetic ratio exhibited kinematically in 84 is a property of the projected line. It cannot be inserted as the coupling of a lone physical vector when both harmonic fields and their sources are retained.

## Solving the spin product Einstein equations

**Proposition 87.2.** Use a direct product of an external Einstein five-space, two constant-curvature surfaces $C_1,C_2$, and the round weak $S^2_r$. Let $C_1$ be a sphere and $C_2$ a surface of genus $g$. The unshifted unit flux in each $C_i\times S^2_r$ gives a source-free magnetic solution with both positive curvatures for $g=0$, or with one negative curvature for every $g\ge2$. For $g=0$ the areas of the two factors must agree. For $g\ge2$ their ratio $t=\mathcal A_2/\mathcal A_1$ is the unique solution $t>\sqrt2$ of
$$
 g-1=\frac{t(t^2-2)}{2t^2-1}.
$$
For all these solutions the full harmonic massive vector has $M^2=3/r^2$, independently of the apparent diagonal projection. These are bosonic product solutions in the classical effective equations.

**Proof.** Put $F=\mathrm{vol}_{w}/(2r^2)$ and $T_2 G_4^{\rm phys}=F\wedge(h_1+h_2)$. In orthonormal area forms its coefficients are $g_i=1/(2T_2 r^2\mathcal A_i)$. For $S=g_1^2+g_2^2$, the weak, external and two surface Ricci coefficients follow from the four-form stress:
$$
 \lambda_w=S/3=1/r^2,\quad \lambda_5=-S/6,\quad
 \lambda_1=(2g_1^2-g_2^2)/6,\quad
 \lambda_2=(2g_2^2-g_1^2)/6 .
$$
The flux is closed, co-closed and has zero square. Gauss–Bonnet gives $\mathcal A_1=4\pi/\lambda_1$, $\mathcal A_2=4\pi(1-g)/\lambda_2$ when $\lambda_2\ne0$. Use $g_1/g_2=t$. At $g=0$ the positive solution of $t^3+2t^2-2t-1=0$ is one. For negative curvature the displayed rational function increases from zero to infinity: its derivative has positive numerator $2t^4+t^2+2$. The remaining absolute radius satisfies
$$
 \frac{r^3}{\ell_p^3}=
 \frac{\pi(2t^2-1)}{4\sqrt3\,t\sqrt{1+t^2}} .
$$
At $g=1$ the same equations separately allow a flat two-torus at $t=\sqrt2$ with its area fixed by flux and its complex shape free. All products are spin and the first Pontryagin class of the internal product of surfaces vanishes. An external anti-de Sitter space realizes the required Ricci tensor. In particular an additional direct flat circle times Minkowski four-space does not realize that external solution.

The scalar Maxwell coefficient along the flux line is $Z_n=n^TJn/(T_2^2V_X)=4r^4S=12r^2$. With $\mathcal A_w=4\pi r^2$, one has $f_\theta^2=C_{\rm ext}/(T_5^2V_X^2\mathcal A_w^2)$. From 87.1 and $T_2^2=2\pi T_5$ the full nonzero mass is $4\pi^2 Z_n/\mathcal A_w^2=3/r^2$. The other combination in this block is massless. $\square$

For $g=22$ and $23$, solving the cubic in Python gives $t=42.0357$ and $44.0341$, and $|\gamma_{\rm line}|=2t/(1+t^2)=0.04755$ and $0.04540$. These bracket the central fitted line coefficient of 78. On both, $r\simeq0.968\ell_p$ for primitive flux. Even the all-sphere case gives $r=0.684\ell_p$. Their radii, additional form fields and missing colour rotations materially change the physical comparison. For unequal nonzero integer periods and two positive curvature surfaces, $1/\sqrt2<|g_1/g_2|<\sqrt2$ and $|\gamma_{\rm line}|=2|g_1 g_2|/(g_1^2+g_2^2)>2\sqrt2/3$. The genus examples exhibit what changing the sign of one curvature accomplishes, and their unit-flux radii leave higher-derivative M-theory effects uncontrolled. Classical linear stability of the entire product has not been established.

## The wrapped M5 needs membrane boundaries

**Proposition 87.3.** On the product six-cycle $D=X\times\Sigma$, take $T_2G_4^{\rm phys}/(2\pi)=m h k$ with $\int_\Sigma k=1$. The cohomological worldvolume Bianchi identity of a wrapped M5 requires M2 boundary class $m\,\operatorname{PD}_X(h)\times[\mathrm{pt}]$ up to orientation. The total charge of these boundaries on the connection $A_v$ is $m^2\int_X h^2$. The classical bare five-brane action and the kinetic coefficient of its period obey
$$
 S_5=T_5 V_X\mathcal A_\Sigma,\qquad
 S_5 f_\theta=\sqrt{C_{\rm ext}}.
$$
In four-dimensional product Einstein conventions the right side is $M_4/\sqrt2$. These formulas give a homology condition and a tension cost. A nonzero quantum insertion additionally requires a permissible brane and its zero-mode measure.

**Proof.** The worldvolume Bianchi equation has cohomological content $[\delta_4(C)]=[T_2G_4^{\rm phys}/(2\pi)]|_D$ where $C$ is the oriented union of M2 boundaries. Poincaré duality on the product gives the stated class. An M2 on a two-cycle $\Gamma$ couples to $A_v$ with charge $m\int_\Gamma h$ by its Wess–Zumino action. Substitution gives the total. With all harmonic vectors retained it gives the full covector $In$ of 87.1. The action follows from the product volume and $f_\theta^2=C_{\rm ext}/(T_5^2 V_X^2\mathcal A_\Sigma^2)$ from 84.1.
