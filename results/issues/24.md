# Gauge-covariant blocking of the interval current response

**Status.** Theorems for a vectorlike interval at a fixed four-dimensional lattice regulator, through one fermion loop and two probe insertions. The finite comparison includes a nonzero contribution from the induced two-current vertex. The repeated-blocking estimate is uniform in the vertical cutoff. The calculation addresses [GitHub issue #24](https://github.com/arivero/conneskk/issues/24).

## Interval, probe and regulator

We vary the grouping and depth of the spectral eliminations and the spacelike probe momentum. We hold the interval length, wall domain, charge, microscopic kinetic coefficients and subtraction point fixed. The observable is the subtracted transverse current response on a compact range of momenta. The spectral projection and its change of frame use [Lemma 14, Theorems 1 and 2](../../lemmas/14_gauge_covariant_reduction.md). The fixed-compression test identifies the role of the frame in a gauge-equivalent pair of inputs. [Result 13](../13_nogo_fixed_compression_not_covariant.md)

Let the lattice spacing and fermion charge be one. Set $\ell=\pi/2$ and take the vertical operator

$$
\mathcal T(A_5)=E(\partial_x+iA_5),\qquad
E=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\mathcal B=\{u\in H^1([0,\ell];\mathbb C^2): u_R(0)=u_L(\ell)=0\}.
$$

The boundary form is $[u^\dagger E v]_0^\ell$. The line at each wall equals its annihilator for this form, so the adjoint domain has the same two conditions. A gauge transformation acts by the same phase on both interval components. With $A_5^g=A_5+i(\partial_xg)g^\dagger$ and $\mathcal B^g=g\mathcal B$, the differentiated product gives $\mathcal T(A_5^g)g=g\mathcal T(A_5)$. The two wall lines are invariant under these phase multiplications.

At $A_5=0$ an orthonormal basis is

$$
u_m(x)=\ell^{-1/2}\binom{\cos(mx)}{\sin(mx)},\qquad
m\in 2\mathbb Z+1,\qquad \mathcal T(0)u_m=m u_m .
$$

Indeed, the left wall fixes the initial vector to the left component and the solution at the right wall requires $\cos(m\ell)=0$. For completeness, $\mathcal T(0)^2$ gives a Neumann–Dirichlet scalar problem on the left component and a Dirichlet–Neumann one on the right. Their cosine and sine bases are the sums and differences of $u_m$ and $u_{-m}$ for positive $m$. Each signed mode has a four-component Dirac spinor of unit vector charge. This is the product operator of Lemma 14 in a signed Dirac mass convention. Left multiplication of the continuum product operator by $\gamma_5$ replaces the horizontal gamma matrices by $-i\gamma_5\gamma_\mu$, which are again Hermitian Euclidean Clifford matrices, and leaves $\mathcal T$ as the mass. A source-independent left factor cancels in normalized determinant ratios. The regulator below includes the species of the naive lattice difference in the loop.

For the finite benchmark keep $|m|<6$ in the regulated interval tower. Let $P$ retain $|m|<2$, let $B$ be the added band when the window is $|m|<4$, and let $C$ be the rest of the regulated tower. Thus the bands are the pairs at absolute mass $1,3,5$. The two nested windows have boundaries strictly inside spectral gaps. More generally $\mathcal I_M$ consists of $|m|\le 2M+1$ for a nonnegative integer $M$.

Let $b_\mu$ be a real four-dimensional link source, sampled at link midpoints, and $d$ the forward lattice exterior derivative on cochains. Its adjoint is for the counting inner product. Write $t=d^*db$, so $t(b+d\lambda)=t(b)$ and its multiplier in midpoint Fourier conventions is

$$
\widehat p^{\,2}\delta_{\mu\nu}-\widehat p_\mu\widehat p_\nu,\qquad
\widehat p_\mu=2\sin(p_\mu/2).
$$

The lifted connection in axial gauge is $b_\mu+f(x)t_\mu$, with $f(x)=\cos(2x)$. Its ordinary charge term is constant on the interval. Its curvature term allows a mixed current while keeping the transformation $b\mapsto b+d\lambda$. The profile is smooth and $f'$ vanishes at the walls. To exercise the moving windows, take $\chi(b;n,x)=(f(x)-1)\overline t_2(n)$, where $\overline t_2(n)$ is the average of the two direction-2 link values incident on site $n$. Set $A_5=\partial_x\chi$ and restore the corresponding horizontal pure gauge with $W_n=e^{-i\chi(b;n,x)}$. A link from $n$ to $n+\hat\mu$ in the axial frame acquires $W_n$ on its left and $W_{n+\hat\mu}^\dagger$ on its right. The axial frame has $W_n(0)=1$ and keeps both wall conditions. Its relation to any other smooth gauge frame is $W^g=g W g(0)^\dagger$. The modes, all windows and the regulator are conjugated by $W$ in the original frame.

The matrix $F_M$ of multiplication by $f$ compressed to $\mathcal I_M$ has entries

$$
(F_M)_{mn}=\frac1\ell\int_0^\ell \cos(2x)\cos((m-n)x)\,dx
=\frac12\,\mathbf 1_{|m-n|=2}.
$$

In particular the mixed overlap is $1/2$ on $(1,3)$ and on $(-1,-3)$. A transverse cosine source of nonzero wavenumber then gives a smooth mixed background. For the diagonal control, use the factorized lift $b_\mu$ alone and $\chi=0$. Each charge vertex acts as the identity on the interval, so every retained–omitted matrix element and induced two-current insertion vanishes in that control.

In the axial frame exponentiate the **compressed connection** on each horizontal link. With $M_0=\operatorname{diag}(m)$ and Hermitian Euclidean gamma matrices, define

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_\mu(n)&=\exp\big(i[b_\mu(n)1+t_\mu(n)F_M]\big),\\
D(b)\psi(n)&=M_0\psi(n)
+\frac12\sum_\mu\gamma_\mu\big[U_\mu(n)\psi(n+\hat\mu)
-U_\mu(n-\hat\mu)^\dagger\psi(n-\hat\mu)\big].
\end{aligned}
$$

This prescription transports the connection on the regulated bundle. Under $b\mapsto b+d\lambda$ its links transform as $U_\mu(n)\mapsto g_n U_\mu(n)g_{n+\hat\mu}^\dagger$ for $g_n=e^{-i\lambda(n)}$. In a general interval gauge, conjugation of the vertical modes induces the same law with the residual endpoint phase $g_n(0)$. All splittings use this one regulator. We can define the normalized effective action $-\log\det(D(b)D(0)^{-1})$ first on a periodic horizontal lattice, on the branch at $b=0$. The Hessian per unit volume has a thermodynamic limit given by normalized Brillouin-zone integrals. The free inverse on each mode is

$$
G_m(k)=\frac{m-i\gamma\cdot s(k)}{m^2+|s(k)|^2},\qquad
s_\mu(k)=\sin k_\mu,\qquad \|G_m(k)\|\le |m|^{-1}.
$$

At zero source, all principal mode blocks are invertible. Perturbations of the links are bounded in operator norm near zero, so the needed inverses exist there. The charge is vectorlike and gauge transformations are finite-dimensional similarities at finite volume, giving an invariant regulated determinant.

Use $p=q e_1$, polarization in direction 2, $K=[1/4,1/2]$, $q_\star=1/4$ and $\sigma(q)=4\sin^2(q/2)$. For the free lattice dispersion the smallest decay energy at mass $|m|$ is $\operatorname{arsinh}|m|$. This follows by putting one momentum component equal to $iE$ in $m^2+\sum_\mu \sin^2 k_\mu$, with the other components real, and minimizing over them. The first omitted one-particle energy is $\operatorname{arsinh}3$. The least pair energy involving an omitted mode is $\operatorname{arsinh}1+\operatorname{arsinh}3$. All $q$ in $K$ lie below both bounds. Let $T_M(q)$ be the direction-2 Hessian of the normalized effective action per unit volume. We use

$$
\Pi_M^{\rm sub}(q)=T_M(q)/\sigma(q)-T_M(q_\star)/\sigma(q_\star).
$$

For each $M$ the Maxwell counterterm is $-\big[T_M(q_\star)/\sigma(q_\star)\big]\|db\|^2/2$, fixed once from the microscopic regulated action and used through both eliminations and their approximations. It imposes the subtraction condition for the exact Hessian. It cancels from the difference defining the observable even when that difference is applied to an approximate Hessian. This prescription uses only this charge counterterm at the fixed regulator, with the same transformed walls in both calculations.

## Exact elimination and moving-window derivatives

**Theorem.** At each finite regulator, any finite grouping of the omitted modes gives the same final Schur kernel and the same normalized determinant of the eliminated modes. The full quadratic current tensor is transverse. For the two-band benchmark the induced two-current vertex contributes strictly to $\Pi^{\rm sub}(q)$ for $q>q_\star$ in $K$.

Use the probe $\alpha b(p)+\beta b(-p)$ and take mixed derivatives at $\alpha=\beta=0$, complexifying the real Hessian for Fourier plane waves. In the original frame put $X_s=(\partial_s W)|_0$ for $s=\alpha,\beta$. These are commuting multiplication operators. For any one of the window projections $J$, its first and mixed variations in that frame are $[X_s,J]$ and $[X_\alpha,[X_\beta,J]]$. For example, the first variation from $u_1$ to $u_3$ is $-i\,\partial_s\overline t_2/2$, which is nonzero for the direction-2 probe at $q\in K$. For the following commutators extend the regulated matrix by zero on the complement of its window. The derivatives of $D^\chi=W D W^\dagger$ at zero source obey

$$
\begin{aligned}
D^\chi_s &=V_s+[X_s,D_0],\\
D^\chi_{\alpha\beta}
&=H+[X_\alpha,V_\beta]+[X_\beta,V_\alpha]
  +[X_\alpha,[X_\beta,D_0]],
\end{aligned}
$$

where $D_0=D(0)$, $V_s=\partial_sD|_0$ and $H=\partial_\alpha\partial_\beta D|_0$. In the moving window the derivative of a block is the derivative of $J W^\dagger D^\chi W J'$ in fixed mode coordinates. The first correction is $-[X_s,D_0]$. The mixed correction is

$$
-[X_\alpha,D^\chi_\beta]-[X_\beta,D^\chi_\alpha]
+[X_\alpha,[X_\beta,D_0]].
$$

Substitution gives $V_s$ and $H$ in those coordinates. This implements projector motion also for off-diagonal blocks and for the ends of the regulated tower.

Write $Q=B\oplus C$. Eliminating $Q$ gives

$$
S_P=D_{PP}-D_{PQ}D_{QQ}^{-1}D_{QP},\qquad
\det D=\det D_{QQ}\det S_P .
$$

If $C$ is eliminated first, define $D^{[C]}_{ij}=D_{ij}-D_{iC}D_{CC}^{-1}D_{Cj}$ for $i,j\in P\oplus B$. Solving the $C$ equation in $D_{QQ}x=D_{QP}$, the $B$ equation becomes $D^{[C]}_{BB}x_B=D^{[C]}_{BP}$. Substituting both solutions into $D_{PP}-D_{PQ}x$ yields

$$
S_P=D^{[C]}_{PP}-D^{[C]}_{PB}(D^{[C]}_{BB})^{-1}D^{[C]}_{BP}.
$$

Subtracting $D_{BC}D_{CC}^{-1}$ times the $C$ row of the $Q$ block from its $B$ row gives $\det D_{QQ}=\det D_{CC}\det D^{[C]}_{BB}$. The row subtraction has determinant one. Normalizing at $b=0$ gives equality of logarithms on the chosen branch. Solving an arbitrary finite list of the omitted block equations by the same substitution proves the kernel identity by uniqueness, while each block row elimination multiplies the determinants of its pivots. Invertibility near zero follows from the free blocks. This proves associativity for the full effective action at the common regulator, including its source-only determinant.

For a kernel with inverse $G$ at zero, differentiating $DD^{-1}=1$ gives $\partial_s G=-G V_s G$. The mixed derivative of the negative log determinant is therefore

$$
\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}=\operatorname{Tr}(G V_\beta G V_\alpha)-\operatorname{Tr}(G H).
$$

Both cross blocks of $D$ between $P$ and $Q$ vanish at zero source. The first derivative of $S_P$ is $V_{s,PP}$, and its second derivative is

$$
H_{PP}-V_{\alpha,PQ}G_Q V_{\beta,QP}
      -V_{\beta,PQ}G_Q V_{\alpha,QP}.
$$

The last two terms enter the Hessian as the negative trace against $G_P$. The source determinant for $Q$ supplies its own bubble and contact term. Successive elimination includes the same terms with $B$ and $C$ in the first induced vertex. When the $B$ determinant is taken, that vertex is traced against $G_B$. This is the $B$–$C$ contribution to the direct heavy determinant.

### Ward identity

The exact effective action is gauge invariant. Each Schur kernel transforms by similarity on its surviving bundle, and each eliminated determinant is invariant on its moving bundle. Differentiating invariance under $b\mapsto b+d\lambda$ gives $\sum_\mu\widehat p_\mu\Gamma_{\mu\nu}(p)=0$, including the common Maxwell counterterm. The identity can also be verified in the axial channel where the dressed part of a longitudinal vertex vanishes. For $q e_1$ the link derivative from a longitudinal source is $V_1(k+p,k)=i\gamma_1\cos(k_1+q/2)$ on each mass. Then $\widehat p_1 V_1(k+p,k)=D_0(k+p)-D_0(k)$. In the bubble, contracting one vertex replaces the two propagators around it by $G(k)-G(k+p)$. Translation of the second integral gives the difference of the other vertices evaluated at $k+p/2$ and $k-p/2$. For polarization 1 this is

$$
i\gamma_1[\cos(k_1+q/2)-\cos(k_1-q/2)]
=-\widehat p_1 i\gamma_1\sin k_1.
$$

It equals $\widehat p_1 H_{11}$ from the second link derivative, and the Hessian subtracts that term. For a second polarization perpendicular to $e_1$, the cosine is independent of $k_1$, so the difference vanishes. The mixed contact derivative vanishes because the two sources enter distinct link directions. This gives the longitudinal cancellation with the same spin trace, moving walls and counterterm as the transverse calculation.

## Finite transverse polarization

Let $L=1+\sigma(q)F_M$. The two direction-2 vertices at momenta $\pm p$ are $i\gamma_2\cos k_2\,L$. The mixed link derivative at zero total momentum is $H=-i\gamma_2\sin k_2\,L^2$. The factors follow by differentiating the forward and backward phases twice and using that $p_2=0$. For a retained–omitted pair $(1,3)$ the first vertex is $i\gamma_2\cos k_2\,\sigma(q)/2$. At $k_2=0$ and each $q\in K$ it is nonzero.

Write $\langle\cdot\rangle$ for the integral on $[-\pi,\pi]^4$ with measure $d^4k/(2\pi)^4$, $s'=s(k+p)$ and $s=s(k)$. Define

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_m&=\left\langle\frac{4s_2^2}{m^2+|s|^2}\right\rangle,\\
B_{mn}(q)&=\left\langle
\frac{4\cos^2k_2\,(2s_2^2-s\cdot s'-mn)}
     {(m^2+|s|^2)(n^2+|s'|^2)}\right\rangle .
\end{aligned}
$$

Indeed, $\operatorname{tr}\gamma_\mu=0$ and $\operatorname{tr}(\gamma_\mu\gamma_2\gamma_\nu\gamma_2)=4(2\delta_{\mu 2}\delta_{\nu 2}-\delta_{\mu\nu})$ by the Clifford anticommutator and cyclicity. Substituting the free inverse gives the bubble numerator shown. The spin trace of $-G_m H$ gives the positive numerator of $A_m$. The change $k\mapsto -k-p$ gives $B_{mn}=B_{nm}$, and changing the signs of both masses leaves either integral invariant. The full expression at this cutoff is

$$
T_M(q)=\sum_m A_m(L^2)_{mm}+\sum_{m,n}|L_{mn}|^2 B_{mn}(q).
$$

For $M=2$, the contact coefficients are $(L^2)_{mm}=1+\sigma^2/2$ for $|m|=1,3$ and $1+\sigma^2/4$ for $|m|=5$. The two orientations of each adjacent pair have weight $\sigma^2/4$ each. The following coefficients are before dividing by $\sigma$ and subtracting. The first contains the full regulated contact derivative restricted to $P$, as in plain compression of the regulated matrix.

$$
\begin{aligned}
T_{\rm comp}&=(2+\sigma^2)A_1+2B_{11}
+\tfrac12\sigma^2 B_{-1,1},\\
T_{\rm ind}&=\sigma^2 B_{13},\\
T_C&=(2+\tfrac12\sigma^2)A_5+2B_{55},\\
T_{B\mid C}&=(2+\sigma^2)A_3+2B_{33}+\sigma^2 B_{35}.
\end{aligned}
$$

In direct elimination the $Q$ determinant is $T_C+T_{B\mid C}$, from both masses and the off-diagonal $3$–$5$ links. The light determinant gives $T_{\rm comp}+T_{\rm ind}$. In successive elimination the first determinant contributes $T_C$, the second contributes $T_{B\mid C}$ and the final light determinant gives the same $T_{\rm comp}+T_{\rm ind}$. There are two $P$–$B$ edges and zero $P$–$C$ edges in the first variation. Each $P$–$B$ edge contributes two orderings in the light contact term, giving the displayed $\sigma^2 B_{13}$.

The induced part of the subtracted observable is

$$
\sigma(q)B_{13}(q)-\sigma(q_\star)B_{13}(q_\star).
$$

It is strictly negative for $q>q_\star$ in $K$. In the integrand of $-B_{13}$ the factor $3+s_1s_1'-s_2^2+s_3^2+s_4^2$ is at least $2-\sin^2(1/4)>0$, using $\sin k_1\sin(k_1+q)=\sin^2(k_1+q/2)-\sin^2(q/2)$. Its derivative in $q$ has absolute value at most one. The only varying denominator is $9+|s'|^2$, whose derivative also has absolute value at most one. Away from $\cos k_2=0$ the absolute logarithmic derivative of this positive integrand is bounded by $(2-\sin^2(1/4))^{-1}+1/9<1$. Since $\sigma'/\sigma=\cot(q/2)>1$ on $K$, the integral of $\sigma$ times this integrand is strictly increasing. This isolates the term from the two-current vertex even if the heavy determinant is also supplied to the compression.

## Repeated local blocking

**Theorem.** In the same interval model, eliminate each successive pair of omitted modes using a covariant polynomial for the inverse in the induced two-current vertex, retaining the heavy determinant at quadratic order. There is a family with increasing depth whose errors in the subtracted response tend to zero in $C(K)$, uniformly in the vertical cutoff.

Use the descending order of absolute masses. For depth $N$, start with $\mathcal I_N$. For a larger $\mathcal I_M$ with $M\ge N$, eliminate the part above $\mathcal I_N$ exactly first. At step $j$, $j=N,\ldots,1$, let $J$ be the pair of mass $\pm(2j+1)$, and let $Y$ be the modes that survive that step. Use the degree-$r$ Neumann polynomial of the bare horizontal kernel $K_J(b)=D_{JJ}^{\rm bare}(b)-M_J$ in the cross term of the Schur kernel:

$$
G_J^{(r)}(b)=\sum_{u=0}^r(-M_J^{-1}K_J(b))^u M_J^{-1},\qquad
M_J=\operatorname{diag}(2j+1,-2j-1).
$$

In the determinant of $J$ use the exact current effective block at the chosen order, including the two-current terms already inherited from higher modes. The polynomial transforms by similarity because $M_J$ commutes with the residual gauge phases. The product of cross blocks and this polynomial gives a covariant modification of the Schur kernel. At quadratic order its new term uses two nearest-neighbor linear link vertices and at most $r$ nearest-neighbor hops from the polynomial. It is local on the lattice at each finite $r$. Derivatives of the polynomial are multiplied by a cross block that vanishes at zero source, so only $G_J^{(r)}(0)$ is needed in the Hessian. The source determinant and every Schur update preserve gauge invariance by the same covariance argument as for the exact Ward identity.

Here are the exact maps on the Taylor data. The free and one-current kernels are the bare restrictions after every step. Let $H$ be the second derivative of the retained kernel and $c$ the mixed derivative of the accumulated source-only action, per unit volume. For each polarization pair and momenta $\pm q e_1$ the maps are

$$
\begin{aligned}
H&\longmapsto H_{YY}-V_{\alpha,YJ}G_JV_{\beta,JY}
                     -V_{\beta,YJ}G_JV_{\alpha,JY},\\
c&\longmapsto c+\operatorname{Tr}(G_J V_{\beta,JJ}G_J V_{\alpha,JJ})
                    -\operatorname{Tr}(G_J H_{JJ}).
\end{aligned}
$$

The trace is normalized by volume. The approximate map changes only the first line, with the polynomial in both insertions. Thus the differences between two inputs on this affine set consist of a quadratic kernel $h$ and a source coefficient $c_{\rm diff}$. On a retained set $X$ use the norm

$$
\sup_{q\in K,\ \mu,\nu}
\left(|c_{{\rm diff},\mu\nu}(q)|
+\sum_{m,n\in X}\frac{4}{\sqrt{|mn|}}\,
  \sup_k\|h_{mn;\mu\nu}(k;q)\|\right).
$$

The kernel is evaluated at zero total momentum, with the specified two insertions. The norm includes off-diagonal vertices and the determinant data. Under a subsequent step $h$ restricts to $Y\times Y$ and $c_{\rm diff}$ changes by $-\operatorname{Tr}(G_J h_{JJ})$. The latter is bounded by $\sum_{m\in J}4\sup_k\|h_{mm}\|/|m|$, since the free inverse is diagonal in mass and the spin trace has dimension four. These terms are removed from the kernel part of the norm. It follows that every later step has Lipschitz constant at most one, including the approximate map. Taking the final full Hessian has the same constant by the same trace bound on $P$. Division by $\sigma$ and subtraction give Lipschitz constant $C_K=2/\min_K\sigma$ into $C(K)$ for the transverse observable.

Let $R_K=\max_K\sigma$ and $\Delta=\min_{m\notin P}|m|=3$. Since $\|K_J(0)\|\le 2$ in this regulator, multiplying the finite geometric series by $1+M_J^{-1}K_J(0)$ gives

$$
G_J-G_J^{(r)}=(-M_J^{-1}K_J(0))^{r+1}G_J,\qquad
\|G_J-G_J^{(r)}\|\le
\frac{1}{2j+1}\left(\frac{2}{2j+1}\right)^{r+1}.
$$

At step $j$ each sign has a single remaining neighbor at absolute mass $2j-1$. For each polarization and $q\in K$, the norm of a cross vertex is at most $R_K/2$. With two orderings and two signs, the local defect in the displayed norm is bounded by

$$
\varepsilon_j\le
\frac{4R_K^2}{(2j-1)(2j+1)}
\left(\frac{2}{2j+1}\right)^{r+1}.
$$

Inserting one approximate map at a time yields a difference of final data bounded by the sum of its input defects times the products of the subsequent Lipschitz constants. Here those products are at most one. The identity

$$
\sum_{j=1}^N\frac{1}{(2j-1)(2j+1)}
=\frac12\left(1-\frac{1}{2N+1}\right)
$$

follows by subtracting reciprocal odd integers. Thus the weighted estimate for the observable is

$$
\|\Pi^{\rm app,sub}_{M,N}-\Pi_M^{\rm sub}\|_{C(K)}
\le 2 C_K R_K^2 (2/\Delta)^{r+1}.
$$

The family $r=N$ makes the right-hand side tend geometrically to zero, for every $M\ge N$ with the specified exact first step if $M>N$. The probe factors enter through $R_K^2$, and charge subtraction uses the same $C_K$ at each depth. The ratio controlling this proof is $2/(a\Delta)$ when the horizontal spacing is $a$ in physical units. For a fixed momentum range separated from zero the argument needs $\Delta>2/a$ for a uniform small ratio. A closing omitted mass gap moves the mixed pair threshold toward zero when the retained masses also tend to zero, so such a family needs a shrinking below-threshold range.

## Regulator limits

The vertical cutoff can be removed in this quadratic observable at fixed horizontal spacing. To see this, rewrite the bare transverse coefficient as the sum of $A_m+B_{mm}$ over modes and

$$
\frac{\sigma^2}{4}(A_m+A_n+2B_{mn})
$$

over undirected adjacent pairs. From the integrals and the propagator norm, $|A_m|\le4/m^2$ and $|B_{mn}|\le4/|mn|$. Thus an edge bracket has absolute value at most $8(1/m^2+1/n^2)$. For $M\ge1$ the omitted diagonal terms have $j>M$, and the omitted edges start at $j=M$ on each sign branch. Summing both tails gives

$$
\sup_{q\in K}|T_\infty(q)-T_M(q)|
\le (16+8R_K^2)\sum_{j\ge M}(2j+1)^{-2}
\le \frac{16+8R_K^2}{2(2M-1)}.
$$

The last inequality is the integral bound for the decreasing function $(2x+1)^{-2}$ starting at $M-1$. The same factor $C_K$ gives a uniform bound after subtraction. For fixed $N$ the approximate and exact data agree immediately after their common exact elimination above $\mathcal I_N$. Their differences at later steps are determined by the displayed difference maps and by the free and one-current blocks of $\mathcal I_N$, which are independent of $M$. Thus the response error for fixed $N$ is independent of $M\ge N$. Taking the vertical cutoff to infinity defines the response with the upper tower treated exactly and $N$ approximate steps below it, at fixed horizontal spacing. The geometric bound holds for that response as $N$ grows. It also gives convergence along $M=N\to\infty$, by adding the vertical tail bound.

Removal of the horizontal regulator remains open here. At fixed physical $\Delta$, the Neumann parameter $2/(a\Delta)$ grows as $a\to0$. Fix the physical probe profile, the target fermion species and the bulk and wall counterterms. For a continuum limit along a chosen vertical cutoff $M(a)$ a separate sufficient estimate is

$$
\big\|\Pi^{\rm dir,ren}_{a,M(a)}-\Pi^{\rm dir,ren}_{a',M(a')}\big\|_{C(K_{\rm phys})}
\ \longrightarrow\ 0
\qquad (a,a'\to0).
$$

Here $K_{\rm phys}$ is fixed in physical units and both responses use the same physical subtraction conditions. A joint limit with approximate blocking also needs the sum of stability-weighted defects to tend to zero along the cutoff path. Independence of the path would require uniform estimates over the claimed class of paths. The fixed-spacing bounds establish the finite-response and repeated-blocking criterion of issue #24.

## Numerical verification

The script is Python 3 with NumPy. It integrates the finite benchmark by the product trapezoidal rule with 48 and 64 samples per direction on the $\pi$-periodic integrands. The Ward test uses the longitudinal cosine of the shifted momentum and the contact term independently of the transverse computation. The additional checks cover the spectral windows and overlaps, threshold inequality, finite reciprocal sum and the propagator remainder.

For a quadrature bound, put $h=1/4$, $c=\cosh h$, and $d=1-4\sinh^2 h>0$. On the complex strip $|\operatorname{Im}k_\mu|\le h$, each propagator denominator has real part at least $m^2-4\sinh^2 h\ge d m^2$. The sine and cosine magnitudes are at most $c$. The magnitudes of the $A_m$ and $B_{mn}$ integrands there are bounded by $4c^2/d$ and $4c^2(6c^2+1)/d^2$, both below 64. Shifting the Fourier integrals by $\pm ih$ in each coordinate bounds Fourier coefficients by $64\exp(-2h\sum_\mu |n_\mu|)$ for integer indices $n_\mu$. A product grid of size $s$ aliases precisely the indices in $(s\mathbb Z)^4$. With $z=\exp(-2hs)$ and $s=64$, the sum over nonzero aliases is $((1+z)/(1-z))^4-1<16z$. Each tabulated coefficient before division and subtraction has sum of absolute weights below 20. Propagating the alias bound gives an error below $10^{-8}$ in the subtracted columns before rounding, apart from floating-point roundoff.

    sine_squared = np.sin(angles) ** 2
    s2 = sine_squared[:, None, None]
    other = s2 + sine_squared[None, :, None] + sine_squared[None, None, :]
    a = dict.fromkeys(masses, 0.0)
    bubble = dict.fromkeys(pairs, 0.0)
    longitudinal = dict.fromkeys(masses, 0.0)
    for k1 in angles:
        s1, shifted = np.sin(k1), np.sin(k1 + q)
        inv = {m: 1 / (m*m + s1*s1 + other) for m in masses}
        inv_shifted = {m: 1 / (m*m + shifted*shifted + other) for m in masses}
        dot = s1 * shifted + other
        transverse = 2 * s2 - dot
        long_trace = 2 * s1 * shifted - dot
        cosine_squared = np.cos(k1 + q/2) ** 2
        for m in masses:
            a[m] += np.mean(4 * s2 * inv[m])
            longitudinal[m] += np.mean(
                4 * ((long_trace-m*m) * cosine_squared * inv_shifted[m] + s1*s1)
                * inv[m]
            )
        for m, n in pairs:
            bubble[m, n] += np.mean(
                4 * (1-s2) * (transverse-m*n) * inv[abs(m)] * inv_shifted[n]
            )
    assert max(abs(value/count) for value in longitudinal.values()) < 2e-12
    return ({m: value/count for m, value in a.items()},
            {pair: value/count for pair, value in bubble.items()})


def response(q, count):
    a, bubble = integrate(q, count)
    b = bubble.__getitem__
    sigma = 4 * np.sin(q/2) ** 2
    shared = sigma ** 2
    plain = (2 + shared)*a[1] + 2*b((1, 1)) + shared*b((-1, 1))/2
    induced = shared*b((1, 3))
    upper = (2 + shared/2)*a[5] + 2*b((5, 5))
    middle = (2 + shared)*a[3] + 2*b((3, 3))
    heavy_direct = middle + upper + shared*b((3, 5))
    middle_after_upper = middle + shared*b((3, 5))
    direct = (heavy_direct + plain + induced)/sigma
    successive = (upper + middle_after_upper + (plain + induced))/sigma
    diagonal = 2 * sum(a[m]+b((m, m)) for m in masses)
    edges = (a[1]+b((-1, 1)) + a[1]+a[3]+2*b((1, 3))
             + a[3]+a[5]+2*b((3, 5))) / 2
    assert abs(direct - (diagonal + shared*edges)/sigma) < 1e-13
    assert abs(direct - successive) < 1e-13
    return np.array([direct, successive, plain/sigma, induced/sigma,
                     heavy_direct/sigma, upper/sigma, middle_after_upper/sigma])
