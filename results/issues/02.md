# Twisted spinors and cap domains on the football

**Theorem.** Let $g=R^2(d\theta^2+q^{-2}\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2)$, $q\ge1$, with the spin structure extending across each cap and a degree-$N$ monopole. Choose the northern potential $A_\varphi=N(1-\cos\theta)/2$, so the southern angular label is $m-N$ when the northern polar spin frame has label $m\in\mathbb Z+1/2$. Both ends are limit point for the first-order Dirac operator. For each $m$ put
$$a=qm,\quad b=q(m-N),\quad C=qN/2,\quad p=1/2+|a-1/2|,\quad r=1/2+|b+1/2|.$$
All nonzero eigenvalues in this column are
$$\lambda=\pm R^{-1}\sqrt{[j+(p+r)/2]^2-C^2},\qquad j=0,1,2,\ldots,$$
omitting a zero radicand. There are exactly $|N|$ zero modes, with signed chirality index $N$.

**Proof.** Remove the measure $\sin\theta$ by the unitary half-density. The two chiral operators are $R^{-1}A$ and $R^{-1}A^*$ with $A=\partial_\theta-U$, $U=(qm-C+C\cos\theta)/\sin\theta$. The northern homogeneous traces have powers $\theta^a,\theta^{-a}$ in the two components and at the south, with $y=\pi-\theta$, powers $y^{-b},y^b$. Since $|a|,|b|\ge1/2$ only one homogeneous trace is square integrable at either tip; at equality the other has logarithmically divergent norm. The corresponding repeated-root logarithm in the squared equation is excluded by the first-order graph norm. In $A^*A=-\partial_\theta^2+U^2+U'$ the admissible powers are $p,r$. Substitution of $\sin^p(\theta/2)\cos^r(\theta/2)\,v(\cos\theta)$ gives the Jacobi polynomials $P_j^{(p-1/2,r-1/2)}$. Their completeness with the induced weight gives the displayed squares, and applying $A$ gives their opposite chiral partners at every positive square. The solution of $Au=0$ is regular at both ends exactly when $0<m<N$; the adjoint condition is $N<m<0$. This proves the index including the negative-flux kernel missed by $A^*A$. For $q=1,N=0$ the levels are $\pm k/R$ of multiplicity $2k$ per sign.

For surplus-angle metrics $0<q<1$, each end with indicial modulus $<1/2$ has two square-integrable traces. A self-adjoint local choice is a Lagrangian line in its two boundary coefficients for the Green form of the first-order system. The cap-compatible separated choice keeps the northern upper trace for $m>0$ and lower for $m<0$, and the southern upper trace for $m-N<0$ and lower for $m-N>0$. For this choice the same Jacobi calculation uses $p=a$ or $1-a$ respectively at the north, $r=-b$ or $1+b$ respectively at the south. Other tip extensions require their own boundary equation. $\square$

**Comparison with the interval.** The two caps select the trace components by the signs just calculated. Both the relative sign and the allowed endpoint behavior are fixed by $m,N$; the scalar Friedrichs axial interval, the mixed-wall odd tower of Lemma 05, and the pair of freely rotatable matrix gradings of Lemma 23 have different domains and spectra. In particular, the bounding spin structure has no $m=0$ column. The monopole can retain a finite chiral kernel even on the shrinking sphere. **Two scaling parameters.** With $R=R_0\sqrt\epsilon$, $q=\epsilon^{-1/2}$, the first untwisted spinor mass is $(q+1)/(2R)$, of order $\epsilon^{-1}$. For $N\ne0$ the first excitation within a zero-mode column is $\sqrt{1+q|N|}/R$, of order $\epsilon^{-3/4}$; the other columns start at order $q/R$ for fixed integral $N$. The scalar axial excitation has order $R^{-1}$ and the first nonaxial scalar has order $q/R$. It is the scalar heat trace that has an intermediate one-dimensional fibre regime. At fixed physical energy all positive masses here diverge. Lemma 30 opens a tower under a separate overall dilation, and the exponent $1/2$ in Lemma 44 comes from a Schur complement with specified off-diagonal scaling. The football path supplies neither of those operations by itself. [Joint limit](../../lemmas/30_massless_limit_as_a_joint_limit.md) · [Seesaw scaling](../44_light_mass_along_scaling.md)

On $S^5\times S^2$ a fixed polar axis is global. On $N(1,0)=\mathbb P(E)\to S^5$, selecting a pole gives a line in $E$ and reduces the nontrivial $SU(2)$ clutching bundle to $U(1)$. Every such line over $S^5$ is trivial and the determinant then trivializes its complement, contradicting the clutching class. An unoriented axis lifts to an oriented one since $S^5$ is simply connected. There is a global replacement for the two evaluation data: the fixed conic intersects a fibre in a length-two divisor, and evaluation with a first jet at tangency reconstructs the degree-one vertical holomorphic kernel. Its monodromy and branch points are part of that construction. [Conic and monopole limit](../53_conic_monopole_window.md)
