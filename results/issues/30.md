# A chiral-condensate transfer to the charged-lepton scale and its held-out tests

We ask whether chiral QCD dynamics can fix the charged-lepton normalization near $313$–$314$ MeV and select $f_\pi$ as the matching scale. What varies is the light down-quark mass $m_d$ at fixed $m_u,m_s,m_b$ and fixed strong input, the hypercharge coupling $g'$ at fixed $g$ and $v$, and, separately, the strong scale. What stays fixed is the measured charged-lepton spectrum and one candidate cross-sector action, specified below with all its coefficients counted. The observables are the four reference scales of the issue, the inverse down normalization $C_d$, the scheme-independent responses of the proposed relations to these variations, and one held-out observable, the leptonic pion decay amplitude. [Issue 30 and the closure plan](https://github.com/arivero/conneskk/issues/30#issuecomment-7189858664)

The numbers below are computed by [`paper/chiral_scale_responses.py`](../../paper/chiral_scale_responses.py) (Python 3, standard library). The common-scale running is the DualSM calculation, which we reran at DualSM commit `19bd9dd` (all 37 checks pass). ## A candidate action and its coefficients

Take QCD with $N_f=2+1$ light flavours, the electroweak theory without charged-lepton Yukawa couplings, and the gauge-invariant dimension-six operator
$$
 \mathcal L_{\rm tr}=\sum_{i}\frac{c_i}{\Lambda^2}\,(\bar L_i e_i)(\bar d\,Q_1)+{\rm h.c.},
$$
with flavour-diagonal real $c_i>0$ for $i=e,\mu,\tau$. This is the $O_{ledq}$ structure of the Warsaw basis. The quark bilinear is the only part that sees chiral symmetry breaking. [Warsaw basis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.4884)

**Proposition 30.1.** At first order in $\mathcal L_{\rm tr}$ and leading order in chiral perturbation theory, $m_i=c_i|\langle\bar dd\rangle|/(2\Lambda^2)$. The product $c_i(\mu)\langle\bar dd\rangle(\mu)$ is renormalization-group invariant under QCD. The chiral normalization carried to the leptons is $|\langle\bar dd\rangle|$, which equals $F^2B_0$ with $B_0=M_\pi^2/(m_u+m_d)$ at leading order. It is $f_\pi^2$ times a scheme-dependent mass parameter, and $f_\pi^2$ alone does not appear. The action has three independent coefficients $c_i/\Lambda^2$ for three masses, and
$$
 C_\ell=\frac{|\langle\bar dd\rangle|}{18\Lambda^2}\Big(\sum_i\sqrt{c_i}\Big)^2 ,
$$
so $C_\ell$ is proportional to one free combination of coefficients.

**Proof.** The $SU(2)$ down component of $(\bar L_ie_i)(\bar dQ_1)$ contains $(\bar e_{iL}e_{iR})(\bar d_Rd_L)$. In the chirally broken vacuum $\langle\bar d_Rd_L\rangle=\langle\bar d P_Ld\rangle=\langle\bar dd\rangle/2$, so the operator contributes $-(c_i/2\Lambda^2)|\langle\bar dd\rangle|\,\bar e_{iL}e_{iR}+{\rm h.c.}$ for $\langle\bar dd\rangle<0$. The bilinear $\bar d_Rd_L$ renormalizes inversely to the mass, so $c_i(\mu)$ runs as $m(\mu)$ and the product is invariant. The Gell-Mann–Oakes–Renner relation $(m_u+m_d)|\langle\bar qq\rangle|=F^2M_\pi^2$ gives the leading chiral form. The perturbative mixing of $\bar dd$ with $m_d^3$ is smaller than the condensate by a factor below $10^{-5}$. Taking square roots and summing gives $C_\ell$. $\square$

With $|\langle\bar qq\rangle|^{1/3}=272$ MeV ($\overline{\rm MS}$, $2$ GeV, FLAG 2019) the transfer scales $\Lambda/\sqrt{c_i}$ needed for the observed masses are $75.3$ MeV for the $\tau$, $308.6$ MeV for the $\mu$ and $4.44$ GeV for the electron. Written with the same relation, the proposed equality $C_\ell C_d=f_\pi^2$ is equivalent to
$$
 \frac{\Lambda}{\sum_i\sqrt{c_i}}=w_d\sqrt{\frac{m_d}{2(m_u+m_d)}}\,\frac{F}{f_\pi}\,M_\pi=59.1\ {\rm MeV}=0.438\,M_{\pi^0},
$$
with the coefficients at the scale where $C_d$ is evaluated. In this action the relation therefore fixes the operator scale at a fraction of the pion mass. The chiral dynamics supplies $|\langle\bar dd\rangle|$, and the relation remains a condition on a free coefficient.

## Responses to the light-quark masses and to hypercharge

**Proposition 30.2.** At fixed $m_u,m_s,m_b$ and strong input,
$$
 \frac{\partial\log C_d}{\partial\log m_d}=w_d\quad\text{exactly},\qquad
 R_d:=\frac{\partial\log(C_\ell C_d/f_\pi^2)}{\partial\log m_d}
   =w_d-\frac{2m_d}{m_u+m_d}\,\kappa_f+\kappa_\ell ,
$$
where $\kappa_f=d\log f_\pi/d\log\hat m$ and $\kappa_\ell=\partial\log C_\ell/\partial\log m_d$. In the action of Proposition 30.1, $\kappa_\ell=\kappa_\Sigma:=\partial\log|\langle\bar dd\rangle|/\partial\log m_d$. All three terms are scheme independent, since mass ratios and their logarithmic derivatives are invariant in a mass-independent scheme.

**Proof.** $C_d=9S^{-2}$ with $S=\sum_jm_j^{-1/2}$, and $\partial S/\partial\log m_d=-\tfrac12m_d^{-1/2}$, so $\partial\log C_d/\partial\log m_d=m_d^{-1/2}/S=w_d$. The pion decay constant depends on $\hat m=(m_u+m_d)/2$ at this order, and $\partial\log\hat m/\partial\log m_d=m_d/(m_u+m_d)$. The last term is the definition, and Proposition 30.1 gives its value in the candidate action. $\square$

**Numerical values (Python 3).** At next-to-leading order in $SU(2)$ chiral perturbation theory $f_\pi=F(1+\xi\bar l_4)$ with $\xi=M_\pi^2/(4\pi F)^2=0.01541$, so $\kappa_f=\xi(\bar l_4-1)=(f_\pi/F-1)-\xi=0.049$. We assign it $\pm0.017$, the FLAG ratio error combined with an allowance of size $\xi$ for higher orders. With $m_u/m_d=0.462$, $m_d/(m_u+m_d)=0.684$. The size of $\kappa_\Sigma$ is of order $\xi$ at next-to-leading order. The lattice ratio $\langle\bar ss\rangle/\langle\bar ll\rangle=1.08(16)$ across a factor $27$ in mass corresponds to an average logarithmic slope of $0.02$, with $0.07$ at its upper one-standard-deviation end, and we use $|\kappa_\Sigma|\le0.07$. Then
$$
 R_d=0.731\ (\kappa_\Sigma=0),\qquad 0.641\le R_d\le0.821
$$
over $m_u/m_d\in[0.38,0.53]$, the $\kappa_f$ error and the $\kappa_\Sigma$ range. The same numbers with the mixed-scale weight $w_d=0.7955$ differ in the third digit. A relation that holds as an identity along this deformation has $R_d=0$. Proposition 30.1 instead gives $R_d\simeq0.73$: the relation holds on a hypersurface of quark-mass space through the physical point, and moving $m_d$ by one per cent violates it by $0.73\%$. In the chiral limit $C_\ell C_d/f_\pi^2\to0$ as in the issue comment, since $C_d\sim9m_d$ and $f_\pi\to F$.

At fixed $g$ and $v$, $\alpha(0)B=\alpha(0)g'v/2$ at tree level with $e^2=g^2g'^2/(g^2+g'^2)$, so
$$
 \frac{\partial\log[\alpha(0)B]}{\partial\log g'}=2\cos^2\theta_W\,\frac{\alpha(0)}{\alpha(M_Z)}+1=2.450 ,
$$
with the on-shell $\cos^2\theta_W=M_W^2/M_Z^2=0.77666$; the factor $\alpha(0)/\alpha(M_Z)$ is the one-loop running with fixed charges, and the tree value is $2.553$. In the candidate action $C_\ell$ depends on $g'$ only through QED corrections to the lepton masses. The one-loop mass anomalous dimension bounds that response by $0.08$ for the electron and $0.035$ for the $\tau$ at operator scales up to $1$ TeV. The weak-to-lepton ratio therefore responds with slope about $-2.4$, and its measured value $0.998022(349)$ is a point coincidence in this action. Varying the strong scale at fixed current masses moves $C_\ell\propto|\langle\bar qq\rangle|$ as its cube and $m_N$ and $f_\pi$ linearly, so $C_\ell/(m_N/3)$ responds with power $2$ and $C_\ell C_d/f_\pi^2$ with power $1$.

## The held-out pion decay amplitude

The coefficients $c_i/\Lambda^2$ are fixed by the three lepton masses. The reserved dimensionless observable is the ratio of the pseudoscalar amplitude for $\pi^-\to\ell^-\bar\nu$ generated by the same operator to the Standard Model amplitude.

**Proposition 30.3.** If a fraction $x_\ell$ of $m_\ell$ comes from Proposition 30.1, the same operator gives, at leading order in chiral perturbation theory,
$$
 \frac{\mathcal A_P}{\mathcal A_{\rm SM}}(\pi\to\ell\bar\nu)
 =x_\ell\,\frac{M_\pi^2}{\sqrt2G_FV_{ud}(m_u+m_d)|\langle\bar qq\rangle|}
 =x_\ell\,\frac{v^2}{V_{ud}F^2}=8.3\times10^6\,x_\ell ,
$$
independently of the lepton, with $v^2=1/(\sqrt2G_F)$.

**Proof.** The $SU(2)$ up component of the same operator contains $(\bar\nu_{iL}e_{iR})(\bar d_Ru_L)$, and its conjugate gives $(c_i/\Lambda^2)(\bar e_{iR}\nu_{iL})(\bar u_Ld_R)$. With $\langle0|\bar ui\gamma_5d|\pi^-\rangle=\sqrt2f_\pi M_\pi^2/(m_u+m_d)$ from the divergence of the axial current, its amplitude is $(c_i/\Lambda^2)f_\pi M_\pi^2/[2\sqrt2(m_u+m_d)]\,\bar u_\ell(1-\gamma_5)v_\nu$. The Standard Model amplitude is $G_FV_{ud}f_\pi m_\ell\,\bar u_\ell(1-\gamma_5)v_\nu$, with the same spinor structure. Their ratio is $(c_i/\Lambda^2)M_\pi^2/[2\sqrt2G_FV_{ud}m_\ell(m_u+m_d)]$. Inserting $c_i/\Lambda^2=2x_\ell m_\ell/|\langle\bar dd\rangle|$ cancels $m_\ell$, and the Gell-Mann–Oakes–Renner relation gives the second form. With $F=86.53$ MeV and $V_{ud}=0.97373$ the number is $8.32\times10^6$; with the physical $f_\pi$ in that relation it is $7.35\times10^6$. $\square$

The measured $\pi\to\mu\nu$ rate agrees with the Standard Model prediction from lattice $f_\pi$ at the per-cent level. An amplitude ratio below $0.01$ requires $x_\mu$ below about $1.4\times10^{-9}$, and the same bound holds for each lepton because the ratio is universal. The ratio $\Gamma(\pi\to e\nu)/\Gamma(\pi\to\mu\nu)$ is unchanged when all three fractions are equal, so the absolute rate is the test.

**No-go theorem 30.4 (exact scope).** Hypotheses: QCD with $N_f=2+1$ at leading order in chiral perturbation theory; a charged-lepton mass generated at tree level from the light-quark condensate through a gauge-invariant local four-fermion operator of dimension six ($O_{ledq}$, or $O^{(1)}_{lequ}$ with the up condensate, which has the same charged-current partner), with coefficients independent of the quark masses and gauge couplings. Then (i) $C_\ell$ is proportional to a free coefficient and receives no prediction; (ii) the relations $C_\ell C_d=f_\pi^2$ and $C_\ell=\alpha(0)B$ have responses $R_d\in[0.64,0.82]$ and about $-2.4$ in $\log g'$, so each holds only on a hypersurface; (iii) the pion decay amplitude limits the condensate share of every charged-lepton mass to below $1.4\times10^{-9}$.

Known escapes. A dimension-eight neutral operator $(\bar LHe)(\bar dH^\dagger Q)$ has no tree-level charged-current partner. Its scale for the observed masses is $\Lambda=c^{1/4}\times3.6$, $7.3$ and $27.8$ GeV for $\tau$, $\mu$ and $e$, at most $12.8$, $26.0$ and $98.5$ GeV for $c\le16\pi^2$, all below $v=246$ GeV, where that effective theory no longer applies. A mediator lighter than about $75$ MeV, so that the transfer is nonlocal at the QCD scale, is outside this scope. So are composite leptons with strongly coupled $c_i$, coefficients that depend on $m_d$ (the literal seesaw with a quark-mass mediator, which DualSM excludes by its family spread), and radiative transfer through current correlators of the kind Weinberg considered in 1976. The direction this points to: a mechanism that passes test (ii) must make the lepton normalization scale as $m_d^{-w_d+2(m_d/(m_u+m_d))\kappa_f}\approx m_d^{-0.73}$ at fixed strong input, which is the behaviour of an inverse law.

**Positive companion: Proposition 30.5.** Let a mechanism give $C_\ell=M^2/C_d$ with $M$ independent of $m_d$, as on the full-rank branch of five-flavour SQCD, where the meson vacuum values obey $h_i=S/m_i$ and a lepton-type mass $\lambda h_i$ gives $M^2=\lambda S$. Then $\partial\log(C_\ell C_d/M^2)/\partial\log m_d=0$ identically, and the relation $C_\ell C_d=f_\pi^2$ reduces to the single condition $\lambda S=f_\pi^2$ on the one free Kähler-normalized coefficient. **Proof.** $C_\ell C_d=M^2$ identically, and $M$ does not depend on $m_d$. The SQCD form is the F-term equation $m_ih_i=S$ of the DualSM five-flavour note. $\square$ This class satisfies the response test that excludes the condensate transfer, and it moves the open question to the value of $\lambda S$, which neither the holomorphic data nor chiral QCD fixes. It also inherits the DualSM failures of the direct image: the inverse phase $0.190$ rad against $0.2222$ rad for the leptons, and mass-ratio quotients $0.845$ and $3.90$. [Five-flavour inverse law](https://github.com/arivero/dualsm/blob/master/papers/paper-2-meson-mass-sum-rules/FIVE-FLAVOUR-MATCHING.md)

## The constituent-mass comparison

In the quark-level linear sigma model of Delbourgo and Scadron $M_q=2\pi F/\sqrt{N_c}$. With the FLAG chiral-limit $F=86.53(78)$ MeV this gives $313.9(2.8)$ MeV, equal to $C_\ell$ within its uncertainty. With the model's own $f_\pi^{\rm CL}=89.8$ MeV it gives $325.8$ MeV, and with the physical $f_\pi$ it gives $334.0$ MeV. The value is a model statement about an effective constituent mass, and the agreement depends on which chiral-limit decay constant is inserted. It is one more comparison among the several in the issue, with the corresponding multiple-comparison risk, and the model contains no lepton sector. [Delbourgo and Scadron](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/9910242)

## Status against the closure criterion

The plan's criterion asks for a mechanism that selects a chiral normalization and yields a held-out, scheme-independent prediction. In the candidate action the held-out prediction exists (Propositions 30.2 and 30.3) and fails: the pion amplitude excludes the mechanism by about nine orders of magnitude, and the chiral response is $0.73$ where a dynamical relation needs zero. No-go theorem 30.4 is a negative result in the stated scope. Two models stand behind it; wider consensus is needed before it is relied on in general, and the research question stays open. Proposition 30.5 identifies the inverse-law class as the one that passes the response test, with one coefficient still free. The next step is a mechanism in that class, or a nonlocal or radiative one, that fixes $\lambda S$ (or its analogue) from a current correlator and is tested on the pion decay amplitude and the weak-to-lepton ratio.
