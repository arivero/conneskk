# Selection of the charged-lepton determinant

We vary the three charged-lepton singular values of the finite Dirac operator at fixed second and fourth moments, keeping all other flavour data fixed. The observables are the determinant of the squared Yukawa matrix, the constrained Higgs and wall vacuum, and the lepton contribution to the inverse Thomson charge. The fixed-moment comparison in issue 20 uses the stationary polynomial Higgs potential. We first study the exact flat charged trace at a prescribed Higgs norm, then specify a wall action in which the norm varies. [Issue 25 and its closure criterion](https://github.com/arivero/conneskk/issues/25#issuecomment-7189862549) · [Stationary vacuum and threshold residual](20.md#a-charged-lepton-threshold-residual-at-a-fixed-stationary-vacuum)

## Positive fixed-moment spectra

**Proposition.** Let $x_1\le x_2\le x_3$ be the positive squared singular values, $S=\sum_i x_i$, $T=\sum_i x_i^2$ and $P=\prod_i x_i$. The unordered spectrum is uniquely specified by $P$ on the interval below. Put
$$
a=S/3,\qquad b=\sqrt{(T-S^2/3)/6}.
$$
The positive family exists for $S>0$ and $S^2/3\le T<S^2$. If $b=0$ it consists of $(a,a,a)$ at $P=a^3$. Otherwise its upper endpoint is
$$
(a-b,a-b,a+2b),\qquad P_+=(a-b)^2(a+2b).
$$
The lower end is as follows.

- For $b<a/2$ ($T<S^2/2$), it is $(a-2b,a+b,a+b)$ at the included determinant $P_-=(a-2b)(a+b)^2$.
- For $b=a/2$ ($T=S^2/2$), it is the excluded zero-mass boundary $(0,S/2,S/2)$.
- For $b>a/2$ ($T>S^2/2$), it is the excluded zero-mass boundary with distinct positive heavy roots $x_{2,3}=(S\mp\sqrt{2T-S^2})/2$. In both zero-mass cases the interval of positive determinants is $(0,P_+]$.

**Proof.** Cauchy–Schwarz gives $T\ge S^2/3$. Positivity of the pair products gives $T<S^2$, which is equivalent to $b<a$. Subtracting the mean gives the circle $\sum_i(x_i-a)=0$, $\sum_i(x_i-a)^2=6b^2$. The three cosines spaced by $2\pi/3$ have sum zero and sum of squares $3/2$. Their cosine and sine coefficient vectors form a basis of the plane, giving all points of the circle. In the ordered sector we can write
$$
(x_1,x_2,x_3)=a\,(1,1,1)+2b\bigl(
\cos(\theta+2\pi/3),\cos(\theta-2\pi/3),\cos\theta\bigr),
\qquad 0\le\theta\le\pi/3.
$$
The smallest root decreases from $a-b$ to $a-2b$. If it vanishes before the end of the sector, its first zero occurs at $\theta=\arccos(-a/(2b))-2\pi/3$. At zero the other two roots have sum $S$ and product $(S^2-T)/2$, yielding the listed boundary. The cubic with these roots is
$$
p(X)=X^3-SX^2+\frac{S^2-T}{2}X-P.
$$
Using $4\cos^3\theta-3\cos\theta=\cos 3\theta$ in the centred cubic gives $P=a^3-3ab^2+2b^3\cos 3\theta$. This decreases strictly in the interior of the ordered sector. The endpoints and the determinant range follow, and the sorted roots of $p$ give the inverse parameterization. At $T=S^2$ the only ordered nonnegative spectrum at $S>0$ is $(0,0,S)$. $\square$

## The exact charged trace

**Proposition.** Work at zero gauge field in flat Euclidean four-space, with constant Higgs norm $t>0$ and masses $m_i=t\sqrt{x_i}$ in a fixed mass normalization. Define $\lambda=t^2/\Lambda^2$ and take $F\in C^1([0,\infty))$ nonnegative with $\int_0^\infty uF(u)\,du<\infty$. Write
$$
I(v)=\int_v^\infty (u-v)F(u)\,du,\qquad H(\lambda,P)=\sum_i I(\lambda x_i).
$$
The charged trace per unit volume is $\Lambda^4 H/\pi^2$ in the unprojected convention with four Euclidean spin components, two chiral mass-block entries and their charge-conjugate copies per family. The sign result holds with any positive common multiplicity. For distinct roots,
$$
\partial_P H=[x_1,x_2,x_3]h'=\tfrac12\lambda^3 F'(\lambda\xi),
\qquad h(x)=I(\lambda x),\qquad x_1<\xi<x_3.
$$
On a nonsingleton family, if $F'<0$ on the sampled interval for each interior spectrum, the minimum at fixed $t$ is the upper double-root spectrum. If the profile is flat on the entire sampled range, the trace has the same energy at every $P$. These profiles supply no isolated stable interior determinant from the charged trace.

**Proof.** The substitution $u=(|p|^2+m_i^2)/\Lambda^2$ in the flat momentum integral gives $\Lambda^4 I(m_i^2/\Lambda^2)/(16\pi^2)$ per component. Differentiating $I$ gives $I'(v)=-\int_v^\infty F(u)\,du$, $I''=F$, and $I'''=F'$. Differentiating the root equation at fixed $S,T$ gives $dx_i/dP=1/p'(x_i)$. The sum $\sum_i h'(x_i)/p'(x_i)$ is the leading coefficient of the degree-two Lagrange interpolant to $h'$ at the three roots. Subtract that interpolant and apply Rolle's theorem twice to obtain the stated mean-value formula. The divided difference extends continuously at repeated nodes using the corresponding derivatives of $h'$. Thus no factor of $1/p'(x_i)$ diverges in the trace derivative at an endpoint.

The derivative and continuity on the closed interval compare the endpoint energies even when the lower endpoint is approached only as a positive-mass limit. More generally $F'\le0$ gives a nonincreasing trace. An interior zero of the derivative of a monotone cutoff requires flatness across the sampled interval at that spectrum. To see this, write the second divided difference of a function $g$ as the difference of its secant slopes divided by $x_3-x_1$. Express the slopes as averages of $g'$ and subtract $g'(x_2)$ from both averages. Integrating $g''$ and interchanging the two integrals gives
$$
[x_1,x_2,x_3]g=
\frac1{x_3-x_1}\left(
\int_{x_1}^{x_2}\frac{u-x_1}{x_2-x_1}g''(u)\,du
+\int_{x_2}^{x_3}\frac{x_3-u}{x_3-x_2}g''(u)\,du
\right).
$$
Both weights are positive inside their intervals. For $g=h'$ and $F'\le0$, vanishing of the integral forces $F'$ to vanish on the whole sampled interval by continuity. A strictly positive sign throughout the sampled branch would select the lower endpoint, or give the infimum at the excluded massless limit. For a nonmonotone profile a zero of the divided difference is the fixed-$t$ stationarity condition and the radial and second-variation equations remain separate. The full trace at zero gauge field has the radial monotonicity of issue 20 under its hypotheses. $\square$ [Exact flat trace](20.md#the-stationary-higgs-norm-of-the-finite-spectral-action) · [Divided-difference sign test](https://github.com/arivero/conneskk/issues/25#issuecomment-7143145405)

**The constant-Higgs term of order $\Lambda^{-2}$.** For $F\in C^2$ near zero, Taylor expansion at fixed $t,x_i$ gives
$$
\frac{\Lambda^4}{\pi^2} H
=\frac{\Lambda^4}{\pi^2}
\left(3 I(0)+\lambda I'(0)S+\frac{\lambda^2}{2}F(0)T\right)
+\frac{t^6}{6\pi^2\Lambda^2}F'(0)\sum_i x_i^3
+O(\Lambda^{-4}).
$$
The Newton relation $\sum_i x_i^3=S^3-3S(S^2-T)/2+3P$ follows by summing $p(x_i)=0$. Thus the order-$\Lambda^{-2}$ slope in $P$ is $t^6 F'(0)/(2\pi^2\Lambda^2)$. At finite cutoff the exact slope samples $F'$ across the three masses.

For $F(u)=F_0 e^{-u}$ with $F_0>0$, $I(v)=F_0e^{-v}$ and $F'(0)=-F_0$. The truncated and the exact forces have the same strictly negative sign at nonzero $t$, and the exact slope divided by the truncated one is $e^{-\lambda\xi}$. A flat control is $F(u)=F_0$ for $0\le u\le1$ and $F_0\exp(-(u-1)^2)$ for $u>1$. This is $C^1$. With $c=1+\sqrt\pi/2$, integration of $F$ and $uF$ gives $I'(0)=-F_0c$ and $I(0)=F_0c$. For $0\le v\le1$, $I(v)=F_0[c(1-v)+v^2/2]$ exactly. When $\lambda x_3\le1$ across the branch, its entire trace is fixed by $S,T$ and the order-$\Lambda^{-2}$ coefficient vanishes.

## The wall triplet and Higgs minimum

**Specified action.** Place a real flavour triplet $y=(y_1,y_2,y_3)$ on one flat interval wall. Use the induced four-dimensional kinetic density $f^2\sum_i(\partial y_i)^2/2$ with $f>0$, and the canonical Higgs kinetic density $|D\mathbf H|^2/2$ with $t=|\mathbf H|$. The wall Yukawa block is $Y_e(y)=\sqrt{s}\,\operatorname{diag}(y_1,y_2,y_3)$ with fixed $s>0$, in the convention $m_i=t\sqrt{s}\,|y_i|$. It is diagonal in the marked flavour basis. Two auxiliary wall scalars $\sigma,\tau$ enter the potential through
$$
\sigma\left(\sum_i y_i^2-9\right)+\tau\left(\sum_i y_i^4-51\right)
$$
in units $F_0\Lambda^4/\pi^2$. Their field equations enforce both moment constraints pointwise. With $z_i=y_i^2$, the resulting spectra have $S=9s$, $T=51s^2$ and $P=s^3\prod_i z_i$. There is no flavour mixing variable in this action. The constraints have independent gradients because the nonzero $z_i$ take at least two different values throughout this family. The wall triplet gives a regular local field description even at a vanishing Yukawa or a repeated root.

From the endpoint formulas with $a/s=3$, $b/s=2$, the closure has $0\le P/s^3\le7$. Its endpoint spectra in $z$ are $(0,(9-\sqrt{21})/2,(9+\sqrt{21})/2)$ and $(1,1,7)$. Use $q=P/(7s^3)$ and $w=s t^2/\Lambda^2$. After enforcing the constraints, take the dimensionless potential to be
$$
U(w,q)=\frac{A}{2}(w-w_0)^2+\frac{B}{2}q^2+Jq+E(w,q),
\qquad E(w,q)=\sum_i e^{-w z_i(q)} .
$$
This potential includes the exact exponential charged trace and a prescribed radial polynomial and wall potential. The action's independent inputs are the two wall constraint levels, the Yukawa normalization $s$, the wall kinetic scale $f$, the cutoff profile amplitude $F_0$ and cutoff scale $\Lambda$, the radial coefficients $A,w_0$, and the wall coefficients $B,J$. The Higgs kinetic normalization defines $t$. The multiplier normalizations can be absorbed into $\sigma,\tau$, and a field-independent energy shift drops out. The example fixes
$$
\begin{gathered}
s=10^{-3},\quad f=\Lambda=10\ {\rm GeV},\quad F_0=20,\\
A=1800,\quad w_0=1/200,\quad B=10^{-4},\quad
J=-B/2+\eta,\quad |\eta|\le B/4 .
\end{gathered}
$$
These are stipulated inputs of an effective wall theory. The central example has $\eta=0$. The $Jq$ term is allowed by sign changes and permutations of the wall scalars. The constraints and field symmetries leave its coefficient independent of $B$. Neither lepton masses nor the measured charge enter these choices.

**Proposition.** For the specified coefficients and every displayed $\eta$, the minimum of $U$ over $w\ge0$ and $0\le q\le1$ is broken and has positive, distinct charged-lepton masses. The constrained two-field Hessian is positive definite. The vacuum is unique in $(w,q)$, with at most discrete sign and permutation copies in the triplet, and $P_*$ decreases strictly as $J$ increases.

**Radial equation and endpoint comparison.** The stationary equations in the interior are
$$
A(w-w_0)=\sum_i z_i e^{-w z_i},\qquad Bq+J+E_q(w,q)=0 .
$$
For each $q$, $U_w(0,q)=-Aw_0-9<0$ and $U_{ww}=A+\sum_i z_i^2 e^{-w z_i}>0$. The right side of the first equation is positive and less than $9$ at every $w>0$, so its unique solution $w(q)$ lies between $w_0$ and $w_+=w_0+9/A=1/100$. At $t=0$ the radial energy decreases for small positive $t$. This excludes the origin from the vacuum comparison.

To compute the shape derivatives, let $p_q(Z)=Z^3-9Z^2+15Z-7q$ and $g(Z)=e^{-w Z}$. The root derivative gives $E_q=7[z_1,z_2,z_3]g'$. For the second derivative, write the first divided difference expression using the residue identity
$$
E_q=\frac{7}{2\pi i}\oint \frac{g'(Z)}{p_q(Z)}\,dZ .
$$
The contour encloses the three roots and can be held fixed. Differentiating gives a denominator $p_q(Z)^2$ and numerator factor $49$. The resulting residues give the divided difference $E_{qq}=49[z_1,z_1,z_2,z_2,z_3,z_3]g'$, with confluent nodes. These formulas extend across the endpoints. The general divided-difference mean-value formula, obtained by the same interpolation and Rolle argument with repeated nodes taken by continuity, gives
$$
-7 w^3/2\le E_q<0,\qquad E_{qq}\ge0.
$$
For the second inequality the fifth derivative of $g'$ is $w^6e^{-w Z}$, with divisor $5!$ in the mean-value formula. Differentiating $E_q$ in $w$ uses the second $Z$ derivative of $\partial_w g'$,
$$
\partial_Z^2 \partial_w g'=w^2(wZ-3)e^{-w Z}.
$$
Here $0\le wz_i\le7/100$. Consequently $E_{wq}<0$ and $|E_{wq}|\le 21w_+^2/2$ at $w=w(q)$. This is the mixed Higgs and flavour entry in $(w,q)$.

Let $R(q)=U(w(q),q)$. Differentiating $U_w=0$ gives $w'(q)=-U_{wq}/U_{ww}$, and
$$
R'=Bq+J+E_q,\qquad R''=B+E_{qq}-\frac{E_{wq}^2}{A+E_{ww}}.
$$
Thus $R''\ge B-(21 w_+^2/2)^2/A>0$. At $q=0$ the inward derivative is negative because $J\le-B/4$. At $q=1$ the slope in increasing $q$ is at least $B/4-7 w_+^3/2>0$. This makes both endpoint energies strictly larger than at the unique zero of $R'$. The endpoint comparison applies on the signed triplet manifold. At the zero-mass end $q=(15/7)y_1^2+O(y_1^4)$ in a local chart using $y_1$. At the double-root end $1-q=(72/7)\theta^2+O(\theta^4)$ using the circle angle extended past the fold. Both ends admit energy-lowering shape perturbations.

**Hessian and variation of the wall coupling.** At the interior solution the constrained Hessian in $(w,q)$ is
$$
\begin{pmatrix} A+E_{ww} & E_{wq}\\ E_{wq} & B+E_{qq}\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its positive radial entry and positive Schur complement $R''$ give positive definiteness. The coordinate change to the Higgs norm and wall tangent is nonsingular there. In particular $dw/dt=2st/\Lambda^2>0$ and $dy_i/dq=7/(2y_i p_q'(z_i))$ in a fixed signed chart. The restricted wall kinetic coefficient $f^2\sum_i(dy_i/dq)^2$ is positive, so the Hessian also gives positive squared masses in the two scalar directions with the specified kinetic terms. Differentiating $R'(q_*;J)=0$ yields
$$
\frac{dq_*}{dJ}=-\frac{1}{R''(q_*)}<0 .
$$
The determinant therefore varies even though the cutoff, both wall constraints, and all other displayed coefficients are held fixed. $\square$

The radial, mixed and shape entries of the dimensionless Hessian are $1847.8438$, $-9.5677706\times10^{-4}$ and $1.0000000\times10^{-4}$. Here $R''=9.9999505\times10^{-5}$. ## Thomson matching at the selected minimum

**One-loop prescription.** Set the common QED matching scale to $\mu_*=5$ GeV, above the three selected lepton thresholds. As a declared boundary condition identify the matched $\overline{\rm MS}$ inverse charge $A_*$ with the tree gauge normalization from issue 20 at $f_0=F_0$. Thus $g_Y^2=3\pi^2/(10F_0)$, $g_2^2=\pi^2/(2F_0)$, and $A_*=4\pi(g_Y^{-2}+g_2^{-2})=64F_0/(3\pi)$. This prescription sets the common finite ultraviolet conversion to zero at the matching scale. Hold the additive hadronic vacuum-polarization contribution to the inverse charge at the illustrative value $\mathcal H=1$. The boundary condition and this fixed input specify the numerical comparison.

For one unit-charge Dirac fermion the zero-momentum one-loop polarization gives the inverse-charge decoupling difference
$$
A_{n-1}(\mu)-A_n(\mu)
=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^1 du\,u(1-u)\log\frac{\mu^2}{m^2}
=\frac{2}{3\pi}\log\frac{\mu}{m}
$$
at the order used here. It vanishes at $\mu=m$. Between thresholds $dA_n/d\log\mu=-2n/(3\pi)$ for the lepton contribution, where $n$ counts active unit-charge Dirac species. Integrate first with $n=3$ to the heaviest mass, then with $n=2$ to the middle mass and with $n=1$ to the lightest. Below the lightest threshold add the fixed hadronic term to obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{\ell,\mathrm{IR}}&= A_*+\frac{2}{3\pi}
\left(3\log\frac{\mu_*}{m_3}+2\log\frac{m_3}{m_2}+\log\frac{m_2}{m_1}\right),\\
\alpha(0)^{-1}&= A_{\ell,\mathrm{IR}}+\mathcal H .
\end{aligned}
$$
The three logarithms combine to $\sum_i\log(\mu_*/m_i)$. Since $m_1m_2m_3=t^3\sqrt P$, comparison at the same Higgs norm and other matching inputs gives $\Delta\alpha(0)^{-1}=\log(P_{\rm ref}/P)/(3\pi)$. If both radial solutions are used instead, the difference also contains $(2/\pi)\log(t_{\rm ref}/t)$. The massless boundary is outside the three-positive-threshold prescription. [QED matching and beta coefficients, equations (6)–(11)](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.03265#S3)

With the tree hypercharge projection $B_Y=g_Yt_*/2$, the example gives $\alpha(0)B_Y=43.584568$ MeV and $\alpha(0)B_Y/H_N=0.139260$. The observed $H_N=(m_p+m_n)/6=312.972919$ MeV is supplied separately. [Nucleon mass input and electromagnetic conventions](19.md)

**Perturbative uncertainty.** The table and determinant check use tree masses and continuous one-loop charge matching. The pure-lepton two-loop term in $dA_n/d\log\mu$ is $-n\alpha/(2\pi^2)$. Inserting the one-loop charge along each interval gives the sum
$$
\delta A_{\beta,2}=\frac{3}{4\pi}\log\frac{A_{\ell,\mathrm{IR}}}{A_*}.
$$
For matching at a running $\overline{\rm MS}$ mass evaluated at its own scale, the two-loop term in $\alpha_{n-1}/\alpha_n$ at vanishing matching logarithm is $-13\alpha_n^2/(48\pi^2)$ for one lepton. Its inverse-charge contribution is $+13\alpha_n/(48\pi^2)$. Using the tree masses as leading inputs in this scheme gives $+0.001566$ from the running term and $+0.000603$ from the three finite decouplings. These quantify two pure-lepton truncation effects in the absolute inverse charge. Replacing the tree mass inputs by shifted thresholds gives $\delta\alpha(0)^{-1}=-(2/(3\pi))\sum_i\delta\log m_i$ at first order. Shifts in the ultraviolet and hadronic input enter additively. The physical error budget also requires their uncertainties, mass running and finite mass conversions, and electroweak matching if a pole comparison is made. [QED beta coefficients](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.03265#S3) · [Two-loop decoupling, equations (3.4)–(3.5)](https://arxiv.org/html/1812.04100#S3)

## Status of the selection question

The exponential and flat profiles give the endpoint and flat-direction results at fixed nonzero Higgs norm for the exact flat charged trace. The specified wall action gives a stable isolated determinant and a reproducible one-loop matching consequence conditional on its inputs. Physical selection remains open. The wall construction supplies no independent equation for $J$, and the permitted continuous variation of $J$ changes $P_*$. A physical closure requires a microscopic wall or geometric action that fixes its determinant-dependent coefficients, as well as the ultraviolet and hadronic matching inputs for the physical charge. The observed nucleon mass is a separate conversion in the benchmark. [Issue 25 closure criterion](https://github.com/arivero/conneskk/issues/25#issuecomment-7189862549)

## Reproducing the numerical checks

Run the following Python 3 script with the standard library. From the repository root the code can be extracted and run with `sed -n '/^```python$/,/^```$/p' results/issues/25.md | sed '1d;$d' | python3 -I -`. The shape derivatives are computed by differentiating the power-sum recurrence of the cubic, independent of the confluent divided differences used in the proof. The script also compares the quadrature of each cutoff to its closed form and the sequential threshold sum to the determinant formula.

```python
from math import acos, cos, exp, expm1, factorial, fsum, isclose, log, pi, prod, sqrt

s, cutoff, amplitude = 0.001, 10.0, 20.0
radial_curvature, w0, wall_curvature = 1800, 1 / 200, 1e-4
mu, hadronic = 5.0, 1.0
pmax = 7
w_max = w0 + 9 / radial_curvature
beta1 = 2 / (3 * pi)
high = 64 * amplitude / (3 * pi)


def spectrum(q):
    if q == 0:
        return 0, (9 - sqrt(21)) / 2, (9 + sqrt(21)) / 2
    if q == 1:
        return 1, 1, 7
    theta = acos((pmax * q + 9) / 16) / 3
    return tuple(3 + 4 * cos(theta + shift) for shift in (2*pi/3, -2*pi/3, 0))


def check_spectrum(q):
    z = spectrum(q)
    assert 0 <= z[0] <= z[1] <= z[2] <= 7
    assert isclose(sum(z), 9) and isclose(sum(x*x for x in z), 51)
    assert isclose(prod(z), pmax*q, abs_tol=1e-13)
    assert isclose(sum(x**3 for x in z), 9**3 - 3*9*15 + 3*pmax*q)


def shape_derivatives(w, q, degree=18):
    powers, first, second = [3, 9, 51], [0, 0, 0], [0, 0, 0]
    determinant = pmax * q
    for n in range(3, degree + 1):
        powers.append(9*powers[n-1] - 15*powers[n-2] + determinant*powers[n-3])
        first.append(9*first[n-1] - 15*first[n-2]
                     + determinant*first[n-3] + pmax*powers[n-3])
        second.append(9*second[n-1] - 15*second[n-2]
                      + determinant*second[n-3] + 2*pmax*first[n-3])
    weights = [(-w)**n / factorial(n) for n in range(degree + 1)]
    eq = fsum(weights[n]*first[n] for n in range(3, degree + 1))
    eqq = fsum(weights[n]*second[n] for n in range(3, degree + 1))
    ewq = fsum(n*weights[n]*first[n]/w for n in range(3, degree + 1))
    return eq, eqq, ewq


def increasing_zero(function, left, right):
    assert function(left) < 0 < function(right)
    for _ in range(60):
        middle = (left + right) / 2
        if function(middle) < 0:
            left = middle
        else:
            right = middle
    return (left + right) / 2


def radial(q):
    z = spectrum(q)
    return increasing_zero(
        lambda w: radial_curvature*(w-w0) - fsum(x*exp(-w*x) for x in z),
        w0, w_max,
    )


def energy(w, q, eta):
    linear = -wall_curvature/2 + eta
    return (radial_curvature*(w-w0)**2/2 + wall_curvature*q*q/2 + linear*q
            + fsum(expm1(-w*x) for x in spectrum(q)))


def solve(eta):
    def reduced_slope(q):
        eq, _, _ = shape_derivatives(radial(q), q)
        return wall_curvature*(q-0.5) + eta + eq

    q = increasing_zero(reduced_slope, 0, 1)
    return radial(q), q


def simpson(function, left, right):
    panels = 8192
    step = (right - left) / panels
    odd = fsum(function(left + k*step) for k in range(1, panels, 2))
    even = fsum(function(left + k*step) for k in range(2, panels, 2))
    return step*(function(left) + 4*odd + 2*even + function(right))/3


assert isclose(w_max, 0.01) and mu > cutoff*sqrt(w_max*7)
assert wall_curvature - (21*w_max**2/2)**2/radial_curvature > 0
assert wall_curvature/4 - pmax*w_max**3/2 > 0
tilts = [-wall_curvature/4, 0, wall_curvature/4]
solutions = [solve(eta) for eta in tilts]
assert solutions[0][1] > solutions[1][1] > solutions[2][1]
for eta, (w, q) in zip(tilts, solutions):
    check_spectrum(q)
    print(f"eta={eta:+.8f}  w={w:.10f}  q={q:.9f}")

w, q = solutions[1]
z = spectrum(q)
determinant = pmax*s**3*q
t = cutoff*sqrt(w/s)
eq, eqq, ewq = shape_derivatives(w, q)
for value, more_terms in zip((eq, eqq, ewq), shape_derivatives(w, q, 24)):
    assert isclose(value, more_terms, rel_tol=1e-12)
root_dq = [pmax / prod(x-other for j, other in enumerate(z) if i != j)
           for i, x in enumerate(z)]
assert isclose(eq, fsum(-w*exp(-w*x)*dx for x, dx in zip(z, root_dq)),
               rel_tol=1e-10)
```
