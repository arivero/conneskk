# From elliptic holonomy centralizers to the spin-stabilizer algebras

**Proposition.** Let $E$ be an elliptic curve with origin and its principal polarization, $L$ the $A_2$ coroot lattice, and $A=L\otimes E$. A point of $A$ specifies a maximal-torus-framed flat $SU(3)$ connection on $E$. There is a stratum-by-stratum construction of the algebra of Lemma 49 and a Weyl-equivariant isogeny $A\to\operatorname{Pic}^0(A)$ supplying a flat line on the four-torus.

**Proof.** Write $z=(z_1,z_2,z_3)$, $\sum z_i=0$. Via $E\simeq\operatorname{Pic}^0(E)$ the three flat characters have trivial product. Commuting unitary holonomies are simultaneously diagonalizable, and the unframed moduli space is the space of effective divisors $z_1+z_2+z_3\sim3[0]$, namely $|3[0]|\cong\mathbb{CP}^2$. Under the plane cubic embedding this linear system is its dual plane: repeated-point divisors are the tangent lines, forming the dual sextic. Its nine cusps correspond to flexes, the divisors $3[z]$ with $3z=0$. Indeed a double intersection imposes tangency, a triple one gives third-order contact; the degree of the dual smooth cubic is $3(3-1)=6$. The coarse quotient is smooth at the orbifold strata. The $SU(2)$ calculation gives divisors $z+(-z)$ in $|2[0]|=\mathbb{CP}^1$, with four central pairs $z\in E[2]$ and generic torus centralizer. [Friedman–Morgan–Witten, §§2–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/9701162)

Let $C_z$ be the compact holonomy centralizer, $T$ its framed diagonal maximal torus. Then $C_z=S(\prod U(m_i))$ and $N_{C_z}(T)/T=\prod S_{m_i}$, where $m_i$ are coincidence multiplicities. Take this finite group's action on $T_0A$, its preimage in $\mathrm{Spin}(4)$ for the periodic spin bundle, and the real star algebra it generates on $\Sigma_4$. On the generic stratum the lifts are $\{\pm1\}$ and the algebra is $\mathbb R$. On a mirror the transposition lifts to $x$ with $x^2=-1$, giving $\mathbb C$. At a triple point the binary dihedral group acts through a complex character on one chirality and irreducibly of quaternionic type on the other, giving $\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb H$. Thus the unitary groups are $\{\pm1\}$, $U(1)$, and $U(1)\times SU(2)$. Changing the framing conjugates the construction. In the spin crossed product these are the represented isotropy algebras. The continuous gauge group of the flat connection on $E$ is $C_z$ itself. A surjective homomorphism $SU(3)\to U(1)\times SU(2)$ is impossible by simplicity and dimensions at Lie algebra level. The construction instead takes a normalizer quotient and a spin representation. [Spinor representation proof](../../lemmas/49_spinor_fibre_is_the_quaternionic_sheet.md)

In a simple-coroot basis normalize the root length squared to two. The integral Cartan pairing $L\to L^*$, tensored with the polarization of $E$, is
$$A\longrightarrow \widehat A,\qquad (u,v)\longmapsto(2u-v,\,2v-u).$$
Its kernel consists of $(u,2u)$ with $3u=0$, precisely the central triples in coordinates $(u,-u+v,-v)$. It has degree nine and is Weyl-equivariant. Pulling back the Poincaré bundle on $A\times\widehat A$ along this map assigns the desired flat character on $A$ to each elliptic holonomy tuple. It has a flat unitary representative, defined modulo the integral dual lattice; assigning that character to the Dirac operator in Lemma 51 is an explicit coupling choice. The nine central elliptic tuples give the same trivial character on $A$. Multiples of the pairing give different isogenies, so the pairing normalization matters to the assigned Wilson line. $\square$ [Four-torus Wilson operator](../../lemmas/51_torus_wilson_line_and_torsion_yukawa.md)
