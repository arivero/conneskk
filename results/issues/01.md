# Dimension, boundary spinors and weak symmetry in the interpolation

Fix the charge lattice and distinguish the radius of the collapsing fibre, the length of the surviving axial direction, and the energy of the probe. The observables are the heat trace, the surviving real Dirac module, the four-dimensional chiral representation and the rank of its massless gauge group. The nine calculations below use the numbering of §9 of the revised issue.

## 1. Dimension in the two-scale reduction

We use **spectral dimension** for the intermediate regime of the football. Let $K(t)=\mathrm{Tr}\exp(-t\Delta)$ for the scalar Laplacian and $d_s=-2\,d\log K/d\log t$. In the energy band where five base directions and one axial tower are resolved and nonaxial harmonics are frozen, the product with four flat spacetime directions has $d_s\simeq4+5+1$. This requires a band between the axial and nonaxial gaps, and a probe above the base curvature scale. For example, the axisymmetric tower of a football of radius $R$ has eigenvalues $l(l+1)/R^2$, $l\ge0$, and the first nonaxial gap is of order $q/R$ for $q\gg1$. On a round sphere with one shrinking radius the slope passes through 10 at a single energy during the crossover. For each positive smooth radius the metric dimension counts all geometric coordinates, while the high-energy fibre contribution of a finite truncation is zero. Real KO-dimension is determined separately by the signs of the antiunitary and grading. A Weyl parent on a six-dimensional internal manifold has genuine spacetime dimension ten. ## 2. A real chiral cylinder domain

**Proposition.** Let $D_5$ be the self-adjoint spin Dirac operator of a closed spin five-manifold, with $J_5^2=-1$ and $J_5D_5=-D_5J_5$. On the two copies of its spin bundle over $K_5\times[0,\ell]$ take
$$D=\sigma_1 D_5-i\sigma_2\partial_s,\qquad \gamma=\sigma_3,\qquad J=\sigma_2\,\operatorname{diag}(J_5,J_5).$$
There is a self-adjoint domain with compact resolvent, invariant under $J$ and $\gamma$, on which $J^2=1$, $JD=DJ$ and $J\gamma=-\gamma J$. Whenever $\ker D_5$ is nonzero, the finite zero sector realizes these signs on a pair of opposite chiralities.

**Proof.** Put $B=\sigma_3D_5$. At $s=0$ allow only $B<0$ among the nonzero modes, and at $s=\ell$ allow only $B>0$. Write the quaternionic zero space of $D_5$ as the orthogonal sum $L\oplus J_5L$. At both ends allow the upper component in $L$ and the lower in $J_5L$. Take the $H^1$ domain with these trace conditions. The normal matrix $-i\sigma_2$ exchanges the two signs of $B$ and pairs the upper and lower zero sectors. The allowed subspace at each end is its own annihilator for the Green form. The two symmetries preserve the conditions, since they commute with $B$ and $J$ exchanges the allowed upper and lower spaces. The matrix identities give the stated KO signs and $D\gamma=-\gamma D$.

In the factorization $D=(-i\sigma_2)(\partial_s+B)$, integration by parts gives
$$\|D\psi\|^2=\|\partial_s\psi\|^2+\|B\psi\|^2+
   [\langle\psi,B\psi\rangle]_{0}^{\ell}.$$
The last term is nonnegative. On each finite $|D_5|$ eigenspace the separated first-order problem is self-adjoint by the Green form. Their orthogonal sum is self-adjoint. The estimate and tangential elliptic regularity identify its graph domain with the stated $H^1$ domain, which embeds compactly in $L^2$. Hence its kernel consists precisely of constant upper $L$ and lower $J_5L$. Periodic flat $T^5$ supplies a nonempty example. For ordinary untwisted spinors positive scalar curvature gives an empty harmonic spinor space on round $S^5$. The twisted operator of §4 gives a charged round example. $\square$

This domain uses tangential spectral projections. An algebra of boundary observables preserving it and satisfying order one would require a further construction. Lemma 23 instead freely specifies two matrix gradings and their relative angles. The smooth caps of a bounding-spin football fix the two polar traces by the angular label and flux. These three domain choices give different mass spectra. [Two-gradings construction](../../lemmas/23_two_gradings_interval.md) · [Cap conditions](02.md)

## 3. The lowest sphere sector

For ordinary scalar functions the round sphere has a one-dimensional constant space. Its untwisted spinor Dirac operator has first eigenvalues $\pm1/R$ and empty kernel. The product spin geometry on $K_5\times S^2$ has internal KO-degree $5+2=7$ mod 8. Restricting a real Dirac module to a finite invariant subspace preserves its inherited signs. Projecting onto one charged sector alone can lose the real structure.

For positive monopole degree $N$ the intrinsic spin zero space is $H^0(\mathbb{CP}^1,\mathcal O(N-1))$, the irreducible representation of spin $(N-1)/2$. For $N=2$ this is a complex doublet. Compressions of multiplication by complex functions span $M_2(\mathbb C)$ on it. Indeed normalized evaluation vectors run over all complex lines in $\mathbb C^2$, whose rank-one projectors span the matrices. Integration against test functions has this same span by finite dimensionality and approximation of point masses. The doublet admits the equivariant antiunitary $j(z_1,z_2)=(-\bar z_2,\bar z_1)$. The **complex-linear matrices commuting with $j$** form a real copy of $\mathbb H$. Specifying that real algebra and retaining conjugate charge data are extra operations. Charge conjugation of the monopole coefficients takes degree $N$ to $-N$. The projective product in §8 and the spectral cylinder of §4 realize the full finite module with specified extra coefficient actions. ## 4. Matching at the nine-dimensional end

For the Einstein-term Berger $S^5$ with radius $R$, Hopf squashing $\eta>0$, fixed coefficient $C_9$ and the singlet charge unit,
$$\frac{e^2}{g_s^2}=\frac{3}{32}\left(1+\frac{3}{\eta^2}\right).$$
Impose the inherited relative spectral normalization $g_3^2=g_2^2=(5/3)g_Y^2$ at matching and $e^{-2}=g_2^{-2}+g_Y^{-2}$. It gives $\sin^2\theta_W=3/8$, $\cos^2\theta_W=5/8$ and $e^2/g_s^2=3/8$. This single equation on $(R,\eta)$ selects $\eta=1$ for every $R>0$. Both squared matching couplings then vary as $R^{-7}$. If the cutoff moment $f_0$ is independently fixed as an **absolute** normalization as well, $R^7=12 f_0/(C_9\pi^5)$. The weak angle is inherited at the matching scale. The round isometry is enhanced to $SO(6)$, so retaining just the specified subgroup requires a projection or breaking sector. This calculation uses tree-level coefficients, zero matching thresholds and no fitted $\alpha$, $G_F$ or strong scale. The interval calculation gives an explicit one-loop, threshold-matched pure-glue convention. Comparing to five-flavour QCD or to a nucleon mass requires different inputs. ### A radius selected by a quantized five-form

**Proposition.** Fix the nine-dimensional Einstein coefficient, a flux unit $\Phi$ and a nonzero integer $N$. In the bosonic action
$$
S=\frac{1}{2\kappa_9^2}\int\sqrt{|g|}\left(\mathcal R-2\Lambda-\frac{1}{2\cdot5!}F_{M_1\ldots M_5}F^{M_1\ldots M_5}\right)
$$
take an unwarped Berger $S^5$ with $\int_{S^5}F_5=N\Phi=\pi^3 Q$. At $\Lambda=0$ there is one homogeneous stationary point with relative subgroup normalization $e^2/g_3^2=3/8$. It has
$$
\eta=1,\qquad R^8=\frac{3Q^2}{56},\qquad
g_3^{-2}=\frac{\pi^3 R^7}{12\kappa_9^2}\,\frac{10}{3},\qquad
e^{-2}=\frac83\,g_3^{-2}.
$$
The Hessian in the breathing and squashing directions is positive. Here $e$ is the central-circle coupling in the spinor charge convention. The four-dimensional product factor is anti-de Sitter.

**Proof.** Write the metric as $R^2(g_{\rm FS}+\eta^2\theta^2)$ on the Hopf circle over $\mathbb{CP}^2$, with $\mathrm{Ric}_{\rm FS}=6g_{\rm FS}$ and $d\theta=2\omega$. Its scalar curvature is $(24-4\eta^2)/R^2$ by the circle submersion formula, and its volume is $\pi^3R^5\eta$. Set $b=R\eta^{1/5}$, $h=\log\eta$ and $F_5=f\,\mathrm{vol}_5$ with $f=Q/b^5$. The Bianchi and Maxwell equations hold. Passing to the four-dimensional Einstein metric $g_E=b^5g_4$ in fixed reference-length units gives the potential in the convention $\mathcal R_E-U$,
$$
U=b^{-5}\left[2\Lambda-4b^{-2}e^{2h/5}(6-e^{2h})+\tfrac12Q^2b^{-10}\right].
$$
Its $h$ derivative is $(48/5)b^{-7}e^{2h/5}(e^{2h}-1)$, so every stationary shape is round. At $h=0$ the radial stationarity equation is $4\Lambda b^{10}+3Q^2-56b^8=0$. For $\Lambda=0$ this gives the stated radius and the Hessian in $(\log b,h)$ is $R^{-7}\mathrm{diag}(1120,96/5)$. Direct substitution in the nine-dimensional Einstein equations gives $\mathrm{Ric}_4=-16g_4/(3R^2)$, hence $L_{\rm AdS}^2=9R^2/16$ in the product metric. The Einstein-frame squared radius acquires the constant factor $b^5$ in the stated units. Requiring a flat four-dimensional factor instead imposes $\Lambda=8/R^2$ and $R^8=Q^2/8$; this tunes the cosmological coefficient to the flux sector. Its two Hessian entries are $320/R^7$ and $96/(5R^7)$.

For the gauge kinetic term, gauge a round Killing field $K$ by a connection $A$. Let $k=K^\flat$. The variation of $f\,\mathrm{vol}_5(dy+K A)$ in the Bianchi identity is cancelled at linear order by $-dA\wedge\omega_K$, with $d\omega_K=f*_5k$. The linear Maxwell equation imposes $\delta\omega_K=0$. Since $\delta dk=8k/R^2$, the solution is $\omega_K=(fR^2/8)*_5dk$. Vanishing of $H^3(S^5)$ and $H^4(S^5)$ gives existence and uniqueness of the co-closed primitive. Integration by parts gives $\int|\omega_K|^2=(f^2R^2/8)\int|K|^2$. The metric kinetic term and five-form kinetic term contribute these two norms with the same prefactor. In charge-connection coordinates the coefficient of $-F^a_{\mu\nu}F^{b\,\mu\nu}/4$ is therefore
$$
(g^{-2})_{ab}=\frac{1+f^2R^2/8}{2\kappa_9^2}
            \int_{S^5}g(K_a,K_b)\,dV .
$$
A constant Weyl rescaling in four dimensions preserves this coefficient. The factor is $10/3$ for $\Lambda=0$ and $2$ on the flat solution. Inserting the round generator norms of Lemma 47, with $\mathrm{tr}_3T^2=1/2$ and circle charge unit $2/3$, gives the claimed couplings. $\square$ [Freund and Rubin, flux compactification](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-2693(80)90590-0) · [Barnes et al., metric and form-field gauge terms in supergravity](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0507146) · [Einstein-term generator norms](../47_berger_radius_at_matching.md)

At fixed microscopic inputs the shape and radius are isolated before any fit to $\alpha$, $G_F$ or a strong scale. Varying the flux unit or $\kappa_9$ changes the predicted dimensionless charge. This is a quadratic, tree-level calculation for the massless vectors, with zero loop thresholds. Full stability requires the other Kaluza–Klein modes. The round isometry has centralizer $U(1)$ for the colour $SU(3)$, so a separate weak sector is required to read the subgroup ratio as $\sin^2\theta_W$. To obtain $G_F$, $\alpha(0)$ and a hadronic mass one still needs a charged-current vacuum, the charged spectrum, threshold matching and low-energy colour dynamics.

### The colour and electromagnetic zero modes at the round endpoint

**Proposition.** On the round Berger endpoint $S^5_R$, let $S$ be the rank-four complex spin bundle with its Levi-Civita connection. Give a second copy $K$ the Killing spinor connection $\nabla^K_X=\nabla^S_X-c(X)/(2R)$, with $c(X)^2=-|X|^2$. Choose the orientation and sign for which the parallel spinors transform as the $\mathbf4$ of $\mathrm{Spin}(6)=SU(4)$. Twist the ordinary spin Dirac operator by $S^*\otimes\Lambda^\bullet K$, with the dual Levi-Civita and induced exterior connections. Its kernel as a $\mathrm{Spin}(6)$ representation and its first positive singular value are
$$
 W_9=\Lambda^\bullet\mathbf4,\qquad \operatorname{gap}=\sqrt5/R .
$$
For the charge unit of §4 this kernel is equivariantly isomorphic to one generation, written with all fermions left-handed and restricted to colour and electromagnetism.

**Proof.** The Killing connection is unitary and flat at the round radius. Equivalently its four parallel spinors are the restrictions of constant spinors of one chirality on the flat six-dimensional cone. Parallel trivialization separates $\Lambda^\bullet K$ from the remaining operator on $\operatorname{End}S$. The complex Clifford bundle in dimension five has two simple ideals. For an oriented orthonormal frame put $\Omega=i^3 c(e_1)\cdots c(e_5)$ and $p_\pm=(1\pm\Omega)/2$. Clifford action identifies one ideal with $\operatorname{End}S$, preserving the Levi-Civita connections. On the full Clifford bundle, identified as a vector bundle with complex forms, left Clifford multiplication followed by the covariant derivative gives $d+d^*$. Right multiplication by $p_\pm$ commutes with this operator. The only harmonic forms are the constants and the multiples of the volume form. Their projections give one line per ideal. On the unit sphere the first positive scalar eigenvalue is five. For coexact forms of degrees $0\le p\le4$ the positive eigenvalues are $(k+p)(k+4-p)$ with $k\ge1$. Exact forms have the positive eigenvalues of the preceding coexact degree. The minimum five occurs on each ideal because a first scalar harmonic multiplied by $p_\pm$ is a nonzero eigenvector of the square. Rescaling proves the gap. [Bär, Killing spinors on the sphere](https://www.math.uni-potsdam.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Prof-Geometrie/Dokumente/Publikationen/sphere.pdf) · [Ikeda and Taniguchi, Theorem 4.2](https://doi.org/10.18910/6956)

Write $\mathbf4=\mathbf3_{-1/3}\oplus\mathbf1_{+1}$ for the lifted $U(3)$. Exterior degrees zero and four give two neutral singlets, degree one gives the displayed $\mathbf4$, degree three its conjugate, and degree two gives $\overline{\mathbf3}_{-2/3}\oplus\mathbf3_{+2/3}$. Thus the charged pairs are precisely the up triplet, down triplet and electron with their conjugates. Schur's lemma gives the freedom in a unitary identification with the all-left charge module of §8. Once a first identification is fixed, the others form $U(1)^6\times U(2)$, with the last factor on the two neutrals. A chosen conjugation or weak action gives further restrictions. $\square$

The twist accounts for the even values of $3Q$ in this calculation. One Hopf turn rotates all three coordinate planes and lifts to $-1$ on an ordinary spinor. Thus its Hopf weights lie in $\mathbb Z+1/2$, and $3Q$ in the present normalization is twice that weight. In $\operatorname{End}S$ the two signs cancel. The turn then acts by $(-1)^k$ on exterior degree $k$ of $K$. The signed antilinear Hodge star $(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}\star$ from the invariant Hermitian form and volume on $\mathbf4$ squares to $+1$ and pairs the conjugate sectors of $W_9$. With one four-dimensional Weyl field for each kernel entry, a pairing of the neutrals gives eight Dirac fields counted with colour. A complex nine-dimensional Dirac parent supplies both spacetime chiralities for each entry. A physical reduction to that Weyl count requires an additional fermion condition.

**Selecting the unbroken group.** A parallel rank-one Hermitian projector $P$ on $K$ selects a line in the same $\mathbf4$. Its connected stabilizer is $S(U(3)\times U(1))$. In a reduced adjoint scalar action take $P^\dagger=P$, $P^2=P$, $\mathrm{tr}\,P=1$ and a kinetic term $(f_P^2/2)\,\mathrm{tr}(D_\mu P D^\mu P)$. For $P=\mathrm{diag}(0,0,0,1)$ the six off-diagonal generators mixing the last coordinate with the first three have $\mathrm{tr}([T,P]^\dagger[T,P])=1/2$ in the normalization $\mathrm{tr}(T^2)=1/2$. With a canonically normalized gauge field and coupling $g$, their squared mass is $g^2 f_P^2/2$. These six real vector directions transform as $\mathbf3_{-4/3}\oplus\overline{\mathbf3}_{+4/3}$ and change colour. The other nine generators commute with $P$. This gives an endpoint with the required unbroken Lie algebra and both signs of the electric charges at the flux-selected radius. The twisting connection and projector action are specified inputs. How the higher-dimensional dynamics chooses them and evolves the chiral product of §8 into this endpoint remains open.

### The cylinder over the same endpoint

**Proposition.** The preceding five-sphere operator occurs in two cylinder reductions with the same charged zero modes and KO-six signs. For one choice of local walls the constant axial channel contains the whole five-sphere spectrum. For the spectral walls of §2 only the finite kernel stays below a fixed energy as $\ell\to0$.

**Proof.** Let $D_5$ denote the twisted round operator. At the unbroken group write its parallel coefficient space as $V\oplus\bar V$ with $V=\mathbf3_{2/3}\oplus\mathbf3_{-1/3}\oplus\mathbf1_{-1}\oplus\mathbf1_0$, choosing the neutral pair. Let $E$ act by $+1$ on $V$ and $-1$ on $\bar V$, and put $j_F(v,\bar w)=(-w,\bar v)$. These maps are parallel and compatible with colour and electromagnetism. On the Clifford ideal let $j_0$ be form-degree parity after complex conjugation. Each operation exchanges the volume idempotents, so their product preserves the ideal, squares to $+1$ and anticommutes with $d+d^*$. Then $j_5=j_0\otimes j_F$ has $j_5^2=-1$, $j_5D_5=-D_5j_5$ and $j_5E=-Ej_5$. The quaternionic choice uses the selected subgroup.

Take two copies of the coefficient space with Pauli matrices $\tau_i$ and ordinary conjugation in the copy basis. Let $\sigma_i$ act on the two axial components and extend $j_5$ to the copies. In both models use $\gamma=\sigma_3$, $J=\sigma_2 j_5$. At a tangential zero the allowed boundary values are upper $L=V_1\oplus\bar V_2$ and lower $j_5L=\bar V_1\oplus V_2$ at both ends. The constant solutions give 32 states with $J^2=1$, $JD=DJ$ and $J\gamma=-\gamma J$.

For the local problem set
$$
 D_{\rm loc}=\sigma_1\tau_1D_5-i\sigma_2\partial_s,\qquad
 B_{\partial}=\sigma_3\tau_3 E,\qquad B_{\partial}\psi=\psi\quad(s=0,\ell).
$$
The two copies use opposite extensions of the odd-dimensional Clifford action, written in the basis in which $\tau_1$ exchanges them. The tangential term commutes with $B_{\partial}$ and the normal matrix anticommutes with it. The allowed boundary space is its own annihilator for the Green form and is preserved by $J,\gamma$. For $D_{\rm loc}^2=D_5^2-\partial_s^2$ the positive boundary sector has Neumann conditions and the negative sector has Dirichlet conditions. Cosines and sines give complete separated blocks on which the first-order operator is Hermitian. Their orthogonal sum has the stated $H^1$ trace domain, by the sum of squares, and is self-adjoint. If $\lambda$ is a five-sphere eigenvalue, the squared energies are $\lambda^2+(n\pi/\ell)^2$. At $n=0$ only the positive boundary sector is present. On it $\sigma_1\tau_1$ exchanges the two allowed components for each $E$-sector, giving $D_5\oplus(-D_5)$ up to unitary equivalence. At every fixed energy this doubled five-dimensional operator is exactly the surviving channel when $\ell$ is small enough. The complementary resolvent at $i$ has norm at most $\ell/\pi$. The gap above the 32 zero modes is $\min(\sqrt5/R,\pi/\ell)$.

For the spectral walls use $D_{\rm sp}=\sigma_1D_5-i\sigma_2\partial_s$ on the same copies and the nonzero-mode boundary conditions of §2. For $\lambda\ne0$, each axial component vanishes at one end. The one-end Poincaré bound and the nonnegative boundary form of §2 give squared energy at least $\lambda^2+\pi^2/(4\ell^2)$. On the tangential kernel the first nonconstant axial modes have energy $\pi/\ell$. This proves the stated difference in the two reductions. $\square$

**Finite algebra and masses.** Identify $V_1,V_2$ with left and right particles and their conjugates with the corresponding antiparticles. On the spectral-wall cylinder the representation of $\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb H\oplus M_3(\mathbb C)$ from §8 acts by parallel coefficient matrices preserving $L$ and $j_5L$. It preserves the full domain and commutes with $D_{\rm sp}$. Let a Hermitian $Y_p$ link $V_1$ to $V_2$ by the four scalar Yukawa masses of §8, acting as the identity on each colour factor. Extend it on the conjugates by $-j_F Y_p j_F^{-1}$ and add $\sigma_1Y$ to $D_{\rm sp}$. The two minus signs under $J$ give $J(\sigma_1Y)J^{-1}=\sigma_1Y$. On the kernel the opposite action is common to the left and right members of each particle mass pair, and the left action is common on each conjugate pair. This proves order zero and order one fibrewise for the specified left and opposite actions, hence also on the cylinder for this constant algebra. The bounded perturbation is self-adjoint on the same domain. It preserves the 32-state subspace, which stays isolated when $\|Y\|<\tfrac12\min(\sqrt5/R,\pi/\ell)$. The finite algebra and Yukawas are supplied data. On the local-wall kernel the same finite matrices can be specified. On the full local-wall cylinder a parallel coefficient matrix $a$ with $[\tau_1,a]\ne0$ contributes $\sigma_1[\tau_1,a]D_5$ to the commutator. Extending the weak algebra there while retaining bounded commutators requires another construction.

## 5. The weak centre and neutral metric modes

**Proposition.** In primitive bundle notation let $M^{pq}=(S^5\times S^3)/U(1)_{(q,-p)}$, with $\gcd(p,q)=1$. The manifold is spin precisely for $p$ odd and $q$ even. In those cases the central $-1$ of the weak $SU(2)$ acts trivially on tensor fields and by $(-1)^{p-3q/2}$ on the ordinary spin bundle.

**Proof.** Over $\mathbb{CP}^2\times\mathbb{CP}^1$ the residual circle has Euler class $ph+qk$ up to orientation, since $(p,q)$ is the primitive character killing $(q,-p)$. The tangent bundle stably pulls back from the base, whose $w_2=h\bmod2$. The mod-two Gysin sequence gives kernel $\langle ph+qk\rangle$ in degree two, proving the spin criterion. The weak centre is the phase pair $(1,-1)$ on the product of spheres. It belongs to the quotient circle exactly for $q$ even (then $p$ is odd). It acts identically on every tensor functor of the tangent. The quotient is simply connected, so a spin structure, when it exists, is unique.

Follow the weak path $\operatorname{diag}(e^{it},e^{-it})$, $0\le t\le\pi$, and compensate by phases $(e^{-iqt},e^{ipt})$ in the circle being divided out. The resulting closed path on $\mathbb C^3\oplus\mathbb C^2$ has five rotation windings with sum
$$-3q/2+(p+1)/2+(p-1)/2=p-3q/2.$$
The two radial normals and the quotient-circle generator give an equivariant framing of the three spare real lines. A $2\pi$ plane rotation lifts to $-1$ in Spin. Thus the parity of this sum gives the spin lift on $TM^{pq}$. The examples $(3,2)$ and $(1,0)$ give $+1$ and $-1$ respectively. This agrees at $(3,2)$ with the canonical-bundle lift in Lemma 32 and the integer weak spins of the $M^{111}$ spectrum. The labels $M^{111}$ of the supergravity literature and $M^{3,2}$ of the primitive bundle refer to the same example. $\square$ [Witten](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(81)90021-3) · [Castellani, D’Auria and Fré](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(84)90265-7) · [Fabbri et al., harmonic analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9903036) · [Bundle and spin lift](../../lemmas/32_witten_spaces_hypercharge_quotient.md)

For $p\ne0$, colour-singlet scalar and symmetric metric perturbations have zero charge under the residual phase circle, called $Y$ in the issue. At a point of $S^5=SU(3)/SU(2)$ the real tangent representation is $\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb C^2$. The invariant one-form is the contact form and the invariant symmetric horizontal form is the round horizontal metric. Both are invariant under the first Hopf phase. Mixed terms with $S^3$ can use only that same invariant one-form. Consequently a colour-invariant tensor of these types has first-phase weight zero. The quotient condition $q m_1-pm_2=0$ then gives $m_2=0$. An axisymmetric neutral vev preserves $T_3$ and $Y$ separately. Several nonaligned neutral vevs can break more of $SU(2)$ while still leaving $Y$ unbroken. Charged coefficient bundles, antisymmetric fields and localized charged matter require their own representation calculation.

## 6. A chiral monopole example and its anomalies

**Proposition.** On $M^4\times S^2$ a specified set of six-dimensional Weyl fermions with a monopole connection gives one four-dimensional anomaly-free generation, including a sterile neutrino. Use one parent chirality, a unit monopole, and coefficient charges $N=2$ for $Q,L$ and $N=1$ for $u^c,d^c,e^c,\nu^c$. Assign the spectator colour representations and hypercharges in the order
$$(\mathbf3,1/6),(\mathbf1,-1/2),(\bar{\mathbf3},-2/3),
(\bar{\mathbf3},1/3),(\mathbf1,1),(\mathbf1,0).$$
The equivariant zero-mode formula of §3 supplies the doublets and singlets. Four-dimensional chirality is fixed by the product of parent chirality and sphere chirality. The cubic colour coefficients are $2-1-1=0$. The mixed colour and weak coefficients are proportional to $2(1/6)-2/3+1/3=0$ and $3(1/6)-1/2=0$. The gravitational hypercharge sum is $1-1-2+1+1=0$. The cubic sum is $1/36-1/4-8/9+1/9+1=0$. The weak doublet count is $3+1=4$, even. $\square$ [Six-dimensional monopole compactification](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(83)90247-X)

The gauge group in this four-dimensional statement is colour, lifted weak rotations and hypercharge. The six same-sign parent fermions alone have an irreducible six-dimensional gravitational anomaly of twelve Weyl units. There is a local cancellation with additional specified fields. Pair each parent fermion with a negative six-dimensional Weyl fermion of the same spectator colour and hypercharge and monopole charge zero. The untwisted round sphere has empty spin kernel, so the added fermions leave the displayed four-dimensional representation unchanged.

**Local six-dimensional anomaly polynomial.** Write $x=c_1(U(1)_{\rm monopole})$, $y=c_1(U(1)_{6Y})$ on the covering product group, $a_i=6Y_i$ and $R_i$ for the spectator colour representation. In integral characteristic-class conventions, the local anomaly of all twelve parent fields is
$$I_8=\left[\widehat A(T)\sum_i \mathrm{ch}(R_i)e^{a_i y}(e^{N_i x}-1)\right]_8
   =x^2\left(-3c_2(SU(3))+36y^2-p_1(T)/2+3x^2\right).$$
Indeed the $x$-linear coefficients are precisely the four-dimensional colour, hypercharge and mixed anomaly sums above. With $d_i=\dim R_i$ one has $\sum d_iN_i^2=24$, $\sum d_iN_i^2a_i^2=144$, $\sum d_iN_i^4=72$ and $\sum d_iN_i^3a_i=0$. The sum of $N_i^2$ over the three coloured species is six and $\mathrm{ch}_2(\mathbf3)=\mathrm{ch}_2(\bar{\mathbf3})=-c_2$. These coefficients give the factorization. For a gauged monopole circle the factorization $I_8=x^2 X_4$ admits a local Green–Schwarz cancellation. An unconstrained two-form $B$ transforms by descent for $x^2$ and couples to $X_4$ with the cancelling sign. Global anomalies, the integral tensor data and any restrictions of a particular supergravity theory still require a calculation. A single chiral tensor has a square anomaly and requires its own factorization test. The background fluxes, spectator charges and two-form coupling are inputs.

On the ten-dimensional route a flag bundle gives chiral colour multiplets, while on $\mathbb{CP}^2\times\mathbb{CP}^1$ the canonical spin$^c$ twist $\mathcal O(-4,1)$ already gives $(\mathbf3,\mathbf2)$ under rotations. The intrinsic index on the conic surface can differ from the ambient twisted kernel. On a smooth, closed seven-dimensional internal space an ordinary eleven-dimensional bulk spinor carries both four-dimensional chiralities for a given internal mode. A boundary, singularity or separate projection changes this last argument. [Bulk and conic calculations](../53_flag_chiral_multiplets.md) · [Localized conic mode](../53_conic_localized_mode.md)

## 7. Fibrewise weak action beyond homogeneous metrics

**Proposition.** An oriented smooth $S^2$ bundle carrying one fixed $SO(3)$ acting fibrewise, transitively and effectively on every fibre is an equivariantly trivial product. The statement also applies to a weak $SU(2)$ with central kernel on each sphere.

**Proof.** Each orbit is $SO(3)/SO(2)$. Local equivariant trivializations exist by the compact-group slice theorem because the transverse slice represents the base tangent, on which the stabilizer acts trivially since the action fixes the base. The changes of equivariant identification of the model sphere belong to $N(SO(2))/SO(2)=\mathbb Z_2$, whose elements act by the identity and the antipodal map. Only the identity preserves the orientation of $S^2$. The local oriented identifications therefore agree globally. $\square$

The nontrivial bundle $N(1,0)\to S^5$ can collapse with a monopole kernel and a nontrivial induced connection. The fixed conic and its tangent jets give global coordinates on the surviving bundle, even though there is no global choice of poles. The theorem excludes an additional single weak rotation group acting transitively along those fibres. A group which moves the base, a fibre-dependent gauge group, or a weak action on kernel multiplicities changes that hypothesis. [Limiting twisted Dirac operator](../53_conic_monopole_window.md)

**Proposition (classification over the Berger base).** Suppose that a smooth surjective submersion with sphere fibres is $SU(3)$-equivariant over the standard transitive colour action on $S^5=SU(3)/SU(2)$. Its equivariant diffeomorphism type is either $S^5\times S^2$ or $SU(3)/U(1)_{(1,-1,0)}$. On the second space every connected weak $SU(2)$ commuting with colour acts trivially on the manifold. On the first, a nontrivial commuting weak action makes the combined action transitive. These conclusions allow arbitrary metrics and do not assume that the weak action preserves the projection.

**Proof.** Local sections of $SU(3)\to S^5$ identify the total space with $SU(3)\times_{SU(2)}S^2$ for the stabilizer action on a single fibre. Average a metric there and uniformize its conformal structure. A connected compact subgroup of the orientation-preserving conformal group of the sphere is conjugate into $SO(3)$. A homomorphism from $SU(2)$ into these rotations is trivial or the double cover up to conjugacy. The latter gives the displayed homogeneous total space. The principal $SU(2)$ bundle represents the nonzero class of $\pi_4(SU(2))=\mathbb Z_2$, and covering $SO(3)$ preserves that group. Thus its rotation sphere bundle has the nontrivial clutching class. [Lafont and Neofytidis, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.10440v3)

An equivariant diffeomorphism of a transitive $G/H$ is determined by the image of the identity coset in $N_G(H)/H$. In $SU(3)/U(1)_{(1,-1,0)}$ the distinct isotropy weights give connected centralizer of the colour action $T^2/U(1)$. A commuting homomorphism from $SU(2)$ to this circle is trivial. On the product, a commuting action induces a weak action on the quotient by the colour orbits, which is $S^2$. If that action is trivial, its restriction to each colour orbit maps $SU(2)$ to $N_{SU(3)}(SU(2))/SU(2)=U(1)$ and is trivial. Otherwise the quotient action is transitive; colour is transitive on every orbit above it. There is then no proper nonempty invariant wall. $\square$

**The dimension bound.** Witten's equality case also covers smooth seven-manifolds without a specified submersion. Let a compact group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{su}_3\oplus\mathfrak{su}_2\oplus\mathfrak u_1$ act infinitesimally effectively on a connected smooth seven-manifold. Then the identity component acts transitively. In particular such a manifold has no boundary or proper nonempty invariant chiral locus. [Witten, the minimal homogeneous space, pp. 416–417](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0550321381900213)

Indeed a nonzero ideal in the principal stabilizer algebra would generate a connected normal subgroup fixing the dense principal set, hence every point. The principal algebra therefore projects injectively off the central $\mathfrak u_1$ and is the graph of a character on its image in the two simple factors. A full projection to $\mathfrak{su}_3$ would contain the pure $\mathfrak{su}_3$ ideal: the kernel of projection to $\mathfrak{su}_2$ has positive dimension and its image is an ideal of $\mathfrak{su}_3$. The character vanishes there. If the projection to $\mathfrak{su}_2$ is full, its kernel under projection to $\mathfrak{su}_3$ is zero by the same pure-ideal argument. A proper compact subalgebra of $\mathfrak{su}_3$ has dimension at most four. A reducible one preserves a complex line. An irreducible one has zero centre by Schur's lemma, and a proper rank-two algebra contains at most one pair of roots of $A_2$, since any two distinct pairs generate the third. Rank one gives dimension at most three. A proper subalgebra of $\mathfrak{su}_2$ has dimension at most one. The stabilizer thus has dimension at most five and the principal orbit has dimension at least seven. A seven-dimensional orbit in the interior is both open and compact, hence is the whole connected manifold. A finite ineffective kernel leaves the argument unchanged.

**Corollary (a dense regular stratum).** Let a compact group with the same Lie algebra act continuously on a Hausdorff space $X$. Suppose it preserves a dense connected smooth seven-dimensional stratum $U$, acts smoothly there, and has discrete kernel on $X$. Then $X=U$ and the identity component acts transitively. For each group element its fixed set is closed, so an element fixing $U$ fixes all of $X$. The preceding dimension bound applies on $U$. Its orbit is compact, hence closed in $X$. Its density gives equality. This also applies to the underlying space of an orbifold with such a regular stratum. A local enhancement of the gauge algebra carried by fields at a singularity is a different hypothesis. $\square$

**Example (two regular components).** Take two copies of $S^7$ and identify one fixed point in each. On the first unit sphere in $\mathbb C^3\oplus\mathbb R^2$ let colour act on $\mathbb C^3$. On the second unit sphere in $\mathbb C^2\oplus\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb R^2$ let weak $SU(2)$ and $U(1)$ act on the two complex summands respectively. Choose both fixed points in the real planes and let the remaining factors act trivially on each sphere. The wedge is a compact connected Hausdorff space with a faithful action of the entire product and one fixed junction. Its regular part has two components with different kernels. This identifies a way to obtain a geometric singular locus. Matter carrying both colour and weak charge at the junction would need an additional construction.

This answers the smooth equivariant sphere-bundle question for the Berger base. Other possibilities include gauge enhancement on a singular locus, a change of geometric symmetry at the endpoint and an action on coefficient fibres. The [two-interval family](../54_chiral_family_over_the_five_sphere.md) uses coefficient rotations and a reflected ten-dimensional chiral locus.

## 8. Helical deformations and the weak mass ratio

A circle in an effective $T^2$ which closes with primitive period has primitive integral slope $(a,b)$. A continuous family of such one-dimensional subtori has constant slope. Relabelling the two angles by $GL(2,\mathbb Z)$ preserves the dimension of each stabilizer. On a smooth principal torus region with two end degenerations one can choose a common collapsing circle exactly when their primitive vectors agree up to sign. For distinct vectors the absolute determinant is a basis invariant and the orbit description retains the two end conditions. Changing to a helical coordinate leaves a two-torus-invariant metric with two Killing fields. An actual deformation that breaks symmetry needs a charged field, new orbit data or a change of boundary conditions.

For one complex weak multiplet of spin $T$ with a neutral vev of weight $m=-Y$ and $Y\ne0$, its canonical kinetic term gives $\rho=[T(T+1)-Y^2]/(2Y^2)$. A charged doublet with $T=|Y|=1/2$ gives $\rho=1$ and a single surviving $Q=T_3+Y$. This supplies an explicit symmetry-breaking representation if an independent link, gauge bundle or defect supplies the doublet. The monopole model below gives such charged scalar modes and their radius dependence in a specified matter action.

### A common monopole model for chirality and breaking to the photon

**Proposition.** On a round sphere of prescribed radius $r$, fix the degree-one Hermitian line $L=\mathcal O(1)$ and its invariant monopole connection. Gauge weak $SU(2)$ through its lifted rotations on the sphere and on $L$. Take colour and hypercharge as four-dimensional spectator gauge fields and use the covering groups with integer charge $6Y$. There is a fixed choice of scalar and fermion action with these two phases as $r$ varies.

- For $r\ge r_c$ the scalar vacuum vanishes. The massless fermion kernel gives one chiral generation with a right-handed neutrino under colour, weak rotations and hypercharge.
- For $0<r<r_c$ two equal orthogonal weak doublets condense. The connected unbroken group has algebra $\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak u(1)_Q$ with $Q=T_3+Y$. Near onset, nonzero up, down, charged-lepton and neutrino Yukawa couplings pair the chiral zero modes into charged or neutral Dirac fermions.

**Proof.** Give two scalar sections $\Phi_+,\Phi_-$ of $L$ hypercharges $+1/2,-1/2$. Write $P=|\Phi_+|^2+|\Phi_-|^2$ and choose the internal energy
$$
\int_{S^2_r}\left(\sum_{\pm}|\nabla_A\Phi_\pm|^2+
         (m_0^2-\zeta\,\mathcal R_{S^2})P+\lambda P^2\right)dV .
$$
Keep $\lambda,m_0^2>0$ and $q=2\zeta-1/2>0$ fixed. The line-bundle identity $\nabla_A^\dagger\nabla_A=2\bar\partial_A^\dagger\bar\partial_A+1/(2r^2)$ gives the coefficient $m_0^2-q/r^2$ of $P$. Thus $r_c=\sqrt q/m_0$. When $r<r_c$, completing the square shows that a minimum requires holomorphic sections and $P=\nu^2=(q/r^2-m_0^2)/(2\lambda)$ pointwise. This bound is attained. In the homogeneous coordinate basis $(s_1,s_2)$ of $H^0(L)$, $|s_1|^2+|s_2|^2=1$. For the two sections with coefficient matrix $C$, constancy at the specified norm is equivalent to $z^\dagger C^\dagger C z=\nu^2$ on every unit line $[z]\in\mathbb{CP}^1$. Hence $C^\dagger C=\nu^2 I$. At and above $r_c$ the unique minimizing scalar field is zero.

An orthogonal pair of equal length is equivalent by a weak rotation and phases to a $T_3=-1/2$ vector for $\Phi_+$ and a $T_3=+1/2$ vector for $\Phi_-$. Its connected electroweak stabilizer is the diagonal generator $Q$. With the usual horizontal scalar kinetic term, $v_{\rm EW}^2=2\int P\,dV=8\pi r^2\nu^2$, so $M_W^2=g_2^2v_{\rm EW}^2/4$, $M_Z^2=(g_2^2+g_Y^2)v_{\rm EW}^2/4$ and $\rho=1$ at tree level. One explicit four-dimensional gauge action integrates the pullback of the horizontal field-strength norm over the sphere with constant coefficient $1/g_{i,6}^2$. It gives $g_i^2=g_{i,6}^2/(4\pi r^2)$ and $M_W^2=g_{2,6}^2\nu^2/2$. The connection on the associated sphere bundle defines the horizontal derivative. This prescription fixes the monopole as a background and includes no independent internal hypercharge field. If such a field is varied, its equation also contains the scalar hypercharge current.

Assign $Q_L,L_L$ six-dimensional chirality $+$ and monopole charge two. Assign $u_R,d_R,\nu_R,e_R$ chirality $-$ and charge one. All have their usual physical colour and hypercharge representations. In this orientation, vertical spin zero modes of degree $n>0$ are $H^0(\mathcal O(n-1))$ in one chirality. Thus colour and hypercharge are supplied by the parent fields while weak doublets and singlets come from the same monopole geometry. Multiplication by $\Phi_-$ maps $H^0(\mathcal O)$ to the up component of $H^0(\mathcal O(1))$, and $\Phi_+$ maps it to the down component. For example, $\bar Q_L\Phi_-u_R$ and $\bar Q_L\Phi_+d_R$ are neutral for both abelian charges. The same contractions work for the leptons. Since $\int |s_i|^2dV=2\pi r^2$ and the normalized constant singlet is $(4\pi r^2)^{-1/2}$, the light masses at first order are $|y_{6,i}|\nu/\sqrt2$. The multiplication operator coupling the higher modes has norm at most a constant times $\max_i|y_{6,i}|\nu$. If this is small compared with the gap $\Delta$ to the other modes, eliminating them costs $O((\max_i|y_{6,i}|\nu)^2/\Delta)$. This proves pairing in a neighbourhood on the broken side for fixed nonzero Yukawas. $\square$ [Monopole spin kernel](02.md) · [Dolan and Szabo, equivariant reduction with flux](https://arxiv.org/abs/0901.2491)

**Local anomaly and a product lift.** The four-dimensional representation has the usual anomaly cancellations and four weak doublets. For the six-dimensional local fermion anomaly add opposite-chirality partners of degree zero with the same spectator representations. Take their Yukawa couplings to vanish. Their untwisted sphere Dirac operators have empty kernel. In the characteristic-class conventions of §6, the polynomial is
$$
\left[\widehat A\sum_i\chi_i\,{\rm ch}(R_i)e^{a_i y}(e^{n_i x}-1)\right]_8
=x^2\left[-c_2(SU(3))-12y^2-p_1/6+(7/3)x^2\right] .
$$
In the order $(Q,L,u,d,\nu,e)$, $a_i=6Y_i=(1,-3,4,-2,0,-6)$, the first two chiralities are positive, and the $n_i$ are $2,2,1,1,1,1$. The signed dimension sums of $n_i^2$, $n_i^2a_i^2$, and $n_i^4$ are $8,-48,56$. The colour sum of $\chi_i n_i^2$ is two, and the odd-power mixed sums vanish. For a varying weak connection $x$ is the equivariant class of $L$. Factorization allows the local two-form cancellation described in §6. Integral tensor data, boundary inflow and global anomalies require additional choices.

For a fermionic product realization, tensor this construction with two magnetized flat two-tori of unit degree on each factor and a periodic circle divided by reflection. The torus product has one spin zero mode of the chosen four-dimensional internal chirality. Give each complex eleven-dimensional parent Dirac fermion the same projection at both ends of the interval, written with a fixed interval orientation, choosing its sign species by species. On the doubled circle the even ten-dimensional Weyl component has a constant level and the odd component has none. Its product with the torus kernel gives the specified six-dimensional chirality. The two scalar Yukawa couplings join opposite ten-dimensional parities, as required for a reflection-even Dirac action. Fixed torus areas and interval length give a gap to the other product modes. This uses charged bulk fermions and background lines as data. [Same-type wall spectra](07.md) · [Magnetic torus zero modes](16.md)

This construction puts the chiral spectrum, Higgs vacuum, residual generator and leading fermion masses in one spherical model. The radius is an external parameter, colour is a spectator and the four-dimensional gauge kinetic coefficients have been prescribed. The round sphere has a single geometric radius. A parametrically wide intermediate six-dimensional internal window needs a second collapsing scale. The flat-background gauge prescription also drives $e$ large as $r$ tends to zero. A quantum limit with controlled couplings, a real finite algebra, and a dynamical connection to the flux-selected Berger endpoint of §4 remain open.

### Colour, the finite real module and matching on one product

**Proposition.** Replace the two tori in the fermionic lift by $\mathbb{CP}^2_b$, with metric $b^2g_{\rm FS}$ of reference holomorphic sectional curvature four. Use the complex spin$^c$ Dirac operator on
$$
 M^4\times\mathbb{CP}^2_b\times S^2_r\times[0,\ell] .
$$
Fix the two lifted rotation groups, the monopole lines and scalar action of §8, the species-dependent equal interval projections, and the charge-conjugate copies described below. This product realizes colour and weak representations through its two geometric rotation groups. Its light states admit an explicit finite real module of KO-degree six.

**Zero modes and masses.** On $\mathbb{CP}^2=\mathbb P(\mathbb C^3)$ use the canonical spin$^c$ module $\Lambda^{0,*}$. Give each physical quark the coefficient line $\mathcal O(-4)$ and each lepton the trivial line. Equivariant Serre duality gives $H^{0,2}(\mathcal O(-4))=H^0(\mathcal O(1))^*=\mathbf3$, and the other cohomology vanishes. For $\mathcal O$ the only group is $H^{0,0}=\mathbf1$. Both kernels have even internal parity. The centre of $SU(3)$ acts trivially on the base and by the fundamental character on the quark twist. The twist shifts the determinant of the canonical spin$^c$ structure from $\mathcal O(3)$ to $\mathcal O(-5)$ for quarks. On the sphere the degree-two and degree-one spin twists supply the doublet and singlet as above. The interval projection retains one of the two ten-dimensional chiralities for each species. These choices give a sixteen-dimensional particle space $P$, including $\nu_R$. The manifold requires spin$^c$ data since $w_2(\mathbb{CP}^2)\ne0$. [Product Dolbeault calculation](../53_flag_chiral_multiplets.md) · [Interval projection](07.md)

Take the scalar fields constant on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ and even on the interval, with the additional product derivatives included as positive kinetic terms. They attain the same completed-square bound on every sphere. The Higgs multiplication preserves each quark coefficient line and acts by the identity on the normalized triplet. If the eleven-dimensional amplitude and Yukawa coefficient are $\nu_{11}$ and $y_{11}$, the normalized six-dimensional ones are $\nu_6=\sqrt{\ell V_4}\nu_{11}$ and $y_6=y_{11}/\sqrt{\ell V_4}$, where $V_4=\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2_b)$. Their product is independent of this convention. The compression gives the four masses $|y_{11,i}|\nu_{11}/\sqrt2$. At fixed product radii the gap to the other fermions is bounded below at zero vev by the minimum of the two first nonzero twisted Dirac gaps and $\pi/\ell$, by the sum of squares on products. For Yukawa norm well below this gap the higher-mode errors are quadratic as in §8.

**Finite algebra and reality.** On $P\oplus\bar P$ let $J$ exchange the two summands antilinearly, let $\gamma$ distinguish left and right on particles and take the opposite signs on conjugates, and set $D_F=D_Y\oplus\bar D_Y$ for the Hermitian off-diagonal compressed Yukawa operator. The grading on particles includes the interval parity of each species. Compressions of smooth complex scalar functions on the two projective spaces span $M_3(\mathbb C)$ on the colour triplet and $M_2(\mathbb C)$ on the weak doublet. Indeed the rank-one evaluation projectors on the linear holomorphic sections run over every projective line. The colour statement follows on the Serre-dual kernel by conjugation. On the weak doublet choose the real subalgebra commuting with its invariant quaternionic antiunitary. It is $\mathbb H=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(1,i\sigma_1,i\sigma_2,i\sigma_3)$. Compressions of real scalar functions on the sphere include the Pauli matrices and generate the full complex matrix algebra as a real algebra.

Specify the left action of $(\lambda,q,m)\in\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb H\oplus M_3(\mathbb C)$ by $q$ on particle doublets, $\lambda$ on $u_R,\nu_R$, and $\bar\lambda$ on $d_R,e_R$. Take $m$ on all antiquarks and $\lambda$ on all antileptons. On particles the opposite action $J\,a^*J^{-1}$ is the same on the two chiralities of each colour or lepton line and commutes with the Yukawa maps. On antiparticles $D_F$ commutes with the left action. This proves order zero and order one, along with $J^2=1$, $JD_F=D_FJ$, $J\gamma=-\gamma J$ and $\{\gamma,D_F\}=0$. The conjugate doubling, quaternionic restriction and left/right assignments are specified parts of the construction. [Connes, the finite KO-six grading](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0608226) · [Chamseddine, Connes and Marcolli, §2 on unimodularity](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0610241)

For a unitary the determinant of the left action on all 32 states is $(\lambda\det m)^4$. The particle factors cancel between up and down. Four anti-triplet factors and four antileptons give the displayed determinant. Connected unimodularity imposes $\det m=\lambda^{-1}$. Write $\lambda=e^{it/2}$ and identify physical colour with the conjugate of the $SU(3)$ factor of $m$. The lift $uJuJ^{-1}$ gives hypercharges $(1/6,-1/2,2/3,-1/3,0,-1)$ for $(Q,L,u,d,\nu,e)$. With the unit circle parameter $e^{it/6}$ and integer charges $6Y$, the kernel on this generation is generated by $(e^{2\pi i/3}1_3,-1_2,e^{i\pi/3})$. Thus the lifted finite representation agrees with the charges of the monopole Yukawas and leaves the same $Q=T_3+Y$ below $r_c$. $\square$

**Scale windows and gauge normalization.** On the same product take a scalar heat-trace probe of energy $E$. If $b^{-1},r^{-1}\ll E\ll\pi/\ell$, Weyl growth on $\mathbb{CP}^2\times S^2$ and the constant interval mode give six resolved internal dimensions, ten in total. For $b^{-1},\ell^{-1}\ll E\ll r^{-1}$ the constant scalar sphere mode leaves five internal directions on $\mathbb{CP}^2\times I$. In the fermion count a collapsed sphere retains its twisted zero modes. A probe above all three inverse lengths resolves all seven internal dimensions. These bands require the indicated hierarchies, and the finite KO signs concern the further projection onto the fermion kernel.

At tree level keep only the Einstein term for the two isometry kinetic coefficients and a separate hypercharge term with coefficient $1/g_{Y,11}^2$ in the $Y=Q-T_3$ basis. Let $V=4\pi r^2 V_4\ell$. On $\mathbb{CP}^2$, for traceless Hermitian $T$ the horizontal Hopf norm is $b^2[z^\dagger T^2z-(z^\dagger Tz)^2]$. Its projective average is $b^2\,\mathrm{tr}(T^2)/4=b^2/8$ for $\mathrm{tr}(T^2)=1/2$, by the first two moments of a unit vector in $\mathbb C^3$. The generator $T_3$ has average squared Killing length $2r^2/3$ on the unit-normalized weak sphere. Integrating these norms with the Einstein coefficient $1/(2\kappa_{11}^2)$ gives
$$
 g_3^{-2}=\frac{V b^2}{16\kappa_{11}^2},\qquad
 g_2^{-2}=\frac{V r^2}{3\kappa_{11}^2},\qquad
 g_Y^{-2}=\frac V{g_{Y,11}^2}.
$$
With zero matching thresholds the inherited relations $g_3^2=g_2^2=(5/3)g_Y^2$ give
$$
 b^2=\frac{16}3r^2,\qquad r^2=\frac{9\kappa_{11}^2}{5g_{Y,11}^2},\qquad \ell>0 .
$$
Thus there is one free length before fitting the electromagnetic, Fermi or strong scale. In the broken phase the horizontal tree action gives $v_{\rm EW}^2=2V\nu_{11}^2$ and $M_W^2=3\kappa_{11}^2\nu_{11}^2/(2r^2)$. Varying $\ell$ at the matched radii varies $e^2$ and $G_F$ as $\ell^{-1}$ while preserving this leading $W$ mass. The four-dimensional approximation requires the pole to stay below the relevant higher-mode gaps. No running to the Thomson or hadronic scale is used. Dynamical background fluxes, boundary gauge terms or additional kinetic terms would change the matching equations. [Einstein reduction and the form-field correction in a separate flux action](#a-radius-selected-by-a-quantized-five-form)

This matching also makes a limitation quantitative. The five-internal-direction band needs $b/r$ large, whereas the matched ratio is $4/\sqrt3$. Varying $\ell$ alone at these couplings cannot make that band parametrically wide. The five-dimensional geometric base in that hierarchy is $\mathbb{CP}^2\times I$, with colour descending from isometries and the photon acting on the surviving matter. A dynamical path to the Berger $S^5$ with its geometric electromagnetic circle, anomaly and boundary data for the spin$^c$ parent, and a derivation of the finite left/right assignments remain open.

## 9. The Atiyah–Witten cone

For the cone on $\mathbb{CP}^3$ the specified circle quotient is topologically $\mathbb R^6$. The fixed loci give two intersecting D6-branes at supersymmetric angles. Open strings there give one complex chiral multiplet $\Phi$. Its relative $U(1)$ acts as a global symmetry in this noncompact example. A vev recombines the two three-planes to a single locus of topology $S^2\times\mathbb R$ in the smooth geometry. For the flag link there are instead three intersecting branes, three chiral fields and a cubic superpotential allowing at most one vev. These are the two distinct examples after equations (3.5) and (3.6). The isometries descending as ordinary symmetries of a circle quotient must preserve the circle orbits. A compact gauged weak group and the Standard Model charges would require further data. [Atiyah and Witten, §3, pp. 23–24](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/0107177)

The spectral interpolation has a common classical realization over the five-sphere. A reflected outer interval supplies a ten-dimensional Weyl field, a scalar doublet couples the weak representations, and one winding action selects the wall-angle scaling that recovers the full vectorlike tangential spectrum. The weak group acts on coefficients. In the projective product of §8 it acts instead by rotations of the sphere. A single compactification combining the chiral geometric weak action, the conic sphere collapse and the Berger endpoint, together with global anomaly and quantum threshold matching, remains open.
