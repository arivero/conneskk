# Mirror fermions and reflection positivity: the lattice chiral SU(5) construction and results 78–79

The lattice chiral $SU(5)$ construction assessed by the Yang–Mills group has three left-handed families in $\mathbf{10}\oplus\overline{\mathbf5}$ and must exclude mirror fermions. Results 68, 72 and 79 here contain three complete mirror families, massive in the broken phase. We compare the two on three questions. What varies is the internal finite module (its grading and multiplicities), the common mirror Dirac mass $M$ at fixed weak fit, and the number of active flavours in the running. What stays fixed is the free overlap Weyl field at finite spacing for the reflection question, the $1/L=2$ TeV weak fit of 78 with its ordinary spectrum, normalized currents and Thomson subtraction for the mirror question, and the tree matching action of issue 1 for the running question. The observables are the reflection form $\langle\Theta(F)F\rangle$ on positive-time tests, the spacelike inverse electromagnetic charge at $Q=1$ TeV, the oblique parameters, and the one- and two-loop running coefficients. ## The Lu–Shao obstruction in exact scope

**Setting (Lu and Shao, §2–3).** On the infinite lattice $\mathbb Z^d$, $d=2,4$, at finite spacing, the overlap operator $D$ satisfies the Ginsparg–Wilson relation and $\hat\gamma_5=\gamma_5(1-D)$. Lüscher's Weyl field obeys $\hat P_-\psi=\psi$ and $\bar\psi P_+=\bar\psi$. At each momentum $p\in T^d$ the allowed $\psi(p)$ form the Weyl bundle $\mathcal L_p={\rm im}\,\hat P_-(p)$. It is a line bundle with $C_1=-1$ for $d=2$, and a rank-two bundle with $c_1=0$ and $C_2=1$ for $d=4$, the pullback of the BPST instanton by $\hat h:T^4\to S^4$. The $\bar\psi$ projection $P_+$ is constant, so the $\bar\psi$ bundle is trivial. A reflection is an antilinear involution of the Grassmann algebra that reverses products and exchanges the positive- and negative-time algebras $\mathcal A_\pm$; positivity means $\langle\Theta(F)F\rangle\ge0$ for $F\in\mathcal A_+$. [Lu and Shao, §2.3 and §3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.20084v1#S3.SS2)

**Hypotheses on $\Theta$ (Lu–Shao, §3.2).**
1. $\Theta$ is an antilinear map of the Weyl fields with $\Theta^2=1$ that preserves the relations $\hat P_+\psi=0$, $\bar\psi P_-=0$.
2. It commutes with the fermion-number $U(1)$, so $\Theta\psi$ is built from $\bar\psi$ and $\Theta\bar\psi$ from $\psi$.
3. It is covariant under translations composed with the reflection $\theta(\vec x,x_d)=(\vec x,1-x_d)$.
4. It is local with exponential tails about $\theta x$.

Conditions 2 and 3 give $\Theta\psi(p)=e^{ip_d}\bar\psi(\theta p)\tilde A(\theta p)$ with $\tilde A$ analytic and periodic by condition 4. Condition 1 gives $P_+\tilde A(\theta p)\hat P_+(p)=0$. The rows $r_i(p)^\dagger$ of $P_+\tilde A(\theta p)$ then lie in $\mathcal L_p$, and invertibility makes $r_1(p)^\dagger$ a nowhere-vanishing section, which the nonzero top Chern class forbids.

**Result of Lu and Shao, §3.2 (reference).** The section states its result in the text, with no numbered theorem, on $\mathbb Z^d$ at lattice spacing one; their footnote 6 covers finite tori with antiperiodic time boundary conditions. No $\Theta$ satisfies 1–4 for the free overlap Weyl field in $d=2$ or $d=4$. In $d=4$ none satisfies 1, 3 and 4. Without condition 2 the Nambu field $\Phi(p)=(\psi(p),\bar\psi(-p)^{\sf T})$ has the rank-four projector $\Pi={\rm diag}(\hat P_-,P_+)$ with $C_2=1$; $\Theta$ would make ${\rm im}\,\Pi$ isomorphic to the pullback of its complex conjugate by the orientation-reversing map $p\mapsto-\theta p$, giving $C_2=-C_2$. Escapes stated by the authors: the algebra generated by $q=P_-\psi$, $\bar q=\bar\psi P_+$ at positive times is reflection positive by their reference [54], which their bibliography lists as Kikukawa and Usui (2010), but it omits $P_+\psi$ and its kernel is singular at the would-be doublers; the overlap Dirac field is reflection positive with $\Theta_{\rm D}\psi(x)=\bar\psi(\theta x)\gamma_d$, $\Theta_{\rm D}\bar\psi(x)=\gamma_d\psi(\theta x)$; and the authors expect, without proof, that positivity is recovered in the continuum limit, which probes only a neighbourhood of $p=0$, where the bundle is trivial. Mirror or vectorlike fields, internal modules, massive fields and constant projections are not treated in §3.2; Propositions 31.1–31.3 below are our extension. [Kikukawa and Usui, Phys. Rev. D 82, 114503](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.82.114503)

**What the SU(5) assessment establishes (Appendix A).** At finite cutoff, exact positivity holds for an auxiliary functional $B_k^{\rm aux}$ on even observables supported in a band separated from the reflection plane by a buffer. For each such test $|B_k(F,F)-B_k^{\rm aux}(F,F)|\to0$ as the cutoff is removed at fixed volume, eventually in $k$ and along selected subsequences, so the limit is nonnegative without $B_k\ge0$ at any finite $k$. Positivity is passed first through the ultraviolet limit at fixed volume and then through the volume limit. The final Gram matrices are those of ordered products in positive-time slabs on the reconstructed Hilbert space. The tested algebra (even, buffered, limiting) differs from the one in the theorem (the full Weyl algebra at fixed spacing), so the two statements are compatible, as the assessment says. Identifying the expectations and the reflection with those of the intended chiral gauge theory remains open there.

## Internal modules and the Weyl bundle

Tensor the external overlap field with a finite internal Hilbert space $H$ graded by an involution $\Gamma$ with eigenspaces $H_\pm$ of dimensions $n_\pm$. Lüscher's projection becomes $\psi\in E_p=(\mathcal L_p\otimes H_+)\oplus(\mathcal L_p^\perp\otimes H_-)$ and $\bar\psi\in({\rm im}P_+\otimes H_+)\oplus({\rm im}P_-\otimes H_-)$, where $\mathcal L^\perp={\rm im}\,\hat P_+$. This is the lattice form of the higher-dimensional Weyl condition $(\gamma_5\otimes\Gamma)\psi=-\psi$. Write $\nu=n_+-n_-$.

**Proposition 31.1.** In $d=4$, $c_1(E)=0$ and $C_2(E)=\nu$. (a) If $\nu\ne0$, no $\Theta$ satisfies conditions 1–4, and none satisfies 1, 3 and 4. (b) If $\nu=0$, $E$ is analytically trivial, and the topological obstruction is absent.

**Proof.** $\mathcal L\oplus\mathcal L^\perp$ is the trivial bundle $\mathbb C^4$, so $c(\mathcal L)c(\mathcal L^\perp)=1$. With $c_1(\mathcal L)=0$ this gives $c_1(\mathcal L^\perp)=0$ and $c_2(\mathcal L^\perp)=-c_2(\mathcal L)$. Tensoring with a trivial $\mathbb C^n$ multiplies the second Chern character by $n$, and for $c_1=0$ the class $c_2$ is minus that character, so $C_2(E)=n_+C_2(\mathcal L)-n_-C_2(\mathcal L)=\nu$. (a) With condition 2 the argument of Lu and Shao gives rows $r_i(p)^\dagger\in E_p$, $i=1,\dots,{\rm rank}\,E$, depending smoothly on $p$. They are linearly independent at every $p$: otherwise $\Theta$ would send the fibre $E_p$ into fewer $\bar\psi$ components than its dimension, contradicting invertibility. They form a global frame, so $E$ is trivial and $C_2(E)=0$. Without condition 2 the Nambu projector is $\Pi={\rm diag}(\Pi_E,\Pi_{\bar\psi})$ with a constant second block, so $C_2({\rm im}\,\Pi)=\nu$. The isomorphism of Lu and Shao then gives $\nu=-\nu$. (b) Choose a unitary $V:H_+\to H_-$. The map $1\oplus(1\otimes V^{-1})$ sends $E$ onto $(\mathcal L\oplus\mathcal L^\perp)\otimes H_+=\mathbb C^4\otimes H_+$, analytically in $p$. $\square$

Part (a) extends a published no-go theorem to internal multiplicities. It is recorded here in that scope and awaits a check by a second model. Part (b) is the positive half: with equal multiplicities of the two internal gradings the obstruction vanishes, and the reflection question reduces to the Dirac case. For the submitted $SU(5)$ content, $\nu=3\cdot10+3\cdot5=45$ counting each representation's components, so part (a) applies to its full Weyl algebra at finite spacing.

**Proposition 31.2.** Each of the following has $\nu=0$.
1. The kernel sector of the KO-six cylinder of issue 1, which consists of a constant upper component in $L$ and a lower one in $J_5L$, of opposite chiralities and equal dimension.
2. The interval operator $D(\Gamma_0,\Gamma_1)$ of Theorem 23.1 when $\Gamma_0,\Gamma_1$ have eigenspaces of equal dimension: its zero modes have chirality index $\dim(H_+^0\cap H_+^1)-\dim(H_-^0\cap H_-^1)=0$, and its massive channels are Dirac pairs of mass $\theta_k/\ell$.
3. The ordinary families together with the three mirror families of 72 and 79, in every gauge representation separately.

In each case the projectors that select the modes act on the finite internal factor, and on a product $D=D_4\otimes1+\gamma_5\otimes D_{\rm int}$ the spectral projections of $D_{\rm int}$ in a fixed window are constant in external momentum.

**Proof.** (1) Issue 1 states the kernel and its chirality pairing. (2) The kernel of $D(\Gamma_0,\Gamma_1)$ is the constant $\psi_L\in H_+^0\cap H_+^1$ and $\psi_R\in H_-^0\cap H_-^1$. For projections $P,Q$ on an $n$-dimensional space, $\dim({\rm ran}P^\perp\cap{\rm ran}Q^\perp)=n-\dim({\rm ran}P+{\rm ran}Q)=n-{\rm rk}P-{\rm rk}Q+\dim({\rm ran}P\cap{\rm ran}Q)$. With ${\rm rk}P={\rm rk}Q=n/2$ the difference vanishes. The massive channels are Theorem 23.1, item 2. (3) The mirror prescription of 100 pairs every ordinary Weyl field with a mirror field in the same gauge representation and of opposite chirality. A projection that commutes with $D_4\otimes1$ and acts only on the internal factor is the same at every external momentum. $\square$ [KO-six cylinder](01.md#2-a-real-chiral-cylinder-domain) · [Theorem 23.1](../../lemmas/23_two_gradings_interval.md#two-gradings) · [Mirror pairing](../100_hopf_light_fermion_window_and_hadronic_calibration.md#the-first-omitted-coloured-particle-and-the-subtraction-point)

**The roles of $J$ and $\Theta$.** $\Theta$ is external: it reflects Euclidean time, acts by $\gamma_d$ on the external spinor and sends $\gamma_5$ to $-\gamma_5$. With an antiunitary internal factor $K$, $\Theta_{\rm D}\otimes K$ maps the condition $(\gamma_5\otimes\Gamma)\psi=-\psi$ onto the $\bar\psi$ condition of the same field space when $K\Gamma K^{-1}=\Gamma$. The internal real structure in KO dimension six obeys $J\Gamma=-\Gamma J$. With $K=J$ the image is the field space of the opposite internal grading, the mirror field space. So the time reflection uses an internal conjugation that commutes with the grading, while $J$, composed with $\Theta_{\rm D}$, exchanges the ordinary and mirror field spaces. The same anticommutation gives $n_+=n_-$ on any KO-six module, since $J$ maps $H_+$ antiunitarily onto $H_-$. That doubled module counts each Weyl field together with its conjugate; the physical chirality is fixed by the reality condition, outside the scope of Proposition 31.1.

**Proposition 31.3 (a positive test space).** Give one paired wall mode of mass $m=\theta/\ell>0$ the free Euclidean Dirac action $\bar\psi(\gamma_\mu\partial_\mu+m)\psi$ on $\mathbb R^4$, with Hermitian $\gamma_\mu$. For $t>0$ and spatial momentum $\mathbf p$, $E=\sqrt{\mathbf p^2+m^2}$,
$$
 \gamma_0S(t,\mathbf p)=e^{-tE}P_{\rm pos}(\mathbf p),\qquad
 P_{\rm pos}=\frac{E+h(\mathbf p)}{2E},\quad h=-i\gamma_0\boldsymbol\gamma\cdot\mathbf p+m\gamma_0 .
$$
For test spinors $f_a(t,\mathbf p)$ supported in $t>0$ and $F_a=\int\bar\psi f_a$, the reflection matrix with $\Theta_{\rm D}$ is the Gram matrix of $v_a(\mathbf p)=P_{\rm pos}(\mathbf p)\int_0^\infty e^{-tE}f_a(t,\mathbf p)\,dt$ in $L^2(d^3p)\otimes\mathbb C^4$, and is positive semidefinite.

**Proof.** The momentum-space propagator is $(i\gamma\cdot p+m)^{-1}=(-i\gamma\cdot p+m)/(p^2+m^2)$. For $t>0$ closing the $p_0$ contour on the pole $p_0=iE$ gives $S(t,\mathbf p)=e^{-tE}(E\gamma_0-i\boldsymbol\gamma\cdot\mathbf p+m)/(2E)$. Multiplying by $\gamma_0$ gives $e^{-tE}(E+h)/(2E)$. The matrix $h$ is Hermitian, since $(-i\gamma_0\gamma_j)^\dagger=i\gamma_j\gamma_0=-i\gamma_0\gamma_j$, and $h^2=\mathbf p^2+m^2$ because the cross terms cancel. So $P_{\rm pos}$ is the orthogonal projector onto the positive eigenspace of $h$. The reflected two-point form at times $s,t>0$ is $\gamma_0S(s+t)=e^{-sE}P_{\rm pos}e^{-tE}$, with $P_{\rm pos}^2=P_{\rm pos}=P_{\rm pos}^\dagger$. The reflection matrix is therefore $\int d^3p\,v_a(\mathbf p)^\dagger v_b(\mathbf p)$. $\square$ The extension from this one-particle channel to the full algebra of the free Dirac field is the quasi-free reflection positivity of Osterwalder and Schrader (Helv. Phys. Acta 46, 277, 1973). On a lattice the paired mode can be regularized as a Wilson or overlap Dirac field, which is reflection positive with $\Theta_{\rm D}$, in agreement with Proposition 31.1(b).

## A mirror-sensitive observable in results 78–79

Fix the weak fit, ordinary spectrum, normalized currents and Thomson subtraction of 78 at $1/L=2$ TeV, and vary the common running Dirac mass $M$ of the three unmixed mirror families of 79. Each family has a colour-triplet up quark ($q=2/3$), a colour-triplet down quark ($-1/3$), a charged lepton ($-1$) and a neutral lepton, so $\sum_fN_cq_f^2=3(4/3+1/3+1)=8$. Define the mirror increment of the on-shell-subtracted inverse charge at spacelike $Q$,
$$
 \Delta_{\rm mir}(Q,M)=-\frac{2}{\pi}\,8\int_0^1x(1-x)\log\Big[1+x(1-x)\frac{Q^2}{M^2}\Big]dx .
$$

**Proposition 31.4.** For $Q>0$, $\Delta_{\rm mir}(Q,M)<0$; it increases strictly with $M$ and obeys $|\Delta_{\rm mir}|\le(8/15\pi)\,Q^2/M^2$, so it tends to zero as $M\to\infty$. The matched construction without mirrors has increment zero. At $Q=1$ TeV, $\Delta_{\rm mir}=-0.9750$ for $M=300$ GeV and $-0.0376$ for $M=2.1$ TeV.

**Proof.** For $Q>0$ the integrand is positive on $(0,1)$, so the integral is positive. The derivative of the integrand in $M$ is $-2x(1-x)u/[M(1+u)]<0$ with $u=x(1-x)Q^2/M^2>0$, so the integral decreases and $\Delta_{\rm mir}$ increases strictly with $M$. From $\log(1+u)\le u$ and $\int_0^1x^2(1-x)^2dx=1/30$ the bound follows, and it gives the limit. Without mirrors the charge trace of the extra fields is zero. The numbers are Gauss–Kronrod quadrature in Python. $\square$

At $M=2.1$ TeV the bound gives $0.0385$ against the computed $0.0376$. This one-loop free-fermion quantity distinguishes three massive mirror families from a construction without mirrors; at $M=300$ GeV it is an order-one shift of $\alpha^{-1}$ at $1$ TeV. The other mirror-sensitive observables of 79 under Proposition 79.1's assumptions give consistent values: degenerate doublets give $S^{(0)}_{\rm mir}=3\cdot(3+1)/(6\pi)=2/\pi$ and $T_{\rm mir}=0$, since $F(x,x)=0$; the strong coupling at $1$ TeV is $0.09634$ with mirrors against $0.08879$ without (below); and a root-only scalar gains $6\kappa_Z$ heavy-quark units in its gluon amplitude (79.2). The signed anomaly traces cancel between a family and its mirror. The parity-even traces in these observables add. ## The free-theory control for an interaction claim

**Proposition 31.5.** In a free charged Dirac field coupled minimally to a free Abelian gauge field with coupling $e$, the connected correlation of the current $J_\mu=\bar\psi\gamma_\mu\psi$ with the gauge field is, to first order in $e$,
$$
 \langle J_\mu(x)A_\nu(y)\rangle_{\rm c}=-e\int d^4z\,\Pi_{\mu\rho}(x-z)D_{\rho\nu}(z-y),\qquad
 \Pi_{\mu\rho}(x)=-{\rm tr}\big[\gamma_\mu S(x)\gamma_\rho S(-x)\big],
$$
up to the sign convention of the vertex. The right side is nonzero whenever the field is charged.

**Proof.** Expand $e^{-S_{\rm int}}$ with $S_{\rm int}=e\int A_\rho J_\rho$ to first order. The gauge field contracts with $A_\rho(z)$ into $D_{\rho\nu}$. The four fermion fields of $J_\mu(x)J_\rho(z)$ contract in the single connected way, giving the closed loop with the Fermi sign. In momentum space $\Pi_{\mu\rho}(q)=(q^2\delta_{\mu\rho}-q_\mu q_\rho)\Pi(q^2)$, and the subtracted $\Pi$ is the integral of Proposition 31.4 per unit charge squared. $\square$

So a nonzero connected current–gauge correlation already occurs in the free gauge–fermion theory, through one fermion loop, and it detects charged matter. Its mirror part is $\Delta_{\rm mir}$. An interaction claim for the submitted theory needs the difference between its connected matter-current–clover correlator and the value of the same correlator in the free gauge–fermion theory, with identical insertions, smearings, normalization and subtraction. For a gauge-invariant clover the first free contribution comes at the order at which the clover couples to a fermion loop, and it must be computed with the submission's lattice definitions. Using the mirror-free value as the reference for the submitted $SU(5)$ theory further requires identifying its chiral measure, establishing gauge invariance and excluding unwanted mirrors. This repository provides none of these, so the comparison is stated as a requirement.

## Running coefficients: group theory and matching

**Proposition 31.6.** For $SU(5)$ with three left-handed families in $\mathbf{10}\oplus\overline{\mathbf5}$, $T(\mathbf{10})=(N-2)/2=3/2$, $T(\overline{\mathbf5})=1/2$, the cubic anomalies $A(\mathbf{10})=N-4=1$ and $A(\overline{\mathbf5})=-1$ cancel, and with $\beta(g)=-b_0g^3/16\pi^2-b_1g^5/(16\pi^2)^2$
$$
 b_0=\tfrac{11}{3}\cdot5-\tfrac23\cdot3\big(\tfrac32+\tfrac12\big)=\tfrac{55}{3}-4=\tfrac{43}{3},\qquad
 b_1=\tfrac{34}{3}\cdot25-\tfrac32\sum_{R}T(R)\big(4C_2(R)+\tfrac{20}{3}\cdot5\big)=\tfrac{2156}{15},
$$
the sum running over $R=\mathbf{10},\overline{\mathbf5}$ with $C_2(\mathbf{10})=18/5$, $C_2(\overline{\mathbf5})=12/5$. For colour alone $b_0=11-2n_f/3$, which is $7$ for the six ordinary flavours and $3$ for twelve flavours above a common mirror threshold. For one family ${\rm tr}\,Y^2={10}/{3}$ and ${\rm tr}\,T_3^2=2$, so the hypercharge index is $5/3$.

**Proof.** A Weyl fermion contributes half of a Dirac fermion to both coefficients. The two-loop Dirac term $-T(R)[4C_2(R)+(20/3)C_A]$ reproduces $102-38n_f/3$ for QCD. The Casimirs follow from $C_2(R)\dim R=T(R)(N^2-1)$. The traces are sums over the fifteen Weyl fields of a family, $Q,u^c,d^c,L,e^c$. $\square$

The submission measures a normalized average: $C_k(\Delta B)$ over $k$ ultraviolet steps, with the conversion factor built from the pure-gauge coefficient $55/3$, tends to $43/3$ as the window lengthens and moves to finer scales. Such an average determines neither a pointwise beta function nor $b_1$. A pointwise test would compare the slope at fixed scale with $b_0=43/3$ and the curvature in $1/g^2$ against $\log\mu$ with $b_1=2156/15$, in one stated scheme. With three mirror families added to the same group the Weyl sum doubles and $b_0=31/3$.

**Numerical comparison (Python 3, SciPy).** Starting from $\alpha_s^{(6)}(300\ {\rm GeV})=0.10118$ of 79, simultaneous decoupling of the six mirror quarks at $300$ GeV, $\alpha_s^{(6)}=\alpha_s^{(12)}[1+6\cdot\frac{11}{72}(\alpha_s^{(12)}/\pi)^2]$, gives $\alpha_s^{(12)}(300\ {\rm GeV})=0.10108$. Three-loop twelve-flavour running gives $\alpha_s(1\ {\rm TeV})=0.09634$, and six-flavour running gives $0.08879$; at four loops these are $0.09633$ and $0.08879$. This reproduces 79.

The hypercharge index is group theory. Inferring $\sin^2\theta_W=e^2/g_3^2=3/8$ needs the common tree-level kinetic normalization $g_3^2=g_2^2=(5/3)g_Y^2$ at matching, with $e^{-2}=g_2^{-2}+g_Y^{-2}$. That normalization, the matching scale, the zero threshold terms and the scheme come from the matching action of issue 1, §4. The coefficients $43/3$, $7$, $3$, $5/3$ and $2156/15$ are group theory. The values of $\alpha_s(1\ {\rm TeV})$, the mirror increment and the matching relation $3/8$ depend on the action and its thresholds. ## What is established and what stays open

The finite work of the closure plan is recorded. Proposition 31.4 gives the mirror increment $-0.9750$ at $300$ GeV and $Q=1$ TeV, its sign, its monotone decoupling and the zero mirror-free reference. Propositions 31.2 and 31.3 identify a positive test space: vectorlike internal modules ($\nu=0$), including the paired wall modes, have no Chern obstruction, and the massive paired mode has a positive reflection matrix with an explicit Gram factorization. Proposition 31.6 and the numerical comparison separate group theory from the scheme and matching assumptions.

Proposition 31.1(a) is a negative result. Its scope is the free four-dimensional overlap Weyl field on $\mathbb Z^4$ at finite spacing, tensored with a constant internal grading, and reflections satisfying the four conditions of Lu and Shao (or conditions 1, 3 and 4). Its hypotheses exclude a reality condition on the field, interactions and the continuum limit. Its known escapes are vectorlike content ($\nu=0$, Proposition 31.1(b)), the restricted $q,\bar q$ algebra, reflections outside the four conditions, and the continuum limit, where Lu and Shao expect positivity without proving it. It points to vectorlike regularizations, in which mirror fields are present and are given mass, as the direction in which positivity at finite spacing is available. It is checked only by its author and needs a second model before it is relied on.

The research questions stay open. For the submitted $SU(5)$ theory: the free gauge–fermion value of its own matter-current–clover correlator with identical definitions, the identification of its chiral measure and its gauge invariance, and the exclusion of unwanted mirrors. For this repository: whether the reality condition of a KO-six module changes the Chern count of Proposition 31.1, whether the mirror families of 79 can be made heavy enough for the oblique and gluon-vertex tests while keeping the vectorlike regularization, and a pointwise running test against $b_0=43/3$ and $b_1=2156/15$. All six propositions are new here and are recorded as reported with referees pending.
