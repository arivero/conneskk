# Vacuum alignment of the composite Pati–Salam potential

**Theorem.** Use the composite scalar fields of section VIII of Chamseddine–Connes–van Suijlekom, one generation, with $A=F_0/(2\pi^2)>0$, $B=F_2/(2\pi^2)>0$. Allow the full complex bidoublet $\phi$, the $2\times4$ field $\Delta$, and the Hermitian $1+15$ field $\Sigma$. If $k_R\ne0$ and any Dirac Yukawa is nonzero, **every global minimum has $\Delta=0$**. Thus the unrestricted minimum supplies neither of the two connector-line alignments of Lemma 50. [Printed potential, pp. 20–22 and Fig. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.8050) · [Finite line conditions](../../lemmas/50_majorana_line_and_top_block.md)

**Proof.** Use the eight right indices $(\dot a,I)$. With a choice of matrix transpose for $\phi$, the dressed Dirac map into that space is
$$S=\phi\otimes[k_u1+(k_\nu-k_u)\Sigma]+\widetilde\phi\otimes[k_d1+(k_e-k_d)\Sigma],\qquad \widetilde\phi=\sigma_2\bar\phi\sigma_2.$$
Write $H_{\dot a I,\dot b J}=k_R\Delta_{\dot a J}\Delta_{\dot b I}$, $Q=SS^\dagger$, and $K$ for the Gram matrix of $H$ on its contracted right index, using adjoints so that the mixed contraction in section VIII is $\mathrm{tr}(KQ)$. In row-right-index convention $K=HH^\dagger$. Both forms are positive. With $c=B/A$ the printed polynomial is
$$V/A=\tfrac12\mathrm{tr}\,K^2+2\mathrm{tr}(KQ)+\mathrm{tr}\,Q^2-c\,\mathrm{tr}(K+2Q).$$
Consequently
$$V/A+8c^2=\tfrac12\mathrm{tr}\big[(Q-c1)^2+(Q+K-c1)^2\big]+\mathrm{tr}(KQ)\ \ge0.$$
The last trace is the trace of $K^{1/2}QK^{1/2}$. Choose $\Delta=0$, $\Sigma=0$ for a nonzero quark Yukawa pair or $\Sigma=1$ for a nonzero lepton pair. With $\phi=t e^{i\psi}1_2$ the dressed map is $t(k_1e^{i\psi}+k_2e^{-i\psi})1_8$. Choose the phase with nonzero coefficient, then $t$ to give $Q=c1$. Equality is attained. At equality the first two squares force $Q=c1$ and $K=0$. Finally $\mathrm{tr}\,K=|k_R|^2(\sum|\Delta_{\dot a I}|^2)^2$, so $\Delta=0$. For all four Dirac Yukawas zero the singular values $d_1,d_2$ of $\Delta$ instead give $V=A|k_R|^4(\sum d_i^4)^2/2-B|k_R|^2(\sum d_i^2)^2$. At nonzero optimum both singular values are equal by Cauchy–Schwarz; the minimum is $-2B^2/A$, compared with $-B^2/(2A)$ on the rank-one locus. If $k_R=0$, $\Delta$ drops out. $\square$

**Relative orientations on the rank-one locus.** Let $\Delta=d\,uv^T$, $\Sigma=s\,pp^\dagger$ and $\phi=t\,a b^\dagger$ with unit vectors in the corresponding factors (use the conjugate colour frame for $v$ when it labels a dual). Then $x=|\langle u,a\rangle|^2$, $y=|\langle v,p\rangle|^2$. Put $L_j(y)=|k_j|^2+y(|k_j+s(k'_j-k_j)|^2-|k_j|^2)$ for $(j,j')=(u,\nu),(d,e)$. At fixed norms all orientation dependence is the term
$$2A|k_R|^2d^4t^2\,[xL_u(y)+(1-x)L_d(y)].$$
Its extrema are decided by the four corner values; ties can give flat edges. At an interior critical point with nonzero mixed coefficient its two Hessian eigenvalues have opposite signs in these overlap coordinates. For example $k_\nu=k_u$ and $k_e=k_d$ remove the $y$ dependence. The order-one and unbroken-circle conditions remain meaningful restrictions on finite data independently of this energy.

**Hessian at the published example.** The Fig. 1 caption sets $k_\nu=k_e=1$, $k_R=k_u=k_d=2$, $\phi_{11}=1/2$, $\Delta_{11}=1/\sqrt2$, $\Sigma_{11}=2$, other entries zero. Vary only $\Sigma_{11}=2+\delta$. The dressed bidoublet is $(1/2)1_2\otimes(2I-\Sigma)$. Its two lepton squares become $\delta^2/4$ and the mixed trace acquires $\delta^2/4$. Thus $\Delta V=(A/2-B)\delta^2+A\delta^4/8$. Radial stationarity of $\Delta_{11}$ at the quoted value already requires $B=A$, giving a negative second derivative $-A$. The figure examined the Hessian in the $\Delta$ directions. For generation matrices a scalar bidoublet need not saturate the square bound. The following calculation treats that case with the same composite fields.

## The alignment test for arbitrary generation matrices

**Theorem.** Fix any number $g$ of generations and the five Yukawa matrices. Vary the three scalar fields in the composite potential of section VIII. For arbitrary fixed $\phi,\Sigma$, minimizing over the full complex connector $\Delta$ reduces to at most 24 one-variable rational functions. Each requires only its two endpoints and the roots of a quadratic. This gives a criterion for which lines a nonzero rank-one vacuum selects.

**Proof.** Work on the $8g$ right indices and set $R$ equal to the positive generation Gram matrix in the same $HH^\dagger$ contraction as the mixed term. In the convention $H_{\dot a I,\dot b J}=\Delta_{\dot a J}\Delta_{\dot b I}\,k_R^\dagger$, one has $R=k_R^\dagger k_R$. Use the Dirac matrices as maps into this same right generation space. Then
$$K=(\Delta\Delta^\dagger)\otimes(\Delta^\dagger\Delta)^T\otimes R.$$
A singular-value decomposition with special unitary weak frames writes $\phi=e^{i\psi}\operatorname{diag}(t_1,t_2)$, $t_i\ge0$. Its tilde has exchanged entries and phase $e^{-i\psi}$. A colour frame diagonalizes $\Sigma$ with real eigenvalues $\sigma_j$, $1\le j\le4$. The Gram matrix $Q$ is block diagonal on the eight weak-colour indices. Its blocks are $Q_{ij}=F_{ij}F_{ij}^\dagger$, where
$$F_{ij}=e^{i\psi}t_i[k_u+\sigma_j(k_\nu-k_u)]
       +e^{-i\psi}t_{3-i}[k_d+\sigma_j(k_e-k_d)].$$
No two generation matrices need commute. Put $C_{ij}=\mathrm{tr}(R Q_{ij})$ and $r_m=\mathrm{tr}(R^m)$.

Let $d_1\ge d_2$ be the singular values of $\Delta$. For $\Delta\ne0$ set $z=(d_1^2+d_2^2)^2$, $h=d_1^2/(d_1^2+d_2^2)$ and $D(h)=h^2+(1-h)^2$. The weak and colour diagonals of the two normalized Gram matrices independently fill the convex hulls of the permutations of $(h,1-h)$ and $(h,1-h,0,0)$ by the Schur–Horn theorem. The transpose in the colour factor keeps these real diagonals. At fixed $z,h$ the mixed trace is $z\sum p_i q_j C_{ij}$, bilinear on the two hulls. A minimum is attained at a pair of vertices. For such permutations $\pi$ write $C_\pi(h)=\sum p_i q_j C_{ij}$. Every pair is realized by a rectangular singular-value decomposition.

The connector-dependent polynomial in $V/A$ is
$$\tfrac12 z^2r_2D(h)^2-z[c r_1-2C_\pi(h)].$$
If $r_2>0$, its minimum over $z\ge0$ subtracts $[c r_1-2C_\pi(h)]_+^2/(2r_2D(h)^2)$ from the Dirac part $\mathrm{tr}(Q^2-2cQ)$. Maximize $[c r_1-2C_\pi(h)]/D(h)$ on $1/2\le h\le1$, then use its positive part. Both polynomials have degree at most two. The cubic terms in the derivative numerator cancel, so only the endpoints and the real roots of a polynomial of degree at most two need checking. A constant quotient permits the whole interval. If $R=0$ the connector drops out. This eliminates the connector exactly, including competing rank-two configurations. For fixed $\phi,\Sigma$ and a nonzero rank-one minimizer, $h=1$ picks a smallest $C_{ij}$. All minimizing weak and colour lines have their product of diagonal supports within the cells attaining that value. A strict minimum at a single cell fixes both lines relative to the chosen eigenframes. Ties can leave either orientation free. $\square$

**An open set with vanishing connector.** Suppose $R\ne0$, $T=(k_u+k_d)(k_u+k_d)^\dagger\ne0$ and
$$g-\frac{(\mathrm{tr}\,T)^2}{\mathrm{tr}(T^2)}<\frac1{32}.$$
Every attained global minimum has $\Delta=0$, and allowing $\Delta$ leaves the infimum equal to its value in the $\Delta=0$ sector. To see this, first relax $K$ to an arbitrary positive matrix at fixed $Q$. Completing the square in $K$ gives its minimizer $(c1-2Q)_+$. Each eigenvalue $q$ of $Q$ then contributes $(q-c)^2-\tfrac12[c-2q]_+^2$ to $V/A+8gc^2$. This is nonnegative, and for $q\le c/2$ it equals $c^2/2-q^2\ge c^2/4$. The trial $\Sigma=\Delta=0$, $\phi=t1_2$, optimized over $t$, has deficit $8c^2[g-(\mathrm{tr}\,T)^2/\mathrm{tr}(T^2)]<c^2/4$. Every point as good as this trial must therefore have $2Q>c1$. For such $Q$, deleting any nonzero $K$ strictly lowers $V$. This argument also applies to minimizing sequences. When $T$ is scalar positive the bound is attained with $Q=c1$. For example take $k_u=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)\oplus(2)$, $k_d=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,1)-k_u$ at $g=3$. Both matrices are invertible and fail to commute. Here $T=1_3$. The strict inequality persists on an open neighbourhood, with arbitrary $k_\nu,k_e$.

**A family with degenerate relative orientations.** At $g=3$ take a rank-two flavour projection $P$ diagonal in the Majorana basis. Let $k_u,k_d$ be arbitrary unitaries on $P$, extended by zero, $k_\nu=-k_u$, $k_e=-k_d$, and $k_R=\kappa(1-P)$ with $\kappa\ne0$. Take $\phi=\sqrt c\,ab^\dagger$ with unit weak vectors and $\Sigma=vv^\dagger$ with a unit colour vector. The tilde bidoublet acts on the complementary weak lines, and $1-2\Sigma$ is unitary. Thus $Q=c1_8\otimes P$, while $K$ is supported on $1-P$, and the mixed term vanishes for all choices of $\Delta$. Throughout this flavour family $\mathrm{rank}\,Q\le16$ and $\mathrm{rank}\,K\le4$. The two separate squares give $V/A\ge-16c^2-2c^2$. Equality is attained with $d_1=d_2=d$ and $|\kappa|^2d^4=c$. The overlap of $\Sigma$ with the connector's colour two-plane takes every value in $[0,1]$ on these global minima. It is invariant under simultaneous changes of colour frame.

On the proposed rank-one connector locus the exact minimum instead is $V/A=-16c^2-c^2/2$, attained with $|\kappa|^2d_1^4=c$, $d_2=0$. The two weak lines and the two colour lines can each have any squared overlap in $[0,1]$, independently. This includes fully aligned and fully misaligned vacua of the restricted potential with all three fields nonzero. The larger field space lowers the minimum through a second connector singular value. Gauge symmetry and the printed polynomial alone therefore do not prescribe the two lines. Specific measured Yukawa matrices, constraints on field radii and loop corrections are additional choices. The orientation test above applies directly when matrices are specified. [Composite action, section VIII](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.8050)
