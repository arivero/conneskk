# Global minima of the one-generation composite Pati–Salam potential

**Theorem.** Use the composite scalar fields of section VIII of Chamseddine–Connes–van Suijlekom, one generation, with $A=F_0/(2\pi^2)>0$, $B=F_2/(2\pi^2)>0$. Allow the full complex bidoublet $\phi$, the $2\times4$ field $\Delta$, and the Hermitian $1+15$ field $\Sigma$. If $k_R\ne0$ and any Dirac Yukawa is nonzero, **every global minimum has $\Delta=0$**. Thus the unrestricted minimum supplies neither of the two connector-line alignments of Lemma 50. [Printed potential, pp. 20–22 and Fig. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.8050) · [Finite line conditions](../../lemmas/50_majorana_line_and_top_block.md)

**Proof.** Use the eight right indices $(\dot a,I)$. With a choice of matrix transpose for $\phi$, the dressed Dirac map into that space is
$$S=\phi\otimes[k_u1+(k_\nu-k_u)\Sigma]+\widetilde\phi\otimes[k_d1+(k_e-k_d)\Sigma],\qquad \widetilde\phi=\sigma_2\bar\phi\sigma_2.$$
Write $H_{\dot a I,\dot b J}=k_R\Delta_{\dot a J}\Delta_{\dot b I}$, $Q=SS^\dagger$, and $K$ for the Gram matrix of $H$ on its contracted right index, using adjoints so that the mixed contraction in section VIII is $\mathrm{tr}(KQ)$. In row-right-index convention $K=HH^\dagger$. Both forms are positive. With $c=B/A$ the printed polynomial is
$$V/A=\tfrac12\mathrm{tr}\,K^2+2\mathrm{tr}(KQ)+\mathrm{tr}\,Q^2-c\,\mathrm{tr}(K+2Q).$$
Consequently
$$V/A+8c^2=\tfrac12\mathrm{tr}\big[(Q-c1)^2+(Q+K-c1)^2\big]+\mathrm{tr}(KQ)\ \ge0.$$
The last trace is the trace of $K^{1/2}QK^{1/2}$. Choose $\Delta=0$, $\Sigma=0$ for a nonzero quark Yukawa pair or $\Sigma=1$ for a nonzero lepton pair. With $\phi=t e^{i\psi}1_2$ the dressed map is $t(k_1e^{i\psi}+k_2e^{-i\psi})1_8$. Choose the phase with nonzero coefficient, then $t$ to give $Q=c1$. Equality is attained. At equality the first two squares force $Q=c1$ and $K=0$. Finally $\mathrm{tr}\,K=|k_R|^2(\sum|\Delta_{\dot a I}|^2)^2$, so $\Delta=0$. For all four Dirac Yukawas zero the singular values $d_1,d_2$ of $\Delta$ instead give $V=A|k_R|^4(\sum d_i^4)^2/2-B|k_R|^2(\sum d_i^2)^2$. At nonzero optimum both singular values are equal by Cauchy–Schwarz; the minimum is $-2B^2/A$, compared with $-B^2/(2A)$ on the rank-one locus. If $k_R=0$, $\Delta$ drops out. $\square$

**Relative orientations on the rank-one locus.** Let $\Delta=d\,uv^T$, $\Sigma=s\,pp^\dagger$ and $\phi=t\,a b^\dagger$ with unit vectors in the corresponding factors (use the conjugate colour frame for $v$ when it labels a dual). Then $x=|\langle u,a\rangle|^2$, $y=|\langle v,p\rangle|^2$. Put $L_j(y)=|k_j|^2+y(|k_j+s(k'_j-k_j)|^2-|k_j|^2)$ for $(j,j')=(u,\nu),(d,e)$. At fixed norms all orientation dependence is the term
$$2A|k_R|^2d^4t^2\,[xL_u(y)+(1-x)L_d(y)].$$
Its extrema are decided by the four corner values; ties can give flat edges. At an interior critical point with nonzero mixed coefficient its two Hessian eigenvalues have opposite signs in these overlap coordinates. For example $k_\nu=k_u$ and $k_e=k_d$ remove the $y$ dependence. The order-one and unbroken-circle conditions remain meaningful restrictions on finite data independently of this energy.

**Hessian at the published example.** The Fig. 1 caption sets $k_\nu=k_e=1$, $k_R=k_u=k_d=2$, $\phi_{11}=1/2$, $\Delta_{11}=1/\sqrt2$, $\Sigma_{11}=2$, other entries zero. Vary only $\Sigma_{11}=2+\delta$. The dressed bidoublet is $(1/2)1_2\otimes(2I-\Sigma)$. Its two lepton squares become $\delta^2/4$ and the mixed trace acquires $\delta^2/4$. Thus $\Delta V=(A/2-B)\delta^2+A\delta^4/8$. Radial stationarity of $\Delta_{11}$ at the quoted value already requires $B=A$, giving a negative second derivative $-A$. The figure examined the Hessian in the $\Delta$ directions. Three noncommuting generation matrices, extra scalar couplings, radiative corrections or prescribed radial constraints define other minimization problems; in particular saturation by a scalar bidoublet must be rechecked for three generations.
