# The hypercharge projection at a free electroweak vacuum

**Proposition.** Fix the relative gauge normalization of the full Standard Model module, $Q=T_3+Y$, and a common matching scale. In the one-doublet effective action with independent positive Higgs quadratic coefficient and fixed positive quartic, the tree-level observable $\alpha_* B_Y$ takes every positive value at fixed spectral gauge moment $f_0$. The repository's flat interval also gives an explicit continuous family for the pole version of this observable.

**Proof.** In charge-connection coordinates a neutral Higgs VEV $v/\sqrt 2$ gives kinetic form $K=\operatorname{diag}(g^{-2},g_Y^{-2})$ and mass form $H=(v^2/4)(1,-1)^T(1,-1)$. The charged mass is $gv/2$. Solving $Ha=m^2Ka$ gives a photon along $(1,1)$, $e^{-2}=g^{-2}+g_Y^{-2}$, and $M_Z^2=(g^2+g_Y^2)v^2/4$. Hypercharge acts as $Y=1/2$ on the Higgs doublet. Its canonically normalized diagonal projection is $B_Y^2=H_{YY}/K_{YY}=g_Y^2v^2/4$. The neutral propagator has two poles.

At matching let $g^2=g_3^2=5g_Y^2/3=\pi^2/(2f_0)$. For a reference mass $h>0$ the signed residual is
$$
\Delta_*(v)=\alpha_* B_Y-h
=\frac{3\pi^2}{128}\sqrt{\frac{3}{10}}\frac{v}{f_0^{3/2}}-h.
$$
Its slope at fixed $f_0$ is strictly positive. Thus the full set giving $h=313\ {\rm MeV}$ is the curve $v=128h f_0^{3/2}/(3\pi^2\sqrt{3/10})$. At fixed charge it gives one fitted VEV. For a potential $-m^2|\phi|^2+\lambda|\phi|^4$, the varying parameter is $m^2=\lambda v^2$. A full spectral potential with independently fixed moments could supply an extra equation. The comparison holds $h$ fixed as an external mass.

For an independent pole check take the one-coupling interval at $\theta=\pi/4$, with fixed five-dimensional coefficient $g_5^2$ in the convention $g^2=g_5^2/\ell$. The lowest poles are $(M_W,M_Z)=(\pi/4,\arctan2)/\ell$. Hence with $b=\sqrt{(\arctan2)^2-(\pi/4)^2}$ the pole product is $3g_5^2 b/(32\pi\ell^2)$. Choosing $\ell^2=3g_5^2 b/(32\pi h)$ fits any positive $h$ at tree level. Here the kinetic angle is fixed while the ratio of the pole masses differs from its one-doublet value. The wall specifies the angle and leaves the length free. $\square$ [Interval poles](../23_exact_w_z_masses.md) · [Berger normalization](../47_berger_radius_at_matching.md)

**Running and the independent benchmark.** The two formulas use tree matching with zero finite thresholds. With renormalized single-doublet parameters define $\delta_i=M_{i,\rm OS}^2-M_{i,\rm tree}^2$ in the chosen pole or line-shape convention. Then $B_{\rm OS}^2=B_Y^2+\delta_Z-\delta_W$, and $\alpha(0)/\alpha_*$ supplies a second conversion. The 2026 line-shape inputs in the issue give $\alpha(0)B_{\rm OS}=314.473$ MeV, compared with $H_N=(m_p+m_n)/6=312.973$ MeV and $M_\ell=\sum m_\ell/6=313.850$ MeV. Fitting the first comparison leaves the second independent. The geometry supplies no theoretical uncertainty for a low-energy prediction from this fit. On the heavy path with fixed $\Lambda_5$ in issue 18, two-sided logarithmic screening gives $\alpha_{\rm low} g_Y(\mu_*)v/(2\Lambda_5)=\Theta(v/[\Lambda_5\log^{3/2}(v/\Lambda_5)])$; this concerns the matching proxy.
