# The hypercharge projection at a stationary spectral vacuum

**Proposition.** Fix the relative gauge normalization of the full Standard Model module, $Q=T_3+Y$, and a common matching scale. In the one-doublet effective action with independent positive Higgs quadratic coefficient and fixed positive quartic, the tree-level observable $\alpha_* B_Y$ takes every positive value at fixed spectral gauge moment $f_0$. The repository's flat interval also gives an explicit continuous family for the pole version of this observable.

**Proof.** In charge-connection coordinates a neutral Higgs VEV $v/\sqrt 2$ gives kinetic form $K=\operatorname{diag}(g^{-2},g_Y^{-2})$ and mass form $H=(v^2/4)(1,-1)^T(1,-1)$. The charged mass is $gv/2$. Solving $Ha=m^2Ka$ gives a photon along $(1,1)$, $e^{-2}=g^{-2}+g_Y^{-2}$, and $M_Z^2=(g^2+g_Y^2)v^2/4$. Hypercharge acts as $Y=1/2$ on the Higgs doublet. Its canonically normalized diagonal projection is $B_Y^2=H_{YY}/K_{YY}=g_Y^2v^2/4$. The neutral propagator has two poles.

At matching let $g^2=g_3^2=5g_Y^2/3=\pi^2/(2f_0)$. For a reference mass $h>0$ the signed residual is
$$
\Delta_*(v)=\alpha_* B_Y-h
=\frac{3\pi^2}{128}\sqrt{\frac{3}{10}}\frac{v}{f_0^{3/2}}-h.
$$
Its slope at fixed $f_0$ is strictly positive. Thus the full set giving $h=313\ {\rm MeV}$ is the curve $v=128h f_0^{3/2}/(3\pi^2\sqrt{3/10})$. At fixed charge it gives one fitted VEV. For a potential $-m^2|\phi|^2+\lambda|\phi|^4$, the varying parameter is $m^2=\lambda v^2$. A full spectral potential with independently fixed moments could supply an extra equation. The comparison holds $h$ fixed as an external mass.

For an independent pole check take the one-coupling interval at $\theta=\pi/4$, with fixed five-dimensional coefficient $g_5^2$ in the convention $g^2=g_5^2/\ell$. The lowest poles are $(M_W,M_Z)=(\pi/4,\arctan2)/\ell$. Hence with $b=\sqrt{(\arctan2)^2-(\pi/4)^2}$ the pole product is $3g_5^2 b/(32\pi\ell^2)$. Choosing $\ell^2=3g_5^2 b/(32\pi h)$ fits any positive $h$ at tree level. Here the kinetic angle is fixed while the ratio of the pole masses differs from its one-doublet value. The wall specifies the angle and leaves the length free. $\square$ [Interval poles](../23_exact_w_z_masses.md) · [Berger normalization](../47_berger_radius_at_matching.md)

**Running and the independent benchmark.** The two formulas use tree matching with zero finite thresholds. With renormalized single-doublet parameters define $\delta_i=M_{i,\rm OS}^2-M_{i,\rm tree}^2$ in the chosen pole or line-shape convention. Then $B_{\rm OS}^2=B_Y^2+\delta_Z-\delta_W$, and $\alpha(0)/\alpha_*$ supplies a second conversion. The 2026 line-shape inputs in the issue give $\alpha(0)B_{\rm OS}=314.473$ MeV, compared with $H_N=(m_p+m_n)/6=312.973$ MeV and $M_\ell=\sum m_\ell/6=313.850$ MeV. Fitting the first comparison leaves the second independent. The geometry supplies no theoretical uncertainty for a low-energy prediction from this fit. On the heavy path with fixed $\Lambda_5$ in issue 18, two-sided logarithmic screening gives $\alpha_{\rm low} g_Y(\mu_*)v/(2\Lambda_5)=\Theta(v/[\Lambda_5\log^{3/2}(v/\Lambda_5)])$; this concerns the matching proxy. [PDG electroweak conventions](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2026/reviews/rpp2026-rev-standard-model.pdf) · [Heavy trajectory](https://github.com/arivero/conneskk/issues/18)

## The stationary Higgs norm of the finite spectral action

**Proposition (through order $\Lambda^0$, at tree level in flat space).** Use the Standard Model finite operator with three right-handed neutrinos, and fix $\Lambda$, the cutoff moments $f_0,f_2,f_4$, the Dirac matrices, and the singular values of the symmetric Majorana matrix $Y_R$. Let $a,b$ denote the colour-weighted Dirac traces of degree two and four in equation (3.16) of Chamseddine–Connes–Marcolli. Put $A=Y_\nu^\dagger Y_\nu$, $B=Y_R^\dagger Y_R$, and $e_R=\mathrm{tr}(AB)$. Write $v=2M_W/g_2$ and use their scalar kinetic term $|D\mathbf H|^2/2$. The minimum in the classical Higgs norm at fixed finite operator is
$$
v^2=\frac{[\,2\Lambda^2 f_2/f_0-e_R/a\,]_+}{2\lambda_0},
\qquad \lambda_0=\frac{\pi^2 b}{2 f_0a^2},\qquad B_Y=g_Yv/2 .
$$
Here $[z]_+=\max(0,z)$ and $a,b,f_0>0$. This selects a vacuum at given microscopic input before using a low-energy mass.

**Proof.** The canonically normalized polynomial is $-\mu_0^2|\mathbf H|^2+\lambda_0|\mathbf H|^4$ with $\mu_0^2=2\Lambda^2f_2/f_0-e_R/a$ and $|\mathbf H|=v$ in the neutral vacuum. Let $s_i,t_i$ be the increasingly ordered eigenvalues of $A,B$ for $i=1,2,3$. Takagi factorization realizes every unitary orientation of $B$ by a symmetric $Y_R$. The matrix of squared moduli of the relative unitary is doubly stochastic. Rearrangement bounds $e_R$ between $\sum_i s_i t_{4-i}$ and $\sum_i s_i t_i$, both attained by permutations. Connectedness gives the whole interval. The gauge, Einstein and cosmological coefficients depend only on fixed moments and the separate traces $\mathrm{tr}\,B,\mathrm{tr}\,B^2$. The broken part of the displayed interval therefore gives a continuous residual at fixed gravitational and gauge normalization whenever the mixed trace varies. If the orientation itself is varied in computing the global vacuum of the polynomial, its term at $v>0$ has positive coefficient and the minimum uses the lower trace endpoint. Light seesaw masses can change with this orientation. The leading Majorana-scale equation in Lemma 5.2 uses $v\ll\Lambda$ and fixes a scale at a specified matrix shape. Exact scale stationarity at finite $v$ would also vary the mixed term. $\square$ [Chamseddine, Connes and Marcolli, §§3.5, 4.2, 5.3](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0610241)

**Proposition (exact flat constant-field trace).** Keep all gauge fields zero on Euclidean $\mathbb R^4$, take the Higgs constant with norm $t$, and use the full trace per unit volume with a nonnegative even cutoff written as $F(D^2/\Lambda^2)$. Assume $F$ is bounded near zero and $\int_0^\infty uF(u)\,du<\infty$. The exact bosonic trace is nonincreasing in $t$. If one charged Yukawa is nonzero and $F$ has positive integral on every tail, it decreases strictly. On this ray the full positive cutoff action has no isolated finite Higgs minimum.

**Proof.** The charged masses scale as $t$. In a two-component basis the neutrino mass matrix is $N(t)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&tY\\tY^T&Y_R\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, with fixed normalized Dirac coefficient $Y$. For $t_2>t_1>0$, $N(t_2)=L N(t_1)L^T$ with $L=\mathrm{diag}((t_2/t_1)I,I)$. Since $\|L^{-1}\|=1$, the singular-value inequality applied to the inverse congruence shows that every ordered singular value is nondecreasing. This extends to zero by continuity. For each squared mass $x\Lambda^2$ the momentum integral per component is
$$
\frac{\Lambda^4}{16\pi^2}\int_x^\infty (u-x)F(u)\,du.
$$
Its derivative is $-\Lambda^4\int_x^\infty F(u)\,du/(16\pi^2)$. Multiplicities are positive. With compact support there can be a plateau. The stationary polynomial and the full trace thus give different vacuum tests when the omitted terms matter. A Wilsonian effective potential with renormalized coefficients, curvature or additional interactions requires its own vacuum calculation. $\square$ [Finite mass operator, eq. (5.16)](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0610241#S5.E16)

## A charged-lepton threshold residual at a fixed stationary vacuum

**Proposition (one-loop matching).** Keep one broken polynomial vacuum above, including the Majorana orientation, all quark and neutrino matrices, and the common high-scale gauge coefficients. Vary only the three squared charged-lepton singular values $x_i$ with fixed $S=\sum x_i$ and $T=\sum x_i^2$. This preserves all five scalar traces $a,b,c,d,e_R$, the stationary $v$, and the matched $B_Y$. At tree mass matching $m_i=k\sqrt{x_i}$ with common fixed $k$. For $S^2/2<T<S^2$ a positive hierarchical branch reaches arbitrarily small $m_1$. With $X=x_1$ the other two are
$$
x_{2,3}=\tfrac12\left(S-X\mp\sqrt{2T-S^2+2SX-3X^2}\right),
\qquad P(X)=x_1x_2x_3=X\left[X^2-SX+(S^2-T)/2\right].
$$
These expressions follow by fixing the sum and pair product. Both larger roots are positive at $X=0$, and $P'(X)=(X-x_2)(X-x_3)>0$ on the strictly ordered light branch.

Choose the same matched $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ QED charge above all lepton thresholds for each point. Match continuously at each positive lepton mass. Common strong and electroweak threshold inputs, including the hadronic vacuum polarization, enter the reference $A_0=\alpha(0;X_0)^{-1}$. Using the one-loop Dirac coefficient $d\alpha^{-1}/d\log\mu=-2/(3\pi)$ per unit-charge species gives
$$
A(X)=A_0+\frac1{3\pi}\log\frac{P(X_0)}{P(X)},\qquad
\frac{\alpha(0;X)B_Y}{H_N}-1=\frac{B_Y}{H_N A(X)}-1.
$$
The denominator $H_N$ is an independent hadronic input in this comparison. For the line-shape observable replace $B_Y$ by $\sqrt{B_Y^2+\delta_Z-\delta_W}$, with the $\delta_i$ defined above, including running from the parameter scale to the mass convention. This yields the residual as a function of a finite Dirac datum even with a stationary Higgs scale. $\square$ [QED matching and beta coefficients, §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.03265) · [Electroweak inputs and hadronic vacuum polarization](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2026/reviews/rpp2026-rev-standard-model.pdf)

**Numerical check (Python 3).** To measure the size of the freedom, calibrate $A_0$, the running-width $B_{\rm OS}$ and $H_N$ at the measured reference of issue 19. Hold the last two conversions fixed in the low-energy comparison. Equality to $H_N$ requires $\Delta A=B_{\rm OS}/H_N-A_0=0.65675$. The lepton **mass product** must be $e^{-3\pi\Delta A/2}=0.04528$ times its reference value. The positive solution of the cubic gives masses $(0.023138,\ 105.659613,\ 1776.930000)$ MeV. With the lepton sum reserved throughout, its independent value here is $M_\ell/H_N=1.002543$. At the reference point the corresponding two residuals were $+0.4793\%$ for the pole product and $+0.2802\%$ for the lepton sum. Using the real pole masses obtained from the quoted widths instead gives $m_e=0.03085$ MeV. The deformed electron masses are counterexamples in parameter space. The measured electron mass is $0.510999$ MeV. [Masses, widths and conventions](https://github.com/arivero/conneskk/issues/19)

At the line-shape crossing, the quoted uncorrelated $W,Z$ errors propagate to a multiplicative experimental uncertainty of $1.25$ on the fitted $m_e$. In the interval between the two electron thresholds the two-loop QED beta term is about $3\alpha/(4\pi)=0.00174$ of the one-loop integrand. By itself this shifts $\alpha B_{\rm OS}$ by about $0.0026$ MeV at fixed deformation. This estimates one specified truncation effect. Running Yukawas, finite pole conversions and the electromagnetic response of the nucleon are additional inputs for a full uncertainty. In the ratio above a fractional change in $B_Y/H_N$ propagates linearly, and an additional finite inverse-charge shift $\delta A$ gives fractional shift $-\delta A/A$ at first order. A first-principles 313 MeV prediction requires fixing the lepton determinant, the ultraviolet and hadronic matching, and a vacuum of the chosen action. The heavy trajectory with fixed $\Lambda_5$ described above varies different inputs.

At these fixed moments, the charged trace for a decreasing cutoff is minimized at the upper determinant endpoint. An explicit constrained wall with a radial potential gives an interior minimum whose determinant varies with an independent wall coupling. The threshold residual above still holds at the fixed Higgs norm with the same common matching inputs. [Determinant calculation and wall test](25.md)
