# The shared Hopf circle, the semileptonic contact and the strong matching coefficient

We follow one charged state and one pair of charged-current sources along the spin Hopf family of 94, 97 and 100: the compact weak fit, the finite neck at $b=0.001$ and the uniform weak shrink. Fixed are the spin and charge lift of 94 (circle period six, Hopf weights, auxiliary charges and the volume-tensor vacuum), the five-form and tensor coefficients of 97 ($Z_6=0.49604$, $f_S=5000$ GeV unless varied), the horizontal fermion domain and holomorphic mass links of 100, the mirror prescription of 100 and the four-dimensional Einstein frame of 97. What varies is the weak geometry along the path, and, as a sensitivity test, the locking scale $f_S$ and the finite strong matching coefficient. The observables are the eigenvalue of the photon generator on each state, the photon residue $e_*^2$, the normalized charged-current propagator between left lepton and left quark doublet sources at fixed $q/H_N$, its contact limit, $\Xi=G_FH_N^2$, and the ratios $M_C/H_N$ and (first omitted coloured particle)$/H_N$. [Issue 29 and the closure plan](https://github.com/arivero/conneskk/issues/29#issuecomment-7189858542)

The numerical checks below use Python 3 with NumPy and SciPy through [`paper/hopf_semileptonic_ratio.py`](../../paper/hopf_semileptonic_ratio.py), which rebuilds the pencils of [`paper/hopf_transverse_currents.py`](../../paper/hopf_transverse_currents.py).

## The photon generator on both restrictions

In the issue's labelling, directions $5,\dots,9$ are the five-sphere of radius $B$ with the Hopf circle as direction $9$, and directions $10,11$ are the weak sphere. Let $K_3$ be the axial weak rotation with its magnetic compensator, $P$ the active Hopf weight and $C$ the auxiliary generator of 94. The vortex connection $V$ of 100 is a fourth Abelian generator.

**Proposition 29.1.** Let the background on a member of the family be invariant under $K_3,P,C,V$ (with compensators), and let the condensates be the dissolved vortex $\Phi$ with weights $(K_3,P,C,V)=(0,0,0,2)$, the two roots of weights $(\mp\tfrac12,\pm3,0,v_\pm)$ and the volume-tensor vacuum $S=s_0\Omega$ of weights $(0,3,-2,0)$. Then the unbroken combination of these four generators is unique up to scale and, normalized by its $K_3$ coefficient, equals
$$
 Q=K_3+\frac P6+\frac C4=T_3+Y,\qquad Y=\frac{P+\tfrac32C}{6}.
$$
Its eigenvalue on every fermion of the table of 94 is the integer or third-integer electric charge of that state, and it is the same on every member of the family. The photon residue $e_*^2$ is a modulus of the family. Along the path of 97 it is held at its compact value by the auxiliary norm $I_C$.

**Proof.** Write a candidate as $\alpha K_3+\beta P+\gamma C+\delta V$. Invariance of $\Phi$ gives $2\delta=0$. Invariance of the root of weights $(-\tfrac12,3,0,v_+)$ then gives $-\alpha/2+3\beta=0$, so $\beta=\alpha/6$; the conjugate root gives the same equation. Invariance of $S$ gives $3\beta-2\gamma=0$, so $\gamma=\alpha/4$. The three conditions are independent, so the kernel is one-dimensional, and $\alpha=1$ gives $Q$. The coefficients are fixed by the condensate weights alone and contain no modulus. On a fermion of weak weight $T_3$ and weights $(m,q_C)$ the eigenvalue is $T_3+(m+\tfrac32q_C)/6$. The lepton doublet $(m,q_C)=(\tfrac32,-3)$ gives $Y=-\tfrac12$, the quark doublet $(\tfrac52,-1)$ gives $\tfrac16$, the singlets $(\tfrac52,1)$, $(\tfrac52,-3)$, $(\tfrac32,-5)$, $(\tfrac32,-1)$ give $\tfrac23,-\tfrac13,-1,0$. These weights are eigenvalues of a fixed torus action on fixed bundles. Along a continuous family they vary continuously within a discrete set, so they are constant. The exactness of the massless photon on each member is the statement of 97.1 that the constant $U=1$, $p=b_F=1/6$, $c=1/4$ has zero mass form; the neutral solver checks it at each evaluation of a compact member. The residue is $e_*^2=1/(CVa^2N_\gamma)$, with $N_\gamma$ the Gram norm of that configuration, and it depends on $CV,a$, the shape and $I_C$. The trajectory of 97 recomputes $I_C$ from the fixed photon norm on each member: $I_C=124.20$ at the compact fit and $157.50$ at the neck. $\square$ [Charge lift](../94_spin_hopf_trajectory_and_its_charge_lift.md#global-charge-on-the-hopf-circle) · [Exact photon](../97_hopf_transverse_spectrum_and_current_match.md#the-hopf-metric-and-its-five-form-profile)

The two restrictions of the issue are then the following. On the colour side (directions $5,\dots,9$ with the auxiliary group) the photon acts through $P/6+C/4=Y$. On the electroweak side (directions $9,10,11$) it acts through $K_3+P/6$ together with $C/4$. The Hopf weight $P$ enters both, with coefficient $1/6$ fixed by the root weights. Both decompositions give the same eigenvalue $T_3+Y$ on the same state. The residue is a calibrated input, transported at fixed value.

## The five-dimensional end: twisted circle against product

**Proposition 29.2.** (a) Let $Y\to\mathbb{CP}^2\times S^2_r$ be a principal circle bundle with Chern connection $\eta$, of degree $d\ge1$ on the weak sphere of radius $r$, with metric $g_{\mathbb{CP}^2}+g_{S^2_r}+\rho^2\eta^2$. A minimally coupled scalar of fibre momentum $n\ne0$ satisfies
$$
 M_n^2\ \ge\ \frac{n^2}{\rho^2}+|n|\,\frac{d}{2r^2}.
$$
At fixed $\rho$ and fixed energy $E$, every $n\ne0$ leaves the window $M\le E$ once $2r^2E^2<d$. The surviving $n=0$ spectrum is the spectrum on $\mathbb{CP}^2\times S^2_r$, whose levels below $E$ for small $r$ are those of the constant weak harmonic on $\mathbb{CP}^2$.

(b) On the product $S^5_B\times S^2_r$ the scalar spectrum is $\ell(\ell+4)/B^2+j(j+1)/r^2$. At fixed $B$ the $j=0$ levels, including every Hopf weight $|m|\le\ell$, keep fixed masses as $r\to0$.

**Proof.** (a) The Rayleigh quotient of $e^{in\vartheta}u$ is $\|\nabla_nu\|^2/\|u\|^2+n^2/\rho^2$. The Kähler identity of Exercise 9 of the lecture gives $\nabla_n^*\nabla_n\ge i\Lambda F_n=|n|(2b_2+b_1)$ with $b_1=2\pi d/\mathcal A_1$ and $\mathcal A_1=4\pi r^2$, so $b_1=d/(2r^2)$. Dropping $2b_2\ge0$ gives the bound. For $n=0$ the function is pulled back from the base and the base Laplacian separates; levels with nonconstant weak harmonic have mass at least $\sqrt2/r$. (b) The Laplacian of a Riemannian product is the sum of the factor Laplacians. The degree-$\ell$ harmonics on $S^5$ decompose under the Hopf circle into weights $|m|\le\ell$ with $\ell-m$ even. $\square$ [Circle-bundle bound](../../paper/kk_strings_lecture.tex) · [Product comparison](../../paper/seven_m_theory.tex)

The spin Hopf family is the product case (b): 94 builds it on the smooth spin product $S^5\times S^2$, with the Hopf circle inside the five-sphere. Its uniform shrink therefore keeps the shared ninth direction in the bosonic spectrum, separately from the twisted collapse of (a). For the matter fields the horizontal domain of 94 removes the vertical Dirac mass $|m|/B$ by prescription. On the light fermions the ninth direction is present only through the charge $P$ in $Y$, and Proposition 29.1 shows that this charge is the same on both sides.

## The charged-current propagator between lepton and quark sources

Let $H,K$ be the mass and kinetic forms of the photon-free charged raising pencil of 97, and $j_\ell,j_q$ the sources of the left lepton and left quark doublets, with common weak generator factors removed. For Euclidean momentum $q$ put
$$
 G(q)=(H+a^2q^2K)^{-1},\qquad C_{ij}(q)=j_i^{\sf T}G(q)\,j_j,\qquad
 R(q)=\frac{C_{\ell q}(q)}{C_{\ell\ell}(q)} .
$$
With aligned flavours ($V_{ud}=1$) and the electron and muon doublets having equal sources, $R(0)=C_{e,ud}/(V_{ud}C_{e,\mu})$ is the semileptonic-to-leptonic contact ratio requested in the issue discussion. It uses only the five weak inputs of 97 and no semileptonic rate.

**Proposition 29.3.** On every member with $a/B>0$ the source difference $\Delta=j_q-j_\ell$ is supported on the three Abelian charge-one slots of 97.1 (Hopf metric, auxiliary Maxwell, five-form), where it equals $(1,2,0)\otimes w$ with $w\ne0$ the common circle overlap. Consequently, for every real $q$,
$$
 C_{\ell\ell}(q)+C_{qq}(q)-2C_{\ell q}(q)=\Delta^{\sf T}G(q)\Delta>0,
 \qquad
 R(q)-1=\frac{j_\ell^{\sf T}G(q)\Delta}{C_{\ell\ell}(q)} .
$$
The identity leaves the sign of $R-1$ open.

**Proof.** By 97.1 the charged raising sources are the ordinary weak-density overlaps on the weak stream components, plus the overlaps on the three Abelian slots multiplied by $(m,q_C,0)$. The two left doublets have the same vortex degree $N=2$ and the same spin labels, so their weak densities, and hence their stream components and the circle overlap $w$, coincide. Their weights $(\tfrac32,-3)$ and $(\tfrac52,-1)$ differ by $(1,2)$. The vector $w$ collects the overlaps of the positive weak density $fp$ with the charge-one basis profiles, multiplied by $a/B>0$. Its first entry pairs that density with the positive lowest profile $\operatorname{sech}t$, so $w\ne0$. $H$ is positive definite on the photon-free charged sector and $K$ is positive, so $G(q)$ is positive definite, and $\Delta\ne0$ gives the strict inequality. The expression for $R-1$ is the definition with $j_q=j_\ell+\Delta$. $\square$

**Numerical calculation (Python 3, NumPy and SciPy).** At degree 8 and 12 of the Jacobi basis the compact values of the central fit agree to ten digits.

At $q=H_N=0.312$ GeV one has $R(H_N)-1=4.3325\times10^{-5}$ and $C_{\ell\ell}(H_N)/C_{\ell\ell}(0)=1-1.508\times10^{-5}$, which equals $(H_N/M_C)^2$ for $M_C=80.334$ GeV to the quoted digits. In the collapsed pencil the three Abelian blocks are absent, as 97 shows for the comoving limit, so $\Delta=0$ there.

## The colour side: renormalized coefficient and the hadronic unit

Write $I(\mu)=1/[4\pi\alpha_s(\mu)]$. The tree colour coefficient of 100 is $I_3^{\rm geom}=CVB^2/3$, and 108.5 writes the matching as $I(U)=I_3^{\rm geom}+\delta_{\rm match}(U)$ at $U=B_*^{-1}$. Result 100 identifies $I_3^{\rm geom}$ numerically with $I(250\ {\rm GeV})=0.76985$; this amounts to choosing $\delta_{\rm match}(U)=I(U)-0.76985$, which is $0.2740+\Delta_{\rm bos}$ for the spectator prescription and $0.1232+\Delta_{\rm bos}$ for the paired prescription in the one-loop running of 108.5. The two fermion prescriptions differ by $0.1508$.

The conversion to the hadronic unit uses two nonperturbative inputs: the three-flavour $\Lambda^{(3)}_{\overline{\rm MS}}=0.341(12)$ GeV of ALPHA and the isospin-symmetric nucleon mass $m_N=0.936(25)(22)$ GeV of BMW. Their ratio $c_N=H_N/\Lambda_3=0.915$, with about $5\%$ combined uncertainty, is the nonperturbative input that turns $\Lambda_3$ into $H_N$. In QCD with the light-quark masses held at fixed ratios to $\Lambda_3$ it is a pure number. [BMW nucleon mass](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.3599) · [ALPHA $\Lambda^{(3)}$](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03821)

**Proposition 29.4.** Fix $G_F$, the quark and boson threshold masses and $c_N$, and let the finite matching coefficient change by a constant $\delta$ at a scale above all retained thresholds. At one loop with continuous decoupling,
$$
 \frac{d\log\Xi}{d\delta}=-\frac{16\pi^2}{b_0^{(3)}}=-\frac{16\pi^2}{9}=-17.55 .
$$

**Proof.** At one loop $dI/d\log\mu=b_0^{(n_f)}/(8\pi^2)$ on each segment, and continuous matching at fixed threshold masses adds no constant. A constant shift $\delta$ above the thresholds therefore shifts $I(\mu)$ by $\delta$ at every lower scale. In the three-flavour segment $I(\mu)=(9/8\pi^2)\log(\mu/\Lambda_3)$, so $\delta\log\Lambda_3=-8\pi^2\delta/9$. With $c_N$ fixed, $\log H_N$ moves by the same amount and $\log\Xi=\log G_F+2\log H_N$ moves by twice it. $\square$

**Numerical calculation (Python 3, SciPy).** Running from $\alpha_s^{(6)}(250\ {\rm GeV})=0.10337$ with the $\overline{\rm MS}$ beta function to four loops, decoupling at $m_t=162.5$, $m_b=4.18$, $m_c=1.27$ GeV with the two-loop coefficient $11/72$, gives $\Lambda_3=0.3405$ GeV, which reproduces the $0.341$ GeV of 100. The slope $d\log\Lambda_3/dI(250\ {\rm GeV})$ is $-8.773$, $-8.072$, $-7.887$, $-7.804$ at one to four loops, so $d\log\Xi/dI=-15.61$ at four loops. The factors multiplying $\Xi$ for finite shifts $\delta$ of $I(250\ {\rm GeV})$ are

| $\delta$ | Origin | One loop | Four loops |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
| $\pm0.00415$ | one unit of $b_0$ across the window from $M_g$ to $U$, from 108.5 | $0.930$, $1.076$ | $0.937$, $1.067$ |
| $+0.1508$ | spectator against paired fermion logarithms, paired value as reference | $0.071$ | $0.089$ |
| $-0.1232$ | paired prescription with $\delta_{\rm match}(U)=0$ at fixed $B$ | $8.7$ | none: $\alpha_s>1$ above $m_c$ |
| $-0.2740$ | spectator prescription with $\delta_{\rm match}(U)=\Delta_{\rm bos}=0$ at fixed $B$ | $81$ | none: $\alpha_s>1$ above $m_b$ |

For the two negative shifts the running coupling is $\alpha_s(250\ {\rm GeV})=0.123$ and $0.161$, and the four-loop coupling leaves the perturbative domain before the charm threshold. At fixed $B$, a vanishing matching coefficient at $U$ is therefore incompatible with the observed three-flavour scale; the identification in 100 requires $\delta_{\rm match}(U)$ of order $0.1$–$0.3$. A shift defined at $U$ reaches $250$ GeV unchanged at one loop. At two loops in six-flavour running the flow derivative $\partial I(250\ {\rm GeV})/\partial I(U)=\beta_I(250\ {\rm GeV})/\beta_I(U)$ is $1.008$ at the compact $U=5.496$ TeV. To predict $\Xi$ with the $7\%$ precision of the lattice hadronic input, $\delta_{\rm match}$ must be known to about $0.004$, the size of one unit of $b_0$ across the boson window. The finite eleven-dimensional matching coefficient and the interacting coloured-vector multiplet are not computed in this repository, so $H_N$ is a calibrated input and the table of 100 remains the calibrated result.

At exact weak restoration the charged pole is massless, so the charged-current propagator has no contact limit and $\Xi$ is undefined. With the mirrors also massless, colour has twelve active flavours below the tower, $b_0=11-2\cdot12/3=3$ and $b_1=102-38\cdot12/3=-50$. The two-loop beta function then has an infrared zero at $\alpha_*=4\pi b_0/|b_1|=0.754$, a coupling too large for perturbation theory, and lattice studies of $SU(3)$ with twelve fundamental flavours have not established whether the massless theory confines. At the restored point there is therefore no $c_N$, and the conversion of $\Lambda$ to $H_N$ does not carry over. The comparison at that end needs the propagator at fixed $q$ in units of a scale defined in the twelve-flavour theory.

The physical reference is $\Xi_{\rm phys}=1.1425\times10^{-6}$ with $H_N=(m_p+m_n)/6$ and the muon lifetime. The held-out compact value is $1.0\%$ below it, inside the lattice uncertainty. Over $f_S=5$–$40$ TeV the held-out $\Xi$ moves from $1.1310$ to $1.1301\times10^{-6}$, by $0.08\%$. The two atlas paths of the issue serve as controls. With a fixed hadronic spectrum in GeV and $G_F\to0$, $\Xi\to0$; with every scale comoving at fixed dimensionless inputs, $\Xi$ is constant. On the Hopf path the hadronic unit comoves with the colour radius while the charged pole grows relative to it ($M_C/H_N$ from $257.5$ to $1.6\times10^5$), so $\Xi$ falls by a factor $2.4\times10^{-7}$. Relative to the colour scale the Hopf path is a weak-decoupling path of the first control.

## What is established and what stays open

Proposition 29.1 gives the same charge $Q=T_3+Y$ on the same state from both restrictions on every member, with the Hopf weight shared, and the photon residue as a calibrated modulus held by $I_C$. Proposition 29.2 places the Hopf family on the product branch at the five-dimensional end, where the ninth direction survives, and separates it from the twisted collapse. Proposition 29.3 with its table gives a predicted semileptonic-to-leptonic ratio at fixed $f_S$, an explicit dependence on the free coefficient $f_S$, and a strictly positive nonuniversality of the lepton–quark pair on every member with $a/B>0$. Proposition 29.4 gives the explicit effect of the open strong matching coefficient on $\Xi$.

The closure criterion of the plan asks for a positive construction that predicts both normalizations. That criterion is not met. The strong matching coefficient $\delta_{\rm match}(U)$ and the boson-window coefficient are adjustable. Their effect on $\Xi$ is $d\log\Xi/d\delta=-15.6$ at four loops, a factor $0.94$ per unit of $b_0$ across the boson window, a factor $0.089$ between the two fermion prescriptions, and a nonperturbative three-flavour scale if $\delta_{\rm match}(U)$ is set to zero at fixed $B$. The table of 100 therefore remains the calibrated result, and the entry that becomes an independent prediction is $G_F$ in the held-out rows. The next calculations are the finite threshold coefficient of the coloured tower at $U$ in one scheme, the interacting coloured-vector contribution to $b_0$ between $M_g$ and $U$, and a dynamical selection of $f_S$.
