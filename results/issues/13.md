# The squashed twistor wall near the symmetric radius

**Theorem.** Vary the fibre radius $\rho$ of $\mathbb{CP}^3\to S^4$ at fixed unit base radius, homogeneous line connections and pull-back wall condition (a). Write
$$I(d)=(\sqrt{d^2+d+5/2}-d,\ \sqrt{d-1/2}).$$
For $\rho\in I(\sqrt5)=(0.8842\ldots,1.3176\ldots)$ the complete list of $\mathrm{Sp}(2)$ types on which either line operator can have $|D_k|\le3/(2\rho)$, $k=3,1$, consists of the defining $\mathbf4$. The effective wall has precisely one adjoint kernel copy of that type. Theorem 29.11 consequently gives eight states for sufficiently large mass at each such radius. The signed limiting fibre part is $-w(\rho)$ and $w>0$ throughout $I(\sqrt3)=(0.9572\ldots,1.1100\ldots)$ (decimal endpoints rounded).

**Proof.** Let $d_k(V,\rho)$ denote the least absolute eigenvalue on a nonempty isotypic space. Use the unitary rescaling of the vertical density. The homogeneous twisted Dirac operator there is $D_\rho=A_0+\rho^{-1}A_1+\rho A_2$. Horizontal parallel transport is by lifted isometries of the twisted two-sphere and intertwines its vertical Dirac operator. Horizontal Clifford multiplication anticommutes with the horizontal grading in $A_1=\gamma_5D_{\rm vert}$, so $\{A_0,A_1\}=0$. For the curvature bound, use the horizontal off-diagonal quaternionic matrices with upper-right entry $x/2$, $x=1,i,j,k$, and the two vertical unit rotations with lower diagonal entry $i_\alpha/2$ transverse to the isotropy circle. The lower diagonal bracket of $x,y$ is $(-\bar x y+\bar y x)/4$. Of the six unordered pairs four project to a unit vertical generator and two project to zero. Each ordered Clifford product is unitary, hence the curvature contraction with coefficient $1/8$ has $\|A_2\|\le1$. These conventions also give scalar curvature 12 at $\rho=1$. Let $t=|1/\rho-1|$. Anticommutation gives $\|A_1u\|\le\|(A_0+A_1)u\|\le\|D_1u\|+\|u\|$, where the subscript in $D_1$ here denotes **radius**. Subtraction of the two operators therefore gives, for $t\le1$,
$$d_k(V,\rho)\ge L(d,\rho)=(1-t)d-t-|\rho-1|,\qquad d=d_k(V,1).$$
Solving $L(d,\rho)>3/(2\rho)$ on the two sides of 1 gives $I(d)$. Off the $\mathbf4$ Lemma 29.5 gives $d\ge\sqrt5$ on $\mathcal O(3)$ and $d\ge\sqrt6$ on $\mathcal O(1)$. Subtracting the effective off-diagonal norm $3/(2\rho)$ preserves a strict gap there. On the $\mathbf4$, the $\mathcal O(3)$ component occurs only in positive chirality, so its copy is in the line kernel at every radius. The domain of the chiral block $F$ is entirely in $\mathcal O(1)$ and its perturbation lands in the orthogonal $\mathcal O(3)$ summand. The $\mathcal O(1)$ block remains injective since $L(\sqrt3,\rho)>0$ on $I(\sqrt5)$ (substitution at the two endpoints gives positive lower bounds). Its source and target multiplicities are equal. The one additional target copy thus gives precisely one $\mathbf4$ in $\ker F^*$. This proves $K(\rho)$. For each $0<\delta<\min(2,\kappa_a(\rho))$ use Proposition 29.10 and Theorems 29.7 and 29.9 with that wall gap.

Put $h=3/(2\rho)$ and $l=L(\sqrt3,\rho)$. The two components of a normalized vector in the wall kernel satisfy $\|v_1\|\le (h/l)\|v_3\|$. The $\mathcal O(3)$ contribution has weight one and the other contribution has absolute weight at most one. Thus $w\ge(l^2-h^2)/(l^2+h^2)$ on $I(\sqrt3)$, including $w(1)\ge1/7$. At unit radius the [angular calculation for the pull-back](10.md#the-pull-back-prefactor-and-smooth-split-collars) gives the exact value $2/3$. $\square$

**Finite list at a general radius.** For an arbitrary odd line charge $k$ one can use $s=\min(1,\rho^{-1})$. The same anticommutation gives $d_k(V,\rho)\ge s(d_k(V,1)-1)-\rho$. At radius one Parthasarathy's formula gives $d_k^2=C_2^{SU(4)}+3/2-3k^2/8$ on each eligible $SU(4)$ summand. In eigenvalue normalization $C_2^{SU(4)}(\mathbf4)=15/8$, restricting the sum of squares to the trace-orthonormal $\mathfrak{sp}(2)$ basis gives $C_2^{SU(4)}\ge(a^2+2ab+2b^2+4a+6b)/4$ on the $\mathrm{Sp}(2)$ highest weight $(a,b)$ (long roots squared two, defining representation $(1,0)$). For $k=1,3$ a finite **containing** list at any radius is therefore
$$a,b\ge0,\quad a\ {\rm odd},\qquad a^2+2ab+2b^2+4a+6b\ \le\ 4[1+(\rho+3/(2\rho))/s]^2+15/2.$$
Here the central sign is that of the odd twists in the spin bundle of this slice. Branching at the isotropy group and the finite chiral matrices can trim this list. The explicit interval above is the range on which the proof also checks every candidate.
