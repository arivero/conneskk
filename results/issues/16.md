## A mass bound in specified rotation sectors

**Proof.** Let $x$ be the $3\times9$ real matrix of bosonic coordinates. The kinetic term is $-\Delta/2$, the potential is $V=\sum_{a<b}|x^a\wedge x^b|^2$ and the fermionic term is $B=(i/2)K_{IJ}\vartheta_I\vartheta_J$, up to sign, with $K_{b\beta,c\delta}=\sqrt2\,\epsilon_{abc}x_i^a\gamma^i_{\beta\delta}$. Rotate to singular axes $s_i\ge0$. Since $\mathrm{tr}\,L=0$ and $\det L=2s_1s_2s_3\ge0$, we obtain
$$
\|B(x)\|=4\sqrt2\sum_{j=1}^3|\lambda_j(L)|
=8\sqrt2\,\lambda_{\max}(L)\le C|x|,\qquad C=16\sqrt{2/3}.
$$
The last inequality uses $\mathrm{tr}\,L^2=2|x|^2$ and the sum of the two smaller eigenvalues. Equal singular values attain equality.

## Mass and matrix radius at finite D0 charge

**Proposition.** Fix finite $N\ge2$ in relative $SU(N)$ BFSS, with $g_{\rm YM}=1$, $\mathrm{Tr}(T_aT_b)=\delta_{ab}$, and $Q_\beta^2=H$ on the gauge-invariant space. Let $R_X^2=\sum_{a,i}X_{ai}^2$ be the squared dimensionless matrix radius. For a normalized state in a total $\mathrm{Spin}(9)$ isotypic component of highest weight $\lambda$, put $j=\sum_{i=1}^4\lambda_i$ and $E=\langle H\rangle$. Then
$$
 E\langle R_X^2\rangle\ge\frac{49j^2}{122},\qquad
 E\ge c_j\langle R_X^{-2}\rangle,\qquad
 c_j=\frac{[7j-\sqrt{61}]_+^2}{122}.
$$
The first estimate concerns finite second moments. When $c_j=0$ the second assertion is just nonnegativity. The relevant matrix norm includes all colour components.

**Proof.** Let $J=J_{12}+J_{34}+J_{56}+J_{78}$, and let $\tau_\beta$ be the sum of the four rotation multipliers in (B.5) of Michel–Scheinpflug–Yin. Write $\Xi_\beta$ for the radial Clifford multiplier there. Their algebraic identities extend to finite $N$:
$$
 \sum_\beta\{Q_\beta,\tau_\beta\}=J,\qquad
 \sum_\beta\tau_\beta^2=
 \frac{61R_X^2-45\sum_a X_{a9}^2}{1568}
 \le d R_X^2,\quad d=\frac{61}{1568},\qquad
 \sum_\beta\Xi_\beta^2=8R_X^2 .
$$
Indeed the derivative term uses only the canonical Majorana bracket and the gamma contractions in (B.5)–(B.7). In the interaction term every colour factor multiplies the trace of a product of an odd number, at most five, of gamma matrices. Each such trace vanishes in nine dimensions. The two squares contract equal colour indices by the Majorana bracket, so the number of colour coordinates only changes the range of the sum. For a highest-weight polarization, $J\psi=j\psi$. Cauchy–Schwarz in the unweighted identity and $\sum_\beta\|Q_\beta\psi\|^2=16E$ give $j\le8\sqrt{d E\langle R_X^2\rangle}$. Here $8\sqrt d=\sqrt{122}/7$ and $2\sqrt{8d}=\sqrt{61}/7$. For fixed $\varepsilon>0$ the weighted multipliers in these pairings are bounded. Approximation on the rotation-projected form core and monotone convergence give the stated inverse-square inequality. The invariant forms act on the multiplicity space in each rotation type. This extends the linear bound to every polarization. $$
