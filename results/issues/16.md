## A mass bound in specified rotation sectors

**Proposition.** Use the relative, gauge-invariant $SU(2)$ BFSS operator with $\mathrm{Tr}(T_aT_b)=\delta_{ab}$, positive quartic potential and $\{\vartheta_I,\vartheta_J\}=\delta_{IJ}$. In a $\mathrm{Spin}(9)$ sector with second highest-weight coordinate $\lambda_2>10$, its dimensionless energy satisfies $E\ge 1.53497$. With the mass dictionary below this excludes invariant masses below the Planck scale in those sectors.

**Proof.** Let $x$ be the $3\times9$ real matrix of bosonic coordinates. The kinetic term is $-\Delta/2$, the potential is $V=\sum_{a<b}|x^a\wedge x^b|^2$ and the fermionic term is $B=(i/2)K_{IJ}\vartheta_I\vartheta_J$, up to sign, with $K_{b\beta,c\delta}=\sqrt2\,\epsilon_{abc}x_i^a\gamma^i_{\beta\delta}$. Rotate to singular axes $s_i\ge0$. Conjugation by $\mathrm{diag}(\gamma_1,\gamma_2,\gamma_3)$ takes $K$ to $-\sqrt2\,L\otimes\gamma_1\gamma_2\gamma_3$, where $L$ is symmetric with zero diagonal and $(L_{23},L_{31},L_{12})=(s_1,s_2,s_3)$. The Clifford product has eight real rotation planes. Each skew block of size $\kappa$ contributes energies $\pm\kappa/2$ on the full Fock space. Since $\mathrm{tr}\,L=0$ and $\det L=2s_1s_2s_3\ge0$, we obtain
$$
\|B(x)\|=4\sqrt2\sum_{j=1}^3|\lambda_j(L)|
=8\sqrt2\,\lambda_{\max}(L)\le C|x|,\qquad C=16\sqrt{2/3}.
$$
The last inequality uses $\mathrm{tr}\,L^2=2|x|^2$ and the sum of the two smaller eigenvalues. Equal singular values attain equality.

For fixed colour row $x^a=u$ put $V_a=\sum_{b\ne a}|u\wedge x^b|^2$. The transverse coordinates $z$ are the projections of the other two rows onto $u^\perp$. The transverse oscillator $-t\Delta_z+V_a$ has levels $2\sqrt t(n+8)|u|$. The weight bound of Lemmas 3.1–3.3 in the pinned preprint removes $n\le m$ if $\lambda_2>m+6$. Indeed the first weight coordinate of the fermion module has maximum six. Under one spatial plane rotation each of the three real spin modules has eight rotation planes of frequency $1/2$, contributing at most $8/4=2$ to a Fock weight. The spatial dilation in the oscillator preserves its rotation types.

Sum the three slice inequalities and divide by two. The potentials sum to $2V$, and each coordinate gradient occurs twice under orthogonal projection. This bounds the quadratic form of $-t\Delta+V$ below by the expectation of $\sqrt t(m+9)\sum_a|x^a|$. On a gauge singlet the scalar probability density is colour-rotation invariant. For a uniform unit vector $v\in S^2$, with $w_i=s_i^2/|x|^2$, we have $\sqrt{\sum w_iv_i^2}\ge\sum w_i|v_i|$ and $\mathbb E|v_i|=1/2$. Thus the colour average of the sum of the three row lengths is at least $3|x|/2$. Fubini on each slice and form closure extend the bound from the smooth core.

Take $t=49/100$ and $m=4$. The remaining scalar form is bounded below by $-a\Delta+c|x|$ with $a=1/100$ and $c=273/20-C$. Radial conjugation in 27 dimensions contributes $156a/r^2$ in the lowest angular channel. Consequently
$$

$$
The same confining estimate gives compact resolvent on each of these sectors. $\square$ [Pinned preprint, §§2–3](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/Positive-eigenvalues-of-the-relative-SU-2-BFSS-Hamiltonian-October-5-2026/positive-eigenvalues-relative-su2-bfss.pdf)

## Mass and matrix radius at finite D0 charge

**Proposition.** Fix finite $N\ge2$ in relative $SU(N)$ BFSS, with $g_{\rm YM}=1$, $\mathrm{Tr}(T_aT_b)=\delta_{ab}$, and $Q_\beta^2=H$ on the gauge-invariant space. Let $R_X^2=\sum_{a,i}X_{ai}^2$ be the squared dimensionless matrix radius. For a normalized state in a total $\mathrm{Spin}(9)$ isotypic component of highest weight $\lambda$, put $j=\sum_{i=1}^4\lambda_i$ and $E=\langle H\rangle$. Then
$$
 E\langle R_X^2\rangle\ge\frac{49j^2}{122},\qquad
 E\ge c_j\langle R_X^{-2}\rangle,\qquad
 c_j=\frac{[7j-\sqrt{61}]_+^2}{122}.
$$
The first estimate concerns finite second moments. When $c_j=0$ the second assertion is just nonnegativity. The relevant matrix norm includes all colour components.

**Proof.** Let $J=J_{12}+J_{34}+J_{56}+J_{78}$, and let $\tau_\beta$ be the sum of the four rotation multipliers in (B.5) of Michel–Scheinpflug–Yin. Write $\Xi_\beta$ for the radial Clifford multiplier there. Their algebraic identities extend to finite $N$:
$$
 \sum_\beta\{Q_\beta,\tau_\beta\}=J,\qquad
 \sum_\beta\tau_\beta^2=
 \frac{61R_X^2-45\sum_a X_{a9}^2}{1568}
 \le d R_X^2,\quad d=\frac{61}{1568},\qquad
 \sum_\beta\Xi_\beta^2=8R_X^2 .
$$
Indeed the derivative term uses only the canonical Majorana bracket and the gamma contractions in (B.5)–(B.7). In the interaction term every colour factor multiplies the trace of a product of an odd number, at most five, of gamma matrices. Each such trace vanishes in nine dimensions. The two squares contract equal colour indices by the Majorana bracket, so the number of colour coordinates only changes the range of the sum. [Explicit multipliers and Clifford traces, Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.09837v1#A2)

For a highest-weight polarization, $J\psi=j\psi$. Cauchy–Schwarz in the unweighted identity and $\sum_\beta\|Q_\beta\psi\|^2=16E$ give $j\le8\sqrt{d E\langle R_X^2\rangle}$. For the second estimate put $w=(R_X^2+\varepsilon^2)^{-1}$ and $W^2=\langle w\rangle$. Commuting $Q_\beta$ through $w$ gives $2iw^2\Xi_\beta$. Since $w^2R_X^2\le w$, the same argument gives
$$
 j W^2\le 8\sqrt{dE}\,W+2\sqrt{8d}\,W^2 .
$$
Here $8\sqrt d=\sqrt{122}/7$ and $2\sqrt{8d}=\sqrt{61}/7$. For fixed $\varepsilon>0$ the weighted multipliers in these pairings are bounded. Approximation on the rotation-projected form core and monotone convergence give the stated inverse-square inequality. In the unweighted calculation first cut off at $R_X=K$ with a smooth radial multiplier whose charge commutator has norm $O(1/K)$, and pass to the limit for a finite second moment.

The invariant forms act on the multiplicity space in each rotation type. This extends the linear bound to every polarization. For the product bound, write a general vector as spin basis vectors with multiplicity components $m_i$. Cauchy–Schwarz gives $(\sum_i E_i)(\sum_i r_i^2)\ge(\sum_i\sqrt{E_i r_i^2})^2$, where the forms $E_i,r_i^2$ are unnormalized. The highest-weight estimate applied to each $m_i$ bounds the last expression below by $49j^2(\sum_i\|m_i\|^2)^2/122$. $\square$

The same angular identity recovers singletness at zero energy without assuming a finite matrix radius. Apply the identity to a highest-weight zero mode with $\tau_\beta$ multiplied by a radial cutoff equal to one up to $K$ and zero beyond $2K$. All $Q_\beta\psi$ vanish. The derivative of the cutoff is $O(1/K)$ and $\tau_\beta=O(K)$ on its support. This bounds $j\langle\chi_K\rangle$ by a constant times the probability in $K\le R_X\le 2K$, which tends to zero for an $L^2$ state. Only the trivial rotation type has $j=0$. [Rotation invariance of the ground space](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/0001189v2)

Its Clifford module contains the $\mathbf{84}$, hence the tensor product has the Cartan component $\lambda=\rho+(1,1,1,0)$. On that whole total rotation type take $j=\sum_i\rho_i+3\ge3$. The probability estimate follows by integrating $R_X^{-2}\ge a^{-2}$ on that ball. The second inequality applies also if the moment is infinite. ## The two-particle channel at low spin

We vary the relative matrix configuration and the rotation representation at fixed charge two, unit matrix coupling and periodic supersymmetric boundary condition. The observable is the invariant mass of a normalizable positive-energy state. Throughout this section $R_X=|X|$ is the matrix norm. The longitudinal radius $R_-$ cancels from $M^2=4E/L^2$.

Let $\mathcal H_l$ be the traceless spherical harmonics of degree $l$ on $S^8$. Over the rank-one valley the sixteen fast bosonic oscillators have a unique Gaussian ground state. Filling eight fast fermions gives a neutral fast vacuum. The remaining sixteen real fermions give $S=\mathbf{44}\oplus\mathbf{84}\oplus\mathbf{128}$. Reversing the colour axis exchanges the two D0-branes, sends the spatial direction $v$ to $-v$, fixes the fast vacuum and acts on $S$ by fermion parity. The physical angular sections of the frozen ground space thus carry
$$
 \mathcal G=
 \bigoplus_{l\ {\rm even}}\mathcal H_l\otimes(\mathbf{44}\oplus\mathbf{84})
 \ \oplus\!
 \bigoplus_{l\ {\rm odd}}\mathcal H_l\otimes\mathbf{128}.
$$
Sanjurjo and Zikopoulos give the exchange rule and the list of total rotation types in this representation. Their argument identifies an excited transverse oscillator as the mechanism for confinement. We estimate its energy in the full undeformed Hamiltonian. **Theorem.** In any physical total $\mathrm{Spin}(9)$ type absent from $\mathcal G$, the relative $SU(2)$ Hamiltonian obeys the quadratic-form bound
$$
 H\ \ge\ \frac85 R_X-\frac{425}{R_X^2}.
$$
Its restriction to that type has compact resolvent. In each type occurring in $\mathcal G$, zero belongs to the essential spectrum.

**Proof.** Write $r=s_1$ for the largest singular value of the real $3\times9$ matrix $X$, $u^2=s_2^2+s_3^2$ and $t=u/r$. Then $R_X^2=r^2+u^2$. For $t\le5/8$ the principal axis is simple. Write $X=rnv^T+Y$ with $n\in S^2$, $v\in S^8$, $n^TY=0$ and $Yv=0$, identifying $(n,v)$ with $(-n,-v)$. At fixed axes $Y$ is a $2\times8$ real matrix with norm $u$. We work in the full Euclidean metric before imposing gauge invariance on wavefunctions. Choose the normal frames on both spheres with their connections zero at the angular base point, and use a fixed ambient fermion frame. For angular variations $(a,b)$ the four mutually perpendicular matrix blocks in the differential are
$$
 nv^T\,dr,\quad dY_{\rm normal},\quad(ra-Yb)v^T,\quad n(rb-Y^Ta)^T .
$$
Thus the gradient norm of an arbitrary wavefunction is the sum of the radial and transverse squares and $|D^{-T}\nabla_{\rm ang}\Psi|^2$, with $D(a,b)=(ra-Yb,rb-Y^Ta)$. In other normal frames the angular derivative includes the infinitesimal frame action on $Y$. A moving fermion frame adds its spin connection inside the same square. The pointwise decomposition holds for nontrivial total spin. The volume density, up to the constant from the double cover, is $r^{10}W$, where
$$
 W=\det(I_2-YY^T/r^2).
$$
Drop the nonnegative radial and angular forms before changing the dependent variable. At fixed axes and $r$, flattening the transverse measure by $W^{1/2}$ adds $\Delta_Y\sqrt W/(2\sqrt W)$ to $-\Delta_Y/2$. Write $Z=Y/r$, with rows $z_1,z_2$. Then $W=1-|Z|^2+|z_1\wedge z_2|^2\ge1-t^2$, $\Delta_Z W=-32+14t^2$ and $|\nabla_Z W|\le2t+2t^3$. Inserting these in $(\Delta_Z W)/(4W)-|\nabla_Z W|^2/(8W^2)$ gives a cost at most $15/r^2$ throughout the tube.

Here $V=r^2u^2+|y_1\wedge y_2|^2$, with $y_i$ the rows of $Y$. Split the fermion operator as $B_0+B(Y)$ with $B_0=B(rnv^T)$. For rank at most two, the matrix $L$ in the preceding Clifford calculation has eigenvalues $0,\pm u$, so $\|B(Y)\|=8\sqrt2\,u\le r^2u^2/8+256/r^2$. The remaining flat oscillator $-\Delta_Y/2+(7/8)r^2u^2+B_0$ has levels
$$
 \sqrt2 r\big[\sqrt{7/8}\,(8+m)+k-8\big],
$$
where $m$ counts bosonic quanta and $k$ counts fast fermionic excitations above the filled vacuum. A colour rotation by $\pi$ **about** $n$ sends $Y$ and each fast fermion to its negative and fixes the slow fermions. Its vacuum eigenvalue is $+1$. Gauge invariance therefore gives $m+k$ even. This is a different rotation from the exchange that sends $n$ to $-n$.

At fixed $r$ the projection onto $m=k=0$ intertwines gauge and spatial rotations. Its angular coefficients are sections in $\mathcal G$ for any positive Gaussian frequency. The invariant tube cutoff and $W^{1/2}$ preserve total type. Extend the cut-off vector by zero in each flat transverse fibre. The two losses sum to $271/r^2<377/R_X^2$ there.

On $t\ge2/5$ put $v_0=100/841$. The minimum of $t^2/(1+t^2)^2$ on $[2/5,\sqrt2]$ is $v_0$, giving $V\ge v_0R_X^4$. Keep the full kinetic energy here. Completing the square in $R_X^3$ shows that the outer form dominates $(8/5)R_X-D_o/R_X^2$ with

$$
Localize with the cosine and sine of an angle increasing linearly from $0$ to $\pi/2$ for $2/5\le t\le5/8$, constant elsewhere. The gradients of $u$ and $r$ are orthogonal unit vectors in the transition, as follows from the singular-axis coordinates. Hence $|\nabla t|=(1+t^2)/R_X$ and the IMS localization error is bounded by

This proves the claimed form bound first on a smooth core away from the origin. Radial cutoffs are dense there in the local $H^1$ norm in dimension 27. The estimate also applies to exterior-supported vectors by closure, where its negative term is bounded.
