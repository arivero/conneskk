# Quantized torus torsion below a descending tower

**Proposition.** Let the four-torus have fixed shape, $g_\ell=\ell^2\hat g$, and normalize periods of $H$ in a fixed unit $\Phi>0$. Let the Dirac term use $\eta=\pm s *_\ell H$, with $s=3/4$ for torsion $H$ or $1/4$ for the stated one-third convention. In a unit-coordinate basis and for a cubic metric,
$$H=\Phi\sum_{i<j<k} n_{ijk}\,dx^i\wedge dx^j\wedge dx^k,\qquad
2|\eta|={2|s|\Phi\over\ell^3}\sqrt{\sum n_{ijk}^2},\qquad n_{ijk}\in\mathbb Z.$$
For general shape use the Hodge image of the harmonic integral $H^3$ lattice and the norm of $\hat g$. Restrict to its intersection with the chosen mirror plane for the photon condition. With physical one-form norm of dimension inverse length, $\Phi$ in this convention has units of length squared. On a rectangular torus replace each $\ell^3$ in the components by the corresponding $L_iL_jL_k$.

**Proof.** A unit-period three-form has orthonormal coefficients proportional to $\ell^{-3}$ and the Hodge star is an isometry on orthonormal components. The two singular values are $|A\pm J_w\eta|$. On the rank-one branch take $A=\pm J_w\eta$, with a representative of the flat connection defined modulo the $2\pi$ dual lattice. The nonzero mass equals $2|\eta|$ and the window condition $|A|+|\eta|<\pi/\ell$ becomes
$$0<\|n\|<{\pi\ell^2\over2|s|\Phi}.$$
The bound excludes equality where levels can meet the next channel. Off the rank-one branch $2|\eta|$ is the maximum possible splitting at that flux, and the general window inequality retains $|A|$ explicitly. For other shapes replace $2\pi/\ell$ by the shortest physical momentum. At fixed charge and shape the cubic result is flux divided by a **three-cycle** volume. The four-volume scales as $\ell^4$. [Torus Dirac operator and isolation criterion](../../lemmas/51_torus_wilson_line_and_torsion_yukawa.md)

A fixed nonzero integral mirror covector gives a path as $\ell\to\infty$: the mass is $O(\ell^{-3})$, the next momentum $O(\ell^{-1})$, and their ratio is $O(\Phi/\ell^2)$. For the Weyl torus such a covector exists, for example at an integral product shape with the transposition acting integrally and isometrically; its fixed rational two-plane has a rank-two fixed lattice. If a vector weak pole is required too, a product model with a wall interval of the same length can impose $\sin(2\theta)=\sqrt2\sin(\ell\,2|\eta|)$ in the small-angle branch of Theorem 23.6. It then has $M_W=2|\eta|$ and $M_Z/M_W\to\sqrt{8/5}$. This is an explicit gluing prescription. Deriving it from the manifold would be an additional equation. The new dimensionful input $\sqrt{\Phi}$ permits scale separation; neither flux integrality nor the wall angle fixes a stationary radius. $\square$ [Vector poles](../23_exact_w_z_masses.md)

**Field equations and orientation energy.** In the leading source-free NS–NS string-frame equations a constant harmonic $H$ obeys the form equation at constant dilaton. The internal Einstein equation on a flat torus is $0=-H_{ipq}H_j{}^{pq}/4+2\nabla_i\nabla_j\varphi$. Tracing and integrating on the compact torus forces $H=0$ even for a smooth periodic dilaton. A source-supported solution, curvature balancing the flux, or corrected field equations would have to be specified. A group metric with parallel Cartan torsion illustrates curvature balance, subject also to the dilaton and other background equations. In the isolated heat-trace window at fixed lengths the pair contributes $e^{-t(a+x)}+e^{-t(a-x)}=2e^{-ta}\cosh(tx)$ with $a=|A|^2+|\eta|^2$, $x=2|A\wedge\eta|$. It is minimized at zero area. Quantizing one form leaves the Wilson line continuous and supplies no energy preferring the rank-one alignment. [Heterotic fermion torsion conventions](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0605247)
