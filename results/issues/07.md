# Quark mirror removal and the charge of the retained wall mode

**Proposition.** On $M^{1,3}\times A^4\times I$ take the periodic constant-spinor sector on $A$, a nine-dimensional complex colour triplet, and the same algebraic wall projector at both ends of $I$. On $M^{1,3}\times A^4\times S^1$ take the all-periodic Majorana colour singlet. One choice of wall sign keeps a quark generation and the periodic self-conjugate circle sector gives one lepton generation with $\nu_R$.

**Proof.** Use chiral gradings $\gamma_5=+1$ on four-dimensional left fields and $\Gamma_A=-1$ on $\Sigma_L$, $+1$ on $\Sigma_R$. With skew Clifford multiplication in the ninth spatial direction, $c(e_9)=\pm i\gamma_5\Gamma_A$: the product anticommutes with the other eight generators and squares to one before multiplication by $i$. The sign sets the normal orientation. For $\Xi=\gamma_5\Gamma_A$ its $-1$ eigenspace is precisely $(2,1)\otimes\Sigma_L\otimes3\ \oplus\ (1,2)\otimes\Sigma_R\otimes3$. Its complement is the mirror. The same algebraic projector at the two ends (outward normals are opposite) gives the normal operator of type (a) or its reflected type (b). Each selected component has one normal zero profile, constant for zero bulk mass or exponential for the constant masses of Lemma 09. Reversing the projector selects the opposite multiplets. Type (c) has zero index and no exact normal kernel at finite length. Its light, oppositely localized massive pair for negative large bulk mass does not give this chiral count without extra wall data. [Equivariant interval index](../09_index_from_boundary_conditions.md)

On the physical quarks just selected $\Xi=-1$ on both chiralities. A phase $e^{i\beta\Xi}$ acts with the opposite charge on the **left-handed conjugate** of a physical right singlet. On the Majorana field the independent left variables are already $\Sigma_L\oplus\Sigma_R$; their charges are the two eigenvalues of $\Gamma_A$, and the reality partner transforms conjugately. Thus the all-left charges are
$$
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
 &Q&u^c&d^c&L&\nu^c&e^c\\ \hline
q_\Xi&-1&1&1&-1&1&1\\
B-L&1/3&-1/3&-1/3&-1&1&1 .
\end{array}
$$
The physical $B-L$ generator on the combined module weights the lepton grading charge by $+1$ and the quark grading charge by $-1/3$. It supplies the usual hypercharges together with $T_{3R}$ once this relative normalization is imposed. The number of left $SU(2)_L$ doublets is $3+1=4$, even in the ordinary spin $SU(2)$ anomaly test. A shared gauging of the quark and lepton sectors is an additional identification of the two factors in the construction. [Majorana zero sector](../../lemmas/52_lepton_generation_three_views.md)

For constant normalized profiles at zero normal mass the quark four-dimensional mass bilinear has internal block $Y=c(A_{\rm flat})+i c(\eta)\Gamma_A$. Both terms are odd under $\Gamma_A$. The covariant internal Dirac term anticommutes with $\Xi$, but the Dirac adjoint of a retained field lies in the dual of the opposite projector because $\{\gamma^0,\Xi\}=0$. The Hamiltonian term $\gamma^0 D_A$ therefore preserves the retained subspace. Its two masses are $|A_{\rm flat}\pm J_w\eta|$ on a chosen mirror. For unequal profiles a bulk mixing carries their normalized overlap, while a wall Yukawa carries their boundary values as in Lemma 12. The Majorana singlet permits no abelian Wilson line of this kind; at zero circle momentum its torsion gives both masses $|\eta_\ell|$. In this construction wall chirality is used for the complex triplet, while the real periodic singlet already supplies its independent fields in the vectorlike bulk. A common geometry producing both domains and the charge normalization remains a separate requirement. $\square$ [Torus masses](../../lemmas/51_torus_wilson_line_and_torsion_yukawa.md) · [Boundary Yukawa](../../lemmas/12_boundary_yukawa_interval.md)
