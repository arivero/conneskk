# Four limits of the neutron beta-decay rate

We vary the charged scale, the common mass unit, the left down-quark eigenvectors and the neutron–proton splitting along the four paths of issue 27. We specify the matched strong and electromagnetic inputs for each path. The observables are the direct semileptonic amplitude at momentum transfer $q$ and the continuum and hydrogen $1s$ decay rates in units of $H_N$. We use $\hbar=c=1$, metric signature $(+,-,-,-)$ and a massless electron antineutrino. [Issue 27 and the closure plan](https://github.com/arivero/conneskk/issues/27#issuecomment-7189864138)

## Matched hadronic inputs and order

Use one four-dimensional frame and take the on-shell rest masses $m_n,m_p,m_e$ as inputs, including the matched QCD and QED mass shifts and omitting weak self-energies. Define
$$
H_N=\frac{m_n+m_p}{6},\qquad
\Delta=m_n-m_p,\qquad \delta=\Delta-m_e .
$$
The splitting is the total matched splitting. A mass calculation away from the empirical point requires $\Lambda_{\overline{\rm MS}}$ with its active-flavour prescription, the light-quark masses at a specified subtraction scale $\mu_h$, and the electromagnetic mass shifts or a full QCD+QED determination at those inputs. We use the nonsinglet vector and axial form factors $f_1(q^2),g_A(q^2)$ of pure QCD at the specified renormalized masses and strong coupling, held at the same $\overline{\rm MS}$ values at $\mu_h$ when the photon is removed for this matrix element. In particular $f_1(0)=1$ in the isospin-symmetric normalization. Away from that limit both form factors are independent matched inputs. They can be taken from QCD calculations or from determinations in the same convention with radiative effects removed. The electric input for the final state is $\alpha=\alpha_{\rm Th}$.

We use tree charged-vector exchange and the allowed approximation for the decay rate. We keep the leading terms in the nucleon recoil expansion in the beta energies divided by the nucleon mass. We assume the transfer is below hadronic singularities and expand the form factors about $q^2=0$ for the rate. Weak magnetism, induced form factors and derivative current operators enter beyond this allowed order. We resum the point-charge Coulomb factor of the outgoing $pe^-$ pair, using the static proton and the electron velocity $p_e/E_e$ in the continuum, and the leading nonrelativistic Coulomb Hamiltonian in the atom. The latter keeps the reduced mass in the binding energy. The approximation omits further virtual and real radiative terms, proton structure in the Coulomb factor, and the fine and hyperfine splittings of hydrogen. [Comparison of continuum and bound normalizations, Faber et al., Eqs. (7)–(13)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0906.0959)

## Normalized semileptonic amplitude

**Proposition 27.1, conditional on the matched inputs.** In the specified tree charged-vector sector the interval overlap and $V_{ud}$ enter the direct amplitude as separate factors. At leading allowed order the continuum rate divided by $H_N$ is the product of the squared dimensionless leptonic contact, the squared relative semileptonic overlap, $|V_{ud}|^2$, the unpolarized nucleon spin factor and the dimensionless phase-space integral below.

**Fermion profiles.** Put $y=x/\ell$ on the interval. For a Dirac mass $m_f$, take unit vectors $a_f,b_f$ in the two finite grading spaces with $Ma_f=m_fb_f$ and $M^\dagger b_f=m_fa_f$. This includes a massless channel by taking vectors in the respective kernels. The isometry of Theorem 22.1 maps the left coefficient to the internal profile
$$
\ell^{-1/2}\big(\cos(m_fx)a_f,-\sin(m_fx)b_f\big)
$$
and the right coefficient to $\ell^{-1/2}(\sin(m_fx)a_f,\cos(m_fx)b_f)$ in the right spin component. These expressions follow by exponentiating $\Gamma_F D_F$ on the pair, where its square is $-m_f^2$. The two components of each profile have squared norm summing to $1/\ell$, and integrating over $x$ gives canonical four-dimensional kinetic normalization. The left weak generator $T^+$ acts on the ordinary left doublet component $a_f$ and annihilates its singlet partner in the particle current. This selects the cosine on each side of the compressed matrix element. The real completion supplies the conjugate vertices. [Window isometry](../22_finite_dirac_operator_as_window.md) · [Compression of a multiplication operator](../22_compression_as_average.md)

**Vector profiles.** Let the canonically normalized charged poles have real profiles $h_j$ with $\int_0^1h_i(y)h_j(y)\,dy=\delta_{ij}$. We use the pole schedule
$$
M_j^2=M_W^2+\lambda_j/\ell^2,\qquad M_W=gv/2,
\qquad \lambda_0=0,\quad \lambda_j>0\ \ (j>0).
$$
The shapes and dimensionless spacings are prescribed data. We use a complete orthonormal profile basis for the tower and take $h_0$ positive in the interior. The formulas also give the pole benchmark when only $j=0$ is retained. For example, the mixed-wall profiles $h_j(y)=\sqrt2\cos((j+1/2)\pi y)$ have zero derivative at $0$ and vanish at $1$. Subtracting the first eigenvalue of $-\partial_y^2$ gives $\lambda_j=\pi^2j(j+1)$. The pole schedule supplies the common mass shift in the matched quadratic action. With a flat bulk coupling $g_5$, unit $dx$ vector normalization would use $h_j/\sqrt\ell$ and $g=g_5/\sqrt\ell$. Each coefficient is then fixed by the overlap
$$
I_j(f,f')=\int_0^1dy\,h_j(y)\cos(\ell m_f y)\cos(\ell m_{f'} y).
$$
For the mixed-wall example put $k_j=(j+1/2)\pi$ and $\theta_f=\ell m_f$. Multiplying the three cosines and integrating gives $I_j=(\sqrt2/4)\sum_{\epsilon,\epsilon'=\pm1}\operatorname{sinc}(k_j+\epsilon\theta_f+\epsilon'\theta_{f'})$, with $\operatorname{sinc}(z)=\sin(z)/z$ and its continuous value at zero. The same charges act in each family. In the mass basis the flavour part of the quark vertex is $a_{u_i}^\dagger T^+a_{d_k}=(U_{uL}^\dagger U_{dL})_{ik}=V_{ik}$. The canonical vertex for left fields is therefore $g I_j(u_i,d_k)V_{ik}/\sqrt2$. We use a flavour-diagonal lepton doublet, with $I_j(e,\nu_e)$ at the electron vertex. Matching the charged poles, profiles and QCD inputs to a common supporting higher-dimensional action remains open. The pole schedule is an input for the plate comparisons. [Interval pole and matching conventions](18.md)

**Propagation and contact normalization.** For $q=p_n-p_p=p_e+k_\nu$ set
$$
\begin{aligned}
L^\mu &= \bar u_e\gamma^\mu(1-\gamma_5)v_\nu,\\
H^\nu &= \bar u_p\big[f_1(q^2)\gamma^\nu
                   -g_A(q^2)\gamma^\nu\gamma_5\big]u_n .
\end{aligned}
$$
Writing each left-handed vertex with $1-\gamma_5$ gives a factor $1/2$ per vertex. Contracting the two vertices with the massive spin-one propagator yields the finite-momentum allowed amplitude, up to an overall phase,
$$
\mathcal M(q)=\frac{g^2 V_{ud}}{8}
\sum_j I_j(e,\nu_e)I_j(u,d)
  \frac{L^\mu\big(\eta_{\mu\nu}-q_\mu q_\nu/M_j^2\big)H^\nu}
       {M_j^2-q^2-i0} .
$$
Here the quark current with unit flavour coefficient is matched onto $H^\nu$. The external Coulomb factor is applied separately to the rate. If $Q_\beta$ bounds the components of $q$ in the neutron rest frame and $Q_\beta\ll M_W$, the propagator tensor begins with $\eta_{\mu\nu}/M_j^2+(q^2\eta_{\mu\nu}-q_\mu q_\nu)/M_j^4$. Both corrections are of relative order $Q_\beta^2/M_W^2$ in the momentum expansion. The recoil and form-factor expansions in the rate keep the leading nucleon components and $f_V=f_1(0)$, $g_A=g_A(0)$.

Define the contact sums without flavour factors by
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{ff'}&=\sum_j I_j(e,\nu_e)I_j(f,f')\frac{M_W^2}{M_j^2},\\
\frac{G_F}{\sqrt2}&=\frac{g^2}{8M_W^2}A_{\mu\nu_\mu},
&\mathcal I_{ud}&=\frac{A_{ud}}{A_{\mu\nu_\mu}} .
\end{aligned}
$$
We take the leptonic tree contact to be positive. The quark contact coefficient stripped of $V_{ud}$ is $G_F\mathcal I_{ud}/\sqrt2$. The leptonic normalization is the muon contact in this model. In the single-pole unit-overlap limit it gives $G_F=1/(\sqrt2 v^2)$. For a single pole with general overlaps, $\mathcal I_{ud}=I_0(u,d)/I_0(\mu,\nu_\mu)$. For the tower, Parseval gives square-summable $I_j$ for every external pair. Cauchy–Schwarz gives convergence of the contact sum and of the propagator sum off the poles in the low-energy domain.

## Dimensionless continuum rate

With relativistic one-particle normalization, the leading nucleon current on Pauli spinors is $H^0=2\sqrt{m_nm_p}\,f_V\xi_p^\dagger\xi_n$ and $H^i=-2\sqrt{m_nm_p}\,g_A\xi_p^\dagger\sigma^i\xi_n$. The neutron spin average and proton spin sum give $4m_nm_p|f_V|^2$ in the temporal component and $4m_nm_p|g_A|^2\delta^{ij}$ in the spatial block. This uses $\operatorname{tr}(\sigma^i)=0$ and $\operatorname{tr}(\sigma^i\sigma^j)=2\delta^{ij}$. For the leptons, the trace of $(\slashed p_e+m_e)\gamma^\mu(1-\gamma_5)\slashed k_\nu\gamma^\nu(1-\gamma_5)$ has symmetric part $8(p_e^\mu k_\nu^\nu+p_e^\nu k_\nu^\mu-\eta^{\mu\nu}p_e\cdot k_\nu)$. Moving the chiral factors through the gamma matrices leaves twice the four-gamma trace in the symmetric part. Thus the temporal component is $8(E_eE_\nu+\mathbf p_e\cdot\mathbf k_\nu)$ and the spatial trace is $8(3E_eE_\nu-\mathbf p_e\cdot\mathbf k_\nu)$. Inserting the contact gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}&=16m_nm_pG_F^2|\mathcal I_{ud}V_{ud}|^2\,
\big[\mathcal S E_eE_\nu+\mathcal D\,\mathbf p_e\cdot\mathbf k_\nu\big],\\
\mathcal S&=|f_V|^2+3|g_A|^2,\qquad
\mathcal D=|f_V|^2-|g_A|^2 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The three-body measure is
$$
\begin{aligned}
d\Gamma_{\rm cont}
 &= \frac{\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}F_C}{2m_n}
    (2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}(p_n-p_p-p_e-k_\nu)\\
 &\qquad {}\times \frac{d^3p_p}{(2\pi)^3 2E_p}
    \frac{d^3p_e}{(2\pi)^3 2E_e}
    \frac{d^3k_\nu}{(2\pi)^3 2E_\nu}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The spatial momentum delta function eliminates the proton momentum. At leading recoil set $E_p=m_p$ in the remaining energy delta function and normalization. The factors $2m_n\,2m_p\,2E_e\,2E_\nu$ cancel the mass and energy factors in the angle-independent term. The angular correlation averages to zero because the remaining energy delta function and Coulomb factor are independent of the angle between the leptons. The angular integrals and energy delta function yield $2\pi(4\pi)^2/(2\pi)^6=1/(2\pi^3)$. With $p_e^2dp_e=p_eE_e\,dE_e$ and $|\mathbf k_\nu|=E_\nu=\Delta-E_e$, the continuum result for $\delta>0$ is
$$
\Gamma_{\rm cont}=\frac{G_F^2|\mathcal I_{ud}V_{ud}|^2\mathcal S}{2\pi^3}
\int_{m_e}^{\Delta}dE_e\,p_eE_e(\Delta-E_e)^2 F_C(\alpha,p_e/E_e).
$$
Use $F_C(0,\beta)=1$. In the adopted Sommerfeld prescription at $\alpha>0$,
$$
F_C(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{z}{1-e^{-z}},\qquad z=2\pi\alpha/\beta .
$$
The plane-wave-normalized attractive Coulomb wave has the normalization $e^{\pi\eta/2}\Gamma(1-i\eta)$ at the origin with $\eta=\alpha/\beta$. Its hypergeometric factor is one there. The reflection and recurrence identities of the gamma function give $|\Gamma(1-i\eta)|^2=\pi\eta/\sinh(\pi\eta)$, yielding $F_C$. The nonrelativistic expression with relative velocity becomes the stated static-proton prescription with $\beta=p_e/E_e$. The prescription suffices at this order and has the nonrelativistic threshold limit. [Point-Coulomb prescription, Faber et al., Eq. (13)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0906.0959#page=3)

Put $r=m_e/H_N$, $w=\Delta/H_N$, $d=w-r$ and $\Xi=G_FH_N^2$. Define $\Phi=0$ for $w\le r$. For $w>r$ set
$$
\Phi(w,r,\alpha)=\int_r^w d\varepsilon\,\varepsilon\sqrt{\varepsilon^2-r^2}
 (w-\varepsilon)^2 F_C\!\left(\alpha,\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon^2-r^2}}{\varepsilon}\right).
$$
The dimensionful integral scales as $H_N^5$. Division by $H_N$ leaves
$$
\frac{\Gamma_{\rm cont}}{H_N}
=\frac{\Xi^2}{2\pi^3}\,|\mathcal I_{ud}|^2\,|V_{ud}|^2\,\mathcal S\,\Phi(w,r,\alpha).
$$
This proves the rate in Proposition 27.1. $\square$

## Four plate limits

**Proposition 27.2, conditional on the plate schedules.** Use the same current and propagator on every plate. Start with an open continuum, a nonzero direct vertex and positive matched scales. The phase-space arguments $w,r,\alpha$ stay fixed on plates 16–18. On plate 19 take $w\downarrow r$ at fixed $\alpha>0$ and keep the allowed form-factor combination and interval overlap finite and nonzero.

| Plate | $\Xi^2$ | $\lvert\mathcal I_{ud}\rvert^2$ | $\lvert V_{ud}\rvert^2$ | $\mathcal S$ | $\Phi$ | $\Gamma_{\rm cont}/H_N$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | Falls as $v^{-4}$ | Positive limit | Fixed | Fixed | Fixed | Falls as $G_F^2$ |
| 17 | Fixed | Fixed | Fixed | Fixed | Fixed | Invariant |
| 18 | Fixed | Fixed | $\cos^2\phi$ | Fixed | Fixed | Zero at $\phi=\pi/2$ |

**Plate 16.** Hold $g,g'$, the length, the fermion masses and profiles, the mixing matrix, and all matched strong and electromagnetic data fixed. The growing common vector mass in the assumed pole schedule gives $G_F=A_{\mu\nu_\mu}/(\sqrt2 v^2)$. For each $j$, $M_W^2/M_j^2\to1$. The absolute overlap product sum is bounded by Cauchy–Schwarz, so the limit passes through the sum. Completeness identifies $\lim A_{ff'}$ with the integral of the product of the two cosine-pair densities. The strict wall bound $\ell\lVert D_F\rVert<\pi/2$ makes each cosine positive on the interval, so this integral is positive. With a single positive profile the product of overlaps is positive directly. The ratio $\Gamma_{\rm cont}/(H_NG_F^2)$ therefore has a positive finite limit, and is constant in the single-pole case. The momentum correction tends to zero at fixed probe energy. This path assumes a common increasing mass for the sourced charged poles and fixed low-energy QCD matching.

**Plate 17.** Send $D_F\mapsto tD_F$, $v\mapsto tv$ and $\ell\mapsto\ell/t$ for $t>0$. The arguments $\theta_f$ and the wall block stay fixed. Each wall momentum $(n\pi\pm\theta_f)/\ell$ and each $M_j$ gains a factor $t$. Hold the dimensionless couplings and vector shapes fixed. Move the four-momentum and all thresholds and subtraction points by $t$, including the quark, electroweak, KK and other charged thresholds, the probe energy, and any cutoff used in matching. The form-factor arguments in hadronic units stay fixed. With relativistically normalized spinors, the two external currents together scale as $t^2$ while the propagator scales as $t^{-2}$. This gives the same dimensionless amplitude at the moved momentum, including its $q_\mu q_\nu$ term.

For example, at one loop $8\pi^2/g_s^2(\mu)=b_f\log(\mu/\Lambda_f)$ with $b_f=11-2f/3$ for $f$ active Dirac quarks. Equality of the coupling above and below a running-mass threshold $m_*$ gives $b_-\log(m_*/\Lambda_-)=b_+\log(m_*/\Lambda_+)$, so $\Lambda_-^{b_-}=m_*^{b_--b_+}\Lambda_+^{b_+}$. Holding the coupling at a comoving initial subtraction point first gives $\Lambda_+\mapsto t\Lambda_+$. The threshold equation then gives $\Lambda_-\mapsto t\Lambda_-$ at every crossing. For ordinary six- to three-flavour matching this yields $\Lambda_3=\Lambda_6^{7/9}(m_tm_bm_c)^{2/27}$. Additional thresholds obey the same scale argument with fixed dimensionless matching functions. For QED, the subtracted polarization depends on dimensionless ratios, with its hadronic spectral input also comoving. For example, in the Thomson dispersion relation of result 66, $s\mapsto t^2s$ and $\mu\mapsto t\mu$ leave $\mu^2ds/[s(s+\mu^2)]$ invariant. The fixed dimensionless matching inputs therefore keep $\alpha$ fixed. At fixed quark-to-strong-scale ratios and matched electromagnetic coupling, the hadron masses scale by $t$ and the current charges are unchanged. In particular $H_N\mapsto tH_N$ and $G_F\mapsto t^{-2}G_F$. The factors in the normalized rate stay fixed and $\Gamma_{\rm cont}\mapsto t\Gamma_{\rm cont}$. Holding a dimensionful matching point or an omitted threshold at its old value would require a new matching calculation. The fixed-$g_5^2$ trajectory in issue 18 has a different coupling schedule. [Strong-scale running and threshold matching](18.md) · [Thomson subtraction](../66_neutral_currents_and_hadronic_units.md#responses-in-one-frame)

**Plate 18.** Fix the up basis and the nondegenerate down singular values, and let $U_{dL}=R_{12}(\phi)$, whose upper two rows in the rotated block are $(\cos\phi,-\sin\phi)$ and $(\sin\phi,\cos\phi)$. Apply the same real rotation on the down right partners of the Hermitian mass block and its conjugate on the real completion. This defines a constant unitary $\mathcal U$ commuting with the grading and the real structure. Then $D_F(\phi)=\mathcal U D_F(0)\mathcal U^\dagger$ and $\tan(\ell D_F(\phi))=\mathcal U\tan(\ell D_F(0))\mathcal U^\dagger$. Applied on both interval spin components, $\mathcal U$ commutes with the interior derivative and maps the endpoint domain to the rotated one. Thus all wall eigenvalues and the gap remain exact. The generator $T^+$ stays fixed in the weak basis. Its direct $d$ vertex on each vector pole is $g I_j(u,d)\cos\phi/\sqrt2$. For $s\to u$ it is $-g I_j(u,s)\sin\phi/\sqrt2$. In particular the $s\to u$ vertex on the positive lowest profile survives at $\phi=\pi/2$ since both cosines in its spatial overlap are positive. The matched QCD and QED currents conserve the mass-basis quark flavours. The strangeness-changing current contributes to other processes, while the neutron–proton matrix element selects $d\to u$ at this order. The direct continuum rate has the exact $\cos^2\phi$ factor, with the same factor in hydrogen production. [Exact wall spectrum](../22_finite_dirac_operator_as_window.md)

**Plate 19.** At fixed positive $\alpha$, hold the weak and flavour inputs away from their decoupling limits and vary the isospin-breaking input in $\Delta$. Keep $m_e>0$ and $H_N>0$ finite at the endpoint. In a variation of the light-quark masses the overlap and form factors can vary smoothly with them. Their nonzero limits suffice for the threshold powers. At leading allowed order it is the phase-space integral that reaches zero. The hydrogen channel is evaluated below. $\square$

## Hadronic control from plates 11 and 13

Both plates take $g'\to0$ at fixed $g,v$. In the tree charge convention $e=gg'/\sqrt{g^2+g'^2}$ and $\alpha=e^2/(4\pi)$, hence $\alpha\sim (g')^2/(4\pi)$. The common weak path keeps the pole scale finite in the above schedule with fixed vector profiles. Plate 11 additionally imposes
$$
H_N=\frac{\alpha g'v}{2}\sim\frac{v(g')^3}{8\pi},
\qquad m_{\pi^\pm}=\frac{4H_N}{9}\sim\frac{v(g')^3}{18\pi} .
$$
Thus $H_N$ and the charged pion mass vanish on the imposed hadronic schedule. The baryon splitting, the form factors and the persistence of an allowed neutron window require independent inputs there. Plate 13 keeps the strong scale and positive light-quark masses fixed in the confining QCD theory. Its pion mass and its hadronic unit have finite positive pure-QCD limits as the electromagnetic interaction is switched off. The limiting $\Delta$ and form factors are their values in the matched QCD theory at those masses. In particular the choice of weak path supplies neither the sign of $\Delta-m_e$ nor its sensitivity to the isospin-breaking mass. [Hadronic prescriptions for plates 11 and 13](https://github.com/arivero/conneskk/issues/27)

## Continuum endpoint and hydrogen 1s

**Proposition 27.3, conditional on the allowed Coulomb treatment.** With the weak and hadronic factors smooth and nonzero at threshold, the continuum rate obeys the following powers for $d\downarrow0$ at fixed limiting $r>0$. For the Coulomb-off comparison we remove the distortion while using the same matched masses and weak matrix element. The powers compare two prescriptions for the continuum at the same endpoint.

Put $T=E_e-m_e$ so that $p_eE_e=(m_e+T)\sqrt{T(2m_e+T)}$. With $F_C=1$ this equals $\sqrt2 m_e^{3/2}T^{1/2}[1+O(\delta/m_e)]$ uniformly for $0\le T\le\delta$. After $T=\delta u$, the remaining integral is $\int_0^1u^{1/2}(1-u)^2du=2/3-4/5+2/7=16/105$. Thus
$$
\Phi(w,r,0)=\frac{16\sqrt2}{105}\,r^{3/2}d^{7/2}[1+O(d/r)] .
$$
At fixed attractive $\alpha$, $p_eE_eF_C=2\pi\alpha E_e^2/(1-e^{-z})$. On $0<T\le\delta$, $z$ is bounded below by $z_{\min}=2\pi\alpha(m_e+\delta)/\sqrt{\delta(2m_e+\delta)}\to\infty$. The denominator tends uniformly to one. Integrating $(\delta-T)^2$ gives $\delta^3/3$, so
$$
\Phi(w,r,\alpha)=\frac{2\pi\alpha}{3}\,r^2d^3
             [1+O(d/r)+O(e^{-z_{\min}})] .
$$
The powers $7/2$ and $3$ follow at the stated recoil order. The second expansion takes $\delta\downarrow0$ at fixed $\alpha>0$. [Threshold observation in issue 27](https://github.com/arivero/conneskk/issues/27#issuecomment-7143147666)

For hydrogen put $\mu_r=m_pm_e/(m_p+m_e)$ and $a_B=1/(\mu_r\alpha)$. Acting on $e^{-r_{pe}/a_B}$, the radial Laplacian gives $a_B^{-2}-2/(a_Br_{pe})$ times the same function. The Coulomb Hamiltonian cancels the $1/r_{pe}$ term with this $a_B$ and leaves eigenvalue $-B_{1s}=-\mu_r\alpha^2/2$. The radial normalization integral is $4\pi\int_0^\infty r_{pe}^2e^{-2r_{pe}/a_B}dr_{pe}=\pi a_B^3$. Consequently
$$
|\psi_{1s}(0)|^2=\frac{(\mu_r\alpha)^3}{\pi},\qquad
M_H=m_p+m_e-B_{1s} .
$$
For a massless antineutrino the two-body energy is $(m_n^2-M_H^2)/(2m_n)=(\delta+B_{1s})[1-(\delta+B_{1s})/(2m_n)]$ on the open side. At leading recoil the neutrino energy is $\delta+B_{1s}$.

To project the local allowed production operator onto hydrogen, take a normalized relative wavefunction $\int d^3p\,|\widetilde\psi_{1s}(\mathbf p)|^2/(2\pi)^3=1$. For total momentum $\mathbf P$ define $\mathbf p_e=\mathbf p+m_e\mathbf P/(m_p+m_e)$ and $\mathbf p_p=\mathbf P-\mathbf p_e$. The relativistically normalized atomic state of spin label $A$ is
$$
\begin{aligned}
|\mathbf P,A\rangle&=\sqrt{2E_H}\sum_{s_p,s_e}C_A(s_p,s_e)\\
&\qquad {}\times
\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{\widetilde\psi_{1s}(\mathbf p)}{\sqrt{2E_p\,2E_e}}\,
|\mathbf p_p,s_p;\mathbf p_e,s_e\rangle .
\end{aligned}
$$
The change to total and relative momenta has unit Jacobian. The two relativistic product norms cancel the $2E_p\,2E_e$ denominator, and the wavefunction and spin norms leave $2E_H(2\pi)^3$ times the total momentum delta function for each orthogonal spin state. At leading order set $E_p=m_p$ and $E_e=m_e$ in the projection factor. The weak kernel is local in the relative coordinate and its smooth spinor factors are evaluated at zero relative momentum. Integrating the conjugate wavefunction then gives $\psi_{1s}^*(0)$. The sum over $A$ is the sum over the proton and electron spin products by unitarity of $C_A$. Thus the angle-independent squared amplitude above, evaluated at $E_e=m_e$, is multiplied by $2E_H|\psi_{1s}(0)|^2/(2m_p\,2m_e)$.

In the two-body phase space, the factor $2E_H$ cancels against the atomic normalization. The flux and the remaining neutrino normalization cancel the neutron mass and neutrino energy factors. The spatial delta function and the neutrino solid angle leave $2\pi(4\pi)/(2\pi)^3=1/\pi$. For $E_H=\sqrt{M_H^2+E_\nu^2}$ the energy delta function evaluates $\int E_\nu^2dE_\nu$ on shell with Jacobian $(1+E_\nu/E_H)^{-1}$, which tends to one at leading recoil. With $[x]_+=\max(x,0)$ the rate at this order is
$$
\frac{\Gamma_{1s}}{H_N}
=\frac{\Xi^2|\mathcal I_{ud}V_{ud}|^2\mathcal S}{\pi}
\frac{|\psi_{1s}(0)|^2}{H_N^3}
\left[\frac{\delta+B_{1s}}{H_N}\right]_+^2 .
$$
In particular the bound channel closes quadratically at its own threshold and is open at the continuum endpoint. Under the comoving schedule $B_{1s}$ scales as $t$ and the density at the origin scales as $t^3$, so $\Gamma_{1s}/H_N$ is invariant there. $\square$ [Bound-state projection, Faber et al., Eqs. (5)–(8)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0906.0959#page=2)

| Splitting at fixed $\alpha>0$ | $p+e^-+\bar\nu_e$ | $H(1s)+\bar\nu_e$ |
|---|---|---|
| $\delta\le -B_{1s}$ | Closed | Closed, zero rate at equality |
| $-B_{1s}<\delta\le0$ | Closed, zero rate at $\delta=0$ | Open, with $E_\nu=\delta+B_{1s}$ at leading recoil |
| $\delta>0$ | Open | Open |

The middle row contains only bound beta decay. In the same Coulomb problem excited $ns$ levels have $B_{ns}=B_{1s}/n^2$, and they also open once $\delta>-B_{ns}$. Their thresholds lie above the ground-state threshold. The table and rate here resolve the requested $1s$ member.

## Calculation checks

The following Python 3 script uses SymPy for exact algebra and traces, and numerical quadrature with artificial inputs for the mode overlaps and the endpoint integrals. It checks the normalizations, the continuum powers and their coefficients, the plate scalings and the three threshold rows. The analytic arguments above give the results for general inputs in the stated domain.

```python
from math import cos, expm1, fsum, isclose, pi, sin, sqrt

import sympy as sp
from sympy.physics.matrices import mgamma, msigma

theta, y, scale, g, gp, v = sp.symbols('theta y t g gp v', positive=True)
assert sp.trigsimp(sp.cos(theta)**2 + sp.sin(theta)**2) == 1
assert sp.trigsimp(sp.tan(theta)*sp.cos(theta) - sp.sin(theta)) == 0
modes = []
for j in range(4):
    k = (sp.Integer(j) + sp.Rational(1, 2))*sp.pi
    mode = sp.sqrt(2)*sp.cos(k*y)
    assert sp.diff(mode, y).subs(y, 0) == mode.subs(y, 1) == 0

def integrate_unit(integrand, panels=4096):
    return fsum(integrand((j + 0.5)/panels) for j in range(panels))/panels

def phase_space(endpoint_gap, electron_mass, alpha):
    if endpoint_gap <= 0:
        return 0.0

    def integrand(u):
        kinetic = endpoint_gap*u*u
        energy = electron_mass + kinetic
        momentum = sqrt(kinetic*(2*electron_mass + kinetic))
        density = momentum*energy
        if alpha:
            coulomb = 2*pi*alpha*energy/momentum
            density = 2*pi*alpha*energy**2/(-expm1(-coulomb))
        return density*(endpoint_gap - kinetic)**2*2*endpoint_gap*u

    return integrate_unit(integrand)

def overlap(j, theta_a, theta_b):
    k_j = (j + 0.5)*pi
    arguments = (k_j + a*theta_a + b*theta_b for a in (-1, 1) for b in (-1, 1))
    return sqrt(2)/4*fsum(sin(z)/z if z else 1 for z in arguments)
```
