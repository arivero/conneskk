# The slab regulator, circle eta invariant and the flavour anomaly

**Proposition.** Let $W$ be a closed spin four-manifold. Use the product $W\times S^1_R$, a connection pulled back from $W$ commuting with the constant holonomy, and Witten's $\operatorname{sgn}(0)=+1$ convention. On a charge block put $a_i=\sigma_{\rm spin}+q_i\theta/\pi\bmod1$ with $\sigma_{\rm spin}=0,1/2$, and orient the product so positive-chiral zero modes have eigenvalues $(n+a_i)/R$. Then
$$\eta=\sum_{0<a_i<1}\operatorname{ind}(D^+_{W,i})(1-2a_i)
+\sum_{a_i=0}\dim\ker D_{W,i}.$$
**Proof.** Each nonzero tangential eigenvalue contributes symmetric pairs $\pm\sqrt{\lambda^2+(n+a_i)^2/R^2}$. On a tangential zero mode the signed zeta sum at zero is $\zeta(0,a)-\zeta(0,1-a)=1-2a$. At $a=0$ only the actual zeros remain in this sign convention. A half total shift gives gap at least $1/(2R)$ and zero eta on that block; combining antiperiodic spin and odd-charge half holonomy gives integral total shift and can restore zero modes. For one spin doublet in an instanton of charge $k$ at zero total shift the phase is $(-1)^k$. With periodic spin, quark and lepton doublet holonomies of $3(B-L)$ have weights $+1,-3$; their sawtooth slopes cancel and the combined phase is one, including at the crossings. The separate phases can be complex between crossings. The orbifold determinant needs its own projected measure. For the free massless types (a),(b) the usual circle unfolding is already unitary on their interval Hilbert space; a nonzero interval mass unfolds with a reflection term. $\square$ [Witten, §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05051v1) · [Two interval circles](../../lemmas/31_interval_as_antiperiodic_circle.md)

**Two walls.** Witten's same *outward-normal* $L$ at both walls is mixed type (c) in a single coordinate frame. For $m=-\mu$, $\mu\ell>1$, there is a separated pair of mass $\mu\,\mathrm{sech}(\kappa\ell)$ and index zero. For $m>0$ the first wavenumber lies strictly between $\pi/(2\ell)$ and $\pi/\ell$. A Pauli–Villars field of large positive mass therefore has no boundary zero mode here, and the large-mass slab gives $|\det D_W^+|^2$ on invertible backgrounds. In types (a),(b) a regulator with the **same** normal operator and projector retains the normalized normal zero profile at every regulator mass. For $x$-independent gauge fields its tangential Weyl block is precisely the physical one and opposite statistics cancel this determinant factor. A different regulator, an inflow theory or different domains require a separate calculation. Under a common bulk $SU(2)$ transformation the endpoint maps are homotopic through the interval. The three quark signs and the lepton sign cancel whether or not the profiles overlap. This assumes the same gauge field acts on both sectors; independently gauged walls with an odd number of doublets need additional content or specified inflow. [Physical quark and lepton domains](07.md)

**Measure and bundle.** For admissible Bär–Campos loops of a single left flavour doublet in the vacuum sector the lattice measure twist, the relative continuum Pfaffian sign and the level-one Wess–Zumino phase on the $SU(3)$ extension are all $-1$. The spectral flow counts one representative per conjugate pair. Multiplication gives $(-1)^{N_c}$ for $N_c$ doublets. The internal bundle $SU(2)\to SU(3)\to S^5$ has the nontrivial clutching class: $\pi_4 SU(3)=0$ makes $\pi_5 S^5\to\pi_4 SU(2)=\mathbb Z_2$ surjective. Hence a choice identifying the spheres gives an abstract bundle isomorphism with the anomaly generator. It supplies neither a physical flavour connection nor a pion field. A vectorlike $\mathbf4+\overline{\mathbf4}$ window has trivial total twist; a single end in the defining symplectic representation has the familiar mod-two sign. For the ordinary product spin structure the four global Standard Model groups have $\Omega_4^{\rm Spin}(BG_q)=\mathbb Z^4$, with no torsion theta angle. No central element of the ordinary gauge group acts as fermion parity on all this matter: $\nu^c$ is a singlet. Spin-charge quotients would need other group data. [Bär–Campos, §§4–6](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0001025) · [Bordism computation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.11277)

**Vectorlike measure.** On admissible links with a specified gauge-covariant interpolation $U\mapsto A_U$, use $d\mu_m=Z_m^{-1}e^{-I[U]}\prod_f\det(\slashed D[A_U]+m_f)\,dU$. For $m_f\ge0$ the paired nonzero eigenvalues give $\lambda^2+m_f^2$; zero modes give $m_f$ each. In a regulator with the indicated index the four-dimensional anomaly is recovered on each connection. Full nonabelian flavour chiral symmetry needs equal gauge couplings for the flavours. With one-flavour density $\rho_{V,m}(\lambda)=V^{-1}\langle\sum_k\delta(\lambda-\lambda_k)\rangle_{\mu_m}$, a condensate needs a nonzero density at zero in the Banks–Casher limit: take $V\to\infty$ before $m\downarrow0$ and the spectral probe to zero with the specified density convention. Locality, measure equivalence and that limiting density remain separate questions. Overlap/Ginsparg–Wilson already supplies exact lattice index and chiral symmetry in its admissible setting. [Neuberger](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9707022) · [Hasenfratz–Laliena–Niedermayer](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9801021) · [Lüscher](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9802011)

**A spacetime-dependent grading axis.** If an independent four-dimensional Dirac determinant couples to a smooth $n:W\to S^4$ by the gapped Clifford mass of Abanov–Wiegmann at latitude $\pi/2$, their term is $\theta=\pi$ per copy. The projectors $(1\pm n^a\Gamma_a)/2$ on $\mathbb C^4$ are the pullbacks of the two quaternionic Hopf lines; their second Chern numbers differ in sign and either has magnitude $|\deg n|$. Relative to a positive reference mass the sign from reversing the negative-eigenbundle mass in an adiabatic calculation is $(-1)^{\deg n}$ (the common gravitational contribution of its rank two is even). Two independent copies square this sign. Lemma 29 supplies the Clifford fibre but its tunnelling block is $\lambda I_4$ and its grading axis is marked internal data. Deriving the field $n$, the mass coupling and the number of determinant copies would be necessary to assign this theta term to the original window. [Abanov–Wiegmann, Eqs. 26–29](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/9911025)

## The flavour bundle from a family of monopole kernels

**Proposition.** Give the two-sphere bundle $N(1,0)=\mathbb P(E)\to S^5$ the homogeneous connection and the degree-two relative monopole of Lemma 53. For a smooth $f:S^5_{\rm inflow}\to S^5$ with degree $d$, pull back this whole family of fibre Dirac operators. Its kernel, with its induced connection, is the flavour doublet $f^*E^*$ with the pullback of the homogeneous $SU(2)$ connection. A left Weyl doublet coupled to this bundle has Witten sign $(-1)^d$ on the inflow sphere.

**Proof.** The rank-two fibre zero space is $H^0(\mathbb{CP}^1,\mathcal O(1))$ and fibrewise evaluation identifies its bundle with $E^*$. The induced horizontal transport in Lemma 53 is that of the principal bundle $SU(3)\to S^5$. Taking a kernel and projecting its connection both commute with this pullback. The clutching element lies in $\pi_5(BSU(2))=\pi_4(SU(2))=\mathbb Z_2$. Precomposition by a degree-$d$ sphere map multiplies it by $d$, and the defining representation is pseudoreal so dualizing leaves the class unchanged. The mod-two index for the generator is one. For $N_c$ such doublets multiplicativity gives $(-1)^{N_c d}$. Taking the identity map gives an explicit representative with connection for the identification requested in the issue. Interpreting this family connection as the flavour background and choosing the Weyl multiplicity are physical inputs. [Vertical kernel and induced connection](../53_conic_monopole_window.md) · [Witten's global anomaly](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-2693(82)90728-6)

## The grading-axis term in a specified spectral window

**Proposition.** There is a scalar coupling on the level-one twistor geometry whose single complex Dirac determinant realizes the Clifford mass in the question. Let $Y=\mathbb{CP}^3$ carry its canonical spin$^c$ Dirac operator twisted by $\mathcal O(1)$ and its Fubini–Study connection. Its kernel $P$ is $H^0(Y,\mathcal O(1))=\mathbb C^4$. Let $\Delta$ be the smallest nonzero absolute eigenvalue. On a closed spin spacetime $W^4$ prescribe a slowly varying $n:W\to S^4$ and use the Hermitian mass family
$$\mathcal M_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{-1}D_Y+V_n,\qquad
V_n(y)=\lambda+5\mu\sum_{a=1}^5 n^a x_a(y),\qquad \lambda,\mu\in\mathbb R.$$
Here $x_a$ are the pullbacks of unit-sphere coordinates for the twistor projection, with the orientation of Proposition 50.2. Set $b=|\lambda|+5|\mu|$ and $g=\min(|\lambda+\mu|,|\lambda-\mu|)>0$. When $\Delta/\varepsilon>b+b^2/g$ the relative negative-eigenbundle of the gapped mass family is represented by the negative eigenbundle of $d(n)=\lambda1+\mu n^a\Gamma_a$ in the four-state sector.

**Proof.** Compression of scalar multiplication gives $P(5x_a)P=\Gamma_a$ exactly in this convention. On $1-P$, the diagonal block differs from $D_Y/\varepsilon$ by an operator of norm at most $b$. Its inverse has norm at most $(\Delta/\varepsilon-b)^{-1}$. Multiplying the off-diagonal blocks by $s\in[0,1]$ changes the low Schur complement from $d(n)$ by at most $s^2 b^2/(\Delta/\varepsilon-b)<g$. The family stays gapped uniformly in $n,s$. With the blocks decoupled one may remove $V_n$ on the high sector without closing its gap. This compares the whole internal mass family to the finite mass plus a fixed reference tower. [Compression of the height functions](../50_projectors_as_compressed_height_functions.md)

For $|\mu|>|\lambda|$ the negative eigenbundle of $d$ is the pullback of one quaternionic Hopf line. It has rank two, $c_1=0$ and second Chern number $\pm\deg n$. When $|\lambda|>|\mu|$ it is zero or a trivial rank-four bundle. In the adiabatic complex Dirac determinant with a positive scalar reference mass, the relative sign from the negative-eigenbundle $E_-$ is $(-1)^{\mathrm{ind}\,\slashed D^+_{E_-}}$. The rank-two gravitational term is even, giving $(-1)^{\deg n}$ in the first regime and $+1$ in the second. A constant reference texture has sign $+1$. In the anti-Hermitian kinetic convention a constant axial rotation $e^{i\pi\gamma_5/4}$ on both spinor variables transforms the scalar mass into $i\gamma_5d$. Apply the same rotation to the reference. Its measure Jacobian on the fixed full kinetic bundle cancels from their ratio. At $\lambda=0$ this is the pseudoscalar convention at latitude $\pi/2$ in the second hierarchy of Abanov and Wiegmann. The internal estimate above controls the tower. The determinant statement additionally uses the adiabatic texture and the specified reference and topological counterterm convention. [Abanov and Wiegmann, Sec. IV, Eqs. (26)–(29)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/9911025)

For the complete doubled window of Lemma 29 with an off-diagonal perturbation, take a general invertible block $d:W_0\to V_0$. The sign of the Hermitian mass $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&d^\dagger\\d&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ has off-diagonal block $u=d(d^\dagger d)^{-1/2}$. Its negative bundle consists of $(v,-uv)/\sqrt2$ and is globally isomorphic to the fixed $W_0$. Its relative index sign from this mechanism is $+1$. Using left spacetime chirality on $W_0$ and right on $V_0$ instead retains a single complex Dirac multiplet for the Hermitian block $d=\lambda+\mu n\cdot\Gamma$ and the single-copy calculation applies. The original undeformed tunnelling block is scalar. The scalar height coupling, physical chiral projection and mass determinant above specify where a grading-axis term can arise. The disc-bundle Hamiltonian alone supplies no spacetime dynamics for $n$. $\square$ [Odd mass blocks and their spectra](../46_window_cross_blocks.md)
