# Stationary points of the flat one-loop wall potential below the critical count

**Theorem (partly computer-assisted, Python with integer interval arithmetic).** Keep precisely the flat massless towers, one-loop determinant and boundary conditions of Lemma 26. Write $t=\sin^2\theta$, $w=1-2t$, $u=t(1-t)$, $J(z)=\int_0^\infty x^3/(\sinh^2x+z)\,dx$. Let $h_c=wJ(cu)/J(t)$, $A=h_4$, $H=6h_2+(24/5)h_{16/5}-4n_2 A$. On $0<t<1/2$, the sign of $V'$ is the sign of $H-n_1$. Set $a=54/5-4n_2$, $b=69/50$, $n_*=(4/3)(4n_2-54/5)$. For $n_1>0$ and $0\le n_2<n_c$ the entire interior stationary list is as follows.

- If $n_2\le b$, the left half has one strict maximum for $0<n_1<a$, otherwise no stationary point.
- If $b<n_2<n_c$, put $B(t)=[6h_2'+(24/5)h_{16/5}']/(4A')$, $t_f=B^{-1}(n_2)$ and $m=H(t_f)$. For $0<n_1<m$ there is one left strict maximum on $(t_f,1/2)$ and also one strict minimum on $(0,t_f)$ exactly when $n_1>a$. At $n_1=m$ there is a single degenerate stationary point with no sign change; above $m$ there are none on the left. In particular for $27/10<n_2<n_c$ the exact disappearance curve is $(n_2,n_1)=(B(t),H(t;B(t)))$. The parameter is unique.
- On the right half there is exactly one stationary point, a strict global minimum, if $27/10<n_2<n_c$ and $0<n_1<n_*$. In every other case the unique global minimum is $\theta=\pi/2$. There is no stationary point at $t=1/2$.
- For $n_2=1$ the positive integers $n_1=1,\ldots,6$ give one interior maximum, and all larger integers give none. For $n_2=2$ the corresponding list is $n_1=1,2$. In both cases the endpoint is the unique global minimum. The unrestricted real-count version of the scan conjecture fails: $n_2=2$ and $2.8<n_1<m$ give a metastable minimum. Python quadrature places this fold near $n_1=2.80413$, $t=0.002113$.

**Proof of the classification.** Let $R=[6J(2u)+(24/5)J(16u/5)]/[4J(4u)]$. Lemma 26.1 shows $R$ increases from $27/10$ to $n_c$. On the right half $V'$ is negative when $R\ge n_2$. Where $R<n_2$, the positive function $4(2t-1)J(4u)(n_2-R)/J(t)$ strictly increases to $n_*$, since $u$ decreases and each positive factor increases. Subtracting $n_1$ proves the right-half assertions. Reflection $V(1-t)<V(t)$ for $t<1/2$ excludes a global minimum on the left, including $t=0$. On the left $H=4A(R-n_2)$, $A'<0$: with $e(z)=-zJ'(z)/J(z)$ increasing and $0<e<1$, its log derivative is at most $-2/w+e(t)/(1-t)<0$ since $4u\ge t$. Now $H'=4A'(B-n_2)$, $H(0)=a$, $H(1/2)=0$. It remains to show $B$ increases from $b$ to $n_c$. The two endpoints then give exactly the counts above. [Original potential and elasticity](../../lemmas/26_interior_vacuum_matching_scale.md)

**Small-$t$ proof of monotonicity.** Put $f=J(t)$, $F_c=wJ(cu)$, $U=F_4-f$ and $G=6F_2+4.8 F_{16/5}-5.52F_4-5.28f$. The constants here are exact decimals. If $D_g=fg'-gf'$, then $B=b+D_G/(4D_U)$. Its derivative has the sign of
$$W=(ftG''-Gt f'')D_U-D_G(ftU''-Ut f'').$$
To bound this for $t\le10^{-5}$, put $j=3\zeta(3)/2$, $C=\zeta(3)+\log2-11/6$, $C_2=4\zeta(3)/5+\log2-101/60$. For $z\le4\cdot10^{-5}$ write $s_z=1+|\log z|$. Subtract $j+z(\tfrac12\log z+C)$ from $J$. The remainder and its first derivative are bounded in absolute value by $2z^2s_z,2zs_z$, and $|zJ''-1/2|\le2zs_z$.

Here are details of those constants. Expanding the integrand in $e^{-2x}$ gives $J=3\,\mathrm{Cl}_4(p)/(2\sin p)$ for $p=2\arcsin\sqrt z$, $\mathrm{Cl}_4(p)=\sum_{n\ge1}\sin(np)/n^4$. Its third derivative is $\log(2\sin(p/2))$. Integrate three times with initial slope $\zeta(3)$, using $\log p+\log(\sin(p/2)/(p/2))$. This gives $J=P(z)+zV(z)\log z$ with $h=\arcsin(\sqrt z)/\sqrt z$, $d=(1-z)^{-1/2}$, $V=h^3d/2$, and $P=jhd+zh^3d(\log2+\log h-11/6+\varepsilon)$, where $\varepsilon$ is the normalized integral average of $\log(\sin(v/2)/(v/2))$ along $0\le v/p\le1$. On $|z|=1/4$, the convergent series give $|h-1|<0.05$, $|d|<1.16$, $|p|<1.05$, $|\varepsilon|<0.06$. Hence $|P|<4$, $|V|<1$. Expansion through $z^2$ gives the next term $z^2(\tfrac12\log z+C_2)$. On the remaining powers use $|P_k|\le4^{k+1}$ for $k\ge3$ and $|V_k|\le4^k$ for $k\ge2$. For example, in $J''$ the remaining analytic sum is bounded by $4^4z\sum_{j\ge0}(j+3)(j+2)(4z)^j<0.062$, and the differentiated log tail by $16z\sum_{j\ge0}\big((j+3)(j+2)|\log z|+2j+5\big)(4z)^j<0.004(1+|\log z|)$. Keeping the explicit $z^2$ term yields the three stated estimates.

Set $s=1+|\log t|$. Write $F_c=j+t[(c/2)\log t+k_c]+E_c$, $k_c=c(C+\tfrac12\log c)-2j$. From $F_c'=-2J(cu)+cw^2J'(cu)$ and $F_c''=-6cwJ'(cu)+c^2w^3J''(cu)$ the preceding bounds give $|E_c'|,|tE_c''|\le(4c+2c^2)ts$ for $c=2,16/5,4$. For instance $|\log(1-t)|\le t/(1-t)$ and $|J'(cu)|<0.6s$. Integration gives $|E_c|\le(4c+2c^2)t^2s$. Thus $U=t(1.5\log t+k_U)+E_U$, $|k_U|<1$ and $G=c_Gt+E_G$, where
$$c_G=6\log2+7.68\log(16/5)-11.04\log4-10.56j\in(-23,-19).$$
The respective remainder constants for the three bounds are 50 and 540. Also $1.79<f<1.82$, $|f'|<0.6s$, $|tf''|<0.501$. Substitution gives $|(ftG''-Gtf'')D_U|\le3000ts^2<4.70$, $D_G<-33$, $ftU''-Utf''>2.66$. Hence $W>83$ throughout this interval. $D_U$ diverges logarithmically while $D_G$ has finite limit, so $B(0+)=b$. At $t=1/2$, $A$ has a simple zero and $B=R+(A/A')wR_u$ tends to $n_c$.

**Rational interval calculation on the compact interval.** The following gives the enclosures used in the proof, including the removable zero of $B'$ at $1/2$. For a $t$ interval compute the quotient
$$\frac{B'}w=\left(2-\frac{AA''}{(A')^2}\right)R_u+
\frac{J(4u)}{J(t)A'}(w^2 R_{uu}-2R_u).$$
Use $u=t(1-t)$, the usual sum, product and quotient derivative rules for $A=wJ(4u)/J(t)$ and $R$ above. Enclose every operation outward on the integer grid $2^{-200}\mathbb Z$. For $J^{(n)}([a,b])$, $n=0,1,2$, bound the positive integrand in $(-1)^nJ^{(n)}$ on cells $x_i=20(i/4096)^4$ by integrating $n!x^3$ exactly and using denominators $(\sinh^2x_{i+1}+b)^{n+1}$ and $(\sinh^2x_i+a)^{n+1}$. To enclose $\sinh^2x$ start with $x^2$, use the positive term recurrence $T_{k+1}/T_k=4x^2/[(2k+1)(2k+2)]$, sum 100 terms and add at most twice the next to the upper endpoint. Above 20 use $\sinh x\ge e^x/3$ and the exact integral of $x^3e^{-2(n+1)x}$, bounding $e^{-40}$ by $2^{-57}$. Bisect the $t$ interval at its fixed-point midpoint until $A'<0$ and the lower endpoint of the displayed quotient is positive. The independent integer calculations gave:

| Initial interval | Accepted subintervals | Maximum bisection depth | Lower bound for $B'/w$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $[10^{-5},1/10]$ | 338 | 21 | $>0.295$ |
| $[1/10,1/2]$ | 17 | 5 | $>0.054$ |

For the integer counts one needs only the signs of the derivative of the potential. Another rational enclosure used $x_i=20(i/8192)^2$, grid $2^{-100}\mathbb Z$, and the same Darboux formula through $n=1$. For $(n_2,n_1)=(1,1),\ldots,(1,6),(2,1),(2,2)$ use root boxes of radius $1/250$ about $0.4261694,0.3505523,0.2739115,0.1972354,0.1219059,0.0502110,0.3398101,0.1685399$. On $[10^{-5},1/2]$ bisection certifies $J(t)(H-n_1)>0$ before the box, its derivative negative inside and its value negative after. For $(1,7)$ and $(2,3)$ it is negative throughout, requiring eight and twelve boxes. Monotonicity in $n_1$ handles all larger integers. On $[0,10^{-5}]$ bound $J(ct(1-t))$ between $J(c\,10^{-5})$ and $j$, using 16 terms of $\zeta(3)$ and the integral tail bounds. This confirms the same initial signs. These certificates use rational inequalities at finite positive mesh widths; only the illustrative fold coordinates above use floating-point quadrature. $\square$
