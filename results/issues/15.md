# What spectral projection retains of an unpaired charged multiplet

**Theorem.** Let a self-adjoint mass operator $D$ commute with a unitary action of unbroken $SU(3)\times U(1)$, and take $P=\mathbf1_{[-E,E]}(D)$. Each isotypic space is $R\otimes M_R$, $D=1_R\otimes D_R$ there, and the window contains exactly $R\otimes\mathbf1_{[-E,E]}(D_R)M_R$. In particular a retained $\bar3_{4/3}$ has its original colour and electromagnetic couplings. Preserving the unital algebra gives $P1P=1_{PH}$ for every nonzero window. The absence of an opposite-chirality mass partner supplies no spectral cutoff.

**Proof and finite parent.** The decomposition is Schur's lemma and spectral functional calculus. For a chiral mass matrix let the left multiplicity of $R$ be $k+3$, the right multiplicity $k$, and pair the first $k$ by $m1_k$. For its self-adjoint off-diagonal double $D=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&M\\M^\dagger&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and $0<E<|m|$, precisely three left copies survive, with zero eigenvalue. Take $R=\bar3$ as the right dual of the defining colour module, and let the electric group act by character $4$ in the integral normalization $3Q$. Adjoin the conjugate sector if a real structure is wanted (the conjugate does not add independent left Weyl fields). The order-zero representation is realized by $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb C^3,\mathbb C)$ and its conjugate over $\mathbb C\oplus M_3(\mathbb C)$. One may also take the direct sum with a known gapped factorized window. These operations leave the spectator kernel and its charges intact; anomaly-free gauging of the whole parent would need further matter or inflow. The factorization criteria of Lemma 06 concern real structures on specified modules and impose additional pairing hypotheses. [Factorization criterion](../06_doubling_criterion.md) · [Covariant spectral projection](../14_covariant_spectral_projection.md)

If the retained modes genuinely carry these charges, their covariant derivative contains the gluon term in $\bar3$ and $ie(4/3)A_\mu$. The three independent left Weyl antitriplets contribute $-3,\ 2,\ 64/3,\ 12$ to the cubic colour, colour-colour-charge, cubic charge and mixed gravitational charge traces, respectively, in $T(3)=1/2$. Majorana self-mass requires a real gauge representation. The symmetric/flavour counting in Lemma 21 alone specifies a multiplicity and representation label under the groups chosen there. To reinterpret the same count as colour and electric singlets in a different vacuum requires the parent action of the actual massless gauge generators and a mechanism changing that action. The completely projected-out subspace has no states in $PH$.

For a separate singlet surviving in a parent with gap $\Delta$, write $D=D_{\rm diag}+V_{\rm off}$ in light/heavy blocks. At spectral parameter $|z|<\Delta$ the induced term is $-PVQ(QDQ-z)^{-1}QVP$, bounded by $\|PVQ\|^2/(\Delta-|z|)$ when $\mathrm{dist}(0,\sigma(QDQ))\ge\Delta$. Heavy vector exchange can instead give current-current coefficients $g_1g_2/M^2$ when both vertices are present. These formulas require the parent mixing or currents and supply no universal dark coupling. The scalar reading gives zero Weyl anomaly even for charged scalars, while the separate singlet-fermion reading has zero traces through its different gauge representation. The interval below gives a parent in which that change of gauge group and the residual interaction are explicit. $\square$ [Fixed compression and heavy-sector term](../13_nogo_fixed_compression_not_covariant.md)

## An interval with a broken colour group

**Proposition.** There is a flat matrix-valued interval parent with three left multiplets in $\bar{\mathbf3}_{4/3}$ of a specified **broken** colour and charge group. Its exact light window contains nine massive neutral Weyl components and admits a KO-5 reduction together with the stated visible neutrinos. Its first interaction with a visible vector current is mediated by a calculable tower. This realizes the reading in which the ultraviolet and electromagnetic charge generators are different.

**Gauge groups and masses.** Take $H=SU(3)_c\times U(1)_Q$ and $X=SU(3)_X\times U(1)_X$ on an interval of length $\ell$. Give the four-vector components of $H$ Neumann conditions at both walls. For $X$ impose Neumann at zero and Dirichlet at $\ell$, with hidden gauge transformations equal to the identity at $\ell$. Use the complementary conditions on the fifth components and canonical diagonal tree kinetic terms. Only $H$ has gauge zero modes. The first $X$ vector has mass $M_X=\pi/(2\ell)$. No colour or charge projection is presumed at the start of the interval.

Use three copies of the following left Weyl content at the symmetry-preserving end. All these fermions are neutral under $H$. The displayed integer is $s=3q_X$, and the abelian coupling below is normalized to $s$:
$$(\bar{\mathbf3},4),\quad(\bar{\mathbf3},-2),\quad
2(\mathbf3,-1),\quad 2(\mathbf1,3),\quad(\mathbf1,-6).$$
There is no conjugate $(\mathbf3,-4)$. The cubic colour sum per set is $-2+2=0$ and the mixed colour sum is proportional to $4-2-2=0$. The gravitational charge sum vanishes, and the cubic abelian sum is $3(4^3-2^3-2)+2\cdot3^3-6^3=0$. A visible vectorlike Dirac species can also carry a vector charge $s_V$ under $U(1)_X$ and remain an $SU(3)_X$ singlet. Its two chiralities cancel the perturbative mixed anomalies. The ultraviolet charge assignments and the identity boundary subgroup are model inputs. In Lemma 21 the original $\bar{\mathbf3}$ is a representation of the same colour used to count quarks. Identifying it with this separate broken group changes that premise. [Original charge count](../../lemmas/21_top_counting.md)

Implement these fermions and their reality partners with the pure-derivative interval and the boundary graph $\tan(\ell D_F)$ of Theorem 22.1. The projector at zero respects the listed gauge representations. At the broken end choose a real symmetric Majorana mass block $\mu_g1_3$ for the three components of the first multiplet in copy $g$. Pair it in mass with one visible neutral Weyl mode of the same $\mu_g$. Take $0<\mu_g<E<\mu_{\rm sp}<\pi/(2\ell)$ and give the other hidden fermions mass $\mu_{\rm sp}$ at that end. A vectorlike visible block can have any mass below $E$. The theorem gives the exact desired light masses and puts every excited fermion above $E$. The UV chiral content has no independent conjugate of the selected multiplet. The massive excited levels still pair. The broken-end Majorana bilinear is allowed because the remaining gauge group acts trivially there on these fields.

On all light neutral modes represent $\mathbb C\oplus M_3(\mathbb C)$ on both sides by the same scalar character. The colour ideal acts by zero and the physical left-minus-right abelian generator acts by zero. The unit acts by the identity. In the neutral mass matrix each distinct Takagi singular value has multiplicity divisible by four, since each $\mu_g$ occurs three times in the dark states and once in the visible neutrinos. Lemma 06 therefore permits KO-5 on these finite mass data and on their direct sum with the usual charged vectorlike bimodules. With just the nine equal-mass dark Weyl components the multiplicity would be odd. The wall mass degeneracies and the distinction between the two colour groups are essential to this example. The KO statement concerns the visible algebra and the mass window. **The residual interaction.** Work at zero four-momentum at tree level. On the charge-conjugate block of a dark mode, the hidden generator has signs $(s_D,-s_D)$ with $s_D=4$. It anticommutes with the Majorana mass matrix, whose square is $\mu_g^2$. The two conjugations in Lemma 22's current compression give the dark Weyl current profile $s_D\cos(2\mu_g y)/\ell$. The vectorlike visible mass commutes with its generator and gives $s_V/\ell$. The inverse of $-\partial_y^2$ with the hidden vector conditions is $G(y,y')=\ell-\max(y,y')$. Hence, up to the overall current-exchange sign convention, the coefficient of $J_D^\alpha J_{V,\alpha}$ is
$$C_g={g_{5X}^2s_Ds_V\over2\ell^2}\int_0^\ell
  (\ell^2-y^2)\cos(2\mu_g y)\,dy
 =g_{5X}^2s_Ds_V\,\ell\,{\sin x-x\cos x\over x^3},\qquad x=2\mu_g\ell.$$
Here $J_D$ is a unit-normalized left Weyl current. In four-component Majorana notation it is $-\bar\chi\gamma^\alpha\gamma^5\chi/2$ with the usual left-chirality convention. At $\mu_g\ell\to0$ the coefficient tends to $s_Ds_V(g_{5X}^2/\ell)\pi^2/(12M_X^2)$. Momentum corrections are of relative order $p^2/M_X^2$ near zero. The genuine ultraviolet charge thus contributes through massive exchange while the photon and gluon charges of these same light fields vanish. A vanishing visible hidden charge removes this interaction. Kinetic mixing and quantum thresholds require their own matching. $\square$

For the identical unbroken group the first theorem fixes the charge of every surviving Weyl multiplet. For the distinct broken group this interval fixes both its surviving masses and a residual coupling after its gauge content and walls have been specified. Charged scalars in the scalar reading have zero Weyl anomaly and retain their ordinary minimal gauge coupling. The common multiplicity by itself selects none of these gauge representations.
