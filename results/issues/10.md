# The tunnelling action and the choice of cutoffs

**Proposition.** Fix the operator and shape of Setting 29.0 in case (b), or in case (a) at the Fubini–Study radius. Write $F(r)=\int_0^r f$, $I=F(\ell)$ and $J=\int_e^{\ell-a} f$. With $-\log0=+\infty$,
$$\liminf_{M\to\infty}-M^{-1}\log|\lambda_M|\ \ge\ I/2.$$
In case (b) one can add $F(\ell)-F(b)$ on the right whenever $[b,\ell]$ is a fixed product collar with split connection and constant $f=f_0$. In particular the proposed lower bound with $(1+\eta)J/2$ for **every** $\eta>0$ fails in both cases.

**Proof.** The widths in (H1)–(H3) are witnesses for product and core hypotheses. Smaller positive $e',a'$ are witnesses for the same coefficients, domains and eigenvalue. Apply Proposition 29.8 with them and then take the liminf in $M$ before sending the widths and the Agmon loss to zero. This gives $I/2$. Since $0<J<I$, choose $\eta>0$ with $(1+\eta)J<I$. No positive lower-bound prefactor can then hold at that exponent. [Setting and Agmon estimate](../../lemmas/29_window_on_the_disc_bundle.md)

On the split collar let $\phi_3$ be a unit angular zero mode of $\mathcal O(3)$ in positive chirality. The exact adjoint solution is $p_M=\sqrt{3Mf_0}\exp[(3/2)Mf_0(r-\ell)]\phi_3$. Multiply by a fixed cutoff $\chi$ rising from zero to one in $[b+h,b+2h]$. The localized adjoint spectral space converges to the span of these cut-off wall modes by Theorem 29.7. In matching equivariant frames $|\langle v,\chi p_M\rangle|\to1$; if the fourfold singular value vanishes the estimate is immediate. Testing $T_Mu=\lambda_Mv$ against $\chi p_M$ leaves only the derivative of $\chi$. On the strip the core Agmon estimate costs an arbitrarily small loss from $F(b+h)/2$, and the explicit wall mode supplies $3(F(\ell)-F(b+2h))/2$. Polynomial normalization factors disappear in $-M^{-1}\log$. Taking $h\downarrow0$ gives $F(b)/2+3(F(\ell)-F(b))/2$ as claimed. $\square$

**Joint limit.** Keep the mass schedule of Theorem 30.5, including its original $J$ and loss $\eta_0$. On dilation by $\epsilon^{-\alpha}$ its light mass is $O(\epsilon^p)$ for each
$$p<\alpha+\frac{(1-\alpha)I_{\rm eff}}{(1-\eta_0)J},\qquad
I_{\rm eff}=I\ \text{in (a)},\quad I+2[F(\ell)-F(b)]\ \text{in (b)}.$$
With parallel quaternionic structure the same cutoff argument in Proposition 30.7 gives defect $O(\exp[-M(I-\delta)])$ for every $\delta>0$. Two upper bounds alone leave their ratio undetermined. [Joint scaling and defect](../../lemmas/30_massless_limit_as_a_joint_limit.md)

**A solvable transmission problem.** For comparison put $T=\partial_r+Mq f$ on the unweighted interval, $q>0$, $u(\ell)=0$, so $v(0)=0$ for the adjoint. Its inverse has kernel $-\mathbf1_{s\ge r}\exp[Mq(F(s)-F(r))]$. Removing the indicator gives a rank-one operator. The difference has norm at most $\ell$ because $F$ is increasing. The factors of its norm are $\|e^{-MqF}\|_2$ and $\|e^{MqF}\|_2$. Since $f=r$ near zero and $f=f_0$ near $\ell$, their squared norms are asymptotic to $\sqrt\pi/(2\sqrt{Mq})$ and $e^{2MqI}/(2Mq f_0)$. Thus
$$s_{\min}(T)\sim 2\pi^{-1/4}(Mq)^{3/4}\sqrt{f_0}\,e^{-MqI}.$$
This comparison suggests measuring the transfer of the regular coupled core solution into the wall channel for the specified metric and interpolating connection. The following calculations give its determinant, its exponential rate away from exceptional masses, and the part of its prefactor contributed by a split collar.

## A zero-energy formula on the coupled disc bundle

**Proposition.** Fix the metric, mass profile and connection of Setting 29.0. Work in one multiplicity space of the defining $\mathbf4$ of $\mathrm{Sp}(2)$, in case (b) or in case (a) at the Fubini–Study radius. Use normalized core and wall trial vectors $w_0,v_0$ of Theorem 29.7 or 29.11, with disjoint radial supports. At each large $M$ solve
$$
T_M u=-d_M v_0,\qquad P_+u(\ell)=0,\qquad
\langle w_0,u\rangle=1 ,
$$
with the usual regularity at the zero section and $P_+=(1+Z)/2$. This inhomogeneous zero-energy problem has a unique solution. Its scalar coefficient gives
$$
|\lambda_M|=|d_M|\bigl(1+O(M^{-1})\bigr).
$$
The two sides vanish for exactly the same $M$. All data used to compute $d_M$ are specified at zero energy.

**Proof.** In this multiplicity space there is one small singular value and the complementary inverse is bounded by the inverse of a fixed gap. Let $w,v$ span the exact right and left singular spaces. Choose phases so that their overlaps with $w_0,v_0$ are positive. The window theorem bounds the perpendicular model components by $O(M^{-1/2})$. Normalization then gives $|\langle w_0,w\rangle|,|\langle v,v_0\rangle|=1+O(M^{-1})$. Bordering $T_M$ by the column $v$ and row $w^\dagger$ gives a uniformly invertible operator. Its light block is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\lambda&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, where $\lambda$ may carry a phase, and its other block is the gapped complement. Replacing the row and column by the model vectors changes its norm as a perturbation from graph domain to target by $O(M^{-1/2})$ and preserves invertibility. If $\lambda\ne0$,
$$
\langle w_0,T_M^{-1}v_0\rangle
=\lambda^{-1}\langle w_0,w\rangle\langle v,v_0\rangle+O(M^{-1}).
$$
The error uses one perpendicular component on each side of the complementary inverse. The left side equals $-1/d_M$. When $\lambda=0$, taking the inner product of the forced equation with $v$ gives $d_M=0$. Conversely $d_M=0$ exhibits a normalized kernel vector. $\square$ [Core and wall trial spaces, their errors and the gap](../../lemmas/29_window_on_the_disc_bundle.md)

**Finite determinant.** Absorb the radial half-density. The first-order system then has ten components, five of each wall chirality by Proposition 28.2. Let $U(r)$ have as columns a basis of core-regular homogeneous solutions. There are five columns. Indeed on the pull-back core the indicial operator is the vertical unit-sphere spin Dirac operator, up to sign. It has $|\nu|\ge1$, and radial Clifford multiplication exchanges the two signs on the same multiplicity space. The equation is $u'=(C/r+O(1))u$ at fixed $M$. In $s=-\log r$ the $L^2(dr)$ condition is the square-integrability of $e^{-s/2}u$ in $ds$. Its limiting matrix has five negative eigenvalues and five positive ones. The coefficient error is $O(e^{-s})$, so the stable dimension is five by variation of constants on a sufficiently far tail. Such an $L^2$ solution extends across the smooth core. Testing against a cutoff across a tube of radius $h$ costs $O(h^{1/2})$ in the $L^2$ norm of the derivative of the cutoff times a smooth test spinor. Distributional extension and interior elliptic regularity give the asserted domain. [Multiplicity and smooth-core domain](../../lemmas/28_two_walls_connected_parent.md)

Compute $p$ from $T_M p=-v_0$ with $p=0$ before the wall source. In any orthonormal wall basis set $D=P_+U(\ell)$, $z=P_+p(\ell)$ and $h=\int w_0^\dagger U\,dr$. Because $\int w_0^\dagger p\,dr=0$, the boundary and normalization equations for $u=Uc+d_Mp$ give
$$
d_M=\frac{\det D}{\det\begin{pmatrix}D&z\\h&0\end{pmatrix}} .
$$
The denominator is nonzero by uniqueness of the bordered problem. Any change of regular basis multiplies numerator and denominator by the same determinant. This prescription includes the pull-back case, with its coupled half-cylinder wall vector.

**Split-collar prefactor.** In case (b) let $[b,\ell]$ be a fixed product collar with the line connection and $f=f_0$. Write $k=3Mf_0/2$. There is a unique regular homogeneous solution $w_M$ normalized by $\langle w_0,w_M\rangle=1$ and satisfying the four wall rows of $P_+$ perpendicular to the $\mathcal O(3)$ mode $\phi_3$. Write $w_{3,M}(r)=\langle\phi_3,w_M(r)\rangle$. Then
$$
|\lambda_M|=\sqrt{3Mf_0}\,e^{-(3/2)Mf_0(\ell-b)}
                |w_{3,M}(b)|\bigl(1+O(M^{-1})\bigr).
$$
Indeed in this single wall channel $T=i(\partial_r+k)$ and $v_0=N_M\chi(r)e^{k(r-\ell)}\phi_3$. The cutoff equals one on a fixed neighbourhood of $\ell$, so $N_M=\sqrt{2k}(1+O(e^{-cM}))$ for some $c>0$. Direct integration gives $z=i(2k)^{-1/2}(1+O(e^{-cM}))\phi_3$. Expansion of the nonzero bordered determinant in this column proves uniqueness with the remaining four wall rows and the core normalization. The last wall equation is $w_{3,M}(\ell)+d_M z_3=0$, and the homogeneous collar gives $w_{3,M}(\ell)=e^{-k(\ell-b)}w_{3,M}(b)$. This proves the formula with its normalization and exact zero criterion.

For the schedule $M_\epsilon=L\log(1/\epsilon)$ of Theorem 30.5, $L=2(1-\alpha)/[(1-\eta_0)J]$, the unconditional result is $m_\epsilon=\epsilon^\alpha|d_{M_\epsilon}|(1+O(1/\log(1/\epsilon)))$. It also identifies the missing estimate for a constant-times-power asymptotic. If the split-collar entrance has $|w_{3,M}(b)|\sim C M^\gamma e^{-S_bM}$ with $C>0$, set $S=S_b+3f_0(\ell-b)/2>0$. Then
$$
m_\epsilon\sim C\sqrt{3f_0}\,[L\log(1/\epsilon)]^{\gamma+1/2}
                       \epsilon^{\alpha+LS}.
$$
With parallel quaternionic structure the full-action upper estimate above now gives $\eta_t=O(|\lambda_M|^p)$ for every $0<p<I/S$ under the same entrance hypothesis. A uniform entrance asymptotic is open for general admissible interiors. We can obtain a two-sided rate without this hypothesis on the mass scales specified next.

## The exponential rate away from exceptional masses

**Theorem.** Fix all coefficients of Setting 29.0 and vary only $M$, in case (b) or in case (a) at the Fubini–Study radius. There is a number $S_*$ and a set $\mathcal E\subset[1,\infty)$ with $\int_{\mathcal E}dM/M<\infty$ such that
$$
 \lim_{\substack{M\to\infty\\M\notin\mathcal E}}
   -\frac{\log|\lambda_M|}{M}=S_*,
 \qquad \frac{I_{\rm eff}}2\le S_*\le\frac{13}2 I .
$$
The positive real values of the mass parameter at which tunnelling vanishes form a discrete set. The rate can be specified from transfer at imaginary mass by the support formula below. In particular the fraction of $[R,2R]$ excluded by $\mathcal E$ tends to zero. The theorem allows arbitrarily large exceptional masses.

**The regular determinant.** Replace $M$ by $z\in\mathbb C$. In radial half-density the homogeneous equation in the defining multiplicity space reads
$$
 u'=(C/r+B(r)+zf(r)E(r))u,\qquad E=-Zq .
$$
Here $C$ is the Hermitian vertical indicial matrix from the regular-core calculation above. Its positive and negative eigenspaces have dimension five each, with $|c_j|\ge1$. Subtracting $C/r$ from the geometric coefficient leaves a bounded $B$ on the fixed interval. The Hermitian matrices $Z$ and $q$ commute. Denote by $A^{[j]}$ the induced differential action of a matrix on the $j$th exterior power. Let $a$ be the sum of the five positive eigenvalues of $C$ and $v$ their unit exterior vector. The regular five-plane has exterior vector $W(r,z)=r^aY(r,z)$, where $Y(0,z)=v$. Its Volterra equation has homogeneous propagator $(r/s)^{C^{[5]}-a}$ of norm at most one for $0<s\le r$. Iteration gives an entire solution with $Y=v+O_z(r)$. Starting at $s>0$ with the decomposable vector $s^a v$ and taking $s\downarrow0$ gives the same $W$. Its plane is $O_z(r)$ from the positive indicial plane. On this plane $(\log\|u\|)'\ge \min_{c_j>0} c_j/r-O_z(1)$, so every solution there is square integrable at zero. The dimension and smooth-core extension above identify the regular domain.

Let $\mathcal D(z)=(\bigwedge^5P_+)W(\ell,z)$ in a fixed unit wall volume coordinate. Vanishing means a nonzero regular solution satisfies the homogeneous wall condition. For $z=iy$, $y$ real, the differential expression $D_X^E-yfq$ is formally self-adjoint. Green's identity for a solution has zero left side, and the boundary term on $Zu=-u$ is definite up to the factor $i$. Thus the full wall trace vanishes. Uniqueness of the radial initial-value problem gives $u=0$. In particular $\mathcal D(0)\ne0$, proving discreteness. For sufficiently large real $M$ the complementary gap identifies any such zero with the light singular value.

**The resolvent between the two ends.** Choose fixed disjoint intervals $A$ at the core and $B_w$ at the wall. Away from zeros set
$$
 {\cal N}(z)={\cal D}(z)\,\mathbf1_A T_z^{-1}\mathbf1_{B_w}
$$
on this multiplicity space. This operator has an entire continuation. To see it, take a test vector $u$ supported in $A$ and a source $v_w$ supported in $B_w$. The cofactor formula for their matrix element is the bordered determinant above, using the row $\langle u,\cdot\rangle$ and the inhomogeneous column for $v_w$, with the sign fixed by Cramer's rule. If $U_z(t,r)$ is radial propagation, its integrand at $r\in A$, $s\in B_w$ uses the four-vector
$(\bigwedge^4 U_z(\ell,r))\,\iota_{u(r)^\dagger} W(r,z)$ and the source vector $U_z(\ell,s)(-iZ)v_w(s)$, projected at the wall. There is no overlap term since $r<s$. This expression defines the continuation even when $\mathcal D=0$.

Write $a_4$ for the sum of the largest four eigenvalues of $C$. The logarithmic norm of exterior propagation up to the wall contributes a factor at most $(\ell/r)^{a_4}$ times the bounded-coefficient and mass exponentials. The factor $r^a$ in $W$ leaves $r^{a-a_4}$, with $a-a_4\ge1$, integrable against any $L^2$ test vector. The source stays away from zero. In each evolution the mass term in the logarithmic norm is $(\Re z) f E^{[j]}$, since $E$ is Hermitian. Consequently $\mathcal D$ and $\mathcal N$ obey bounds $C_0 e^{T|\Re z|}$ with finite $T$, uniformly on the imaginary axis. The cofactor integrals are entire by local domination, also in operator norm. At large real $M$ with $\mathcal D(M)\ne0$, the simple singular term of the localized inverse has norm $\|\mathbf1_Aw\|\,\|\mathbf1_{B_w}v\|/|\lambda_M|$. Both factors tend to one by core and wall localization. The complementary inverse stays bounded. Thus
$$
  \|{\cal N}(M)\|=\frac{|{\cal D}(M)|}{|\lambda_M|}(1+o(1)).
$$
In particular $\mathcal N$ is nonzero. Every fixed wall strip contains asymptotically the whole limiting mode, including the coupled half-cylinder mode in case (a). [End localization and the complementary gap](../../lemmas/29_window_on_the_disc_bundle.md)

**Growth on the positive mass axis.** For nonzero scalar $g$ with $|g(z)|\le C_0 e^{T|\Re z|}$, put $h(g)=\limsup_{x\to\infty}x^{-1}\log|g(x)|$. This is a limit outside a set of finite logarithmic measure. For a proof, factor $e^{-Tz}g(z)/C_0$ in the right half-plane into its Blaschke product and a bounded zero-free factor of norm at most one. The negative logarithm of the latter modulus is nonnegative harmonic. Harnack's inequality makes its value at $x$, divided by $x$, nonincreasing for $x>0$. The zeros $b$, counted with multiplicity, satisfy $\sum_b\Re b/(1+|b|^2)<\infty$. For $x\ge1$ and $|x-b|\ge(x+|b|)/8$, the negative logarithm of the Blaschke modulus divided by $x$ is bounded by $C\Re b/(1+|b|^2)$, by the formula $(2x)^{-1}\log(1+4x\Re b/|x-b|^2)$. For each fixed zero this tends to zero. The sum over these far zeros tends to zero by domination.

On $R\le x\le2R$, each nearby zero has $\Re b$ and $|b|$ comparable to $R$. Its negative log modulus integrates to at most $CR$, by scaling the integrable logarithmic singularity. If $n_R$ counts zeros nearby somewhere in the interval, the Blaschke condition gives $\sum_{R=2^j,\ j\ge0} n_R/R<\infty$. Hence for any $\delta>0$ the set where the nearby sum exceeds $\delta x$ has finite logarithmic measure. Taking successively later tails for $\delta=1/j$, each of measure below $2^{-j}$, gives one exceptional set outside which the sum divided by $x$ tends to zero. At every point the Blaschke defect is nonnegative, so the resulting limit equals the full limsup. If the bound on the left half-plane is $C_0 e^{-T_- \Re z}$, then $h(g)\ge-T_-$. Otherwise $e^{(T_-+\delta)z}g(z)$ for sufficiently small $\delta>0$ is bounded on the positive real and imaginary axes. Phragmén–Lindelöf in both right quadrants gives boundedness there, since the function has exponential type. It is bounded on the left and tends to zero on the negative real axis. Liouville's theorem would force $g=0$. [Levin, lectures on half-plane factorization and exponential type](https://doi.org/10.1090/mmono/150)

For an operator-valued function with the same bound, define $h$ using its norm. This is the supremum of the indicators of the nonzero scalar matrix elements. Indeed for any larger exponent the rescaled operators are weakly pointwise bounded on $x\ge1$. Applying uniform boundedness first to output functionals and then to the operators gives a uniform norm bound. Choose countably many elements approaching the supremum and take tails of their exceptional sets with summable logarithmic measures. The scalar lower bounds and the norm limsup give the norm limit outside that union. Applying the two limits to the localized inverse yields
$$
 S_*=h({\cal N})-h({\cal D}).
$$

The earlier Agmon bound gives $S_*\ge I_{\rm eff}/2$. For the other end, in the order $q=(3/2,1/2,-1/2,-3/2)$ the two wall chirality multiplicity lists are $(1,2,2,0)$ and $(0,2,2,1)$. Hence the eigenvalues of $E=-Zq$ are $-3/2$ twice, $-1/2$ four times and $1/2$ four times. The smallest five sum to $-9/2$, so $h(\mathcal D)\ge-9I/2$ by the left-half-plane bound. For positive $\Re z$, the largest sums on exterior powers five, four and one are $3/2,2,1/2$. The mass cost of the cofactor integrand divided by $\Re z$ is at most
$$
 \tfrac32F(r)+2[I-F(r)]+\tfrac12[I-F(s)]
 \ \le\ 2I+\tfrac12[I-F(\inf B_w)] .
$$
Changing the fixed wall strip gives the same $S_*$, since the limits describe the same singular value on the intersection of their good sets. Taking arbitrarily thin fixed strips gives $h(\mathcal N)\le2I$. This proves the claimed upper bound. $\square$ [Angular weights in Proposition 28.2](../../lemmas/28_two_walls_connected_parent.md)

**Determining the rate from imaginary mass.** For either the determinant or a nonzero scalar cofactor $g$, take the compactly supported distribution $k_g$ with $g(iy)=\langle k_g(t),e^{iyt}\rangle$. The Paley–Wiener–Schwartz theorem gives existence from the strip bound. Its right support endpoint is $h(g)$. Indeed a right endpoint $b$ gives $h(g)\le b$ by the transform estimate. Conversely, for $s>h(g)$, applying the quadrant argument to $e^{-sz}g(z)$ bounds it on the right half-plane. The converse support theorem places the right endpoint at most $s$. Thus $S_*$ is the supremum of the right endpoints for the scalar cofactors minus the right endpoint for $\mathcal D$. Both data use only imaginary-parameter radial equations with the specified geometry. Evaluating those support edges for a given interior and determining the first nonzero coefficient at the edge are further calculations. [Hörmander, Theorem 7.3.1](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-61497-2)

For the scalar comparison above, $\mathcal D(z)=e^{-zqI}$ and the cofactor from $s$ at the wall to $r$ at the core has kernel $-e^{-zqF(r)} e^{-zq(I-F(s))}$. Its right support edge is zero because the cuts reach both ends. The determinant edge is $-qI$, giving $S_*=qI$. The endpoint integrals in the comparison also recover the factor $(Mq)^{3/4}$ in that model.

**Joint limit and quaternionic defect.** For $M_\epsilon=L\log(1/\epsilon)\notin\mathcal E$,
$$
 m_\epsilon=\epsilon^{\alpha+L S_*+o(1)} .
$$
Equivalently, for each $\delta>0$, it lies between $\epsilon^{\alpha+L(S_*+\delta)}$ and $\epsilon^{\alpha+L(S_*-\delta)}$ at all sufficiently small such $\epsilon$. The excluded scales have finite measure in $d\epsilon/[\epsilon\log(1/\epsilon)]$. With parallel quaternionic structure the earlier full-action defect bound gives $\eta_t=O(|\lambda_{M_\epsilon}|^p)$ on these scales for every $0<p<I/S_*$, in particular every $0<p<2/13$. This follows by choosing losses with $p(S_*+\delta)<I-\delta'$. A pointwise leading prefactor and a lower bound valid through all large masses still require control of cancellations in the core-to-wall amplitude.
