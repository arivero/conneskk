# The tunnelling action and the choice of cutoffs

**Proposition.** Fix the operator and shape of Setting 29.0 in case (b), or in case (a) at the Fubini–Study radius. Write $F(r)=\int_0^r f$, $I=F(\ell)$ and $J=\int_e^{\ell-a} f$. With $-\log0=+\infty$,
$$\liminf_{M\to\infty}-M^{-1}\log|\lambda_M|\ \ge\ I/2.$$
In case (b) one can add $F(\ell)-F(b)$ on the right whenever $[b,\ell]$ is a fixed product collar with split connection and constant $f=f_0$. In particular the proposed lower bound with $(1+\eta)J/2$ for **every** $\eta>0$ fails in both cases.

**Proof.** The widths in (H1)–(H3) are witnesses for product and core hypotheses. Smaller positive $e',a'$ are witnesses for the same coefficients, domains and eigenvalue. Apply Proposition 29.8 with them and then take the liminf in $M$ before sending the widths and the Agmon loss to zero. This gives $I/2$. Since $0<J<I$, choose $\eta>0$ with $(1+\eta)J<I$. No positive lower-bound prefactor can then hold at that exponent. [Setting and Agmon estimate](../../lemmas/29_window_on_the_disc_bundle.md)

On the split collar let $\phi_3$ be a unit angular zero mode of $\mathcal O(3)$ in positive chirality. The exact adjoint solution is $p_M=\sqrt{3Mf_0}\exp[(3/2)Mf_0(r-\ell)]\phi_3$. Multiply by a fixed cutoff $\chi$ rising from zero to one in $[b+h,b+2h]$. The localized adjoint spectral space converges to the span of these cut-off wall modes by Theorem 29.7. In matching equivariant frames $|\langle v,\chi p_M\rangle|\to1$; if the fourfold singular value vanishes the estimate is immediate. Testing $T_Mu=\lambda_Mv$ against $\chi p_M$ leaves only the derivative of $\chi$. On the strip the core Agmon estimate costs an arbitrarily small loss from $F(b+h)/2$, and the explicit wall mode supplies $3(F(\ell)-F(b+2h))/2$. Polynomial normalization factors disappear in $-M^{-1}\log$. Taking $h\downarrow0$ gives $F(b)/2+3(F(\ell)-F(b))/2$ as claimed. $\square$

**Joint limit.** Keep the mass schedule of Theorem 30.5, including its original $J$ and loss $\eta_0$. On dilation by $\epsilon^{-\alpha}$ its light mass is $O(\epsilon^p)$ for each
$$p<\alpha+\frac{(1-\alpha)I_{\rm eff}}{(1-\eta_0)J},\qquad
I_{\rm eff}=I\ \text{in (a)},\quad I+2[F(\ell)-F(b)]\ \text{in (b)}.$$
With parallel quaternionic structure the same cutoff argument in Proposition 30.7 gives defect $O(\exp[-M(I-\delta)])$ for every $\delta>0$. Two upper bounds alone leave their ratio undetermined. [Joint scaling and defect](../../lemmas/30_massless_limit_as_a_joint_limit.md)

**A solvable transmission problem.** For comparison put $T=\partial_r+Mq f$ on the unweighted interval, $q>0$, $u(\ell)=0$, so $v(0)=0$ for the adjoint. Its inverse has kernel $-\mathbf1_{s\ge r}\exp[Mq(F(s)-F(r))]$. Removing the indicator gives a rank-one operator. The difference has norm at most $\ell$ because $F$ is increasing. The factors of its norm are $\|e^{-MqF}\|_2$ and $\|e^{MqF}\|_2$. Since $f=r$ near zero and $f=f_0$ near $\ell$, their squared norms are asymptotic to $\sqrt\pi/(2\sqrt{Mq})$ and $e^{2MqI}/(2Mq f_0)$. Thus
$$s_{\min}(T)\sim 2\pi^{-1/4}(Mq)^{3/4}\sqrt{f_0}\,e^{-MqI}.$$
This calculation identifies the piece still needed in the disc bundle: the transfer of the regular coupled core solution into the wall channel through the specified metric and interpolating connection. Its nonvanishing, exponent and coefficient, and hence a two-sided power or a defect estimate in terms of the actual $\lambda_M$, remain open.

## A zero-energy formula on the coupled disc bundle

**Proposition.** Fix the metric, mass profile and connection of Setting 29.0. Work in one multiplicity space of the defining $\mathbf4$ of $\mathrm{Sp}(2)$, in case (b) or in case (a) at the Fubini–Study radius. Use normalized core and wall trial vectors $w_0,v_0$ of Theorem 29.7 or 29.11, with disjoint radial supports. At each large $M$ solve
$$
T_M u=-d_M v_0,\qquad P_+u(\ell)=0,\qquad
\langle w_0,u\rangle=1 ,
$$
with the usual regularity at the zero section and $P_+=(1+Z)/2$. This inhomogeneous zero-energy problem has a unique solution. Its scalar coefficient gives
$$
|\lambda_M|=|d_M|\bigl(1+O(M^{-1})\bigr).
$$
The two sides vanish for exactly the same $M$. All data used to compute $d_M$ are specified at zero energy.

**Proof.** In this multiplicity space there is one small singular value and the complementary inverse is bounded by the inverse of a fixed gap. Let $w,v$ span the exact right and left singular spaces. Choose phases so that their overlaps with $w_0,v_0$ are positive. The window theorem bounds the perpendicular model components by $O(M^{-1/2})$. Normalization then gives $|\langle w_0,w\rangle|,|\langle v,v_0\rangle|=1+O(M^{-1})$. Bordering $T_M$ by the column $v$ and row $w^\dagger$ gives a uniformly invertible operator. Its light block is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\lambda&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, where $\lambda$ may carry a phase, and its other block is the gapped complement. Replacing the row and column by the model vectors changes its norm as a perturbation from graph domain to target by $O(M^{-1/2})$ and preserves invertibility. If $\lambda\ne0$,
$$
\langle w_0,T_M^{-1}v_0\rangle
=\lambda^{-1}\langle w_0,w\rangle\langle v,v_0\rangle+O(M^{-1}).
$$
The error uses one perpendicular component on each side of the complementary inverse. The left side equals $-1/d_M$. When $\lambda=0$, taking the inner product of the forced equation with $v$ gives $d_M=0$. Conversely $d_M=0$ exhibits a normalized kernel vector. $\square$ [Core and wall trial spaces, their errors and the gap](../../lemmas/29_window_on_the_disc_bundle.md)

**Finite determinant.** Absorb the radial half-density. The first-order system then has ten components, five of each wall chirality by Proposition 28.2. Let $U(r)$ have as columns a basis of core-regular homogeneous solutions. There are five columns. Indeed on the pull-back core the indicial operator is the vertical unit-sphere spin Dirac operator, up to sign. It has $|\nu|\ge1$, and radial Clifford multiplication exchanges the two signs on the same multiplicity space. The equation is $u'=(C/r+O(1))u$ at fixed $M$. In $s=-\log r$ the $L^2(dr)$ condition is the square-integrability of $e^{-s/2}u$ in $ds$. Its limiting matrix has five negative eigenvalues and five positive ones. The coefficient error is $O(e^{-s})$, so the stable dimension is five by variation of constants on a sufficiently far tail. Such an $L^2$ solution extends across the smooth core. Testing against a cutoff across a tube of radius $h$ costs $O(h^{1/2})$ in the $L^2$ norm of the derivative of the cutoff times a smooth test spinor. Distributional extension and interior elliptic regularity give the asserted domain. [Multiplicity and smooth-core domain](../../lemmas/28_two_walls_connected_parent.md)

Compute $p$ from $T_M p=-v_0$ with $p=0$ before the wall source. In any orthonormal wall basis set $D=P_+U(\ell)$, $z=P_+p(\ell)$ and $h=\int w_0^\dagger U\,dr$. Because $\int w_0^\dagger p\,dr=0$, the boundary and normalization equations for $u=Uc+d_Mp$ give
$$
d_M=\frac{\det D}{\det\begin{pmatrix}D&z\\h&0\end{pmatrix}} .
$$
The denominator is nonzero by uniqueness of the bordered problem. Any change of regular basis multiplies numerator and denominator by the same determinant. This prescription includes the pull-back case, with its coupled half-cylinder wall vector.

**Split-collar prefactor.** In case (b) let $[b,\ell]$ be a fixed product collar with the line connection and $f=f_0$. Write $k=3Mf_0/2$. There is a unique regular homogeneous solution $w_M$ normalized by $\langle w_0,w_M\rangle=1$ and satisfying the four wall rows of $P_+$ perpendicular to the $\mathcal O(3)$ mode $\phi_3$. Write $w_{3,M}(r)=\langle\phi_3,w_M(r)\rangle$. Then
$$
|\lambda_M|=\sqrt{3Mf_0}\,e^{-(3/2)Mf_0(\ell-b)}
                |w_{3,M}(b)|\bigl(1+O(M^{-1})\bigr).
$$
Indeed in this single wall channel $T=i(\partial_r+k)$ and $v_0=N_M\chi(r)e^{k(r-\ell)}\phi_3$. The cutoff equals one on a fixed neighbourhood of $\ell$, so $N_M=\sqrt{2k}(1+O(e^{-cM}))$ for some $c>0$. Direct integration gives $z=i(2k)^{-1/2}(1+O(e^{-cM}))\phi_3$. Expansion of the nonzero bordered determinant in this column proves uniqueness with the remaining four wall rows and the core normalization. The last wall equation is $w_{3,M}(\ell)+d_M z_3=0$, and the homogeneous collar gives $w_{3,M}(\ell)=e^{-k(\ell-b)}w_{3,M}(b)$. This proves the formula with its normalization and exact zero criterion.

For the schedule $M_\epsilon=L\log(1/\epsilon)$ of Theorem 30.5, $L=2(1-\alpha)/[(1-\eta_0)J]$, the unconditional result is $m_\epsilon=\epsilon^\alpha|d_{M_\epsilon}|(1+O(1/\log(1/\epsilon)))$. It also identifies the missing estimate for a constant-times-power asymptotic. If the split-collar entrance has $|w_{3,M}(b)|\sim C M^\gamma e^{-S_bM}$ with $C>0$, set $S=S_b+3f_0(\ell-b)/2>0$. Then
$$
m_\epsilon\sim C\sqrt{3f_0}\,[L\log(1/\epsilon)]^{\gamma+1/2}
                       \epsilon^{\alpha+LS}.
$$
With parallel quaternionic structure the full-action upper estimate above now gives $\eta_t=O(|\lambda_M|^p)$ for every $0<p<I/S$ under the same entrance hypothesis. The entrance asymptotic is open for general admissible interiors. The single-channel comparison earlier does not determine it.
