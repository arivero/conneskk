# The hierarchical Gaussian law for left mixing

**Theorem.** Let $M_s=F X_s G_s$, $s=u,d$, with independent standard circular complex Gaussian entries ($\mathbb E|X_{ij}|^2=1$) and fixed positive diagonal $G_s$. Write $x=f_1/f_2$, $y=f_2/f_3$ and take $(x,y)\to(0,0)$ with arbitrary relative speed. Let $z_i$ be row $i$ of $X_sG_s$, use $\langle v,w\rangle=vw^*$, and, in each sector, put
$$a=\langle z_2,z_3\rangle/\|z_3\|^2,\quad b=\langle z_1,z_3\rangle/\|z_3\|^2,\quad
w=z_2-az_3,\quad c=\langle z_1,w\rangle/\|w\|^2.$$
The last ratio is the $2\times2$ Gram minor $(h_{12}h_{33}-h_{13}h_{32})/(h_{22}h_{33}-|h_{23}|^2)$ for $h_{ij}=\langle z_i,z_j\rangle$. Cauchy–Binet expresses its numerator and denominator as sums of products of minors of $X_s$ weighted by squared products of the corresponding diagonal entries of $G_s$. The jointly limiting absolute coefficients of $(V_{12}/x,V_{23}/y,V_{13}/(xy))$ are the magnitudes of
$$c_d-c_u,\qquad a_d-a_u,\qquad b_d-b_u-c_u(a_d-a_u).$$

**Proof.** Divide $M_sM_s^*$ by $f_3^2$. It is $\mathrm{diag}(xy,y,1)h\,\mathrm{diag}(xy,y,1)$. Almost surely $h$ is positive definite. The largest eigenvalue tends to $h_{33}$ and back-substitution into the top two equations gives heaviest eigenvector entries $(xy(b+o(1)),y(a+o(1)),1+o(1))$. Eliminating its last coordinate in the two light equations replaces $h_{ij}$ by $h_{ij}-h_{i3}h_{3j}/(h_{33}-\lambda)$ for $i,j\le2$. The middle eigenvalue divided by $y^2$ tends to $\|w\|^2$ and its first entry is $x(c+o(1))$ in the phase where its second entry tends to 1. Orthogonality now gives the first column's second and third entries as $-x(\bar c+o(1))$ and $xy(\bar c\bar a-\bar b+o(1))$. Taking the three scalar products in $U_u^*U_d$ proves almost-sure convergence under the fixed-matrix coupling, hence convergence in distribution. **Median and lower tail.** For each coefficient condition only on rows independent of the Gaussian rows remaining in that coefficient. Define columns $v_s=G_s z_{3s}^*/\|z_{3s}\|^2$, $w'_s=G_s w_s^*/\|w_s\|^2$. For $23$ condition on both third rows and put $T=\sum_s\|v_s\|^2$; for $12,13$ condition on both second and third rows and take respectively $T=\sum_s\|w'_s\|^2$ and $T=\|v_d\|^2+\|v_u+(a_d-a_u)w'_u\|^2$. Each conditional coefficient is circular Gaussian of variance $T$. In particular the limiting median is the unique positive root
$$\mathbb E[\exp(-m^2/T)]=1/2.$$
This is an explicit Gaussian integral over just the conditioning rows (density $\pi^{-3}e^{-\|r\|^2}$ per row). The limiting probability of $|V_{ij}|<\epsilon f_i/f_j$ is $\mathbb E[1-\exp(-\epsilon^2/T)]=\epsilon^2\mathbb E[T^{-1}]+o(\epsilon^2)$. For $23,13$ use $\|v_d\|\|X_{3d}\|\ge1$ by Cauchy–Schwarz. For $12$, $\|w'_d\|\|X_{2d}\|\ge1$ since $z_{2d}w_d^*=\|w_d\|^2$. Therefore $0<\mathbb E[T^{-1}]\le3$ and dominated convergence applies. The probability bound concerns first the hierarchical limit, then small $\epsilon$. $\square$

For $G_u=G_d=1$, a Python/NumPy check on 200,000 independent pairs (seed 62710) gives illustrative limiting medians $0.986,\ 0.761,\ 0.960$ in that order. Remark 12.10 used 500 samples at finite hierarchy. Overall rescaling of either right profile cancels; unequal within-sector weights enter the integral explicitly. Sending right-profile ratios to zero at the same time defines another limiting problem.
