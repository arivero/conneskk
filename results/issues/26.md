# The scalar photon response of the descending tower on plate 08

We vary the round sphere radius $a$ on the prescribed product $M^4\times S^3(a)\times S^1(b)\times S^1(d)$. We keep the internal volume $V$, the Einstein kinetic coefficient $C_9>0$, the hypercharge coupling $g'>0$, and the hypercharge circle generator fixed. The probe is one free, massless, minimally coupled complex scalar with periodic circle coordinates and zero curvature coupling. We compute its four-dimensional $Q=T_3+Y$ current spectral density and the leading photon insertion, then use the same spectral density to test the spacelike subtraction. We use $\hbar=c_{\rm light}=1$, with radii in $\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}$ and energies in $\mathrm{GeV}$. The microscopic cutoff $\Lambda_{\rm mic}>0$ is fixed in these energy units. [Issue 26 and the closure plan](https://github.com/arivero/conneskk/issues/26#issuecomment-7189863441)

## The finite charged-harmonic sum

**Theorem 26.1.** Use the mostly-minus Minkowski metric and include the Ward contact term in the time-ordered product. The generator-normalized scalar polarization and density are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Pi_{\mu\nu}(q)&=i\int d^4x\,e^{iqx}\langle T J_\mu(x)J_\nu(0)\rangle\\
&=(q_\mu q_\nu-q^2\eta_{\mu\nu})\Pi_a(q^2),\\
\rho_a(s)&=\pi^{-1}\operatorname{Im}\Pi_a(s+i0),\qquad s=q^2=E^2>0 .
\end{aligned}
$$
For $E<\min(2/b,2/d,\Lambda_{\rm mic})$, the exact free scalar cut is
$$
\rho_a(s)=\frac{1}{576\pi^2}\sum_{\ell\ge 0}
\ell(\ell+2)(\ell+1)^2
\left[1-\frac{4\ell(\ell+2)}{a^2s}\right]_+^{3/2},
$$
where $[x]_+=\max(x,0)$. The sum ends at $\ell=\lfloor\sqrt{1+a^2s/4}-1\rfloor$, with a zero term at equality with a pair threshold. The constant mode is neutral. The first charged shell has total squared charge one and opens at $E=2\sqrt{3}/a$ when this threshold lies in the window.

**Mass and generator normalization.** With $K=S^3(a)\times S^1(b)\times S^1(d)$ the action is
$$
S_\Phi=\int d^4x\,dV_K\,
(\partial_\mu\overline\Phi\,\partial^\mu\Phi-|\nabla_K\Phi|^2).
$$
Expand $\Phi=\sum_\lambda \phi_\lambda(x) u_\lambda(y)$ in a complex orthonormal eigenbasis of $L^2(K,dV_K)$ diagonalizing $Q$. Orthonormality gives kinetic term $|\partial\phi_\lambda|^2$ for every coefficient. Integration by parts in the internal gradient term gives its Laplacian eigenvalue as its mass squared. The modes $u_\lambda$ and $\overline{u_\lambda}$ when distinct have independent complex coefficients.

On $S^3(a)\simeq SU(2)$ the weak group acts on the left with generators $T_i=\sigma_i/2$ in the fundamental representation. Acting on $z\in S^3(a)\subset\mathbb C^2$ by $e^{i\vartheta T_i}$ gives tangent $iT_i z$ of length $a/2$, since $T_i^2=1/4$. These three tangent fields are orthogonal by the Pauli anticommutator and have zero divergence because they are Killing. The scalar Laplacian is therefore $-\Delta_{S^3}=4\sum_i T_i^2/a^2$, where the $T_i$ act as Hermitian differential operators. The matrix elements in the Peter–Weyl decomposition furnish a $(j,j)$ representation at $j=\ell/2$, with Casimir $j(j+1)$. For circle labels $n_b,n_d\in\mathbb Z$ and angular coordinates of period $2\pi$ we obtain
$$
 m_{\ell,n_b,n_d}^2=\frac{\ell(\ell+2)}{a^2}
                   +\frac{n_b^2}{b^2}+\frac{n_d^2}{d^2},
\qquad Q=m_L+kn_b .
$$
Here $m_L=-j,-j+1,\ldots,j$ and $Y=-ik\partial_\varphi$ corresponds to the Killing field $k\partial_\varphi$ on $S^1(b)$, with $k\ne0$. The right magnetic label supplies $\ell+1$ copies of each $m_L$. [Scalar Laplacian, Lindblom, Taylor and Zhang, Eq. (1)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.08020) · [Harmonic representations, Kim, Klose and Plefka, Table 1 and Eq. (12a)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/0306054)

The diagonal current creates a particle and antiparticle of the same four-dimensional field, with opposite internal momenta and total threshold $2m_{\ell,n_b,n_d}$. The kinematic lower bounds for pairs carrying nonzero $b$- or $d$-circle mode labels are $2/b$ and $2/d$, respectively. The first is attained by charged $\ell=0$, $n_b=\pm1$, $n_d=0$ modes. The $\ell=0$, $n_b=0$, $n_d=\pm1$ modes at $2/d$ are neutral. With $n_b=0$ the first charged $d$-circle threshold is $2\sqrt{d^{-2}+3/a^2}$. With both labels nonzero the first is $2\sqrt{d^{-2}+b^{-2}}$. The stated window excludes all of these modes. At $n_b=n_d=0$ the squared charge at level $\ell$ sums to
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{m_L,m_R}Q^2
&=(\ell+1)\sum_{r=0}^{\ell}(r-\ell/2)^2\\
&=(\ell+1)\left(\frac{\ell(\ell+1)(2\ell+1)}{6}
       -\frac{\ell^2(\ell+1)}{2}+\frac{\ell^2(\ell+1)}{4}\right)\\
&=\frac{\ell(\ell+2)(\ell+1)^2}{12}.
\end{aligned}
$$
In particular, $\ell=1$ gives four complex scalars with $|Q|=1/2$, each of mass $\sqrt{3}/a$.

**Two-particle cut.** For one canonical complex field, $J_\mu=iQ(\overline\phi\,\partial_\mu\phi-\phi\,\partial_\mu\overline\phi)$ has pair matrix element $Q(p_+-p_-)_\mu$. Its contraction with $q=p_++p_-$ vanishes by equality of the two masses. The intermediate states are distinguishable. In the pair rest frame, let $E_p=\sqrt{|\mathbf p|^2+m^2}$ and $\beta=\sqrt{1-4m^2/s}$. On shell $E_p=E/2$ and $|\mathbf p|=E\beta/2$. With relativistic state normalization, the spatial momentum delta function reduces the Lorentz-invariant phase space to
$$
d\Phi_2=\frac{d\Omega}{16\pi^2}\int_0^\infty
 \frac{|\mathbf p|^2\,d|\mathbf p|}{E_p^2}\,\delta(E-2E_p)
=\frac{\beta}{32\pi^2}d\Omega .
$$
The Jacobian is $d(2E_p)/d|\mathbf p|=2|\mathbf p|/E_p$. Rotational averaging gives $\langle p_i p_j\rangle=|\mathbf p|^2\delta_{ij}/3$. The spatial optical identity $2\operatorname{Im}\Pi_{ij}=\int d\Phi_2\,J_iJ_j^*$ then gives
$$
2s\operatorname{Im}\Pi_a\big|_{\text{one field}}
=\frac{\beta}{8\pi}\frac{4Q^2|\mathbf p|^2}{3},
\qquad
\rho_a\big|_{\text{one field}}=\frac{Q^2\beta^3}{48\pi^2}.
$$
Contact and subtraction terms are real polynomials on this cut. Inserting the squared charge sum and the pair masses proves the formula. $\square$ [Original cut calculation](https://github.com/arivero/conneskk/issues/26#issuecomment-7054200289) · [Scalar pair normalization in result 96](../96_photon_thresholds_from_vectors_and_spectators.md#a-positive-current-spin-one-comparison)

## Coefficient and uniform remainder

Put $L=a\sqrt s/2$, $X=\sqrt{1+L^2}$, and $n=\ell+1$. The density becomes
$$
\rho_a(s)=\frac{1}{576\pi^2 L^3}
\sum_{1\le n\le X}(n^4-n^2)(X^2-n^2)^{3/2}.
$$
For $j=2,4$ let $f_j(t)=t^j(1-t^2)^{3/2}$ for $0\le t\le1$ and extend it by zero for $t>1$. Its derivative on $(0,1)$ is $t^{j-1}\sqrt{1-t^2}\,[j-(j+3)t^2]$. It rises from zero to one maximum of height at most one and falls back to zero. The total variation is thus at most two. On each interval $[(n-1)/X,n/X]$, the deviation from $f_j(n/X)$ is bounded by the variation on that interval. Integrating over each interval, multiplying by $X$ and adding gives
$$
\left|\sum_{n\ge1} f_j(n/X)-X I_j\right|\le2,
\qquad I_j=\int_0^1 f_j(t)\,dt .
$$
The substitution $u=t^2$ gives $I_j=\tfrac12\,\Gamma((j+1)/2)\Gamma(5/2)/\Gamma((j+6)/2)$. Using $\Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt\pi$ and $\Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z)$ yields $I_2=\pi/32$ and $I_4=3\pi/256$. Consequently
$$
\left|\rho_a(s)-\frac{I_4X^8-I_2X^6}{576\pi^2 L^3}\right|
\le\frac{X^7+X^5}{288\pi^2 L^3}.
$$
For $L\ge1$, $X^8/L^3=L^5(1+L^{-2})^4$ and $X^6/L^3=L^3(1+L^{-2})^3$. The bound on the residual is $O(L^4)$ with an absolute constant. Thus
$$
\rho_a(s)=\frac{a^5s^{5/2}}{3\,2^{19}\pi}
\left[1+O\big((a\sqrt s)^{-1}\big)\right].
$$
On any fixed band $0<s_1\le s\le s_2$ with $\sqrt{s_2}$ in the window, the relative error is uniformly $O(1/(a\sqrt{s_1}))$. The variation estimate holds across individual sphere pair thresholds and for nonintegral $X$.

## Fixed and moving photon energies

In the tree reduction use $g_A^{-2}=C_9\int_K |K_A|^2 dV_K$ for a gauge generator $K_A$ in charge-connection coordinates. The sphere volume is $2\pi^2a^3$ and the circle lengths are $2\pi b$ and $2\pi d$, so $V=8\pi^4a^3bd$. The weak Killing length is $a/2$ and the hypercharge Killing length is $|k|b$. These give
$$
 g^{-2}=C_9Va^2/4,\qquad g'^{-2}=C_9Vk^2b^2 .
$$
In these units $C_9$ has dimension $\mathrm{GeV}^7$ and $C_9V$ has dimension $\mathrm{GeV}^2$. The couplings and the polarizations are dimensionless. [Killing norm convention](../47_berger_radius_at_matching.md) · [Independent kinetic normalizations](../14_normalizations_not_fixed.md)

Write $c_W=\cos\theta_W$ and $s_W=\sin\theta_W$ in the first quadrant. Plate 08 fixes $H_N$ and $m_\tau$ by $m_\tau=\alpha v$ and $H_N=\alpha g'v/2$, with $\alpha=e^2/(4\pi)$. Inserting $e=g'c_W$ and $g=g'c_W/s_W$ gives
$$
g'=\frac{2H_N}{m_\tau},\qquad v=\frac{4\pi m_\tau}{g'^2c_W^2},
\qquad a=\frac{2s_W}{g'\sqrt{C_9V}\,c_W},
$$
$$
b=\frac{1}{|k|g'\sqrt{C_9V}},\qquad d=\frac{V}{8\pi^4 a^3 b},
\qquad e^2 a^2=\frac{4s_W^2}{C_9V}.
$$
The imposed one-doublet tree masses $M_W=gv/2$ and $M_Z=\sqrt{g^2+g'^2}\,v/2$ are $2\pi m_\tau/(g's_Wc_W)$ and $2\pi m_\tau/(g's_Wc_W^2)$. They grow as $c_W$ decreases to zero. The hadronic reference and the Higgs norm are supplied externally along this path. The growing tree masses can leave the effective range of the prescribed model. [Plate 08 inputs](https://github.com/arivero/conneskk/issues/26)

**Fixed energy.** At leading order the canonical photon couples through $eJ_\mu$. Its absorptive insertion is $e^2\operatorname{Im}\Pi_a(s+i0)=\pi e^2\rho_a(s)$. At fixed $0<E<\min(2/b,\Lambda_{\rm mic})$, the condition $E<2/d$ eventually holds as $d$ shrinks. Substituting the tree normalization into $e^2 a^5 E^5/(3\,2^{19})$ gives
$$
e^2\operatorname{Im}\Pi_a(s+i0)
=\frac{s_W^5s^{5/2}}{3\,2^{14}g'^3(C_9V)^{5/2}}
   c_W^{-3}\,[1+O(c_W)].
$$
The error is uniform on fixed positive energy bands in the window. The coefficient is positive. At fixed energy the growing insertion eventually exceeds the range of a small one-loop photon correction. The free current cut remains exact in its stated window.

**Moving energy.** Let $a=a_0R$ and $E=E_0R^{-p}$ with $R$ dimensionless, $a_0,E_0>0$ fixed, and $3/5<p<1$. Then $aE=a_0E_0R^{1-p}\to\infty$. For $n=\ell+1<X$ each shell has $n^2$ scalar fields and at most $n$ neutral fields. The number of open charged species is $X^3/3+O(X^2)=(aE)^3/24+O((aE)^2)$ by summing squares and bounding the neutral count by the sum of $n$. With $b$ fixed and $d\propto R^{-3}$, both circle thresholds and the fixed microscopic cutoff exceed $E$ for large $R$. The coupling relation gives $s_W^2=1/(1+4/(g'^2 C_9Va^2))=1+O(R^{-2})$, hence
$$
e^2\operatorname{Im}\Pi_a(E^2+i0)
=\frac{a_0^3 E_0^5}{3\,2^{17}C_9V}\,
  R^{3-5p}\,[1+O(R^{-(1-p)})].
$$
Thus the one-loop absorptive insertion tends to zero while the number of open charged species diverges. At large $aE$, the small-insertion criterion for the imaginary part is $s_W^2 a^3 E^5/(3\,2^{17}C_9V)\ll1$. Perturbative control of the interacting photon also requires the real subtracted polarization and the other interactions to stay small.

## Fixed-band spacelike dispersion

**Proposition 26.2.** Fix $0<s_1<s_2$ with $\sqrt{s_2}<\min(2/b,\Lambda_{\rm mic})$. For sufficiently large $a$ the entire band lies below $2/d$ and above the first charged sphere threshold. Write $u=q_E^2>0$ for the squared spacelike momentum in $\mathrm{GeV}^2$. With a charge subtraction at zero, the band contributes $-B_a(u)$ to $e^2(\Pi_a(-u)-\Pi_a(0))$, where
$$
B_a(u)= e^2u\int_{s_1}^{s_2}\frac{\rho_a(s)}{s(s+u)}\,ds>0 .
$$
The kernel follows by subtracting $1/s$ from $1/(s+u)$ in the spectral representation. Insert the uniform estimate for $\rho_a(s)$ and use $(1+u/s)^{-1}=1+O(u/s_1)$ on the band. Since $\int_{s_1}^{s_2}s^{1/2}ds=2(s_2^{3/2}-s_1^{3/2})/3$, this yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
B_a(u)&=\frac{2(s_2^{3/2}-s_1^{3/2})}{9\,2^{19}\pi}\,e^2a^5u\\
&\quad\times\left[1+O\big((a\sqrt{s_1})^{-1}\big)+O(u/s_1)\right].
\end{aligned}
$$
Along $a=a_0R$ and $q_E=q_0R^{-p}$ with $q_0>0$ fixed, $e^2a^5u\sim 4a_0^3 q_0^2 R^{3-2p}/(C_9V)$. This positive band contribution grows throughout $3/5<p<1$. For a finite regulator with nonnegative spectral weight and only the charge subtraction, all other bands add with the same sign. [Fixed-band calculation in the issue](https://github.com/arivero/conneskk/issues/26#issuecomment-7143146603)

**Local slope matching.** After fixing the momentum-independent charge term, let $c_\gamma(a)$ be the independently matched coefficient of $u$ in the canonical transverse photon polarization. It multiplies the quadratic higher-derivative photon kinetic operator of the form $F_{\mu\nu}\Box F^{\mu\nu}$, and its sign is defined here by its addition $+c_\gamma(a)u$ to the spacelike difference. Its units are $\mathrm{GeV}^{-2}$. For a nonnegative regulated density $\rho_{a,\rm reg}$ the scalar-loop slope has magnitude $\lambda_a=e^2\int \rho_{a,\rm reg}(s)s^{-2}ds$. At finite $a$ the charged spectrum starts at a positive threshold. Including the slope term, the signed difference is
$$
(c_\gamma(a)-\lambda_a)u
 +e^2u^2\int\frac{\rho_{a,\rm reg}(s)}{s^2(s+u)}\,ds,
$$
up to further local terms or other sectors. This exact split follows from $1/(s(s+u))=1/s^2-u/(s^2(s+u))$. A slope-based perturbative estimate needs a small matched linear term and a bound on the remaining terms at the moving momentum. For the displayed band alone, subtracting its slope leaves a remainder of magnitude asymptotic to
$$
\frac{2(\sqrt{s_2}-\sqrt{s_1})}{3\,2^{19}\pi}\,e^2 a^5u^2
$$
by the same uniform estimate with integrand proportional to $s^{-1/2}$. This term scales as $R^{3-4p}$, so the band's slope subtraction alone still leaves a growing term for $3/5<p<3/4$. The full interacting-photon bound remains open because the microscopic matching data for the local coefficients and a bound on the remaining momentum dependence are unspecified. Gravitational support and the chiral domain are separate inputs. $\square$ [Local matching terms in the Hopf action](../106_hopf_tensor_scalar_gap_and_sextet_matching.md#the-infinite-tower-and-finite-local-matching-terms) · [Chiral parent and support](01.md)

## Strict-wall gap check

The separate atlas panel sends $v\to\infty$ with $m_f=M_W=h=0.300\,\mathrm{GeV}$. In the strict-wall comparison, use wall length $\ell_{\rm w}$ with $M_W=\pi/(2\ell_{\rm w})$. The window requires $\ell_{\rm w}m_f<\pi/2$. The retained mass $m_f$ and the adjacent branch $\pi/\ell_{\rm w}-m_f$ have gap $\pi/\ell_{\rm w}-2m_f$. The given panel makes $\ell_{\rm w}m_f=\pi/2$, so the branches meet and the strict window has zero gap at equality. The circle-free cut above uses the kinematic circle thresholds of its own product family. [Strict-wall spectrum and domain](../22_finite_dirac_operator_as_window.md)

For the canonical three-generation module and common matching-scale CCM mass sum, the six quarks of weight three and the three charged leptons of weight one contribute $(3\cdot6+3)h^2=21h^2=1.89\,\mathrm{GeV}^2$. This already exceeds $8M_W^2=8h^2=0.72\,\mathrm{GeV}^2$. The Dirac neutrino terms are nonnegative. The comparison applies with the canonical field content and normalization at that scale. The following Python 3 script uses SymPy for exact arithmetic, integral coefficients and scaling exponents. It enumerates the magnetic weights, compares two evaluations of the finite sum across sample thresholds, and tests the dispersion coefficients by integrating the finite sum on a fixed band. The bounds for all levels and energies follow from the derivations above.

```python
from math import floor, fsum, isclose, pi, sqrt

import sympy as sp


def charge_trace(ell):
    return (ell + 1) * sum(sp.Rational(2*r - ell, 2)**2
                           for r in range(ell + 1))


ell = sp.symbols('ell', integer=True, nonnegative=True)
r = sp.symbols('r', integer=True)
trace = (ell + 1) * sp.summation((r - ell/2)**2, (r, 0, ell))
assert sp.simplify(trace - ell*(ell + 2)*(ell + 1)**2/12) == 0
for level in range(33):
    assert charge_trace(level) == trace.subs(ell, level)
assert charge_trace(0) == 0 and charge_trace(1) == 1

a, b, d, s, beta = sp.symbols('a b d s beta', positive=True)
spin = ell/2
assert sp.expand(4*spin*(spin + 1) - ell*(ell + 2)) == 0


def pair_threshold(level, nb, nd):
    return 2*sp.sqrt(level*(level + 2)/a**2 + nb**2/b**2 + nd**2/d**2)


assert pair_threshold(1, 0, 0) == 2*sp.sqrt(3)/a
assert pair_threshold(0, 1, 0) == 2/b
assert pair_threshold(0, 0, 1) == 2/d
assert pair_threshold(1, 0, 1)**2 == 12/a**2 + 4/d**2
assert pair_threshold(0, 1, 1)**2 == 4/b**2 + 4/d**2

x = sp.symbols('x', real=True)
angle = sp.integrate(x**2, (x, -1, 1))/2
phase_space = 4*sp.pi*(2*sp.pi)**(-2)*beta/8
one_field = sp.simplify(4*(s*beta**2/4)*angle*phase_space/(2*s*sp.pi))
assert one_field == beta**3/(48*sp.pi**2)
shell_factor = (one_field/beta**3)/12
assert shell_factor == 1/(576*sp.pi**2)


def moment(j):
    return sp.integrate(sp.sin(x)**j * sp.cos(x)**4, (x, 0, sp.pi/2))


i2, i4 = moment(2), moment(4)
assert (i2, i4) == (sp.pi/32, 3*sp.pi/256)
leading = i4*shell_factor/2**5
assert leading == 1/(3*2**19*sp.pi)


def density(z2):
    return fsum(ell*(ell + 2)*(ell + 1)**2
                * max(0.0, 1 - 4*ell*(ell + 2)/z2)**1.5
                for ell in range(floor(sqrt(1 + z2/4))))/(576*pi**2)


assert density(12) == 0 and density(12.1) > 0
for level in (1, 2, 8, 36, 128):
    for offset in (-0.01, 0.0, 0.01):
        z2 = 4*level*(level + 2) + offset
        L = sqrt(z2)/2
        X = sqrt(1 + L**2)
        n_sum = fsum((n**4 - n**2)*max(0.0, X**2 - n**2)**1.5
                     for n in range(1, floor(X) + 1))
        scale = 576*pi**2*L**3
        assert isclose(density(z2), n_sum/scale, rel_tol=1e-10)
        integral = (float(i4)*X**8 - float(i2)*X**6)/scale
        assert abs(density(z2) - integral) <= 2*(X**7 + X**5)/scale

cv, gp, k, cw, sw, tau, hn = sp.symbols('cv gp k cw sw tau hn', positive=True)
volume = (2*sp.pi**2*a**3)*(2*sp.pi*b)*(2*sp.pi*d)
assert volume == 8*sp.pi**4*a**3*b*d
a_path = 2*sw/(gp*sp.sqrt(cv)*cw)
assert sp.simplify(cv*a_path**2/4 - (gp*cw/sw)**(-2)) == 0
b_path = 1/(gp*k*sp.sqrt(cv))
assert cv*k**2*b_path**2 == gp**(-2)
e = gp*cw
assert e**2*a_path**2 == 4*sw**2/cv
fixed_coefficient = sw**5/(3*2**14*gp**3*cv**sp.Rational(5, 2)*cw**3)
assert e**2*sp.pi*leading*a_path**5 == fixed_coefficient
assert sp.pi*leading*4/cv == 1/(3*2**17*cv)
v = 4*sp.pi*tau/e**2
assert e**2*v/(4*sp.pi) == tau
assert (gp*tau/2).subs(gp, 2*hn/tau) == hn
assert gp*cw*v/(2*sw) == 2*sp.pi*tau/(gp*sw*cw)
assert gp*v/(2*sw) == 2*sp.pi*tau/(gp*sw*cw**2)

R, p = sp.symbols('R p', positive=True)
for factor, power in ((R**(-2)*(R*R**(-p))**5, 3 - 5*p),
                      (R**(-2)*R**5*R**(-2*p), 3 - 2*p),
                      (R**(-2)*R**5*R**(-4*p), 3 - 4*p)):
    assert sp.simplify(R*sp.diff(factor, R)/factor - power) == 0
assert sp.solve(3 - 5*p, p) == [sp.Rational(3, 5)]
assert sp.solve(3 - 4*p, p) == [sp.Rational(3, 4)]
count = sp.summation(r**2, (r, 1, ell))
assert sp.limit(count/ell**3, ell, sp.oo)/8 == sp.Rational(1, 24)

s1, s2 = sp.symbols('s1 s2', positive=True)
band_slope = leading*sp.integrate(sp.sqrt(s), (s, s1, s2))
band_remainder = leading*sp.integrate(1/sp.sqrt(s), (s, s1, s2))
delta_s32 = s2**sp.Rational(3, 2) - s1**sp.Rational(3, 2)
delta_s12 = sp.sqrt(s2) - sp.sqrt(s1)
assert sp.expand(band_slope - 2*delta_s32/(9*2**19*sp.pi)) == 0
assert sp.expand(band_remainder - 2*delta_s12/(3*2**19*sp.pi)) == 0

# a = 256 GeV^-1, 1 <= s <= 2.25 GeV^2, q_E = 0.01 GeV.
radius, low, high, u = 256, 1.0, 2.25, 0.0001
step = (high - low)/256
band = [(low + (i + 0.5)*step) for i in range(256)]
for extra_power, coefficient in ((1, band_slope), (2, band_remainder)):
    total = step*fsum(density(radius**2*y)/(y**extra_power*(y + u))
                     for y in band)
    expected = radius**5*float(coefficient.subs({s1: low, s2: high}))
    assert isclose(total, expected, rel_tol=1e-3)

h = sp.Rational('0.300')
wall_length = sp.pi/(2*h)
assert wall_length*h == sp.pi/2
assert sp.pi/wall_length - 2*h == 0
assert (3*6 + 3)*h**2 == 21*h**2 == sp.Rational('1.89')
assert 8*h**2 == sp.Rational('0.72') < 21*h**2
print('Plate 08 spectral, dispersive and strict-wall checks passed.')
```
