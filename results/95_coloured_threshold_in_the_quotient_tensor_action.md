## Exact coloured Hessian in a mixed-dimensional action

**Proposition 95.1.** There is a symmetry-preserving quotient tensor extension of the bosonic fibration action of 71 in which its coloured quadratic operator is unchanged. If
$$
 L^{-2}<(1-1/\sqrt2)\min(3/B^2,\lambda_1(\Sigma_{\rm phys})),
$$
then the first extra coloured boson is the gluon circle harmonic at $1/L$. The finite branch of 92 at $k=6$, $1/L=500$ GeV, the fixed-meridian continuation to $b=.001$, and its subsequent fixed-shape shrink satisfy the inequality uniformly, for the colour input of 92.

**Proof.** Let the preferred bundle have base $Y=M_4\times\Sigma$ and fibre $\mathbb{CP}^2\times S^1$. Retain bundle gauges in the principal $SU(3)\times U(1)_\chi$ action, with the usual finite kernel on the geometric colour action. At each point of $Y$ take the normalized invariant fibre measure. Form a six-dimensional metric by averaging the Schur complement of the fibre metric. Project the metric shift onto the fibre Killing algebra, using its invariant inner product. Under a local change of principal frame the inhomogeneous shift already lies in this algebra, so its projection transforms as a connection. Average the vortex one-form on horizontal vectors of this projected connection. If $A\mapsto A+d\Lambda$, the derivative along a fibre Killing field averages to zero, and the result transforms by $d_Y\langle\Lambda\rangle$. These are fixed prescriptions in this fibration action.

Put the independent nonchiral two-form and its Green–Schwarz couplings on $Y$ with this metric, vertical tangent connection and gauge fields. One can take the ordinary and spectator representations of 90 there, using the projected colour and circle connections for their gauge actions, instead of adding another copy of the light fermions. The weak monopole degree and electric action on the light source kernels are those in 78. The colour and hypercharge background curvatures along $Y$ vanish. The weak and vortex background curvatures are supported on the two-surface. Choose the zero three-form background and a zero form-period representative. Thus the first variation of the tensor action is zero. It adds no stress to the matched backgrounds.

At quadratic order the tensor couples to the first variations of the projected fields. The second derivative of a nonlinear projector would multiply the vanishing first variation. The weak metric and vortex projections are colour singlets. The Killing projection can retain an adjoint connection, but its characteristic form is quadratic in its curvature, which has zero background on $Y$. Consequently there is no linear adjoint transgression source. The Hessian of the tensor sector vanishes on and against all coloured variations, including the auxiliary variables in the constraint calculation of 71. There are also no coloured tensor modes among the selected quotient fields. Thus that full coloured quadratic operator and its exact circle level are inherited in this action. The assertion uses the same root and Wilson curvature terms of 71. It is not an argument that any positive tensor energy preserves a gap relative to the circle.

For the uniform bound take each fitted starting point as the reference and set $x=B/B_*$, $y=L/L_*$, and $R=a^2D_b/(a_*^2D_*)$. The matching equations give $x^6 yR=1$ and
$$

 A=CV_*L_*^2/k^2\simeq7.85,\qquad

On the fixed-meridian segment $D_b\le\sqrt2 P_b$ gives $R<1.58$ up to the round limit. Uniform shrink at fixed shape only lowers $R$. The tensor estimates give $I_Q-I_T>9.89$ on this path and $D_a<3.50$ by $J_b\le4$. If $x\le0.8$ then $A y^2>45$, while $(I_Q-I_T)x^2<6.6$. Therefore $x>0.8$ and $y^2/x^2>0.56$. With the two fitted radii in 92 this gives $L>1.19\times10^{-3}$ GeV$^{-1}$ and $L/B>6.6$. ## Fermion excitations in this prescription

**Proposition 95.2.** A vortex-neutral spectator of 90, with minimal sphere Dirac operator and no additional mass mixing, has $M\ge1/(2\sqrt2\,a)$ on every polar member. An excited weak $N=2$ spinor obeys $M\ge\sqrt{15/512}/a$ before adding Yukawa mixing. These gaps and the $N=1$ singlet complement put the additional **free** matter shells above the circle level throughout the path of 95.1.

**Proof.** In conformal coordinates let $h=f^2(dt^2+d\varphi^2)$ and $u=f^{1/2}\psi$ for a weak Dirac eigenfield. Its equation is $D_{\rm flat}u=\lambda f u$ with antiperiodic spin around the azimuth in this frame. Smoothness at the poles gives the required square integrability. For an untwisted spinor $D_{\rm flat}^2=-\partial_t^2-\partial_\varphi^2\ge1/4$ while $f^2\le2$. This gives the first bound in physical units.

For $N=2$ use the opposite chirality to the two zero modes so every nonzero level has a component in that block. With $x_0=u_t/2$ in the vortex notation, its flat first-order factor is $\partial_t+W$, $W=\ell-1+x_0$, $\ell\in\mathbb Z+1/2$. The squared quadratic form has potential $W^2+2A_\varphi'$. Since $p\le f^2/2=1-x_0^2$, the second term is at least $x_0^2(1-x_0^2)$. If the distance of $x_0$ from the closer of $\pm1/2$ is at least $1/4$, the first term is at least $1/16$. Otherwise the second is at least $15/256$. The two chiral components have equal norm in the physical Dirac eigenspace at nonzero mass. In the conformal variables that means equal weighted norms $\int f|u_i|^2$. Bounding the factors of $f$ by $\sqrt2$ therefore gives the second bound. The degree-one complement has the free bound of 74, $e^{-1/4}\pi/(aP_b)$ in physical mass units. Along this path $a\le1.112\times10^{-4}$ GeV$^{-1}$ and $aP_b\le d$. Using the lower bound for $L$ from 95.1 puts all the stated shells above $1/L$. Small mass perturbations preserve the gaps under the usual operator norm bound. This last statement is conditional on small perturbations, and does not apply to an arbitrary set of Yukawas copied from the compact point. $\square$

In particular the untwisted exotic coloured fermions in the chosen spectator spectrum do not enter the $250$ GeV ordinary matching of 92 at the starting member. This leaves the QCD conversion of 93 as a common calibration with specified light Yukawa functions and colour input. The emergence of that calibration from a microscopic theory is still different from the tree gap calculation.

## A local tensor countertest

A higher-dimensional field carrying arbitrary projective harmonics can mix differently. A simple subblock uses a massive four-dimensional two-form of internal squared eigenvalue $\lambda>0$ and a transverse vortex vector. Normalize their kinetic terms. With electric mass $\mu_e^2$ and magnetic coupling $\mu_m$, dualizing its two-form gives the squared-mass matrix
$$
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \lambda+\mu_e^2+\mu_m^2 & -\sqrt\lambda\,\mu_m\\
 -\sqrt\lambda\,\mu_m & \lambda
 \end{pmatrix}.
$$
This follows by completing the square for the exterior derivative of the external tensor and then eliminating its massive two-form potential. Its determinant is $\lambda(\lambda+\mu_e^2)$ and the smaller eigenvalue is strictly below $\lambda$ for $\mu_m\ne0$, as the negative determinant of the matrix shifted by $\lambda$ shows. Both eigenvalues are positive. Thus even where a bare eigenvalue satisfies the old threshold one needs an estimate of the mixing in the unrestricted mode space. For example a nontrivial projective harmonic can play the role of the common $\lambda$ in a direct product. Increasing the magnetic coupling at fixed geometry lowers the small eigenvalue. This is a local transverse comparison and does not impose the fixed photon budget of 93 or construct a global unrestricted lifted action.

A separate constraint arises in an ordinary invariant source-free reduction of the eleven-dimensional three-form. If a six-dimensional three-form is obtained by contraction $\iota_K G_4$ with the shared circle, then $d(\iota_K G_4)=\mathcal L_K G_4-\iota_K dG_4=0$ under those premises. The eleven-dimensional equation for the dual field can source the other period. It does not by itself supply both nonzero Bianchi classes of 90 for this circle ansatz. Magnetic sources, wall inflow or extra fields are concrete ways to change the premises.
