# The coloured scalar gap in a root-supported fibration

Use the Kähler colour flux, the two roots selecting $Q$, and the internal circle-curvature term on the polar solutions of results 63–65. Keep the action and charge assignments fixed while varying the weak compactification. We specify how the root gradients see mixed metric perturbations and compute the first additional *bosonic* colour threshold. The scalar polarizations require this specification even when the background currents and vector kinetic coefficients have already been fixed.

## Horizontal derivatives and the mass bound

**Theorem 71.1.** Use the following realization of the positive squares in Theorem 63.1. Take the orthogonal metric connection for the three-dimensional fibre $F=S^1_L\times(\Sigma,a^2h_b)$ over the eight-dimensional base $\mathcal B=M^{3,1}\times\mathbb{CP}^2_B$. Use the same coefficient $Z_H>0$ for all eight horizontal components of each root derivative, including its four projective ones. Inside the fibre use its metric, its marked circle, the horizontal weak complex structure and the shifted circle derivative of result 63. Take the circle-curvature coefficient $c_\eta=1$ as in result 65. All coefficients of the remaining internal squares are positive. There is no background kinetic mixing of the independent flux Maxwell fields, and the roots are neutral under the colour-flux Maxwell field. The metric and the two gauge fields fluctuate.

In this classical action the quadratic bosonic spectrum at the compact rooted solutions is nonnegative. Let $\gamma_c=3/B^2$ and $\mu=\lambda_1(h_b)/a^2$. If
$$
                L^{-2}< (1-1/\sqrt2)\min(\gamma_c,\mu),
$$
the only massless coloured bosons are the gluons, and the first additional coloured boson in four dimensions has mass $1/L$. This holds at round canonical matching, on the asymptotic fixed-shape five-dimensional branch, and on the asymptotic fixed-meridian six-dimensional branch. All statements concern the two-derivative classical theory at compact members before taking the limits.

**Linear variables.** The issue with a fixed fibration is that a coordinate variation of a covariant metric–matter solution can change the specified fibre planes. We retain such variations explicitly. Write $a$ for an eight-dimensional index and $m$ for a fibre index in this proof. Let $h_{am}$ be the mixed metric variation with both indices lowered, and $v_a$ the weak Maxwell variation in the unit line connection convention of result 63. On the product background the two root horizontal variations have the universal form
$$
 W_a=\partial_a\delta H-\mathcal U(h_{a\cdot},v_a),\qquad
 \mathcal U(t,v)_\pm=t^mD_m H_\pm+i vH_\pm .
$$
Here $D_m$ is the unshifted derivative on the vortex line; in particular $D_\chi H_\pm=in_\pm H_\pm$. This equation follows by lifting the base derivative to $\partial_a-g_F^{mn}h_{an}\partial_m$ and using the horizontal gauge connection. The marked-circle one-form vanishes on this lift. The internal root squares, to first order inside each square, involve only the fibre metric and fibre field components, the vortex and the dilaton. Every square is zero on the background.

Represent the perturbations of the covariant metric–Maxwell–vortex subsystem by its ordinary light-cone gauge variables and an arbitrary infinitesimal coordinate variation $\xi$ and Abelian gauge variation. This is a change of variables for that subsystem, whose quadratic action vanishes on its gauge directions. The full fibration action still contains any directions to which the roots or lift couple. Use the gauge-covariant Lie derivative on background fields, so the weak gauge change is $\iota_\xi F_A+d\Lambda$. Write $\psi_\pm=\delta H_\pm-\xi^m D_mH_\pm-i\Lambda H_\pm$. With the gauge-fixed fields denoted again by $h,v$, the horizontal variations become
$$
 W_a=\partial_a\psi-\mathcal U(h_{a\cdot},v_a)-\mathcal F(\xi_a),
 \qquad
 \mathcal F(f)_\pm=(D_mH_\pm)\nabla_F^m f .
$$
Indeed $\partial_a\xi_m$ cancels against the derivative of the transported root, whereas the other mixed metric variation is $\nabla_m\xi_a$. The purely internal squares can depend on the remaining marked-circle variations $\xi^m$. They contain no base derivative of these auxiliary variables and no $\xi_a$. A base displacement has no first-order effect on the internal fibre metric of the product or on the internal root equations. The only other occurrence of $\xi_i$ for projective $i$ is through $\partial_\chi\xi_i$ in the curl of $h_{\chi i}$; its $\partial_i\xi_\chi$ term has zero colour curl.

Let $P$ project onto the orthogonal complement of the closure of the real range of $\mathcal F$ in the two-root fibre $L^2$ norm. The auxiliary components $\xi_\mu$ for flat spacetime indices occur only through the displayed term and have no base derivatives. Their equations project each spacetime $W_\mu$ onto that complement. Equivalently, stationarity against arbitrary smooth $\xi_\mu$ makes $W^\mu$ orthogonal to the range, while its component in the complement is fixed. Complex polarizations use the complexification of this real statement. For a colour component we can drop the squared norm of the complementary residual in the range closure, keeping $\|P(\partial_i\psi-\mathcal U_i)\|^2$ as a lower bound. The dropped term and the colour curl are positive spatial terms. At zero circle frequency the colour curl of the metric representative is independent of $\xi_i$ and can be kept exactly. The projection commutes with all base derivatives and the colour group. Thus the kinetic norm is the covariant light-cone norm augmented by $4Z_H\|P\psi\|^2$. Components with zero kinetic norm enter through constraints or purely internal squares.

**Separating the colour contribution.** In the projected comparison the colour contribution to the physical mass form is the colour operator in Theorem 68.1 on the covariant fields plus $4Z_H C_c\|P\psi\|^2$ in a projective scalar representation of Casimir $C_c$. To verify this, the only new term with a mixed colour derivative before eliminating the null constraints is
$$
           -8Z_H\,\mathrm{Re}\langle d_c P\psi,P\mathcal U(h_{i\cdot},v_i)\rangle .
$$
The root couples to the weak and fibre components of the metric momentum and Maxwell Gauss constraints. In the normalizations of Proposition 69.1 their auxiliary shifts give the cross term
$$
   -8Z_H\,\mathrm{Re}\langle P\psi,\,
                P\mathcal U(\nabla^ih_{i\cdot},\nabla^iv_i)\rangle .
$$
For example the additional metric source is $2Z_H\,\mathrm{Re}[(D_mH)^\dagger P\psi]$, summed over roots. The Maxwell source follows by replacing $D_mH$ by $iH$ with the Maxwell kinetic factor in the constraint. These are obtained by varying the horizontal null kinetic term with respect to $h_{+m}$ and $v_+$. The two displayed terms cancel by integration by parts on the compact projective factor. There is no root contribution to the projective momentum source since the background horizontal root derivative vanishes. The independent colour Maxwell constraint is unchanged. The term $4Z_H\|P\mathcal U(h_{i\cdot},v_i)\|^2$ belongs to the fibre vector mass, with $i$ a transverse spectator index. All remaining root terms and changes of constraints contain only fibre derivatives or are algebraic.

The other part of the comparison form is therefore the fibre perturbation form with six transverse base spectators. It is the one obtained on the auxiliary flat product $M^{7,1}\times F$, setting the colour flux and its accompanying potential to zero, keeping the free colour Maxwell fluctuation. This auxiliary product solves the weak equations with the same two roots, circle radius and constant dilatons. In particular the projective flux stress and its potential cancelled externally already, factor by factor. Auxiliary fibre coordinate variables appear only in the internal root squares. They can be kept or minimized there.

**Why the fibre form is nonnegative.** The covariant subsystem on the auxiliary product is the weak vortex of Proposition 69.1 with a flat circle. Its physical quadratic energy on time-symmetric data is nonnegative, by the constraint-square proof and separation of the circle momentum. Adding the roots changes neither the value nor the first variation of their spatial energy on the vacuum because every added square vanishes. In particular their first energy-density variation is zero pointwise. The linearized Hamiltonian constraint for time-symmetric data is exactly that of the covariant subsystem. The momentum and electric constraints are satisfied with vanishing initial momenta. On this common constraint surface the quadratic Hamiltonian changes by the nonnegative spatial root squares, also for pure coordinate variations of the covariant fields. This argument needs the *pointwise* vanishing of the added squares in order to use the same linear constraint.

For the link to the light-cone form, in the auxiliary problem every external index has the same horizontal coefficient and every external coordinate variable is eliminated as above. Its quadratic equations are eight-dimensionally Poincaré invariant, second order and time-reversal invariant on a static magnetic background. In a gauge-fixed light-cone polarization the propagating kinetic norm is positive as given above. A negative physical mass form would give negative spectral support for squared mass and a tachyonic perturbation at zero external spatial momentum. Its real time-even initial displacement has vanishing canonical momenta and negative quadratic energy. Equivalently one can use a real wave packet in a negative spectral subinterval. This contradicts the constrained Hamiltonian calculation. Time components fixed by constraints do not supply independent initial displacements. Internal auxiliary variables without external derivatives enter through positive root squares, so retaining them can only raise their minimized form. This proves the required fibre positivity without transferring a round weak Casimir to a nonround metric.

**Quantitative gap.** For a colour-nonsinglet the colour block has gap $\gamma_c$ off the colour Killing vector with its Kähler magnetic compensator, by the one-factor bound in result 68. The new kinetic root component has $C_c\ge 2\lambda_c$ in such a representation. We detail the sector containing the colour kernel since positivity alone would leave its first threshold undecided.

An external transverse gluon has a projective Killing index and a flat transverse index, accompanied by a colour Maxwell component with the same flat index. Both carry the ordinary scalar Laplacian on $F$. They are annihilated by the root mass square. Among the other colour-kernel polarizations the spectator index lies in $TF$. The relevant block of the comparison consists of the mixed metric $h_{im}$, the colour Maxwell fluctuation along $F$, and the weak Maxwell component $v_i$. The first and third fields form a fibre vector with a base transverse spectator index. Its covariant mass form is the vector form of Proposition 69.1 with the separate circle momentum, including the circle polarization as a weak scalar. The root adds the positive vector mass square above. A fibre vector with a base spectator index has no root scalar or trace fluctuation in this block. The colour Maxwell component along $F$ is a free spectator Maxwell field in the fibre calculation, with mass form the Hodge Laplacian on $F$. These sectors close in the comparison form: the only colour-flux mixing of $h_{im}$ is with that colour Maxwell component; the weak Maxwell component has the spectator colour Casimir. The trace of a base metric, the internal fibre fields, the dilatons and the root scalars belong to the separate scalar block, which already has the colour gap. This follows either from the displayed operators or by counting their transverse spectator indices before resolving the projective dependence.

The combined circle translation and the inverse background root phases label real pairs of relative frequencies $\pm n$. In the vector block at $n\ne0$ the covariant fibre estimate gives $n^2/L^2$, and all additional vector terms are nonnegative. At $n=0$, the free colour Maxwell one-form on $F$ has gap $\mu$ off its sole harmonic direction $d\chi$: weak one-forms have no cohomology and nonconstant weak scalars satisfy the same Poincaré bound. On the remaining uniform circle polarization, the positive curl of $h_{\chi i}$ has diagonal cost $2c_\eta(12/B^2)$ on the normalized colour Killing metric component, by Proposition 65.2. To combine the bounds let $R$ project onto the nonharmonic fibre component of the colour Maxwell form together with the uniform colour Killing metric component along $d\chi$. On the colour-kernel projection $P_c$ with spectator index in $TF$, one has $\|RP_c u\|^2=\|P_c u\|^2/2$. For its uniform circle part the selected half is the metric part and for its orthogonal complement it is the Maxwell part. Therefore the zero-frequency bound is
$$
 (1-1/\sqrt2)\min\{\gamma_c,\mu,2c_\eta(12/B^2)\}.
$$
The invariant subspaces and lower estimates are needed only for the projected comparison; the discarded static squares are nonnegative on the original theory. The exact external gluon with $n=\pm1$ realizes $1/L$ in the original action. It acts trivially on the roots and has zero internal circle curl. This proves both the assertion under the displayed inequality and nonnegativity more generally. The three regions satisfying the inequality are verified in Corollary 68.2 and Theorem 69.2. $\square$

This result uses the equal external and projective root kinetic coefficients in a specific off-background fibration action. Arbitrarily changing those two coefficients separately would prevent the cancellation of the colour derivative in the constraint calculation.
