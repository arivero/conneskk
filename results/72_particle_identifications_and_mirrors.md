# Identifying the particles and counting mirror families

We keep the field action, its bundle lifts and its fermion domain fixed, and vary the weak geometry and the four-dimensional observation energy. A particle assignment uses the four-dimensional spin, the representation of the unbroken gauge group, a normalized mode and its current matrix elements. A mass is obtained from a pole of that current or of an appropriate field correlator. The criteria below apply to the root-supported projective model. The interval wall and heterotic constructions have different reasons for their particle multiplicities.

## The eight gluons and their coupling to a colour triplet

Let $t^a$ be the eight Hermitian traceless generators on $\mathbb C^3$, with $\mathrm{tr}(t^at^b)=\delta^{ab}/2$. Acting on projective lines gives Killing fields $K_a$ on $\mathbb{CP}^2$. Their real Lie brackets have the structure constants of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ in a consistent choice of sign. The connected isometry on points is $PSU(3)$. The lift to matter bundles below detects the centre of $SU(3)$.

* **Spin and wavefunction.** A perturbation $h_{\mu i}=K_{a\,i}G_\mu^a(x)$ has one spacetime index and is a four-dimensional vector. In the Kähler two-form realization the same vector must perturb the colour Maxwell connection by its Hamiltonian $\mu_a$, fixed up to its zero mean by $d\mu_a=-\iota_{K_a}F_c$. The connection-preserving combination cancels the mixed magnetic field strength. The metric cost vanishes because $K_a$ is Killing. Neither the vortex nor its roots varies under this colour action. These are eight independent zero modes. The orthogonal metric–Maxwell combination has a positive mass. The colour Maxwell field with a constant internal scalar profile is an additional Abelian vector, with no minimal matter charge in the prescription used here.
* **Gauge law and normalization.** Make the lifted colour transformation spacetime dependent. Its derivative shifts $G_\mu^a$ as a connection and the commutator acts by $f^{abc}$. The Hamiltonians can be chosen equivariantly, so the background flux preserves this algebra. The coefficient of the curvature square is $I_3=CVB^2/4$, half from the Einstein term and half from the flux. With canonical vector fields the coupling is $g_3=I_3^{-1/2}$ and the curvature contains the non-Abelian term $g_3 f^{abc}G_\mu^bG_\nu^c$.
* **A matter test.** On the normalized projective quark kernel the horizontal four-dimensional derivative is $\partial_\mu-i g_3G_\mu^at^a$. For instance $t^1$ has matrix element $1/2$ from the second colour basis vector to the first. The sum of squared generators on any unit colour state is $C_F=4/3$. On the lepton kernel all eight matrix elements vanish. The generators commute with electric charge. These tests identify the modes as the gluons of the four-dimensional effective theory. In a confining phase their fields occur in gauge-invariant hadronic correlators; free coloured asymptotic particles are a perturbative description.

Multiplication by $e^{in\chi}$ changes this same vector into a circle harmonic of mass $|n|/L$ on its weak-constant branch. It is a massive spin-one colour octet in four dimensions. The $\chi$ polarization of the *zero* colour vector carries no spacetime vector index and is a real adjoint scalar, the colour Wilson scalar. A projective metric shape perturbation can also be a four-dimensional scalar in an octet. In the four-form example one such scalar has negative squared mass. The spin and the presence or absence of a massless gauge transformation distinguish all three octets. ## One quark and lepton family

The fundamental fermions in this model are ten-dimensional spin$^c$ Weyl fields on the quotient by the shared circle. The associated circle character of species $s$ is $n_s=kY_s$, with $k$ a multiple of six. This assigns its hypercharge. A bulk bosonic scalar of circle momentum $n$ has neither the spin nor the Weyl action of these fields.

**Proposition 72.1 (fermion representations).** On the compact round member the following table gives the independent ordinary particle fields in one family. The last column is the charge of the original unit vortex line connection, in addition to the electroweak quantum numbers.

| Field and four-dimensional chirality | $SU(3)_c$ | $SU(2)_w$ | $Y$ | $Q=T_3+Y$ | Vortex charge $N$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $(u_L,d_L)$ | $\mathbf3$ | $\mathbf2$ | $1/6$ | $2/3,-1/3$ | 2 |
| $(\nu_L,e_L)$ | $\mathbf1$ | $\mathbf2$ | $-1/2$ | $0,-1$ | 2 |
| $u_R$ | $\mathbf3$ | $\mathbf1$ | $2/3$ | $2/3$ | 1 |
| $d_R$ | $\mathbf3$ | $\mathbf1$ | $-1/3$ | $-1/3$ | 1 |
| $\nu_R$ | $\mathbf1$ | $\mathbf1$ | $0$ | $0$ | 1 |
| $e_R$ | $\mathbf1$ | $\mathbf1$ | $-1$ | $-1$ | 1 |

**Proof.** Quarks have projective coefficient $\mathcal O(-4)$. Their zero modes are the degree-two Dolbeault kernel. Serre duality gives $H^{0,2}(\mathcal O(-4))=H^0(\mathcal O(1))^*$. Linear forms on homogeneous coordinates span the dual defining representation, so this last dual is $\mathbf3$. For the central matrix $\zeta\,1_3$ with $\zeta^3=1$, the tautological $\mathcal O(-1)$ acquires $\zeta$. Its fourth power acquires $\zeta^4=\zeta$, the quark triality. Leptons have projective coefficient $\mathcal O(0)$ with constant zero section and trivial colour action. Each twist is an input specifying a homogeneous bundle and its lift. The eight colour derivatives above test that lift.

On the weak sphere the spin line is $K^{1/2}=\mathcal O(-1)$. Twisting by the $N$th power of the unit monopole line gives holomorphic zero modes in $\mathcal O(N-1)$ of positive weak internal chirality. For $N=2$ the two linear polynomials form the spin-$1/2$ representation of the lifted rotations, with weights $\pm1/2$. For $N=1$ the single constant has zero weak isospin. The projective form degrees zero and two are both even. The internal six-dimensional parity is therefore the same for these modes, and the prescribed ten-dimensional Weyl signs give left doublets and right singlets in four dimensions. Applying the axial generator plus circle translation now gives each electric charge in the table. The raising generator has unit matrix element from the lower to the upper member of a doublet in the usual normalization and annihilates each weak singlet. $\square$

**A Dirac up quark at a broken member.** The left up component and $u_R$ are independent Weyl coefficients, both in $\mathbf3$ with $Q=2/3$. A root carries the circle-character difference between them and raises the singlet weak section into the up component. Its normalized overlap times the up Yukawa is the Dirac mass matrix entry. A nonzero singular value yields the four-dimensional bilinear $-m(\bar u_Lu_R+\bar u_Ru_L)$ with one Dirac kinetic term. Both chiralities have the same colour vertex $g_3t^a$ and electric vertex $2e/3$. Only the ordinary left component has the weak transition to $d_L$. The down link works with the other root and has electric vertex $-e/3$ on both chiralities. The identical argument on the constant projective kernel gives the charged lepton and, if its Dirac Yukawa is present, a Dirac neutrino. The right neutrino has zero Standard Model charge but can couple to the additional vortex vector. A Majorana relation or mass would require a different neutral-field prescription.

At zero Yukawa a pair of independent massless left and right fundamentals of equal colour charge counts as one Dirac *flavour for colour running*. A four-component mass eigenfield with $m>0$ requires the nonzero mass link just described. The three basis vectors inside a triplet have the same mass. A second or third generation is an independent copy with the same generators acting on a different multiplicity space. Up-type and down-type labels follow from these charges and charged currents. Identifying a particular singular vector as the observed top, or extracting the observed CKM matrix, requires the masses and the relative left diagonalizations of the two Yukawa matrices. In the five-flavour branch selecting the single nonzero up-type singular direction as the top is an input. ## What needs mirror matter

**Proposition 72.2 (anomaly checks for this completion).** An ordinary family in the table cancels its four-dimensional Standard Model gauge anomalies. It has mixed colour and weak anomalies with the additional gauged vortex $U(1)$ at restoration. As a set of six complex rank-one ten-dimensional Weyl fields it also has nonzero signed chiral rank. Adding the same six ten-dimensional bundles with reversed Weyl signs cancels their local gauge, mixed and gravitational anomaly polynomials pair by pair.

**Proof.** Count left Weyl fields and subtract right Weyl fields in their physical representations. Colour is vectorlike. For $SU(3)^2Y$ the hypercharge sum per fundamental index is $2(1/6)-2/3+1/3=0$. For $SU(2)^2Y$ it is $3(1/6)-1/2=0$. The multiplicity-weighted sums of $Y$ and $Y^3$ also vanish. For example the cubic sum is $6(1/6)^3+2(-1/2)^3-3(2/3)^3-3(-1/3)^3-(-1)^3=0$. There are four left weak doublets counting colour, an even number for the global $SU(2)$ test. [Witten's four-dimensional anomaly criterion](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-2693(82)90728-6)

For the vortex charge use $T(\mathbf3)=T(\mathbf2)=1/2$. The ordinary mixed coefficients are
$$
 \mathcal A_{SU(3)^2v}=\tfrac12(2\cdot2-1-1)=1,\qquad
 \mathcal A_{SU(2)^2v}=\tfrac12(3\cdot2+2)=4 .
$$
Also $\sum_LN-\sum_RN=8$ and $\sum_LN^3-\sum_RN^3=56$. Thus an unbroken independently gauged vortex vector would be anomalous with just these ordinary fermions. A mirror has the same charges with opposite four-dimensional chirality, giving the opposite coefficient in every case.

For the parent fermions a single ten-dimensional complex Weyl field on a spin$^c$ manifold contributes, up to the common choice of anomaly sign and normalization, the degree-twelve part of $\widehat A(T)\exp[c_1(\mathcal L)/2]\,\mathrm{ch}(E)$ for determinant line $\mathcal L$ and twisting bundle $E$. Each row here is one rank-one twist in ten dimensions. Its colour and weak multiplicities arise only after solving the internal Dirac equation. With the convention that the two doublet species have positive parent Weyl sign, the signed rank is $2-4=-2$ per ordinary family. In particular the pure gravitational term contains $-2\widehat A_{12}(T)$, whose $p_3(T)$ coefficient is nonzero. Changing a line flux cannot cancel this pure term. Opposite parent chirality on the identical bundle reverses the entire local polynomial. This test refers to a standalone locally gauged ten-dimensional matter prescription; the four-dimensional Standard Model test counted a different spectrum. [Alvarez-Gaumé and Witten, gravitational anomaly polynomial](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(84)90066-X)
$\square$

A mirror up quark has a **right** weak doublet component and a **left** weak singlet component with the same colour and hypercharges as their ordinary counterparts. Its weak raising vertex acts on the right. The antiparticle of an ordinary up quark belongs to the conjugate colour representation and has electric charge $-2/3$. The antiunitary $J$ of the finite real module records that antiparticle and contributes no new independent Weyl determinant. A mirror instead supplies independent fields in the action and changes loop coefficients and thresholds.

The mirror set is an explicit sufficient cancellation mechanism in this fibration theory. Other chiral matter with the required polynomial or a consistent bulk and boundary inflow could change its spectrum. Adding mirrors is a choice with a testable cost. Separate mirror number here forbids a direct ordinary–mirror gauge-invariant mass. At finite breaking each mirror instead takes a mass from its own root Yukawa. Its couplings can be large enough that its poles lie above a chosen low-energy cut. Any anomaly matching for broken gauge currents after integrating out chiral massive fields must retain the induced terms in the effective action. At restoration both roots vanish. All the assigned ordinary and mirror Dirac links then vanish, and with that mass rule all mirrors return. Three ordinary copies provide six colour flavours and three mirror copies provide six more. This is the origin of the twelve-flavour coefficient in Proposition 68.3.

After the flux lift the ordinary three-family four-dimensional anomaly factorizes as $2vK_4$ for an integral class on spin backgrounds. A charge-two axion can then carry its local anomaly matching below a mirror gap. This gives a specific four-dimensional alternative to light mirror poles. A completion still has to cancel the ten-dimensional parent anomaly; and if the mirror masses come entirely from the roots, their heavy quarks leave scalar-to-gluon and neutral-current threshold signals. ## Weak vectors, scalar fields and hadronic states

* **Photon.** At a compact broken member, acting on the vortex and both roots leaves one connected weak-sector generator $Q=K_3+k^{-1}\partial_\chi$ with its monopole lift. Its transverse inverse propagator vanishes at zero mass and its generator acts equally on the two chiralities of each Dirac link. The pole residue on a unit-charge current is $1/I_Q$ at tree level. A Thomson measurement requires the renormalized, infrared-matched current. At restoration several neutral vectors are massless, so a neutral source generally measures a matrix of residues.
* **Charged and neutral weak poles.** The round lifted rotation $T^+$ couples to $\bar u_L\gamma^\mu d_L$ and $\bar\nu_L\gamma^\mu e_L$, with canonical charged vertex $g_2/\sqrt2$. An isolated continuation with those residues is the $W$ candidate. Its mass and four-fermion normalization are separate measurements. In the specified round transverse action the massive angular circle mode at $2/r^2$ has different quark and lepton residues. The orthogonal rotation and vortex mode at $4/r^2$ has a further residue. The neutral weak candidate near restoration has vertex $(g_2^2 T_3-g_Y^2Y)/\sqrt{g_2^2+g_Y^2}$. The lighter vortex pole instead couples to $N/\sqrt{I_v}$, including to $\nu_R$. This identifies which neutral pole enters the empirical mass difference. * **Higgs fields, squarks and gluinos.** Each $H_\pm$ is a spacetime scalar with weak doublet zero modes, colour singlet and $Y=\pm1/2$. Its kinetic term generates weak masses and its product with a singlet spinor provides a Dirac link. Identifying a particular scalar excitation with the measured Higgs boson would also require a normalized scalar pole, its mass and its couplings to the weak vectors and fermions. A squark would have spin zero and nontrivial colour triality with the required weak and electric charges. A gluino would be a spin-$1/2$ colour adjoint. The gluon's circle polarization has spin zero; a supersymmetric identification requires an appropriate fermion and a supercurrent relating the fields. In a heterotic supersymmetric wall example the scalar–fermion pairing follows from the boundary supersymmetry. In the magnetic model it follows from the assumed supersymmetric matter theory. * **Graviton and D0 comparison.** The transverse traceless external metric fluctuation has two helicities at zero internal momentum and couples to the energy–momentum tensor. Nonconstant scalar harmonics of its internal profile give massive spin-two modes. The candidate D0 interpretation uses different evidence: a type-IIA particle must carry the Ramond–Ramond one-form charge and have the protected mass $|n|/(g_s\ell_s)$ that matches momentum on the M circle. A scalar charged under the shared $Q$ alone establishes neither that BPS spectrum nor a spin completion of the projective product. [Lecture, Exercises 7–12](../paper/kk_strings_lecture.tex)
* **Nucleons and pions.** Assign baryon number $1/3$ to each quark in the ordinary QCD matching. A proton interpolator is $\epsilon_{abc}(u^{aT}\mathsf C\gamma_5d^b)u^c$, and exchanging $u,d$ gives a neutron interpolator. The antisymmetric contraction makes each a colour singlet, the free spinor index gives spin-$1/2$ overlap, and the constituent charges give $+1,0$. The rest energies come from the long-Euclidean-time falloff in the appropriate spin and parity channels, with QED switched off or with a specified electromagnetic infrared and mass convention. The geometric colour coupling and one-loop $\Lambda$ do not give those two correlators. A pion is tested by a pseudoscalar or axial current; a chiral pole with $\langle0|A_\mu^a|\pi^b(p)\rangle=iF p_\mu\delta^{ab}$ can supply weak stiffness when that current is gauged. The flavour-adjoint meson charge table of the five-flavour branch identifies charges; spin, baryon number, current and mass data are still needed for any proposed identification with leptons.
