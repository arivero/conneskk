# If the antisquarks of three generations are symmetric diquarks of the light quarks, their counts force (n_u, n_d) = (2, 3), with the top out, and leave three antitriplets of charge +4/3, half of a chiral-multiplet pairing and anomalous as superpartners

**Status.** Proposition (Proposition 21.4), with Corollary 21.5. Stated by the user and Claude Fable 5.1 in conversation on 4 November 2026. The group is SO(6(n_u + n_d)); the window clause holds in reading (a) only. Ledger row `l21_exotic` (depending on `l21_so30`). Stated in the section on the charge-4/3 antitriplets of paper/real_structure_note.tex ("\supp{21}").

**Setting.** Let n_u up-type and n_d down-type light quarks in three colours with their conjugates, span the vector of SO(6(n_u + n_d)). The colour antitriplet of its adjoint contains the symmetric diquarks S^2(n_u + n_d) (x) 3-bar. Three generations of antisquarks need 6 states of charge -2/3 and 6 of charge +1/3 (21_so30_branching.md).

**Statement.**

1. The symmetric diquarks have n_d(n_d + 1)/2 states of charge -2/3, n_u n_d of charge +1/3 and n_u(n_u + 1)/2 of charge +4/3.
2. The antisquark counts 6 and 6 have the unique solution (n_u, n_d) = (2, 3) in nonnegative integers, so the top is out, and the charge +4/3 count is 3. With the top, (3, 3) gives (6, 9, 6), and the vector would need 36 = 30 + 6 real dimensions, against the 32 of SO(32), whose extra plane is colour-neutral.
3. (Corollary 21.5) A Dirac quark of charge 4/3 in each generation needs two complex scalars, so three generations need 6, and the three antitriplets are half of a chiral-multiplet pairing. Read as superpartners, their fermions are three left-handed Weyl fields in (3-bar, 4/3), SU(2) singlets, anomalous on their own: with T(3) = 1/2 and A(3) = 1 the SU(3)^3, SU(3)^2 U(1), U(1)^3 and gravitational U(1) traces are -3, 2, 64/3 and 12, and the meson sector (colour singlets of charges 0 and +-1, six of them charged) cancels none of them for any chirality of its fermions; any other chirality assignment of the three multiplets leaves the SU(3)^3 trace odd.
4. The readings compatible with the counting are (a) the three scalars have no fermion partner, as the Higgs doublet has none in the Standard Model, so supersymmetry is a property of the (6, 6) part alone; or (b) the top is included, which completes the exotic to 6 and breaks the +1/3 count to 9. In the window of the interval parent (lemma 38, Proposition 11.4, the shadow B_ij -> (m_i - m_j) B_ij, distinct masses) the same-flavour diquarks drop out: the counts become (3, 6, 1) in reading (a), with one state of charge +4/3, u and c antisymmetric in flavour with coefficient proportional to m_u - m_c, and (3, 9, 3) in reading (b), with uc, ut and ct. In both readings the window keeps 3 states of charge -2/3, so the match with the antisquarks holds in the parent and fails in the window.

*Scope.* The premise of the whole counting is that five light quarks generate every squark and slepton as a composite, scalars that the finite geometry of Connes et al. does not contain. Under that premise the corollary records a conflict between the count of three generations and the anomaly of the exotic sector.

**Proof.** Lemma 21, Proposition 21.4 and its proof: n_d(n_d + 1)/2 = 6 gives n_d = 3, then n_u n_d = 6 gives n_u = 2, and 2 x 3/2 = 3; a coloured quark adds 6 real dimensions, and 32 - 30 = 2. Corollary 21.5 is counting with the standard anomaly traces.

**What it leaves open.** The selection of (2, 3) is a property of the parent, since the flavour-antisymmetric counts of the window and of s = -1 admit no solution of (6, 6); whether the wall condition that gives Lambda^2 V can be derived is the direction (lemma 21, Direction). The remark that the top cannot form the diquarks is from hep-ph/0512065, and the reading of the charge-4/3 states as an additional quark per generation, with the half-multiplet problem, is in the abstract of the EPJC paper (Rivero 2024) (lemma 21, Provenance). Identifying the states with physical scalar partners, in either reading, needs a field construction, spin and chirality assignments, interactions and dynamics.
