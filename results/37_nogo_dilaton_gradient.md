# For a potential with a regular joint expansion in theta and alpha'/l^2 around the flat family, the type I' dilaton gradient supplies no second scale for Proposition 1.5: its relative variation is O(R^{-2}) along an opening path, a stationary joint branch requires a_0 = 0, and if V(0,l) vanishes for all l it also requires b = 0

**Status.** No-go theorem in its scope (Propositions 10.5 and 10.6). Proved by Claude Opus 5.5 on 4 November 2026. Proposition 10.5 is an upper bound, with its consequence for the potential resting on a regular expansion. Item 3 assumes V(0,l) = 0 for all l and a nonzero quadratic coefficient b. The counterexamples in statement 4 below isolate the role of each hypothesis. Ledger row `d11_gradient`. Stated in the section on the string dualities of paper/connes_kk_dictionary_note.tex (the necessary condition on the constant coefficient at zero angle).

**Setting.** The profile of 37_angle_to_position_map.md with L = 2 pi and alpha' = 2, where z is B plus a function of size at most 8L = 16 pi; a joint family R_eps ~ eps^{-alpha}; and V = l^{-4} [G_0(theta) + (alpha'/l^2) G_1(theta) + ...], theta = l m, with G_0 = a_0 + b theta^2 + ... near 0.

**Statement.**

1. (Proposition 10.5) With B = min h, for large R, R^2 = 3B/(8 pi) + O(1), and the relative variation of the profile across the interval is at most 16 pi/B ~ 6/R^2, an upper bound (zero in the chamber 8 + 8); along the family it is O(eps^{2 alpha}), the map of Proposition 10.1 linearizes and the differences of the dilaton tend to zero. That its corrections to the potential are powers of alpha'/l^2 is the hypothesis of a regular expansion, which small variation alone does not give.
2. (Proposition 10.6, item 1) If a_0 != 0 and the joint expansion with its derivatives is controlled near (theta, s) = (0, 0), the stationarity expression tends to -4 a_0, excluding a stationary joint branch with theta, s -> 0.
3. (item 2) If a_0 = 0, G_1(0) = a_1 > 0 and b < 0, the truncated potential has a minimum at l^4 = 3 a_1 alpha'/(abs(b) m^2), so alpha = 1/2, and 1/l is the geometric mean of m and the string scale up to constants; with regular higher terms this is the leading asymptotic.
4. (item 3) If supersymmetry is exact at theta = 0, and V(0,l) vanishes for all l, smooth even dependence on theta gives V = m^2 l^{-2} H(theta, alpha'/l^2). If b != 0, the stationary condition tends to -2b at theta, s -> 0, excluding a stationary joint branch. If b = 0, take G_0 = theta^6, G_1 = theta^2 and all higher terms zero. The minimum of V = m^6 l^2 + alpha' m^2/l^4 is at l^6 = 2 alpha'/m^4, with l m -> 0. Vanishing of just a_0 and a_1 likewise permits a joint family with b = -1. For G_0 = -theta^2, G_1 = 0 and G_2 = c > 0, the strict minimum has l^6 = 4c (alpha')^2/m^2 and l m -> 0.

*Hypotheses.* A potential with a regular joint expansion in theta and alpha'/l^2 around the flat family with its towers, and the frame identification relating l to R_h; the corpus content, with a_0 > 0 (37_casimir_coefficient.md); the dilaton gradient, as distinct from a dilaton potential. In supersymmetric type I' there is no potential at all, since the D8 positions, B and C are flat directions.

*Escapes, as the lemma states them.* A dilaton potential from string tree level, in non-supersymmetric strings or with tadpoles, for which a term in positive powers of 1/g could grow with l at fixed v through Proposition 10.4; a different frame relation between l and the four-dimensional Planck mass; wall-localized dynamics; the other chambers of the moduli space, where 8 + 8 removes the Romans mass and E8 x E8 is available.

**Proof.** Lemma 37, Propositions 10.5 and 10.6: insert z = B + O(16 pi) in the formula for R; differentiate, with V'' = 8 abs(b) m^2/l^4 > 0 at the stationary point of item 3; V even in theta and vanishing at theta = 0 for every l gives V = theta^2 l^{-4} H.

**What it leaves open.** The result points to three targets, none derived: a bulk energy depending on the electroweak order parameter (Remark 10.8: V = a/l^4 + b v^2 M^3 l gives alpha = 2/5 if the bulk energy vanishes at v = 0); a cancellation a_0 = 0 without supersymmetry at theta = 0; or a fixed shape with an exponentially small ratio, as on the disc bundle of lemma 29 (Remark 10.7).
