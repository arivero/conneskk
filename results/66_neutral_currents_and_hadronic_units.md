## Which neutral pole to compare with the charged pole

**Proposition 66.1.** On the equal-root branch near round dissolution, with canonical relative matching, the two light massive neutral poles in the sourced gauge sector have squared masses in ratios $8/5$ and $2/3$ to the light charged squared mass, up to corrections tending to zero. The first neutral pole couples to the usual neutral weak current at leading order. The second couples to the monopole charge of the probe. In particular a right-handed neutrino with the degree-one singlet lift has a leading residue at this latter pole. These are tree statements in the specified fibration, before strong vacuum polarization.

**Proof.** Use charge connections $A^3,B_Y,V_0$ for the round lifted axial rotation, $Y=k^{-1}\partial_\chi$ and the constant gauge transformation of the charge-one vortex line. The kinetic coefficients of these orthogonal directions are $I_W,I_Y,I_v$, with
$$
 I_W=CV\,4r^2/3,\qquad I_Y=CV L^2/k^2,\qquad
 I_v=16CVa^2=6I_W,\qquad r=\sqrt2\,a .
$$
The magnetic part of the lift of a rotation has zero mean and is orthogonal to the constant. The two roots have equal integrated squared norms and opposite pairs $(T_3,Y)=(\pm1/2,\mp1/2)$, with vortex charge $+1$ for both. Put $v_h^2=2CVZ_H\langle|H_+|^2+|H_-|^2\rangle$ and $g_2^2=1/I_W$, $g_Y^2=1/I_Y$. In the squared-mass quadratic form before kinetic normalization their leading neutral terms are
$$
 v_h^2\big[(A^3-B_Y)^2/4+V_0^2\big].
$$
The terms linear in $V_0(A^3-B_Y)$ cancel between the two roots. A rotation perpendicular to the axis contributes $v_h^2(A^1)^2/4$ for one real charged component. The root squared norms are of order $\sqrt\epsilon$, $\epsilon=e^{-b}$. The vortex itself and elimination of the gapped vector complement contribute at order $\epsilon$. One may use the instantaneous leading kinetic coefficients above, or take their limits in the mass ratios. The eigenvalues at leading order are consequently
$$
 M_C^2=g_2^2 v_h^2/4,\qquad
 M_Z^2=(g_2^2+g_Y^2)v_h^2/4,\qquad M_v^2=v_h^2/I_v .
$$
The photon is the exact diagonal generator at a broken member. At matching $g_Y^2/g_2^2=3/5$. The two ratios follow. A fermion current with weights $(T_3,Y,N)$ has limiting vertices in canonical field units
$$
 e_*Q,\qquad
 \frac{g_2^2T_3-g_Y^2Y}{\sqrt{g_2^2+g_Y^2}},\qquad
 N/\sqrt{I_v},\qquad e_*^{-2}=I_W+I_Y .
$$
For the $\nu_R$ lift $(T_3,Y,N)=(0,0,1)$ only the last of these acts. Other form vectors with zero vertex and zero kinetic mixing are omitted as in result 63. The spectral projectors of the separated light masses converge to these combinations because the splitting between the two neutral masses is already of order $\sqrt\epsilon$. This identifies the pole by its residue on each source. $\square$

The comparison suggested by the spectrum atlas uses the neutral pole identified by its weak current and would use $\sqrt{M_Z^2-M_C^2}=g_Yv_h/2$ to leading order. The lighter vortex pole has a different current and $M_v^2<M_C^2$. The one-mass magnetic branch in dualsm has a diagonal photon and one neutral massive gauge pole in its canonical two-derivative action. Matching the two descriptions on neutral sources now also tests the vortex-charge residue, for example on a singlet with $T_3=Y=0$. Its prospective two-site extension already has the independent charged-pole test of Proposition 63.5. ## The cost of a long six-dimensional window

**Proposition 66.2.** On the reflection-symmetric factorization $r_+r_-=\Phi$ of result 63, the geometrically normalized roots and their projected Yukawa couplings grow on a long neck. For the unit singlet zero mode $e_b$ and unscaled meridian length $P_b$,
$$
 \langle |r_\pm|^2\rangle\asymp\|r_\pm e_b\|^2
       \asymp P_b^{-1}e^{P_b/(2\sqrt2)}
       \qquad(b\downarrow0).
$$
On the same neck the fixed-shape lower-limit charged Green function $S_b$ of Proposition 63.4 satisfies $0<S_b\le C P_b^2e^{-P_b/\sqrt2}$ for the limiting normalized left doublet source. These are two different matrix elements on the same magnetic sphere.

**Proof.** In the conformal meridian coordinate $t$, the squared root norms are exactly $\exp(u/2\pm t)$. The product normalization and reflection fix the multiplicative constants. Let $T_b>0$ be the cross-section $u(T_b)=-1$. The two outer caps have bounded meridian length. On $0\le t\le T_b$, $1-f_b/\sqrt2\le u_t^2/4$ by the first integral. The integral of $u_t^2$ there is at most $2\int|du|\le2$. Thus $T_b=P_b/(2\sqrt2)+O(1)$. For the plus root, translate the positive cap to $\xi=t-T_b$ in the integral of $e^{u/2+t} f_b^2dt$. On $\xi<0$ the integrand after division by $e^{T_b}$ is bounded by $2e^\xi$. On $\xi>0$, the first integral gives $u_t\le-c_0$ for a fixed $c_0>2/3$ once $b$ is small, and $f_b^2\le C e^u$. This gives an integrable majorant. A fixed interval about $\xi=0$ contributes a strictly positive lower bound by smooth cap convergence. Reflection gives the other root. Now $\mathcal A_b\asymp P_b$ and the unit singlet density lies between fixed multiples of $1/\mathcal A_b$ by the exact formula of result 55. These facts give the first estimate.

For the second estimate work in the zero-circle-frequency, unit axial-weight sector of the limiting form $\mathcal H_b$ in result 63. The sphere Korn estimate of Proposition 62.2 controls $\|X\|_{H^1}$ uniformly by the symmetrized derivative. The bound $f_b^2\le2$ gives $\|z\|\le\sqrt2\|dz\|$ and the same for $w$. Since $F_A$ is bounded, $\|dz\|$ is controlled by $\|dz+\iota_XF_A\|+\mathrm{const}\,\|X\|$. All these norms use the same area-average measure. The source contains the cross product of the cap spinors and its first-derivative terms. Its dual norm is bounded by $\mathrm{const}\,\mathcal A_b e^{-T_b}$ times the square root of $\mathcal H_b$, by the pointwise cap estimates of Proposition 62.2, now with $z$ an independent field. The circle term has vanished in the fixed-shape lower limit. Squaring gives the result. The source has nonzero overlap with a smooth vortex gauge profile supported away from the poles for every finite $b$. $\square$

For a long *finite* middle member one can choose the Yukawas once with margins and transport the light window as in Proposition 63.2. On the sequence $a=d/P_b$, the projected value of $Bm_i$ at first order in a fixed Yukawa instead grows proportionally to $|y_i|e^{P_b/(4\sqrt2)}/\sqrt{P_b}$. A uniform small-Yukawa expansion on an indefinitely lengthening middle neck fails this test. Retaining the cap mass potential gives a mixed-boundary half interval for each electric charge and a lowest mass approaching $\pi/d$. ## Responses in one frame

Let $H>0$ be a comparison energy in the same four-dimensional product frame, $h=BH$ and $x>0$. When stable nucleons exist in the matched theory we may take $H=H_N$. At a compact broken member let $\lambda_j$ be the generalized eigenvalues of the complete dimensionless charged form in Proposition 63.4, including the roots, relative to $\mathcal K_b$. Use $\mathcal K_b$-orthonormal vectors and let $j_\ell$ be the transition amplitude of the chosen normalized lepton on each vector. Write $\lambda_C$ for the first eigenvalue with a nonzero vertex. In the following sum the weights are summed within any degenerate eigenspace. Direct inversion of the kinetic and mass forms gives
$$
 \mathcal R(x)=H^2 C_{\ell\ell}(x^2 H^2)
 =\frac{h^2}{32I_3}
     \sum_j\frac{|j_\ell|^2}{\lambda_j+x^2 h^2 a^2/B^2},
 \qquad
 \frac{M_C}{H}=\frac{B}{a h}\sqrt{\lambda_C}.
$$
Here $CV B^2=4I_3$ and a canonical $V-A$ source carries the factor $1/8$. The same expression uses the actual fermion overlap when Yukawa mixing is retained. A compact middle member with a six-dimensional scalar band need not have a weak pole inside that band.

## How much weak exchange the same metric allows

**Proposition 66.3.** Fix a compact broken member with nonzero roots and a nonzero normalized charged source in Proposition 63.4. Vary only the external root coefficient $Z_H>0$, holding the colour and photon tree norms, the fermion operator and the remaining microscopic data fixed. At any fixed spacelike $t\ge0$ the contact or finite-momentum response runs continuously and strictly decreasingly through $(0,C_{\ell\ell}(t;0))$. The endpoint at zero coefficient denotes the limit from the positive actions. The upper contact bound $C_{\ell\ell}(0;0)$ can itself be made arbitrarily large by moving sufficiently close to compact round dissolution on the matched trajectory.

**Proof.** Let $D_0(t)$ be the positive inverse propagator in the charged sector with the root mass form omitted. The nonround metric and magnetic fields give it a gap even for $t=0$. Write $D(t)=D_0(t)+Z_H P_H$, where $P_H$ is the nonnegative root form. It has trivial kernel on this charged sector. Indeed an infinitesimal real field of zero root energy must preserve both pointwise root norms. On the sphere away from its poles they are proportional to $\exp(u/2\pm t_{\rm ax})$, and $|u_t|<2$ there. Preservation forces the meridian component of $X$ to vanish. A solenoidal, purely azimuthal vector at circle frequency zero is independent of azimuth, whereas this sector has absolute axial weight one. Thus $X=0$. The two root equations then give $z+n_\pm a w/L=0$, so $z=w=0$. Complexifying the real mass form gives the same conclusion for a raising polarization.

The identity
$$
 \partial_{Z_H} C_{\ell\ell}(t)
   =-\tfrac18\|P_H^{1/2}D(t)^{-1}j\|^2
$$
now has strictly negative right side. More explicitly put $v=D_0(t)^{-1/2}j$ and $A=D_0(t)^{-1/2}P_H D_0(t)^{-1/2}$. The smooth source has finite energy dual norm. Its response is $\langle v,(1+Z_H A)^{-1}v\rangle/8$. The positive operator $A$ has zero-dimensional kernel. Its spectral measure and dominated convergence give zero as $Z_H\to\infty$ even if its spectrum accumulates at zero. At $Z_H\downarrow0$ the response tends to $\|v\|^2/8$. Finally the vortex-only charged mass tends to zero at compact round dissolution and the lifted doublet raising residue has a nonzero limit. Its contact consequently diverges in this last comparison. $\square$

Every value of $Z_H$ defines one fixed-action trajectory. At fixed weak geometry, classical vacuum support and tree gauge matching leave this current freely adjustable. The massive spectrum entering quantum threshold matching can change in such a comparison. For example, in the fixed-metric root matter sector the opposite real changes $\delta H_\pm=\pm h(z)H_\pm$ preserve the holomorphic and product squares. For a first projective harmonic $h$, its colour gradient gives squared mass $12Z_{H,c}/(B^2 Z_H)$ in that sector. Full mixing with gravitational perturbations needs a separate calculation. At five $Z_H$ drops out of the leading *fixed-coefficient* response, while Proposition 66.2 allows an arbitrarily small positive $S_b$ by varying the finite shape. Thus the large-$Z_H$ and collapsing-area comparisons have different orders of limits.

## A test using the historical mass ruler

López Castro and Pestieau used the weak gauge-basis mass parameter in a set of empirical heavy-particle mass relations. At tree level for a doublet it is $\sqrt{M_Z^2-M_C^2}=g_Yv_h/2$. The observable here uses two poles identified by their currents. Put $R_N=H_N/[\alpha_{\rm Th}\sqrt{M_Z^2-M_C^2}]$ wherever these masses and the Thomson charge exist. Direct substitution gives the exact cancellation of the hadron input
$$
 \frac{\Xi}{R_N^2}=\alpha_{\rm Th}^2G_F(M_Z^2-M_C^2).
$$
Approaching restoration from the broken side in the tree action, the leading inverse charged mass in $G_F$ cancels the leading mass difference, yielding $4\sqrt2\,G_F(M_Z^2-M_C^2)\to g_Y^2$. For this statement first extract the contact at positive breaking. A finite heavy-pole contact contributes a term vanishing in the product. The same product on the one-mass magnetic branch, if a lepton had charged vertex $c_\ell g_m$, would give $c_\ell^2g_Y^2$. That vertex remains to be identified in the dual theory.
