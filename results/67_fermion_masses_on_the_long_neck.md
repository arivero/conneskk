# Fermion masses on the six-dimensional neck at fixed Yukawa couplings

Use the supported diagonal vacuum, the circle-equivariant Weyl matter and the choice of internal volume matrix for the mass link in Proposition 63.2. Keep both ordinary Yukawa couplings of one doublet nonzero and fixed, and keep the two fluxes, electric charges, gauge norms and meridian length $d$ fixed. Vary the shape $b\downarrow0$ with $a=d/P_b$ and the matched $B,L\asymp P_b^{1/7}$. We compute the actual light Dirac masses in the projective kernel. This is the trajectory that produces the limiting six-dimensional scalar density. At each finite shape it also permits the different operation of shrinking $a$ further at fixed $b$.

## A half interval for each electric charge

**Theorem 67.1.** For each of the two ordinary electric charges in this doublet, the $j$th positive fermion mass in the projective zero sector satisfies
$$
             m_{j}(b)\ \longrightarrow\ \frac{(2j+1)\pi}{d},
             \qquad j=0,1,\ldots .
$$
Here the projective multiplicity is counted separately. The limiting squared operator acts on an interval of length $d/2$, with a natural Neumann condition at the surviving pole and a Dirichlet condition at the equator. Opposite electric charges see opposite halves. Nonzero fixed mirror Yukawas have the same leading thresholds. In particular $B m_0\to\infty$ on this path. The bulk sphere-constant scalar tower of result 59 still carries charged circle modes.

**Fermion operator.** With the canonical complex orientation the spin Dirac operator on the sphere twisted by the charge-$N$ line is the Dolbeault Dirac operator on $E_N=K^{1/2}\otimes V_1^N$. In particular $E_1=\mathcal O(0)$ and $E_2=\mathcal O(1)$. The two singlet fields are copies of $E_1$, with distinct hypercharges, and the doublet is carried by $E_2$. The holomorphic mass map on this sphere is
$$
 Y_b(s_1,s_2)=\frac{\sqrt{\zeta_0}B_0}{B}
                (y_1 r_+ s_1+y_2 r_-s_2).
$$
The two prescribed circle characters make both terms sections of the same equivariant doublet bundle. Constant flavour phases can be absorbed into the singlets. On the two-term complex, put the singlets in bundle degree zero and the doublet in bundle degree one. The differential is the sum of $\sqrt2\,\bar\partial$ in the physical sphere metric and $(-1)^pY_b$ on Dolbeault form degree $p$. Its square vanishes since $Y_b$ is holomorphic. Adding its adjoint gives precisely the spin Dirac and the Hermitian Yukawa link with the weak volume sign. The projective Dirac operator separates by the product chirality. The proposition concerns this specified link realization. With both $y_i\ne0$, the two roots never vanish together and give the exact holomorphic sequence
$$
 0\ \longrightarrow\ \mathcal O(-1)\
 \longrightarrow\ \mathcal O(0)^{\oplus2}\
 \xrightarrow{\ Y_b\ }\ \mathcal O(1)\ \longrightarrow\ 0 .
$$
Its kernel line has vanishing $H^0$ and $H^1$ on the sphere, so the total complex has no zero mode at finite broken shape. Equivalently, the map on holomorphic sections is a two-by-two isomorphism and all the individual $H^1$ vanish.

**Proof of the limit.** Let $D_0,D_1$ denote the two successive differentials of the total complex. The positive spectrum of its squared Dirac operator on degree one consists of the positive singular values squared from degree zero and degree two. This follows by the orthogonal decomposition into the ranges of $D_0$ and $D_1^\dagger$, with the finite-dimensional harmonic space just computed. The degree-two quadratic form is at least the squared adjoint weak Dirac form on $E_2$-valued $(0,1)$-forms. These have no zero modes and have first squared eigenvalue at least $c/a^2$ on the long neck by the charge-two gap in result 55. Thus every bounded mass comes from degree zero. Its form is the sum of the two nonnegative singlet derivative energies and $\|Y_b(s_1,s_2)\|^2$. Terms of different output form degree are orthogonal.

Fix the electric charge of the first central singlet. Its angular index is the central northern spin label $m=1/2$ in result 55. The other singlet in that same charge sector has label differing by one, because their hypercharges differ by one. The noncentral charge-one gap bounds the physical $L^2$ norm of the other singlet by $C a$ times the square root of the total energy. In charge sectors containing neither central singlet both derivative energies have that gap. The same reasoning with the singlets exchanged applies for the opposite member of the weak doublet.

Set $x=s_{\rm mer}/P_b$ in unscaled meridian arclength, so the physical distance from the north pole is $d\,x$, with $0\le x\le1$. Factor the exact singlet zero spinor out of the central component, absorbing its normalization in its scalar coefficient $v(x)$. The squared norm and the physical derivative energy, up to the same harmless normalization, are
$$
 \int_0^1 w_b|v|^2\,dx,\qquad d^{-2}\int_0^1 w_b|v'|^2\,dx,
 \qquad w_b=e^{u/2}.
$$
The weights tend uniformly to one on every compact subinterval of $(0,1)$. The endpoint trace estimate in result 55 gives compactness in these varying norms and prevents loss of norm into the ends for any bounded-energy sequence. Local weak $H^1$ limits give the ordinary unweighted derivative lower bound on the entire interval by exhaustion.

On compact subintervals of $(1/2,1)$ the conformal meridian coordinate $t$ is positive of order $P_b$ and $u\to0$. Indeed $t\ge (x-1/2)P_b/\sqrt2$ by $f_b\le\sqrt2$, and the cap with $u\le-\eta$ has bounded meridian length for each $\eta>0$. The plus root has $|r_+|=\exp(u/4+t/2)$, so $\inf|y_1r_+|/B\to\infty$ there. The other root divided by $B$ is uniformly small there. A bound on $\|Y_b(s_1,s_2)\|$ therefore forces the central component to converge to zero on the whole open right half. There can be no cancellation by the other singlet whose multiplier tends to zero on these subintervals. Its limiting $H^1$ coefficient vanishes at the midpoint. On the left half, $|r_+|\le1$ all the way to the pole. Conversely any smooth left-half trial coefficient vanishing at the midpoint, extended by zero to the right, with the other singlet set to zero, has Yukawa energy bounded by $\mathrm{const}\,|y_1|^2/B^2$ times its norm. Functions constant near the surviving pole give regular form trials and are dense in the limiting domain. Dominated convergence gives their unweighted derivative energy. This establishes matching lower and upper forms and compactness for the min–max principle. The left half has free trace at zero and zero trace at $1/2$. Its eigenvalues in mass units are the stated odd multiples of $\pi/d$. Reflection interchanges the roots and the two halves. For a normalized eigenvector in any fixed limiting level, its central derivative energy saturates the limiting eigenvalue by the lower bound and the matching trials. All other nonnegative parts of its degree-zero energy tend to zero. In particular the doublet component $Y_b(s_1,s_2)/m_j$ of its normalized degree-one partner tends to zero in norm. The limiting massive pair is carried by the surviving singlet and its derivative. This conclusion concerns the state projection. A physical current with a growing KK normalization still requires its matrix element. $\square$

This half-interval spectrum has different charged-current kinematics from the free singlet tower, whose interval has two free ends and includes zero. At finite nonround shape the subsequent $a\downarrow0$ limit of Proposition 63.2 has $m_i$ of order $1/B$ for every fixed nonzero ordinary or mirror Yukawa in its isolated window. The two limiting operations thus have different fermion content, despite sharing the same unbroken $Q$. In the unbounded six-dimensional neck the quarks and their mirrors of this matter prescription are above the four-dimensional colour matching scale $\xi/B$. Using ordinary six-flavour running there would give the wrong field count even if the colour vacuum were stabilized. A long finite middle followed by a fixed-shape collapse remains available. A massless singlet with exactly zero corresponding Yukawa is a separate control and can propagate through the whole neck. ## A five-flavour branch for the magnetic comparison

**Proposition 67.2.** Take three ordinary quark families, a rank-one up-type Yukawa matrix and a zero down-type Yukawa matrix. Take full-rank diagonal Yukawas on the independent mirror families. Keep these choices fixed, and assume separate mirror number as in result 63. The bulk colour action and flux can be either of the supported classical choices. In the projective kernel there are exactly two up-type and three down-type ordinary massless Dirac quarks, including both chiralities, at every finite broken member. On the indefinitely long six-dimensional neck all other ordinary and mirror quark masses are separated from these by a positive gap in units of $1/d$. For a sufficiently small fixed $\xi>0$ the quark content below $\xi/B$ is therefore precisely five Dirac fundamentals and their antiparticles for sufficiently long necks. This last statement counts the quarks. The colour gauge and Wilson sectors have the independent tests of results 64–65.

**Proof.** At one projective zero vector take global holomorphic sections of the bundle differential in the preceding proof. The two singlet spaces each have one weak section per family and the doublet space has two. Both weak line bundles have vanishing $H^1$. The induced map on sections, in the two independent root weights, is the direct sum of the up and down Yukawa matrices multiplied by nonzero constants. Its kernel and cokernel are the two chiralities of the massless four-dimensional modes. Each has up-charge multiplicity $3-\operatorname{rank}Y_u$ and down-charge multiplicity $3-\operatorname{rank}Y_d$. The projective triplet supplies the colour factor. The mirror map is an isomorphism on sections at finite breaking. Charge conjugation gives the antiparticle module.

Choose a constant singular-vector basis for the sole nonzero ordinary matrix. In the third family the up singlet has the mass potential $|y_t r_+|^2\zeta_0 B_0^2/B^2$, and the down singlet is free. The lowest positive central up mass tends to $\pi/d$ by the half-interval proof. The free singlet has one zero and its first positive central mass also tends to $\pi/d$ by the full Neumann interval in result 55. Other singlet angular labels and degree-two modes cost at least a constant times $1/a$. The extra degree-one harmonic is the cokernel of the bundle map and has the bottom charge. In the two entirely free families the same Neumann and transverse gaps hold. Theorem 67.1 gives the mirror gap. Nonzero projective Dirac modes in the quark twists cost at least $\delta_c/B$ by product separation. Taking $\xi<\delta_c$ proves the assertion. Identifying the single selected up singular direction with the top is a flavour input. Small nonzero fixed Yukawas change the infinite-neck result; the exact rank condition matters. $\square$

**The weak and strong measurements on this branch.** A complete ordinary lepton family with zero Yukawa is a clean current probe. Its normalized left doublet is exactly the two cap modes at every broken member. The full charged mass form of result 63 dominates the metric, circle-gradient and magnetic terms used to bound this source in Proposition 66.2. At finite radius the circle vertex also occurs with coefficient $n_\ell a/L$. Thus with $t_E\ge0$ and $h=BH$ one obtains uniformly on the long-neck curve
$$
 0\le H^2 C_{\ell\ell}(t_E)\le H^2 C_{\ell\ell}(0)
 \le C\frac{h^2}{I_3}\,\mathcal A_b^2
       (1+n_\ell^2a^2/L^2)\exp[-P_b/\sqrt2].
$$
Indeed $\|z\|$ is controlled by the magnetic term and the Korn estimate even with the independent vortex gauge profile, and dropping the positive root term can only increase the exchange. This proves vanishing at any bounded $BH$, including at finite spacelike momentum measured in such units. The fixed matching coefficient on $Q$ coexists with screening from an exactly massless charged lepton in the low-energy Abelian approximation.

If the actual coloured bosonic thresholds have been lifted above a perturbative $\mu_*=\xi/B$ and the colour-preserving vacuum has five massless Dirac quarks, continuous one-loop running gives $B\Lambda_5=\xi\exp[-24\pi^2 I_3(\mu_*)/23]$. With one real adjoint active above a perturbative mass $M_S<\mu_*$ this becomes
$$
 (B\Lambda_5)^{23/3}
   =\xi^{43/6}(B M_S)^{1/2}\exp[-8\pi^2 I_3(\mu_*)].
$$
In either fixed-ratio matching $G_F\Lambda_5^2\to0$ for the specified lepton current. Converting this to $G_FH_N^2$ needs baryon masses of the five-flavour theory, including a choice of electromagnetic convention. The measured three-flavour nucleon conversion in result 61 belongs to different light masses. A colour tachyon excludes hadronic matching about the top-form saddle.

**What dualsm can supply.** With the electric unit inherited from $Q$, the light flavour space is $2_{2/3}\oplus3_{-1/3}$. Its traceless endomorphisms have twelve neutral, six positive-unit and six negative-unit weights. This recovers the flavour adjoint counted in the earlier papers, now on the light quarks of a geometric spectral limit. The bifundamental meson labels of the five-flavour magnetic model have the same charge differences, with one more neutral direction before trace removal. The projective metric, its supporting Abelian gauge fluctuation and the original vortex bosons all descend under the projective $PSU(3)$ action. Their tensor harmonics are trivial under the centre of $SU(3)$. None can supply elementary colour-triplet squark modes in this field content. The quarks owe their nontrivial central character to their projective twists. Equivariant bosonic line-bundle sections or a different colour lift would permit a corresponding scalar representation. A gluino and supersymmetric interactions would still require microscopic fields and a supercurrent. Thus a common five-flavour charge table, a neutral-current test and field-content requirements can be confronted with the sibling theory independently. ## One small mass in units of the colour matching scale

The five-flavour electric theory in dualsm is deformed by one mass, leaving four massless flavours on its partially massive branch. In the geometric limit of Proposition 67.2, adding another nonzero *constant* Yukawa removes that flavour from the comoving window. There is a fixed local dilaton coupling that instead supplies one continuously adjustable mass *within* that window.

**Proposition 67.3.** On the long middle trajectory choose one previously free down-type singlet in an otherwise free doublet family. Turn on its ordinary holomorphic root link with coefficient $y(s)$ depending on the scale dilaton $a=e^s$. For any $\eta>0$ there exists a smooth fixed such function for which its lowest physical mass satisfies
$$
                 B m_{\rm one}\ \longrightarrow\
                        \sqrt{\zeta_0}B_0\,\eta .
$$
Four other ordinary quarks remain exactly massless. The next level of the deformed singlet stays separated in physical units. A choice of small $\eta$ therefore realizes four massless quarks and one quark of tunable mass below $\xi/B$, with the same classical support and photon. This Yukawa function is new microscopic input.

**Proof.** Put $N_b=\|r_-e_b\|$, reversing the root labels if needed for the selected charge. At sufficiently large $P_b$ set $y(s)=\eta/N_b$ with $P_b=d e^{-s}$. The meridian integral is smooth and strictly increasing with $\epsilon=e^{-b}$, since its integrand in the variable $v=-u-b$ is proportional to $\sqrt{q/(1-q)}$ with $q=\exp[-v+\epsilon(1-e^{-v})]$. Thus this defines a function of one local scalar. Extend it smoothly outside the long-neck range, in particular to finite values at round dissolution. It is of order $\eta\sqrt{P_b}\exp[-P_b/(4\sqrt2)]$ on the selected curve. A fermion bilinear with this coupling has zero bosonic background stress.

In the central singlet sector the degree-zero squared operator is the weighted Neumann derivative in the proof of Theorem 67.1 plus the positive multiplication potential $V_b=\zeta_0 B_0^2 |y(s)r_-|^2/B^2$. The other column of this family's mass map is zero. Normalize $\mu_b=w_b dx/\int w_b dx$ to have unit mass. The constant trial function has potential expectation $\overline V_b=\zeta_0 B_0^2\eta^2/B^2$ exactly. The probability measure $\nu_b=V_b\,d\mu_b/\overline V_b$ concentrates in the cap where $r_-$ is large. We need to control evaluation there because the supremum of $V_b$ does not estimate its low-energy effect.

For $x\le1/2$, write $J_b(x)=\int_x^{1/2}du'/w_b(u')$ and use the reflected expression on the other half. Uniformly for long necks,
$$
                  \int J_b\,d\mu_b+\int J_b\,d\nu_b\le C .
$$
Indeed $w_b$ is bounded below in the neck cut at $u=-1$. In either outer cap put $y=P_b\min(x,1-x)$, the unscaled distance from its pole. The first integral and smooth pointed cap convergence give $w_b\asymp\min(y,1)$ on this bounded cap, including its simple linear zero in polar arclength. Thus $J_b\le C[1+P_b^{-1}|\log\min(y,1)|]$. Also $|r_-|^2\le C e^{T_b}$ everywhere: its squared norm is monotone toward its preferred pole and the translated polar limit after division by $e^{T_b}$ is finite there. Proposition 66.2 gives $N_b^2\asymp e^{T_b}/P_b$. Hence in a cap $d\nu_b\le C\min(y,1)dy$, and the logarithm is integrable. This proves the bound.

Choose the normalized lowest eigenfunction $v_b$. Its weighted derivative energy is $O(\overline V_b)$ by the constant trial. Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz bounds $|v_b(x)-v_b(1/2)|^2$ by $C J_b(x)\overline V_b$. Integrating first with $\mu_b$ and then with $\nu_b$ shows $|v_b(1/2)|=1+O(1/B)$ and $\int |v_b|^2d\nu_b=1+O(1/B)$. The potential expectation alone now gives the lower bound $\overline V_b(1-O(1/B))$, while the trial gives the matching upper bound. The next squared eigenvalue is bounded below by the first positive unperturbed Neumann eigenvalue, tending to $(\pi/d)^2$, by min–max and positivity of $V_b$. The other angular and degree-two gaps and the four remaining zeros follow as before. $\square$

The quantity $m_{\rm one}/\mu_*$ can now be specified independently of the shape at the comoving matching scale. If this mass is perturbatively above the ordinary nonsupersymmetric strong scale and below the omitted thresholds, continuous decoupling gives $\Lambda_4^{25/3}=m_{\rm one}^{2/3}\Lambda_5^{23/3}$. In electric supersymmetric $SU(3)$ with five flavour pairs the holomorphic decoupling law instead reads $\Lambda_{4,\rm hol}^{5}=m_{\rm hol}\Lambda_{5,\rm hol}^{4}$. At small magnetic deformation the canonical vector poles and their current residues further require the magnetic kinetic data. The geometric threshold supplies a candidate mass parameter for comparison. Identifying it with a holomorphic parameter, or identifying physical nucleons across the two theories, requires additional matching.
