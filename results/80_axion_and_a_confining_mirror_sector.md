# The flux axion and an electroweak-symmetric mirror threshold

Keep the three ordinary families, their circle and monopole charges, and the $p=1$ flux term of result 77. We ask whether the four-dimensional theory can retain the ordinary weak current while the mirrors acquire a mass from a different source. The checks are the gauge anomaly after removing the mirrors, the gauge-invariant operators carrying their quantum numbers, and the colour beta coefficient on either side of such a threshold. The construction in the second part is a four-dimensional supersymmetric strong-sector control. Its higher-dimensional matter content is an additional model-building question.

## The ordinary-family anomaly left below a mirror gap

**Proposition 80.1.** On a spin four-dimensional spacetime the local anomaly polynomial for the three ordinary families, including their vortex charge, admits the descent $I_6=2vK_4$. The four-form $K_4$ is an integral characteristic class even for Standard Model bundles with group $S(U(3)\times U(2))$ and independent vortex line. Hence a $2\pi$-periodic axion of charge two has a properly periodic Wess–Zumino term which can match this perturbative anomaly while preserving the Standard Model gauge currents.

**Proof.** On a lift to the product group let $x$ be the integral curvature class for the charge $6Y$, and $v=F_v/(2\pi)$. Use physical left Weyl fields minus right Weyl fields. The traces per ordinary family are $\sum\chi(6Y)^2N=-48$, $\sum\chi(6Y)N^2=0$, $\sum\chi N^3=56$, and $\sum\chi N=8$. The fundamental colour and weak second Chern characters are $-c_2^c$ and $-c_2^w$. Their charge-weighted multiplicities are two and eight. With the usual $\widehat A_4=-p_1/24$, the three-family polynomial is
$$
 I_6=v[-6c_2^c-24c_2^w-72x^2+28v^2-p_1(T)].
$$
The terms independent of $v$ cancel and each family also has an even number of weak doublets. Now write $E_3,E_2$ for the two defining unitary bundles of the quotient group, with $h=c_1(E_2)=-c_1(E_3)$. On a lift, $h=6x$, $c_2(E_3)=c_2^c+12x^2$ and $c_2(E_2)=c_2^w+9x^2$. Thus the required half coefficient is globally expressible as
$$
 K_4=-3c_2(E_3)-12c_2(E_2)+3h^2+14v^2-p_1(T)/2 .
$$
The spin class $p_1/2$ is integral. In the descent preserving the Standard Model currents, adding the term of sign opposite to the ordinary determinant, $2\pi i\int(\theta/2\pi)K_4$ with that sign, cancels its local variation since $\delta\theta=2\lambda_v$. A change of axion period produces an integer multiple of $2\pi i$ in the action. This is an integrality check on the characteristic polynomial and its local descent; torsion and global gauge anomalies of a proposed ultraviolet completion need their own test. On configurations admitting a global smooth fixed-radius Stückelberg section the allowed vortex bundles are themselves restricted. No necessity for exactly three families is inferred by testing unrestricted vortex bundles. $\square$

Restricted to a pure electromagnetic background the signed $Q^2N$ trace is eight for three ordinary families, so the electromagnetic term in $K_4$ is twice the square of the electric curvature in unit-charge normalization. Its colour term on an ordinary $SU(3)$ bundle is $-3c_2^c$. These are anomalous-current matching coefficients. The axion of 77 is eaten by the heavy vector. Interpreting it as a propagating axion with a two-photon decay would require another uneaten scalar mode.

The residual transformation at the round member is $e^{i\pi N}$. On the ordinary or mirror fermions of 72 it acts as fermion parity times the weak centre: it is even on the weak doublets and odd on the singlets. The unit monopole scalar doublets and the degree-two vortex triplet obey the same relation with their bosonic parity. This follows directly from the displayed representations, or from $2j=N-1\bmod2$ on weak monopole spinors. A mass mechanism must respect this residual action. A vortex vector mass alone does not supply the Wess–Zumino coefficient. Also the irreducible $p_3(T)$ term on the six rank-one *ten-dimensional* ordinary Weyl bundles survives if their parent mirrors are simply deleted. The four-dimensional polynomial establishes a low-energy possibility; the parent still needs anomaly-cancelling fields, inflow or a different local domain. ## A hidden symplectic sector with the same vortex anomalies

**Proposition 80.2 (four-dimensional strong-sector control).** For each mirror family there is an ultraviolet matter list with its Standard Model chiral index and its vortex gauge anomalies, no mixed hidden gauge anomaly and an explicit electroweak-symmetric mass map in the s-confining description at fixed axion spurion. Its threshold is set by a hidden scale rather than by either electroweak root.

**Construction.** Use the complex conjugate of the odd-$N$, $N=5$ s-confining construction of Razamat and Tong. The group $SU(5)$ labels the Standard Model representations. Only its Standard Model subgroup needs to be weakly gauged. Introduce a separate hidden $Sp(1)=SU(2)_h$ per mirror family. Take six hidden fundamental chiral superfields in the flavour decomposition $\mathbf5+\mathbf1$, and hidden-singlet chiral fields in $\overline{\mathbf{10}}+\overline{\mathbf5}$ that couple to their antisymmetric products. The following are charges of the independent *left Weyl fermions*. The scalar in each chiral superfield is assigned vortex charge $N+1$. The hidden colour multiplicity gives two Standard Model $\mathbf5$s. One and its $\overline{\mathbf5}$ are vectorlike including the vortex charges. The remaining $\mathbf5+\overline{\mathbf{10}}$ has the mirror chiral charges. The neutral fermions have total $N$ and $N^3$ traces both equal to one, exactly those of the mirror sterile singlet. Explicitly the linear trace is $2(-3)+3+3+3-1-1=1$ and the cubic trace is $2(-27)+3+27+27-1-1=1$. The hidden $SU(2)_h^2 U(1)_v$ coefficient is $(3-4-3)/2+2=0$, including its charged gaugino. The hidden mixed hypercharge trace is proportional to $3(-1/3)+2(1/2)=0$. It has six hidden doublets, which passes the global $SU(2)_h$ mod-two test. Thus at energies above this sector's gap, its ordinary gauge, mixed and gravitational four-dimensional anomalies cancel those of the ordinary family just as the original mirrors did.

To verify the interactions, let a spurion $T$ have imaginary part $\theta$ and shift $T\mapsto T+2i\lambda_v$. With scalar charges one greater than the fermion charges and gaugino charge one, the hidden gauge Yukawa vertex is invariant. In the supersymmetric comparison the superpotential carries charge two. For each invariant contraction $A^{ij}q_i q_j$ or $P^i q_i q_0$, multiply its coefficient by $\exp[(2-\sum N_{\rm scalar})T/2]$. Every exponent is integral. The colour-colour contraction, for example, has scalar charge six and uses $e^{-2T}$, whereas the colour-weak and weak-weak contractions have zero and use $e^T$. The singlet masses use the same rule on their quadratic terms. At fixed $T$ all coefficients can be nonzero. This can also be read in components as ordinary axion dressing of interactions with even total vortex charge. The spurion adds no propagating fermion to the table; promoting the axion and all external gauge and gravity fields to a gauged supergravity multiplet would change the anomaly calculation.

In the supersymmetric hidden theory write $\mathcal M_{IJ}=q_I q_J/\Lambda_h$, $1\le I<J\le6$, contracting the hidden indices antisymmetrically. These fifteen mesons are $\mathbf{10}+\mathbf5$, with the Standard Model charges conjugate to those of the fifteen singlet partners $A,P$. The infrared superpotential has a full-rank bilinear $\Lambda_h(A,P)\lambda(T)\mathcal M$ and the cubic Pfaffian of $\mathcal M$. The Pfaffian carries the required charge two, since the six scalar fundamental charges sum to two. At the origin the derivative with respect to every partner sets every meson to zero, and the meson derivatives set every partner to zero. Their Hessian is nonsingular for nonzero flip coefficients. The four extra singlets have their independent masses. This proves the mass map and unbroken spectator symmetry in the s-confining description, assuming regular positive meson kinetic terms at its smooth origin. Physical masses involve those kinetic normalizations. Confinement without chiral symmetry breaking in this supersymmetric theory is the cited four-dimensional dynamical input. $\square$

No electroweak root appears in that mass matrix at fixed $T,\Lambda_h$. Thus the six mirror-quark derivatives producing $6\kappa_Z$ in 79.2 are absent in the decoupled strong-sector control. Below the hidden threshold the ordinary anomalous vortex current needs the local matching of 80.1. A quantum completion must also control threshold polarization, mixing with the ordinary families and any supersymmetry breaking from gauging the spectator groups. This construction does not give a ten-dimensional local lift of the new fields. It gives an explicit representation and operator test for one.

## The colour cost and the comparison with dualsm

In the energy region where the hidden elementary multiplets are all active, their total colour Dynkin index per copy is $1$ from $q_c$, $3/2$ from $A$, and $1/2$ from $P$. Each chiral superfield supplies one Weyl fermion and one complex scalar. Its change in $b_{0,c}$ is therefore minus its index. Starting with six ordinary nonsupersymmetric quark flavours and the gluons gives $b_{0,c}=7-3(3)=-2$ above three such sectors, compared with $7$ below them while the six ordinary flavours remain active. Extra coloured compactification modes, if present, require their own counting. This result fixes the sign of weak-coupling colour running in the stated four-dimensional interval and requires a UV completion if this matter is to persist to arbitrarily short distances.

There is a precise magnetic possibility to compare with the sibling project. If the ordinary coloured sector is also supersymmetrized and a colour gluino is supplied, there are fifteen fundamental–antifundamental colour pairs in total. Their one-loop supersymmetric magnetic coefficient is $3(3)-15=-6$. Seiberg's free magnetic window then allows an electric $SU(12)$ theory with fifteen flavours, $b_{0,e}=36-15=21$, whose magnetic rank is three. Its full meson matrix has 225 chiral entries. Gauging the required flavour subgroups, supplying the flip superpotential, accounting for those mesons and reaching the non-supersymmetric infrared are substantive operator and phase-matching requirements. The hidden $Sp(1)$ used above confines completely. The five-flavour $SU(3)$ electric theory in dualsm instead has magnetic group $SU(2)$ with a two-dimensional fundamental and a one-mass branch with a surviving photon; its weak-vector candidate must still couple to the measured quark and lepton currents. The separate symplectic weak dual provides an additional comparison of thresholds and meson representations. These are distinct physical uses of dual groups.
