# The full K3 charge of a wrapped electric string

Fix the K3 lattice, the unit weak flux $h$ of 88 and the two ADE root fields $E_{65},E_{75}$. Vary the hyperkähler periods while keeping the M2 and M5 charge units fixed. We ask whether the actual wrapped M5 can generate the two-root surface coupling and whether its tension action is large in the same normalization that gives the weak and strong couplings. The comparison retains all K3 form charges and uses the leading unwarped reduction.

**Proposition 91.1.** In the full harmonic K3 theory the two ADE root fields alone do not saturate the charge of a unit electric surface of the two-form from the wrapped M5. In the orientation of 87.3 the additional membrane charge class is
$$
 \delta=h-r_{65}-r_{75}=e+4f+4\mathbf e_5 .
$$
It is neutral under the chosen vortex, hypercharge and colour, and has square $-8$. At full $A_6$ enhancement, any collection of additional wrapped membranes with net class $\delta$ has a bare total tension mass of at least
$$
 \frac{M_4}{\sqrt{2 I_3}}\sqrt{\frac{40}{7}} .
$$
This is a charge and calibration statement about the stated sources. A wall, a charged condensate or an altered global lattice could absorb the same charge by a different operator.

**Proof.** An M5 wrapped on K3 is the solitonic heterotic string in the fluxless K3 comparison. On the product six-cycle with the weak sphere its worldvolume Bianchi equation requires the membrane boundary class $h$ for the flux of 88. Pairing with just the vortex, hypercharge and colour tests only part of this condition. The two membrane roots have sum $r_{65}+r_{75}=\mathbf e_6+\mathbf e_7-2\mathbf e_5$. Subtracting this sum gives $\delta$ with the stated pairings. In a theory keeping all form gauge transformations, the unimodular K3 pairing detects this nonzero class. More generally if all available charged fields come from a negative-definite ADE lattice, a sum of them cannot equal the positive-square class $h$ used here.

At full enhancement the span of the ADE roots is orthogonal to the positive three-plane. The projection of $\delta$ to the complementary space is the same as for $h$, with square $40/7$ as in 88.1. Its positive projection has squared norm at least this value. Every membrane volume is bounded below by the norm of its three Kähler periods, including for a non-calibrated representative. The triangle inequality then gives the same lower bound for a collection with the specified net class. Converting the tension via $T_2\sqrt{2V_X}=M_4/\sqrt{2I_3}$ gives the displayed result. $\square$ [Wrapped K3 heterotic string](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9703062) · [Membrane boundary condition on the M5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.06999)

There are lattice carriers of the missing charge. In a second hyperbolic plane take $e_2,f_2$ with $e_2\cdot f_2=1$ and $e_2^2=f_2^2=0$. The two classes $4f+2\mathbf e_5+e_2+f_2$ and $e+2\mathbf e_5-e_2-f_2$ each have square $-2$ and the same three retained neutrality tests as $\delta$. Their sum is $\delta$ and their mutual pairing is $-2$. Their difference is a nonzero null class. Hence both cannot be collapsed in the negative-definite orthogonal complement of a positive three-plane. The calculation identifies charges available in the lattice. An actual vacuum expectation value of a corresponding field needs a mechanism compatible with its geometry and the full gauge action.

**Proposition 91.2.** Suppose the weak rotations are gauged by the stated positive product Einstein and monopole kinetic terms, retaining the Cartan compensation of 88. At a round weak sphere the classical tension action $S_5$ of the wrapped electric M5 obeys
$$
 S_5=\frac{(M_4r)^2}{2 I_3}
 \ \leq\ \frac32\left(\frac{I_W}{I_3}-\frac{J(h,h)}6\right).
$$
At full $A_6$ collapse and with the illustrative tree identification $I_W=2.35$, $I_3=0.896$, the upper bound is about $1.58$.

**Proof.** The volume formula is $S_5=T_5 V_X(4\pi r^2)$, and $T_2^2=2\pi T_5$. Eliminating $T_2^2 V_X$ using the colour and Einstein coefficients of 88.2 gives the equality. The weak inverse coupling has positive Einstein part $(M_4r)^2/3$ and the monopole norm contributes $I_3J(h,h)/6$. The form-field contributions in the stipulated action are nonnegative. This gives the bound. Use $J(h,h)\ge66/7$ at $A_6$ collapse for the numerical comparison. $\square$
