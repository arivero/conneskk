# Two charged poles on a monopole doublet and the magnetic-current test

Fix the helical geometry and the absolute colour and photon matching of [result 59](59_shared_circle_and_two_scales.md). Approach its round member at fixed microscopic Einstein coefficient, meridian length and winding. We compute the electric charges and the transverse weak response of specified smooth fermion sources. The vector action is the divergence-free Einstein quadratic form of result 57. For the probe we specify minimal coupling to the lifted internal generators; tensor vertices and independent bundle kinetic terms are additional interactions. The one-mass branch of the five-flavour colour dual provides a separate current to test against this calculation. It has different microscopic fields.

## Spinorial domain and charge normalization

Use the canonical spin$^c$ module on $\mathbb{CP}^2$, twisted by $\mathcal O(-4)$ for quarks and the trivial line for leptons. On the weak sphere use spinors twisted by the $N$th power of the magnetic line of degree one in result 55, with its rotation-invariant connection at the round member. Take $N=2$ for a doublet and $N=1$ for a singlet. On the circle choose periodic spin structure and $k$ a positive multiple of six. For each species fix a flat coefficient connection $d-i n_s d\chi$ with $n_s=kY_s$, and lift circle translations to act by ordinary pullback in this periodic trivialization. The lift is part of the physical specification of the charge. The flat holonomy alone cannot determine it. Equivalently, gauge away the integer connection and transform the lift with it. In the horizontal probe action transport the coefficient connection with the KK connection, so that the horizontal derivative for a circle shift couples to the translation generator. Holding its coordinate components fixed in a metric variation would define a different current. For the continuation choose the fixed topological round monopole connection $A_0$ on the pullback from the sphere. Write the north-axis vortex connection as $A_0+u_b$ in a common bundle identification. The difference $u_b$ is a global one-form, tending to zero at dissolution in the rotation-equivariant polar gauges of result 55. On each slice rotate this difference with the axis, with zero $d\chi$ component in the fixed product coordinates, and add it to $A_0$. This gives a smooth screw-invariant connection with the round *product* limit. The full pullback by $(\chi,y)\mapsto R_3(-k\chi)y$ would also transport $A_0$ and introduce a mixed curvature proportional to $d\chi\wedge\iota_{K_3}F_0$ even at dissolution. We use the product-limit prescription for the charged-current comparison. Add conjugate sectors with their conjugate connections. These are elliptic spin$^c$ operators on the closed product with Sobolev $H^1$ domain.

**Proof.** On the round product the squared Dirac operator is the sum of the projective and weak twisted Dirac squares and $(-i\partial_\chi-n_s)^2/L^2$. The projective kernel is degree zero for the trivial coefficient and degree two for $\mathcal O(-4)$, where Serre duality gives the defining colour triplet. On the weak sphere the positive kernel consists of the holomorphic sections of $\mathcal O(N-1)$. These give the rotation doublet and singlet respectively. All other circle modes are separated by at least $1/L$, and the first positive tangential gaps on both compact factors are positive. The lift gives $Q=T_3+n_s/k$ in the window. In a smooth family near the round member the combined spectral projection inside a smaller fixed gap varies continuously. Colour and the lifted helical $Q$ commute with each operator. ## The transverse current at the upper endpoint

Let $r=d/\pi$ be the round weak radius, and use the compact symmetric metric of result 59. For each doublet form the unit left-handed transition from its lower to its upper weight, with $T_+=(\sigma_1+i\sigma_2)/2$. Use canonical spinor norm, project quark sources onto their colour singlet, and use the rotation lift preserving the monopole. For $X=f(y)\partial_\chi$ take the natural spin lift along the circle, transporting the flat coefficient as above. We measure the coefficient of the minimally coupled spacetime vector current. Our $V-A$ convention assigns $g_2^2/8$ to the charged residue of a normalized pure weak pole.

**Theorem 60.2 (the complete round colour-singlet exchange for these probes).** Put $I_W=CV\,2r^2/3$ and $I_D=CV\,L^2/3$. For $t=p_E^2>0$ the coefficient between doublet sources $s,s'$ is
$$
 C_{ss'}(t)=\frac{1}{8 I_W t}
       +\frac{4 n_s n_{s'}}{9}\frac{1}{8 I_D(t+2/r^2)} .
$$
In particular at the round canonical relative normalization, the ratio of the massive residue to the massless one is $1/5$ for two lepton sources, $1/45$ for two quark sources and $-1/15$ for a quark and a lepton. Singlet records have zero intra-singlet charged matrix element. This calculation also applies to the left-current component of a vectorlike Dirac probe. In the full vectorlike theory its mirror doublet has a weak coupling.

**Proof.** The two members of a doublet have the same circle mode. Translation invariance forces a contributing gauge vector to have zero circle frequency. Colour-singlet projection leaves no projective vector component, since the projective stabilizer fixes no tangent vector. On the sphere the bilinear of two zero modes transforms as $\mathbf2^*\otimes\mathbf2=\mathbf1+\mathbf3$. The raising transition is a triplet component. In the zero-frequency solenoidal vector sector the complete triplets are the three spherical Killing fields $K_i$ and the circle fields $D_i=x_i\partial_\chi$, where $x_i$ are the three unit-sphere coordinates. Indeed the poloidal degree-one fields have nonzero divergence, with no circle derivative in this sector to compensate it. Higher angular momenta and scalar singlets have zero matrix elements by equivariance.

The $K_i$ act by $T_i=\sigma_i/2$ and have mass zero and kinetic norm $I_W$. Each $D_i$ has squared mass $2/r^2$ by the product Killing-field calculation in result 59. The mean of $x_i x_j$ is $\delta_{ij}/3$, giving norm $I_D$. In the spinorial derivative of $D_i$, the mixed Clifford term from $dx_i\wedge d\chi$ takes one weak chirality to the other and has zero compression. The surviving term is $n_s P x_i P$. The rank-one evaluation density on $H^0(\mathcal O(1))$ is $(1\pm x\cdot\sigma)$ in unit area-average measure. The sign depends on the common choice of dual representation and orientation. Integration gives $P x_i P=\pm\sigma_i/3$ for both species with the same twist. Consequently the two triplets have relative unnormalized vertices $\pm2n_s/3$. Their kinetic inner product vanishes by product orthogonality. Inserting the two canonical propagators proves the formula. With $L^2/k^2=10r^2/9$ and $n_\ell=-k/2$, $n_q=k/6$, the three residue ratios follow. $\square$

This test has kept a charge that also acts on the colour-side circle. The second pole is the first angular harmonic of that circle's metric vector. A pure weak rotation would miss it. For example the difference of the two normalized lepton and quark sources cancels the massless pole exactly and leaves a positive massive residue proportional to $(n_\ell-n_q)^2$. The ratio tests the assumed geometric lift and kinetic action. A new kinetic term or a different tensor current needs its own normalization.

**Corollary 60.3 (near-round broken members).** Hold $0<\beta<\pi/2$ fixed and let $\epsilon=e^{-b}$ tend to zero on the absolutely matched path. On its isolated transported doublets use a smooth family of the above vector-current vertices, with the equivariant spinorial derivative and a smooth extension of the monopole lift. The lightest charged geometric pole has $M_C^2=c_C\epsilon^2+O(\epsilon^3)$ for a positive $c_C$ in product-frame units. Its residue tends to $1/(8I_W)$ on each of the displayed pairs. After subtracting the *actual* light pole and residue the contact at $t=0$ tends to $r^2(4n_s n_{s'}/9)/(16 I_D)$. The tree unit-source Fermi coefficient consequently grows as $\sqrt2/(8I_W M_C^2)$ toward the compact symmetric endpoint.

**Proof.** At finite compact radii the metric, twisted Dirac graph domains after unitary identification, and smooth sources vary smoothly near the round member. For an analytic mass expansion use the analytic vortex and radius family of result 57 and Proposition 59.2. In the full solenoidal form there is one round charge-raising Killing vector. The complement in that charge sector is separated by a positive gap. The effective second-order form in result 57 is strictly positive on either transverse generator, using its positive weights and the degree-two metric deformation. A round rescaling contributes no Killing defect. Smooth projections give convergence of the coupling to this canonical eigenvector. In the orthogonal complement the inverse at zero converges on the smooth sources by the elliptic form estimate and the uniform gap. A derivative of a test vector field in the spinorial vertex can be integrated by parts on the closed internal space, giving a smooth $L^2$ source. Theorem 60.2 evaluates this remaining matrix element at the round member. $\square$

## What would identify a magnetic weak current?

Use the reference two-derivative action of the one-mass branch of five-flavour supersymmetric colour duality. Its diagonal photon has $e^{-2}=g_m^{-2}+g_Y^{-2}$. The pole positions are $M_C^2=g_m^2f^2$, $M_N^2=(g_m^2+g_Y^2)f^2$. In this ansatz, $s_p^2=1-M_C^2/M_N^2$ and a source with the ordinary unit left-doublet vertex would have charged residue $e^2/(8s_p^2)$. The magnetic colour dual itself supplies no observed chiral quark doublet with this vertex. Its exactly massless infrared charged branch screens the Thomson residue. Comparing with geometric tree matching requires a common scale or a massive threshold completion. **Proposition 60.4 (two spacelike measurements).** Let a normalized equal-source transverse exchange be a convergent positive spectral sum $C(t)=\sum_j r_j/(t+m_j^2)$, with $r_j\geq0$, lightest supported mass $M_C$, and no independent source contact. For $t_2>t_1>0$,
$$
 (t_2+M_C^2)C(t_2)-(t_1+M_C^2)C(t_1)
 =(t_2-t_1)\sum_j\frac{r_j(m_j^2-M_C^2)}
 {(t_1+m_j^2)(t_2+m_j^2)} .
$$
The left side vanishes exactly for spectral support at the one mass. Equality to $e^2/(8s_p^2)$ for the normalized combination at both momenta also fixes its residue to the magnetic reference value whenever $0<s_p^2<1$. Tensor vertices, unfixed local source counterterms and momentum-dependent vacuum polarization change the ansatz. The identity follows by subtracting the two fractions mode by mode. In Theorem 60.2 either charged equal-source correlator with $n_s\ne0$ has a strictly positive difference with $M_C=0$. The mixed quark–lepton spectral weights need not be positive. A possible magnetic description of both sources must account for this extra pole or its low-energy matching contribution and the opposite signs of its vertices.
