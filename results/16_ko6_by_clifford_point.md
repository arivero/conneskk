# The product of the truncated coarse triple with the Clifford point (C, C^2, 0, sigma_1 K, sigma_3) has the KO-6 signs, order zero, order one and dim H = 2 k^2, and fails finite orientability

**Status.** Corollary (Corollary 16.4). Proved by Claude Opus 5.5. Ledger row `l16_truncation`, shared with Corollary 16.3. Item 3 of Proposition H of paper/towerless_intervals.tex.

**Setting.** The truncation T_F = (A_F, V (x) conj(V), D_F, J_F, Gamma_F) of Corollary 16.3 (16_coarse_correspondence_order_one.md), of dimension k^2, and the Clifford point (C, C^2, 0, sigma_1 K, sigma_3).

**Statement.**

1. The product (A_F, V (x) conj(V) (x) C^2, D_F (x) 1, J_F (x) sigma_1 K, Gamma_F (x) sigma_3) has signs (+, +, -), order zero and order one, and dim H = 2 k^2.
2. It fails the usual zero-dimensional orientability axiom: every zero-cycle built from the left and opposite actions acts as T (x) 1_2, while the grading is Gamma_F (x) sigma_3.
3. The pair (A_F, J) is reducible: the projection 1 (x) (1 + sigma_1)/2 commutes with A_F and J. With centre C, J gamma = -gamma J is impossible in the Chamseddine-Connes classification.

*Scope.* The Clifford point is an input, as the finite KO-6 factor F_0 is in Theorem D of the main paper. For k = 4, dim H = 32 is the count of the Chamseddine-Connes classification; in examples (b) and (c) this is a count and nothing more, since the classification's algebra M_2(H) + M_4(C) acts differently.

**Proof.** Lemma 16, Corollary 16.4; the proof is there: (sigma_1 K)^2 = 1 and sigma_1 K sigma_3 = -sigma_3 sigma_1 K, the D term is untouched, and a° is that of T_F tensored with 1.

**What it leaves open.** The irreducible form of the classification comes from a parent with two sheets (16_two_sheet_parent.md); a grading-reversing structure as in lemma 08 is a candidate for the KO-6 sign that lemma 16 has not tested (Caveat 4).
