# The nonround Hopf colour gap and its first bosonic level

Fix the round Hopf colour factor, magnetic five-form, weak monopole, weak tensor coefficients and horizontal charge lift of 94 and 97. We vary the weak metric along the finite meridian and the fixed-shape shrink. The observable is the first positive squared mass among the additional coloured bosons. We specify a fibration action with quotient Higgs fields and a constrained weak Maxwell field. Its colour stabilization uses the intrinsic squares of 98 in the physical colour metric, without the alternative weak-area rescaling used there for a frozen round reduction.

## The nonround constraint split with a magnetic top form

**Proposition 99.1.** For the covariant metric, five-form, dilaton and weak vortex core of the product, the physical colour and weak mass forms add in light-cone gauge. The colour term is the one-factor $S^5$ problem with six flat longitudinal dimensions, evaluated at each weak position. The weak term is the physical weak vortex form of 69.1 with the five colour directions among its spectators. Adding
$$
 \frac{\eta}{2}Z(\sigma)B_0^2e^{2\sigma}|\nabla_5(*_5F_5)|^2
 +\varrho B_0^2e^{2\sigma}|\operatorname{Ric}^{\rm TF}(g_5)|^2
$$
as intrinsic spatial energy on the physical colour leaves preserves this split in the coloured quadratic problem. With $\eta=5/2,\ \varrho=3/10$ and unit colour dilaton kinetic coefficient, every nonconstant mode of the active one-factor scalar-derived colour block has squared mass at least $8/B^2$.

**Proof of the split.** The two independent magnetic systems each supply a vacuum with flat directions tangent to the other system. In particular their potential densities including the corresponding curvature vanish at the background. Use $h_{-M}=0$ and set gauge-potential components with a minus index to zero. The minus-minus Einstein equation gives zero trace on the total transverse space. Write $V_I=\nabla^M h_{MI}$ there. The minus-transverse equations put $V_I=J_{c,I}+J_{w,I}$, with $J_c$ supported on the colour directions and $J_w$ on the weak directions. In the top-form convention of 98 the former is $Z\langle\iota_I F_5,c_4\rangle$ with the all-colour component of the potential variation. The latter has the Maxwell and vortex currents of 69.1. The squared constraint sources have no mutual term since their indices are disjoint.

For colour $i$ and weak $a$ the mixed derivative in $2\delta R_{ia}$ is $\nabla_a V_i+\nabla_i V_a$ in addition to the product Lichnerowicz term. The differentiated top-form stress has $Z\langle\iota_i F_5,\iota_a d c_4\rangle$. In its all-colour four-form component
$$
 (\iota_a d c_4)_{C^4}
       =\nabla_a(c_4)_{C^4}-d_C(\iota_a c_4)_{C^4}.
$$
The first term is exactly $\nabla_a J_{c,i}$ and cancels the weak derivative of that constraint current. The remaining term is the intrinsic colour coupling to the form with one spectator index. Equivalently the same cross term in the mass form is removed by integration by parts against the weak derivative of the mixed metric. The Maxwell and Higgs derivative in the other direction cancels $\nabla_i J_{w,a}$ as in 69.1. In the weak Maxwell Gauss square the term pairing $\operatorname{div}_C a$ with the vortex fluctuation cancels the colour integration by parts of $|\partial_C\delta\Phi-i a_C\Phi|^2$. This fixes the possibly mixed source from the nonconstant vortex.

The top-form Gauss equation has no background metric vertex with an upper plus index because $\delta g^{+N}=0$ in this gauge. The free form derivatives after eliminating its null components combine into the product Hodge form. In the remaining variations of $\operatorname{div}(ZF_5)$ the differentiated metric terms carry a contracted colour derivative from the background $F_5$, or the divergence $V_I$ along colour. In $V_I$ only $J_c$ contributes to that contraction. The two dilatons have product scalar Laplacians and couple only to their own kinetic flux coefficients and scalar potentials. The algebraic curvature and stress terms split by the product and the vanishing longitudinal background stresses. This leaves exactly the two one-factor forms, with the tangent directions of the other factor treated as spectators in the respective metric and form components. Each term is evaluated before adding the other. In particular a colour form with a weak one-form spectator has the ordinary weak Hodge energy. This calculation is for the covariant core; independent fibration fields will be specified below.

An infinitesimal coordinate variation at the product changes the pullback of the colour metric and the all-colour flux on a leaf only by their colour Lie derivatives. Their intrinsic trace-free Ricci and flux-gradient residuals vanish there. Therefore the new squares annihilate the linear coordinate variations, also when those have arbitrary weak dependence. They have no null shift term and do not change the linear constraint elimination. The conformally adjusted squares of 98 instead involve the local weak magnetic density, whose variation need not vanish on a weak-coordinate variation at a nonround point. **Proof of the colour estimate.** Write $\lambda=4/B^2$, $z=\nu/\lambda$ for a colour scalar harmonic of eigenvalue $\nu$. To retain the two weak directions as longitudinal spectators in the one-factor problem there are now $n=5$ external spatial conformal coefficients. Use the notation of 98.2 with equal external exponent $A$, colour exponent $H$, flux coefficient $t$, dilaton $S$, and $D=t-5H$. The Hamiltonian constraint is $nA+4H=t/z$. In the static energy the external spatial derivative term is $-z\,n(n-1)A^2$ and the cross with $H$ is $-8znAH$. The other change from 98 is the total density weight $3A\mapsto nA$. The kinetic before this constraint is $n\dot A^2+5\dot H^2-(n\dot A+5\dot H)^2+\dot t^2/z+c\dot S^2$. The shift cancels as in 98.2. With $0<c\le1$ the resulting quadratic forms for $n=5$ are
$$
 \begin{aligned}

 (1+6/(5z)+4\eta z)D^2+16DS+(40+cz)S^2,\\
 A_z&=\frac{(4z-5)(9z-30)}{5z}
            +36\varrho z(4z/5-1),\qquad

        \frac{8(4z-5)}{5z^2}\dot H\dot D+
        \frac{5z-4}{5z^2}\dot D^2+c\dot S^2 .
 \end{aligned}
$$
For $j\ge2$ one has $z\ge3$. At the chosen coefficients, $A_z\ge43.96$, $|B_z|\le9.6$ and the diagonal $D^2$ entry is at least $31$. ## A quotient Higgs action retaining the broken Killing pair

**Proposition 99.2.** There is a fixed projected bosonic action, with the unadjusted stabilizers above, whose first positive coloured boson at a finite weak shape has squared mass
$$
 \min\!\left\{\mu,\quad
  \frac{8+s/2-\sqrt{64+s^2/4}}{B^2},\quad
  \frac{15-\sqrt{120}}{B^2}\right\},\qquad
 \mu=\lambda_1(\Sigma_{\rm phys}),\quad s=\frac{f_S^2B^2}{I_3}.
$$
Here the zero coloured modes are the gluons. The three entries are respectively a nonconstant weak scalar excitation of the gluon, the broken-Killing vector with its form partner and a $j=2$ colour vector with its form partner. The projection specified in the proof is part of the action. The conclusion does not assert the first level for the unrestricted eleven-dimensional weak Maxwell field coupled to the full volume tensor.

**Proof.** Write $Y=M_4\times\Sigma$ for the six-dimensional quotient by colour leaves of the marked fibration. Project the metric shifts to the round $\mathrm{SO}(6)$ Killing algebra with its fixed invariant fibre measure. Local fibre rotations give a principal connection since the inhomogeneous gauge term lies entirely in that algebra. Let the charged weak-neutral oscillator $S$ have its fixed length, valued in the orbit of the ambient volume tensor under $\mathrm{Spin}(6)\times U(1)_C$, with the centre identified as in 94 for the fermion lifts. Use the positive orbit kinetic term on $Y$, including its weak directions. No extra coloured amplitude modes are carried by this field. The ray of $S$ specifies the ambient complex structure. In a frame adapted to that ray, use the $\mathfrak u(3)$ projection of the rotational connection and the independent auxiliary connection. In particular the Hopf connection $A_P$ and the auxiliary connection $A_C$ are separate slots, with the phase of $S$ supplying the stiffness of $3A_P-2A_C$. This gives the Abelian phases and finite relative polarization of 97. The added root fields live as colour-independent coefficients in the rotated volume lines. Their weak internal derivatives remain the holomorphic and product squares. Their external derivative projected to the volume line gives the same weak and Hopf currents and no mass for the rotations perpendicular to the line. The auxiliary Maxwell field is basic on $Y$. The two-period tensor of 92 uses the projected weak metric and vortex field and the residual colour characteristic form as in 95. At zero projected colour curvature its Hessian has no linear coloured source.

For definiteness remove the component of the original weak Abelian connection tangent to the colour leaf along the round co-closed Killing one-forms. Its projection is invariant under Abelian gradients, and the first-order colour variation of the background pullback connection under a coordinate change is an exact colour derivative. The restriction therefore removes a physical polarization rather than a compensating gauge parameter. Retain the remaining weak covariant vortex fields of 62. In particular the weak quadratic comparison of 69 applies on the complementary colour sectors. On the background the weak fields remain pullbacks and the orbit field is covariantly constant. The added first variations vanish as in 94. Hence this is one action with the same product solutions, auxiliary norm and sourced weak pencil as 97. This field prescription supplies a quadratic bosonic result. Nonlinear gauge and anomaly matching with the chiral fermion domain is a separate construction.

To compute the omitted levels, first recall the ordinary $\mathrm{SO}(6)$ harmonics of a colour leaf. The adjoint has no scalar or symmetric colour two-tensor, since its restriction to the $\mathrm{SO}(5)$ isotropy is $\mathbf{10}+\mathbf5$. It has one copy in each of the degrees one to four of the colour form space. The exact and coexact pairs are generated by $K\mapsto dK^\flat$ and $*_5dK^\flat\mapsto *_5K^\flat$. The removed weak Maxwell polarization is precisely the extra colour one-form in this adjoint; the other weak fields have no adjoint scalar harmonics. Thus in the adjoint the only coupled gauge block is the metric vector with its five-form partner. The first form pair is a free six-dimensional massive field with internal eigenvalue $8/B^2$. For the coupled pair use the full six-dimensional vector connection on $Y$. Its invariant five-form and Einstein reductions give two equal Maxwell kinetic terms, with the gauge-invariant square of their difference of coefficient $8/B^2$ in normalized units. The orbit field supplies standard Stückelberg gauge variables of the broken generators. In unitary gauge it gives the positive mass $f_S^2/I_3$ on the metric vector in each broken direction and zero in the unbroken Killing sector. No weak Abelian polarization in this adjoint remains coupled. Both vectors are then diagonalized by the same constant matrix at every weak point. The positive massive modes are Proca fields on $Y$, so their four-dimensional masses add the weak Hodge eigenvalues. $H^1(\Sigma)=0$ excludes a zero internal vector. The zero unbroken colour vectors are the gluons, with first extra mass squared $\mu$. The broken pair is the matrix of 94.5, whose smaller eigenvalue is the second entry in the proposition.

## Ordering on the matched path

For the central compact comparison of 97 the meridian is $d=0.00049673$ GeV$^{-1}$ and $B^{-1}=5.496$ TeV. The matched neck at $b=.001$ has $P_b=18.6396$ and $B^{-1}=4.704$ TeV. At both points the projected first boson is the broken-Killing pole, near $3.961$ and $3.936$ TeV respectively. The exact competing $j=2$ masses are $11.054$ and $9.461$ TeV. This ordering holds over the finite leg. Indeed $\mu\ge\pi^2/d^2$ from nonnegative Gaussian curvature and the meridian diameter, whereas the two equal metric/form profiles give the trial upper bound $m_-^2\le f_S^2/(2I_3)<\pi^2/d^2$. To bound $B$ on this leg note that $D_b/P_b\ge4/(\pi\sqrt2)$. In the $v$ variable of 93, $D_b/P_b$ is the mean of $\sqrt{2q}$ with weight $\sqrt{q/(1-q)}$. As $\epsilon$ increases the integrand increases, and the logarithmic derivative of that weight is $(1-e^{-v})/[2(1-q)]$. It decreases with $v$ since $q(x)=x e^{\epsilon(1-x)}$ is concave in $x=e^{-v}$. The mean therefore increases as well, by the positive covariance of two decreasing functions. At $\epsilon=0$ the mean is $4/(\pi\sqrt2)$. The same integrand is bounded above by $\sqrt2$. Since $B^7D_b/P_b^2$ is fixed and $P_\star\le P_b\le18.64$, these bounds give $0.93<(2/\pi)^{1/7}\le B/B_\star<1.32$ on the finite leg. The $j=2$ level stays above $m_-$ there.

On subsequent uniform shrink of the weak sphere $\mu$ grows, whereas $B$ grows as $a^{-2/7}$. Put $\gamma_0=15-\sqrt{120}$. The two colour levels cross once at
$$
 s_*=\frac{\gamma_0(16-\gamma_0)}{8-\gamma_0}=12.22984\ldots .
$$
For $f_S=5000$ GeV and $I_3=0.76985$ this is $B^{-1}=1.630$ TeV, or $a\simeq6.52\times10^{-7}$ GeV$^{-1}$ for the fixed-shape continuation from the stated neck. Thus the first extra boson is exactly the $j=2$ shell at sufficiently small $a$. This changes the colour cutoff to $\sqrt{\gamma_0}/B$ relative to the round Killing comparison. It is a tree result for the specified spin fibration action. Fermionic thresholds and the renormalized strong scale need their own matching.
