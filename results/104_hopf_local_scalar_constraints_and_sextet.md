## The temporal constraints of the coloured problem

**Proposition 104.1.** At a finite weak member of 103 the coloured spin-zero quadratic problem has nonnegative static energy and nonnegative reduced kinetic form. This uses the local augmented derivative, the unadjusted stabilizers of 99 and the specified quotient tensor. In particular a propagating coloured scalar with positive kinetic norm has nonnegative squared mass. The conclusion gives no uniform massive gap on the shrink.

**Proof.** To obtain the local Hessian, start with the ordinary Maxwell action for $C$, the real Frobenius kinetic term $Z_M|DM|^2/2$ and the potential $W$ of 102.1. On the product $DM_0=0$ and the background auxiliary curvature vanishes, $W(M_0)=dW(M_0)=0$. Hence derivatives of the measure, inverse metric and $Z_M$ multiply a vanishing background residual in this part of the quadratic action. In a product orthonormal frame let $S=\delta M$, $c_A=\delta C_A$ and $L_A=\delta\nabla^E_A$. At a fixed sign convention for the Abelian connection each static slot has energy
$$
 \frac{Z_M}{2}\left|\partial_A S+\rho(L_A)M_0-2i c_A M_0\right|^2 ,
$$
and the time slot has the same positive coefficient in the kinetic form. The Maxwell part is the ordinary electric and magnetic norm of $dc$ with coefficient $Z_C/2$. In a horizontal slot with colour shift $X$, varying the horizontal bracket and the metric-compatible frame gives $L_A=n^\flat\wedge X^\flat/B+d_C X^\flat/2$ in physical units with the normal orientation of 103. For $X=\nabla_C\xi$, the second term vanishes. Differentiating the dressed quotient mean of 102.2 gives the orbit projection of $\langle S\rangle_0$, since the gauge dressing has zero mean at first order. These calculations give the metric and matter entries of the extra Hessian prior to constraint elimination.

Use time-symmetric initial data in the four-dimensional rest frame. The derivative and potential of the volume tensor have zero background residual, as does the auxiliary curvature. Their spatial energy is a sum of nonnegative quadratic terms. Their first variation vanishes pointwise, so the linear Hamiltonian constraint is the old covariant core constraint. Momentum and electric constraints hold at zero canonical momenta. Before the local volume term, the covariant core coloured mass form is the sum of its colour and weak forms in 99.1, with nonnegative weak vortex contribution. This assertion concerns the core before any orbit stiffness is added. In the Killing colour scalar sector one can also set $m_A=0$ in 101.1 to see the nonnegative core directly. The intrinsic spatial terms of 99 are included. The independent auxiliary Maxwell spatial energy is nonnegative. The quotient root and two-period terms have no linear colour-nonsinglet source from their averaged fields. Old coordinate directions in the metric and matter fields remain null for the covariant core. If such directions enter the local spatial squares, imposing their auxiliary equations takes an infimum of a nonnegative form. This proves the static assertion on the common constraint surface.

Here is the sign check for the temporal elimination. On the time slice let $N$ be the shift, viewed as a one-form. With velocities set to zero the Einstein contribution in the common action normalization is
$$
 \|\operatorname{sym}\nabla N\|^2-\|\operatorname{div}N\|^2
     =\tfrac12\|dN\|^2-\int\operatorname{Ric}(N,N).
$$
This follows by integration by parts and the one-form Bochner identity. Decompose into the two curved factors and external spatial directions. The separate pure colour and weak terms are as below. The remaining mixed components of $dN$ add nonnegative squares. The external three-space is flat, and the rest-frame calculation has no external spatial derivatives.

In the colour term let $N_C=d_C f+N_C^\perp$ be the fibrewise Hodge decomposition, with $N_C^\perp$ coexact. Write $\lambda_C=4/B^2$. Weak derivatives of its coefficient do not enter the full codifferential because all its indices are colour indices. It is orthogonal both to $d b_0$ and to $\iota_{N_C^\perp}F_5$. Thus the electric term is at least $\lambda_C\|d_C f\|^2$. This cancels the negative Ricci coefficient on the exact shift. The remaining colour shift contribution is bounded by

since the first coexact one-form level is $2\lambda_C$ on the round sphere.

The background's electric and scalar shift weights satisfy $(Z/2)|\iota_XF|^2+G|D_X\Phi|^2=K|X|^2$, with the notation of 69.1. ## The vertical orientation bound and exact shifts

**Proposition 104.2.** On each round colour leaf put the linear metric variation $h$ in divergence-free gauge. The part of the nonconstant volume orientation orthogonal to both the phase and the gradient-generated orientations has vertical derivative energy at least $5/B^2$ times its own kinetic coefficient and squared norm. The result is a bound on one component of the spatial form. The exact external-coordinate shifts in the local action impose a corresponding projection of the horizontal derivative.

**Proof.** Work first on the unit leaf with the norm and flat ambient frame of 103. Write $\tau=\operatorname{tr}h$, $u=\delta(B_\sigma^{-1})$ in unit-radius variables, and let $\mathcal C_i$ be the linear variation of the augmented vertical connection in the symmetric orthonormal frame. Its normal rotation is $(u e_i+\tfrac12 h_i)\wedge n$, where $h_i=h(e_i,\cdot)^\sharp$. The tangential skew slots are
$$
 \langle\mathcal C_i e_j,e_k\rangle
          =\tfrac12(\nabla_j h_{ik}-\nabla_k h_{ij}).
$$
Indeed varying $g(\nabla_i e_j,e_k)$ by the Koszul formula gives half the sum $\nabla_i h_{jk}+\nabla_j h_{ik}-\nabla_k h_{ij}$ before orthonormalizing the frame. The frame variation $\delta e_j=-h_j{}^k e_k/2$ cancels the first derivative in this sum. Varying $B_\sigma^{-1} X$ in the normal-to-tangent slot of 103 contributes $u e_i+h_i/2$ in the moving frame. In taking the divergence, the ambient derivative differentiates the basis of endomorphisms as well as their coefficients. Ordinary tangential divergence of the tangential slots gives $\tfrac12 d(\operatorname{div}h)$, since the round curvature commutators cancel in their antisymmetrization. The normal divergence of the first term is $(du+\tfrac12\operatorname{div}h)\wedge n$. The additional commutator with $e_i\wedge n$ subtracts $\tfrac12(\operatorname{div}h-d\tau)\wedge n$ from it. The tangential commutators of the normal rotations vanish in the sum by symmetry of $h$. Thus in divergence-free gauge the entire divergence is
$$
                 \nabla^{E,i}\mathcal C_i=d(u+\tau/2)^\sharp\wedge n .
$$
Here $d(\cdot)^\sharp$ denotes the gradient. The gauge exists by solving the elliptic divergence equation for the symmetric gradient modulo Killing fields. The local augmented derivative is natural under colour diffeomorphisms. At the background the mean of their skew action on $M_0$ agrees with the Killing projection in the quotient couplings. This follows by rotation equivariance: the round Killing representation occurs once in vector fields, and on it the skew action is the ambient generator. Hence the non-Killing part of this gauge has no additional quotient variation in the coloured linear problem.

Write $s$ for the matrix variation. Let $\mathcal G$ be the image in fibre $L^2$ of $f\mapsto \rho(\nabla f\wedge n)M_0$. Let $\mathcal G_-$ be its projection perpendicular to the pointwise phase line $i\mathbb R M_0$ within the orbit tangent space. These ranges and their orthogonal complements are invariant under the flat ambient component Laplacian. In fact if $f$ is a scalar harmonic of degree $\ell$ then $\nabla_{S^5}f\wedge n$ is the restriction of $\nabla_{\mathbb R^6}f_{\ell}\wedge x$, with $f_\ell$ the homogeneous harmonic extension. It is a homogeneous harmonic of degree $\ell$ because the extra second derivative term is the antisymmetrization of the Hessian of $f_\ell$. Taking the representation action on the constant $M_0$ or projecting off its phase preserves the scalar degree. The ranges can equivalently be taken with their $L^2$ closures.

Let $s_\star$ be the projection of the nonconstant orientation onto the complement of $\mathcal G_-$ and the phase. In the perpendicular vertical derivative the auxiliary $C_V$ has no contribution. The integral pairing of $d_Es_\star$ with $\rho(\mathcal C)M_0$ is zero by the divergence formula. Its pairing with the gradient-image part is zero by Laplacian invariance. The normal amplitudes are pointwise orthogonal to the orbit tangents. Thus the squared norm of the vertical derivative is at least $\|d_Es_\star\|^2$, which is at least $5\|s_\star\|^2$ on the unit sphere. Restoring the physical metric gives the squared scale $5/B^2$.

For the external derivative use the nonconstant pure external-coordinate variation of 103, now with weak-dependent parameter allowed. Its old covariant energy is zero including the metric changes in the other spectator slots. It leaves the colour metric variation unchanged. The projected quotient fields see zero fibre mean in the colour-nonsinglet comparison. The local volume term in a four-dimensional slot changes by $\rho(\nabla_C\xi_\mu\wedge n)M_0$ in unit angular variables, up to the sign convention for the shift. Therefore eliminating this algebraic slot projects the new derivative in that slot to $\mathcal G^\perp$, including the phase in the definition of the full $\mathcal G$. The comparison of the resulting phase, metric and auxiliary Maxwell kinetic forms is still needed for a quantitative bound on the complete scalar mass. $\square$ [Exact external-coordinate shifts](103_hopf_local_connection_transverse_shell.md) · [Sphere eigenforms](https://doi.org/10.18910/6956)

## The exact normal scalar tower

**Proposition 104.3.** In the locally coupled action the normal sextet and radial amplitudes of 102 have exact product spectra. In particular the lowest sextet is a physical spin-zero particle of charge $-2/3$ and squared mass $\kappa/B^2$, with its charge conjugate. It can lie below the weak-constant transverse vector on late shrink even in actions whose projected derivative comparison had the original vector threshold.

**Proof.** In the ambient frame at the vacuum, each variation of the augmented skew connection acts on $M_0$ along the orbit. The full auxiliary connection also acts through the phase direction. Hence a normal matrix variation enters the derivative solely by its ordinary derivative with respect to the product coordinates. Both radial and sextet amplitudes are orthogonal to the full orbit tangent pointwise in that frame. The derivative of the quotient orbit projection annihilates them and the volume potential supplies precisely the normal Hessian of 102.1.

The first variation of $\operatorname{tr}(M^\dagger M)$ is $2s_0r$. The linear parts of the two-by-two minors with fourth row and column are $s_0 A_{ij}$ for the symmetric $3\times3$ normal $A$, and all the others vanish. The sum of squared minors is the second elementary symmetric polynomial of $M^\dagger M$. Thus $W^{(2)}=\alpha s_0^2 r^2+\beta s_0^2\|A\|^2/2$. The Euler equations on the normals divide twice these coefficients by $Z_M=z_MB^2$ and add the positive product Laplacian $\Delta_{S^5_B}+\Delta_\Sigma$. The time and static quadratic operators on the normals separate from metric, Gauss and orbit fluctuations. In particular the normal modes have their own positive kinetic term. With $\mu_n$ the weak scalar eigenvalues including zero, their squared masses are respectively
$$
 m^2_{r,\ell,n}=\frac{2\alpha s_0^2/z_M+\ell(\ell+4)}{B^2}+\mu_n,
 \qquad
 m^2_{6,\ell,n}=\frac{\kappa+\ell(\ell+4)}{B^2}+\mu_n .
$$
The constant sextet saturates its separate gap. It retains the charge of the $\operatorname{Sym}^2\mathbf3$ normal in 102, is a weak singlet, and has no inhomogeneous linear gauge variation at the vacuum.

For example take $\kappa=9/2$. Then $15-\sqrt{120}<\kappa<4.99246$, so the projected derivative retained its original vector threshold on shrink. With the local derivative the constant sextet is below both the limiting weak-constant transverse vector of 103 and the spin-two scalar-harmonic scale $5/B^2$.
