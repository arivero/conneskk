# A colour-adjoint stability test for the projective flux

The colour matching in result 63 assumes that the four-dimensional theory at the chosen threshold can retain ordinary QCD. An unstable field charged under colour would invalidate that assumption about the vacuum. We keep both flux quanta, the constant background dilatons, and the weak vortex and its roots fixed in a scalar–metric test. The observable is the sign of the quadratic energy in the first nontrivial projective harmonic at zero circle momentum. We compare an internal four-form with a magnetic Kähler two-form.

## A flux–metric fluctuation

**Proposition 64.1.** On the supported product of results 62–63 the four-form realization has a negative physical second variation in a colour adjoint. In the restriction to the nine-dimensional metric and magnetic four-form the squared mass in product units is
$$
 m_{\rm trial}^2=\frac{23-\sqrt{753}}{7}\frac{6}{B^2}<0 .
$$
This is a variational instability of the stationary product with the stated field content. The full coupled eigenvalue can be lower. It prevents treating this saddle as a stable ordinary-colour vacuum.

**Reduction.** Freeze the weak internal metric, the two gauge scalars and their vortex profiles, and the dilatons. Allow the remaining nine-dimensional metric to vary in a five-dimensional Minkowski (four-space and circle) times projective scalar mode, with no circle momentum or mixed circle connection. The weak part of the curvature cancels the vortex contribution pointwise by Theorem 62.1. The added root squares and their first variations vanish throughout this restriction. Integrating the weak measure leaves ordinary nine-dimensional Einstein gravity with a fixed cosmological coefficient and a rescaled four-form kinetic term. In particular the upper round member does not need a long-neck expansion in this test. For time-symmetric initial data the full Hamiltonian constraint in this restricted sector reduces pointwise to the nine-dimensional one, the weak-sphere part cancelling its own stress. Momentum and electric Gauss constraints hold with vanishing initial momenta. The modes below permit this time-symmetric choice. Thus the restricted negative direction is available in the full physical initial-data space. Including additional positive-kinetic dynamical scalar fields does not remove a negative potential direction already present on this slice. The argument uses the usual Lorentzian Einstein and form constraints, not an unrestricted internal conformal variation in a Euclidean action.

**Proof in the restricted sector.** Write $d=5$, $q=4$, $D=d+q$, $\lambda=6/B^2$ and let $Y$ be a projective scalar harmonic with $-\nabla^2Y=\nu Y$. The lowest octet has $\nu=2\lambda$ by Theorem 59.1. The background has $F=f\,\mathrm{vol}_q$, $Z f^2/2=\lambda$ and $2U=(q-1)\lambda$. The norm convention is that of Theorem 62.1. Write $\Box_d=m^2$ on each external coefficient. The scalar gauge may be chosen as
$$
 h_{\mu\nu}=2(A g_{\mu\nu}+\partial_\mu\partial_\nu E)Y,\quad
 h_{ij}=2H g_{ij}Y,\quad h_{\mu i}=0,\quad
 \delta C_{q-1}=-(f/\nu)t\,{*}_q dY .
$$
The perturbed coordinate flux has fractional amplitude $tY$. Its physical density has fractional amplitude $(t-qH)Y$ and the total flux variation integrates to zero. This harmonic has a nonzero traceless Hessian since its squared norm for $\int Y^2=1$ is $\nu[(q-1)\nu/q-\lambda]>0$. Thus both the traceless-Hessian equation and the form amplitude are present. The exceptional conformal harmonic of a round sphere would have different gauge counting.

The external and internal traceless Einstein equations give $\nu E=(d-2)A+qH$ and $dA+m^2E=-(q-2)H$. In the trace-reversed equations the change of the common external Ricci coefficient is $-2(q-1)\lambda(t-qH)/(D-2)$, while the extra internal flux coefficient changes by $2\lambda(t-qH)$. The internal trace after using the traceless constraint and the linearized Maxwell equation therefore read
$$
 (\nu-m^2-2\lambda)H=D_0\lambda(t-qH),\qquad
 (\nu-m^2)t=2(q-1)\nu H,\qquad
 D_0=\frac{2(d-1)}{D-2}.
$$
For the second equation the form divergence includes the change in volume from $dA+m^2E-qH$. The mixed Einstein constraint is $(d-1)A+(q-1)H=\lambda t/\nu$. Substitution recovers the external trace $(\nu-m^2)A=-2(q-1)\lambda(t-qH)/(D-2)$. Set $x=(\nu-m^2)/\lambda$. The determinant is $x^2+(qD_0-2)x-2(q-1)D_0\nu/\lambda=0$. For the octet this gives $7x^2+18x-96=0$, yielding the displayed negative branch. The branch has nonzero $t-qH$, a fluctuation of the physical flux density. Its negative squared mass is in flat external space, without an AdS stability allowance. This is an application of the Freund–Rubin shape calculation to the nonconformal first harmonic on $\mathbb{CP}^2$. [Brown and Dahlen, §§5.2.1–5.2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.6360#page=34) $\square$

The classical vacua of result 63 are still exact stationary solutions. Their spectra and current forms about the specified background retain that interpretation. The ensuing ordinary-QCD matching and Fermi-to-nucleon ratio remain conditional until the colour sector is stabilized. A flux with anisotropic internal stress, a different compactification, or additional stabilizing terms change this variational problem.

## A Kähler flux with the same colour normalization

**Proposition 64.2.** Replace the colour four-form by an Abelian two-form $F_c=q_c\omega_{\rm FS}$ and take $Z_c=Z_0e^{2\sigma}$, $U=U_0e^{-2\sigma}$, with $Z_0q_c^2=12 B_0^2$, $U_0=6/B_0^2$. Keep the other terms of the action fixed. The same product metrics, vortex roots and dilaton moduli solve the classical field equations, with the same total colour kinetic coefficient $I_3=CVB^2/4$. In the fixed-modulus nine-dimensional colour scalar sector just examined, the two octet mass-squared values in units of $\lambda$ are now $(31\pm\sqrt{65})/7$, both positive.

**Proof.** With $F_c=f\omega_c$ in the physical metric, $\omega_c$ is parallel, $|F_c|^2=2 f^2$, $f=q_c/B^2$ and $\langle\iota_iF_c,\iota_jF_c\rangle=f^2g_{ij}$. The external Ricci contribution vanishes for $U=Z_cf^2/2$ and the internal coefficient is $\lambda=Z_cf^2/2$. The scalar equation is $2U'+Z_c'|F_c|^2/2=0$. Maxwell's equation holds on the product. For a colour Killing field $K$ its zero-mean Hamiltonian obeys $d\psi_K=\iota_K\omega_c$ up to orientation and has scalar eigenvalue $2\lambda$. Consequently the flux compensator's norm contributes $Z_cf^2/(2\lambda)=1$ times the Einstein norm, now by $\|d\psi_K\|^2=2\lambda\|\psi_K\|^2$. These massless gluon modes and kinetic norms thus retain the absolute matching of result 63.

For the restricted octet let $\eta=(J\nabla Y)^\flat$, with the complex orientation $\iota_{\nabla Y}\omega_c=\eta$. It is a Killing one-form. The Hessian of this Killing potential is Hermitian, and $\langle\omega_c,d\eta\rangle=-\nu Y$. Use the same metric gauge and $\delta A_c=f w\,\eta$. The fractional metric variation of the two-form norm contributes $-4H Y$ while the form variation contributes $-\nu wY$ relative to the background norm. The external Ricci coefficient changes by $2\lambda(\nu w+4H)/(D-2)$. In the internal equations the extra anisotropic variation is $4\lambda w\,\mathrm{Hess}\,Y-2\lambda H Yg$. Therefore the internal traceless equation now gives $2H+dA+m^2E=-4\lambda w$. The Maxwell divergence uses $\delta d\eta=\nu\eta$ and the inverse-metric and volume factors give $dA+m^2E$ for $q=4$. Putting $h=\lambda w$, $x=(\nu-m^2)/\lambda$, the remaining equations reduce to
$$
 (x+2)H=2(2h+4H)/7,\qquad (x+4)h=-2H .
$$
The mixed Einstein equation is $4A+3H=-h$, consistent with $\nu E=3A+4H$ and the external trace. The determinant is $7x^2+34x+32=0$, giving the stated masses. A Kähler two-form varies its traceless internal stress in this octet, unlike a top form. $\square$

This replacement resolves the particular fixed-modulus instability. The full scalar Hessian includes the dilatons, the weak area and vortex fluctuations, other projective representations, and possible charged fields coupled to the new Maxwell connection. Positive curvature of this restricted two-mode block alone does not establish stability of the complete solution. The new Abelian vector also needs its physical charge assignment or an independent lifting mechanism. The calculation gives a positive next test with a preserved colour norm. It adds no fitted hadron or weak mass. [Related magnetic compactification on the two projective factors](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0102307)

## Allowing the round scalar moduli to fluctuate

At the round member the weak-rotation singlet sector can be reduced first on the weak two-sphere. For the Kähler colour two-form choose nine-dimensional Einstein frame, with weak radius multiplied by $e^\omega$ and nine-metric multiplied in the uplift by $e^{-4\omega/7}$. The colour potential and kinetic coefficient depend on $\chi_c=\sigma+2\omega/7$ as $e^{-2\chi_c},e^{2\chi_c}$. The scalar kinetic terms for $\sigma,s,\omega$ have respective coefficients $1,1,18/7$. The weak curvature, magnetic flux and vortex potential combine into a positive multiple of $e^{-18\omega/7}\sinh^2(s-\omega-\mathrm{const})$ around this solution. This follows by keeping the quantized two-form fixed in coordinate units: the competing terms in the sphere potential are proportional to $e^{2s-4\omega}+e^{-2s}-2e^{-2\omega}$ after constant shifts. Its first derivative vanishes. Omitting its positive Hessian gives a lower mass test with the same linear gravitational constraints.

**Proposition 64.3.** In the round colour-octet scalar/colour-flux sector of Proposition 64.2, allowing the two dilatons and the weak overall radius preserves a positive gap of at least $\nu=12/B^2$ in squared mass. Masses here are measured in the constant background product units, equivalently after a common constant frame conversion. This statement is for weak-rotation singlets.

**Proof.** First omit the positive weak Hessian and diagonalize the constant kinetic form with one coordinate $\chi_c$ of kinetic coefficient $c>0$. Its reciprocal with the displayed terms is $1+2/63$. The orthogonal free scalar combinations have squared mass $\nu$. Write $S$ for the $\chi_c$ perturbation. Relative to the last calculation, the change of the external Ricci coefficient in units of $\lambda$ is $2(2h+4H-4S)/7$, and the additional isotropic internal stress includes $2S$. The Maxwell coefficient has perturbation $2S$ and the scalar equation comes from the opposite exponentials. The three equations are
$$
 (x+4)h=2(S-H),\qquad (cx+8)S=2(2h+4H),\qquad
 (x+2)H=(cx/7+2)S .
$$
Their determinant is proportional to $x[7cx^2+(34c+56)x+32c+168]$. Both nonzero roots are real and negative for $c>0$, and $x=0$ gives $m^2=\nu$. Restoring the positive weak Hessian cannot lower the eigenvalues by the variational principle. At the nonround member, variations of the weak profiles can mix with these fields. This computation does not assign them the round gap. $\square$
