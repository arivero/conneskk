# With P = M_2(H) + M_4(C), a connector at one wall and an assumed five-dimensional Yang-Mills action of one coupling, the lowest W and Z masses are sin^2(M_W l) = sin^2(2 theta)/2 and sin^2(M_Z l) = (4/5) sin^2(2 theta), with constants 2 and 16/5 at small theta and M_W^2/M_Z^2 -> 5/8 at both ends

**Status.** Theorem (Theorem 23.6), conditional on the assumed five-dimensional Yang-Mills action and wall conditions (ledger status conditional). Proved by Claude Opus 5.5, with the simpler form of the mass formulas and the proof of the global minimum at theta = pi/4 by Terra 6.1; checked by Claude Opus 5.5 in Python (the boundary problem on Sp(2) x SU(4) solved as a linear system on a grid in k l: the two lowest roots at four angles equal to the formulas to three digits, and the intersection of the stabilizers of dimension 9) and by Terra 6.1 by hand, with a second derivation through the principal angles between the Neumann subspaces (historical/reviews/2026-11-03_terra_check_23.md). Ledger row `l23_wz`. Theorem 7 of paper/boundary_condition_note.tex; its small-angle limit is used in paper/joint_limit_note.tex.

**Setting.** P = M_2(H) (+) M_4(C) acting on E = Hom(V, W) on the left and on the right, with universal fermions of mass theta/l as in Example 23.5. Assumed: a five-dimensional Yang-Mills field valued in sp(2) (+) su(4) with the trace form Tr_W + Tr_V, so one coupling (g_3 = g_2 = sqrt(5/3) g_Y); at x = 0, Neumann for the stabilizer of p_0 and Dirichlet for its complement; at x = l, Neumann for the stabilizer of p_l and of a connector, the a with [a_W, Gamma_p] = 0, a_W w = lambda w and a_V v = lambda v for a line w in the +1 eigenspace of Gamma_p and a line v in V (Results 2 and 5 of lemma 20), and Dirichlet for the complement. The connector is prescribed gauge-breaking wall data.

**Statement.**

1. The modes massless for all theta are exactly nine, the photon and the eight gluons, with three additional electroweak zero modes at theta = 0 and theta = pi/2. The photon has 1/e^2 = (8/3)/g^2.
2. sin^2(M_W l) = sin^2(2 theta)/2 and sin^2(M_Z l) = (4/5) sin^2(2 theta); equivalently tan^2(M_W l) = sin^2(2 theta)/(1 + cos^2(2 theta)) and tan^2(M_Z l) = 4 sin^2(2 theta)/(1 + 4 cos^2(2 theta)).
3. As theta -> 0, with m = theta/l, M_W^2 -> 2 m^2 and M_Z^2 -> (16/5) m^2. As theta -> pi/2, M_W, M_Z -> 0 while m -> pi/(2l). In both limits M_W^2/M_Z^2 -> 5/8.
4. At theta = pi/4, M_W l = pi/4, tan(M_Z l) = 2 and M_W^2/M_Z^2 = ((pi/4)/arctan 2)^2 = 0.5032..., the global minimum, so the ratio stays in [0.5032, 0.625).
5. m/M_W runs from 1/sqrt(2) at theta -> 0 to infinity at theta -> pi/2, and equals sqrt(8/3) at theta = 1.0589.

*Scope.* The constants 2 and 16/5 are the Hilbert-Schmidt quotients ||[D_F, a]||^2/||a||^2 for universal mass, and the coefficients of the lower bounds for the poles of the fully massive family (18_fully_massive_family.md), whose small-mass pole coefficients are 3 and 24/5. The spectral-action Taylor mass for universal m is M_W^2 = 3 m^2, larger by the factor 3/2 of Proposition F of paper/towerless_intervals.tex, since this theorem assumes the Yang-Mills commutator norm alone. If the connector is instead a term in the fermion boundary operator, the parent order conditions are kept and the affected fermion tower changes, so the universal fermion formula must be recalculated for that channel. The limit theta -> pi/2 is a geometric form of heavy fermions with light vectors: at antipodal points both walls have the same stabilizer in sp(2).

**Proof.** Lemma 23, Theorem 23.6; the proof is there. In the gauge A_5 = 0 the generators split by the frequency of Ad F, 0 on S and 2 on the plane (A, O); the conditions at l in the frame rotated by Ad exp(theta F) give the charged and neutral equations, and item 4 holds because [arcsin(a/sqrt 2)/arcsin(2a/sqrt 5)]^2 decreases with a = sin(2 theta).

**What it leaves open.** Whether the spectral action of the parent itself, with its tower, reproduces these formulas (lemma 23, open question 4); lemma 25 relates the two normalizations at leading order (25_sum_rule_effective_dimension.md). In the flat toy this family covers M_W^2/M_Z^2 in [0.5032, 0.625) and does not fit the measured top and weak ratios together (lemma 23, comparison in Python); the directions are bulk masses, warping, running, and combinations with a wall scalar.
