# In the displayed charge-2/3 channel of the (10, 6) or the (5, 15), a pure frequency-2 channel carries an exact massless coloured exotic, and a wall mass of any finite positive size leaves a coloured state of charge 2/3 with 0 < m < M_W

**Status.** Proposition (Proposition 26.3c), with Proposition 26.3b. Proved by Claude Opus 5.5, with the self-adjoint boundary problem and the monotonicity of the root by Terra 6.1. Ledger rows `l26_plane` (depending on `l23_wz`) and `l26_wall_mass` (depending on `l26_plane`), with prior work recorded as partial: Cacciapaglia-Csaki-Park hep-ph/0510366, Adachi-Maru 1804.06012, Hosotani-Oda-Ohnuma-Sakamura 0806.0480. Cited in the section on the gauge sector of paper/boundary_condition_note.tex ("\supp{26}").

**Setting.** The (10, 6) with F the generator J_45 of so(5); the frequency-2 planes (A^a, O^a), A = (L - R)/sqrt(2), O in (2, 2), S = (L + R)/sqrt(2). O^+ is the left-handed top, with (T_3L, R_3) = (+1/2, +1/2), and its partner A^+ contains the right-handed top R^+ with weight 1/sqrt(2), together with the triplet component L^+. phi = 2 theta, A' = cos(phi) A + sin(phi) O, O' = -sin(phi) A + cos(phi) O. At x = 0, psi_R is Neumann on S, A and Dirichlet on O, and psi_L has the complementary conditions. For the wall mass, u = (S + A')/sqrt(2), v = (S - A')/sqrt(2), and a wall Weyl field chi couples to g_u at x = l: H(f, g, chi) = (-g', f', w g_u(l)) on the domain f_u(l) = w chi, g_{O'}(l) = 0, f_v(l) = 0, with omega = w^2 l, a = m l and s = sin(2 theta).

**Statement.**

1. (Proposition 26.3b) If at x = l psi_R is Neumann on S and A' and Dirichlet on O', then (O^+, A^+) is a pure frequency-2 channel, and S_R is an exact massless coloured mode of charge 2/3. If instead psi_R is Dirichlet on O' and on S + A' and Neumann on S - A', the determinant is proportional to cos(a)(2 sin^2 a - sin^2 phi), so the angle-dependent tower obeys sin^2(m l) = sin^2(2 theta)/2, the equation of the charged vectors, and m_t = M_W at every theta, with an additional angle-independent half-integer tower. In the (5, 15) the same holds with the chiralities exchanged, the plane containing U_R and (t_L + X)/sqrt(2), with X the exotic of Y = 7/6.
2. (Proposition 26.3c) The nonzero eigenvalues solve 2 a Delta sin(a) = omega cos(a) (Delta + sin^2 a), Delta = sin^2 a - s^2, where omega = 0 and omega -> infinity give the two cases of item 1. For every theta in (0, pi/2) and every omega in (0, infinity) there is an eigenvalue with 0 < m < M_W.
3. At theta = pi/4 the equation factorizes as cos(a) (omega cos(2a) - a sin(2a)) = 0: m = pi/(2l) = 2 M_W for every omega, and the other factor has a root in (0, pi/4) for every omega > 0, moving from 0 to M_W l = pi/4.
4. The eigenvalue m = 2 M_W occurs at omega = 4 u_W sin(2 u_W)/(1 + 2 cos(2 u_W)), u_W = M_W l; at theta = 1 this is omega = 2.04, with another state at 0.85 M_W (Python).

*Scope.* These statements concern the displayed channel. A gauge-invariant completion needs conditions on complete wall multiplets, and the other modes of the multiplet also enter the potential; complete wall multiplets, other boundary mixings, bulk masses and the other towers can add still lighter states. By item 4 a higher eigenstate, with nonzero O_L and R_R components, can sit at 2 M_W, so identifying it with the top is not excluded; its physical identification and the couplings of the lighter state need analysis. E = (4, 4bar) has no frequency-2 channel and no such partner.

**Proof.** Lemma 26, Propositions 26.3b and 26.3c; the proofs are there. With f' = m g and g' = -m f, the conditions at 0 fix the profiles, and the three conditions at l give equation (3) as a determinant, used before any division so that it covers cos(2 theta) = 0. On 0 < a < u_W, omega = 2 a tan(a) (s^2 - sin^2 a)/(s^2 - 2 sin^2 a) increases strictly from 0 to infinity.

**What it leaves open.** A top at 2 M_W in such a channel has a lighter partner whose couplings are not analyzed. Warping and larger representations are the standard directions (lemma 26, Two regimes), and the comparison is with the top-mass limits for low representations in flat gauge-Higgs models.
