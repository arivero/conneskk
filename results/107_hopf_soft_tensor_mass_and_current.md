## A sharp fixed-shape mass limit

**Proposition 107.1.** In the isotropic local-curvature action, with any fixed positive sextet stiffness, the first additional coloured boson on sufficiently late shrink lies in the first colour scalar harmonic. It is one complex colour triplet of spin one, with the conjugate charge assignment, and
$$
 M^2\ \sim\ C_{\rm soft}\,\frac{a^2}{B^4},\qquad
 C_{\rm soft}=\frac{\nu A_h}{Z_D}
       \inf_{\ L_e(u)=0,\ L_m(u)=1}\mathscr H_\nu[u],\qquad \nu=5 .
$$
Here $h$ is the unscaled weak metric, $A_h=\operatorname{Area}(h)$, $L_i(u)=\int_\Sigma\beta_i(u)$ for the source profiles $u=(X,z)$, and
$$
 \mathscr H_\nu[u]=3I_3 H_w[u]
        +Z_6\nu\,\|d_h^{-1}\beta_e(u)\|_{L^2(h)}^2
                  \qquad (L_e(u)=0).
$$
The inverse is the coexact weak primitive. The positive constant is about $6598$ at $b=.001$. In the physical kinetic norm the weak-constant magnetic dual vector carries fraction $1-O((a/B)^2)$ of this pole.

**Proof.** For a fixed colour scalar harmonic let $\lambda_C=\nu/B^2$ and $\lambda_n=\lambda_C+\mu_n$. Write $u$ for the weak metric and vortex vector profiles using the old physical vector form. The old colour-tangent vector and auxiliary Maxwell sectors have no such weak source. Weak exact coordinate profiles are removed from the physical norms. ## The covariant harmonic operator and the Pauli coefficient

**Proposition 107.2.** The light triplet has exact Ward charge $Q=\pm1/6$. Its minimally covariant weak-harmonic action has the charged massive two-form kinetic term. The enhanced coefficient obtained by eliminating the weak sources is
$$
 Z_{\rm eff}\ \sim\
       \frac{B^2}{4\pi^2\mathcal A_\Sigma}
       \left\|L_m\right\|_{\mathscr H_\nu^{-1}}^2 ,
$$
where the dual squared norm is taken on $L_e=0$. The massive-vector dual of this minimal derivative comparison has $g=1$. This identifies a covariant term of the reduction, without prescribing additional curvature vertices of the interacting theory.

**Proof.** The residual photon acts on the first colour harmonic by the signed Hopf weight divided by six. The metric and tensor profiles used above have zero weak azimuthal weight. The vortex and tensor transformations needed for the photon are compensators in the invariant field strengths; they leave this action on the physical pole unchanged. The horizontal exterior derivative contains also an insertion of the connection curvature on internal form legs. The external two-form in this slot has none. The genuinely mixed photon five-form contribution carries two external legs and three colour legs. Its wedge with the external $B_2$ and the linear weak magnetic source, which has at least one external leg, exceeds external degree four. The weak harmonic magnetic coupling at the minimally covariant level is therefore $B_2\wedge D_Q X_m$ with source $X_m=L_m(u)/\sqrt{\mathcal A_\Sigma}$ and the multiplier normalization $1/(2\pi)$.

## A finite physical-current comparison

$$
 0.00163116\,g^2-0.00151531\,g+0.00071646 .
$$
Its minimum is about $0.00036454$ and the value at $g=1$ is about $0.00083231$. A Ward charge alone gives no such positive lower bound on this finite window when more form factors are allowed.

**Proof.** Use the physical pair density of 96 with total charge weight $3(1/6)^2=1/12$. Setting $u=s/M^2$ the subtracted dispersion kernel for this difference gives
$$
 \frac{1}{48\pi}\int_4^9
 \frac{(1-4/u)^{3/2}
 [2+g^2u+(1+(g-1)u/2)^2]}{(u+1)(u+4)}\,du .
$$
The coefficients follow by integrating and minimizing this quadratic (Python, SciPy). This assumes free pair kinematics and the prescribed vertex over precisely this window.
