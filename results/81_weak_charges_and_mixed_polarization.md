# A neutral-target weak charge and its hadronic polarization

Keep the mirror-complete fibration and the minimal currents of 72 and 78. The observables here use both quarks and leptons. At zero momentum a conserved proton vector charge counts two up and one down valence charge, while a neutron counts one up and two down. Sea pairs have zero net flavour charge. For photon–vortex mixing at nonzero momentum the full QCD current correlator is required. This distinction allows two independent infrared comparisons without identifying a hadron mass with a geometric eigenvalue.

## A source that cancels the ordinary neutral tower

**Proposition 81.1.** At any finite equal-root polar member consider the normalized weak zero-mode probes with common profiles within each species type. The sum of the vector sources of a proton and an electron has odd reflection parity in the neutral vector pencil. It annihilates the photon, the ordinary even $Z$ branch and every other even profile. Its static amplitude on an electron axial source is independent of a photon kinetic-mixing counterterm at fixed mass form and vertices. In the small-breaking light reduction,
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_W^p&=\rho(1-4s)-9d_v,\qquad Q_W^n=-\rho-9d_v,\\
 Q_W^e&=\rho(-1+4s)-3d_v,\qquad Q_W^p+Q_W^e=-12d_v .
 \end{aligned}
$$
The sum is a combination of vector charges as measured by a parity-violating electron probe. Finite-momentum nuclear or atomic form factors and process-specific radiative corrections must be removed before interpreting it as a zero-momentum charge.

**Proof.** Use the neutral profiles of 78. Reflection acts on $(U,Z,W,P)(t)$ as $(U,-Z,W,P)(-t)$. Both the neutral mass and kinetic forms commute with it. Denote the normalized north and south doublet densities by $d_-(t),d_+(t)$, interchanged by reflection; the singlet density $d_0$ is even. In $2j_{u,V}+j_{d,V}+j_{e,V}$ use $j_{f,V}=j_{f,L}+j_{f,R}$ before the common factor $1/2$ in the vector–axial decomposition. The coefficient of $U$ from the electric weights on the left states is $(4/3)(d_+-d_-)$, and that of $k\eta W$ is $(d_+-d_-)/3$. The remaining left term is $2(d_++d_-)(U'/4+2Z)$. The singlet hypercharges cancel in the right-source sum, leaving $4d_0(U'/4+Z)$. Each expression integrates to zero on an even profile. In particular the ordinary $Z$ has a vanishing vertex on the combined target even when the individual neutral angular poles do not have pointlike doublet vertices. The right-source relation $2j_{u,R}-5j_{d,R}+3j_{e,R}=0$ also follows directly from their common singlet density and hypercharges.

Both the combined target and the electron axial source annihilate the electric generator by their measured charges. For any symmetric gapped mass form modulo its photon kernel, the limit of $j_1(\mathcal H+t\mathcal K)^{-1}j_2^\dagger$ on two such sources is the inverse form on the quotient. This value is independent of the kinetic representative of that quotient. Thus adding derivative kinetic mixing alone cannot change this static amplitude. A correction to the mass form or to a current vertex has a different effect.

For the light formulas use $\mathcal L_{\rm PV}=(G_F/\sqrt2)\sum_f C_{1f}\bar e\gamma_\mu\gamma_5e\,\bar f\gamma^\mu f+\cdots$. The weak pole gives $C_{1q}=2\rho T^e_3(T^q_3-2sQ_q)$ and the analogous electron coefficient. The vortex has $N_L-N_R=1$ on the electron and $N_L+N_R=3$ on every charged Dirac target here, adding $3d_v/2$ to each $C_{1f}$. Now use $Q_W^p=-2(2C_{1u}+C_{1d})$, $Q_W^n=-2(C_{1u}+2C_{1d})$ and $Q_W^e=-2C_{1e}$. This gives the assertion. In particular the part sensitive to a common weak mixing angle cancels in the proton–electron sum. At the finite central member the higher poles leave a small odd-sector contribution beyond the light formula. $\square$

**Independent probes at the finite member.** The full radial calculation gives $Q_W^p=0.06531$, $Q_W^n=-1.02321$, $Q_W^e=-0.08408$, and $Q_W^p+Q_W^e=-0.018776$ before radiative corrections. The leading value $-12d_v$ is $-0.018774$. The right-handed-neutrino matrix element distinguishes the vortex pole responsible for the leading contribution. For a muon-neutrino probe the corresponding up and down left couplings are about $0.353,-0.424$ and the right couplings $-0.153,0.0790$. The full sums of squares are $0.30416$ and $0.02978$. The difference enters the isoscalar Paschos–Wolfenstein combination after matching the charged current; strange and charm distributions, isospin breaking and radiative corrections matter in a cross section. Qweak reported $Q_W^p=0.0719\pm0.0045$, with a reference Standard Model calculation $0.0708\pm0.0003$. SLAC E158 reported an effective angle $0.2397\pm0.0010\ {\rm(stat.)}\pm0.0008\ {\rm(syst.)}$ at $Q^2=0.026\,\mathrm{GeV}^2$, with its then-reference expectation $0.2381\pm0.0006$. Their angle uses an electron–electron coefficient $\rho^{ee}=1.0012$. To illustrate the importance of the subtraction, express each experiment as a change from its *own* Standard Model reference and keep a new heavy contact to first order. The changes are about $+0.0011$ on the proton and $4\rho^{ee}(0.2397-0.2381)\simeq+0.0064$ on the electron. Their sum gives the illustrative central diagnostic $d_v\simeq-0.00063$ if the sole extra contribution is $-12d_v$. Treating the quoted uncertainties as independent gives a scale of about $0.00060$ on this diagnostic. It favours examining smaller positive vortex exchange. The two published baselines use different vintages, kinematics and radiative corrections. A modern joint fit would have to use a common reference, proton and electron box corrections, parameter correlations and the extra thresholds in 79. Directly adding the two raw experimental numbers or interpreting this diagnostic as an exclusion would bypass those steps. ## Photon–vortex running from measured QCD currents

**Proposition 81.2.** In the physical-mass, parity-invariant ordinary QCD comparison, the vector part of the vortex current on quarks is $3/2$ times the quark-number current. For the same bare vector-current normalizations as the lattice weak-HVP calculation,
$$
 \bar\Pi_{Qv}^{\rm had}=18\left[(1/2-s_0)\bar\Pi_{\gamma\gamma}^{\rm had}
                                      -\bar\Pi_{Z\gamma}^{\rm had}\right].
$$
The input $s_0$ must be the same one used to define $j^Z$ in that calculation. The extracted vortex correlator does not depend on this convention when the neutral current is transformed with it.

**Proof.** The vector part of $T_3$ on a Dirac quark is half its doublet value. The doublet value is $Q_q-1/6$. Thus $j^Z_{\rm vector}=(1/2-s_0)j^\gamma-j_{\rm number}/12$, while $j^v_{\rm vector}=(N_L+N_R)j_{\rm number}/2=3j_{\rm number}/2$. Take the subtracted electromagnetic cross correlator of these operator identities. Parity eliminates the vector–axial correlator in the QCD vacuum. This derivation also identifies the quark current as $(9/2)j_B$. It needs the disconnected hadronic contributions in the mixed channel. $\square$

For numerical central values use the Mainz conventions $s_0=0.23857$, $\Delta\alpha_{\rm had}=4\pi\alpha(0)\bar\Pi_{\gamma\gamma}$ and $(\Delta\sin^2\theta_W)_{\rm had}=-4\pi\alpha(0)\bar\Pi_{Z\gamma}/s_0$. Their Table 6 gives the pairs $(0.003829,-0.003907)$ and $(0.008464,-0.008485)$ at spacelike $Q^2=1$ and $9\,\mathrm{GeV}^2$. The resulting dimensionless $\bar\Pi_{Qv}^{\rm had}$ values are $+0.01353$ and $+0.03700$. The charged leptons instead have vortex vector charge $3/2$ and electric charge $-1$, giving $-1.5$ times their subtracted electromagnetic polarization, or $-0.20827$ and $-0.26809$. The combined ordinary increments are consequently $-0.19474$ and $-0.23110$ in the current basis. These are subtracted polarizations; the inverse kinetic entry runs with the opposite sign. The two hadronic inputs are correlated, so no independently propagated precision is assigned to their difference here. These calculations show where the neutral-target cancellation helps. Photon mixing modifies the apparent coupling of a massive vector to a *charged* individual target. A neutral vector sum and an axial electron carry zero total electric charge at zero momentum, removing this specific subtraction ambiguity.
