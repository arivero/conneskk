## A. Connes-Lott as the minimal spectral truncation of a chiral interval

**Claim** (lemma 05, Part 3; lemma 09, Theorem 9.6). The minimal nonzero grading-preserving spectral truncation of the Dirac operator on [0, l] with R(0) = 0, L(l) = 0 is unitarily equivalent to the Connes-Lott two-point triple (C + C, D = [[0, m], [m, 0]]) with |m| = pi/(2l); the compressed operator system P C([0, l]) P equals C + C; the truncated end states are at distance 2l/pi. The deconstructed chain of Part 1 has Connes-Lott as its N = 2 member.

**Verdict: PARTIAL.** The interpretation of the two-point space as a discrete or Kaluza-Klein fibre, the truncation machinery, the deconstruction picture and the lowest mixed-boundary mass pi/(2l) are all in print. We found no source that identifies the Connes-Lott triple as a spectral truncation of an interval Dirac operator with chiral boundary conditions, computes the compression P C([0, l]) P for such an interval, or relates |m| to the interval length.

## B. The two limits of the massive chiral interval

**Claim** (lemma 09, Theorems 9.8-9.9, Corollary 9.10). As l -> infinity at fixed bulk mass, the light pair becomes two chiral wall modes and the rest of the spectrum obeys a one-dimensional Weyl law above the gap mu. As l -> 0 with mu(l) l ~ log(1/(m_f l)), a Connes-Lott pair of fixed mass m_f survives while the interval collapses; at fixed mu no light fermion survives.

**Verdict: PARTIAL.** The spectral physics is classical: wall zero modes, exponentially small mixing of the two boundary modes on a finite slab, and the resulting light mass ~ 2 mu e^{-mu l}. Collapse of spectral triples is an active topic in NCG, with a general result for collapse to a point. We found no source stating either limit as a theorem about families of finite or interval spectral triples, and no source for the tuned collapse mu(l) that keeps a Connes-Lott pair.

## C. Towerless Yukawa textures

**Claim** (lemma 12). For bulk fields Q, U on [0, l] with bulk masses and a boundary Yukawa matrix X at x = l: an exact secular equation det(lambda^2 - A X B X^dagger) = 0 (Theorem 12.4); the one-flavour, massless spectrum (n pi +- arctan|X|)/l (Example 12.5); singular-value bounds (Theorem 12.6); in the limit l -> 0 at fixed c and Y_hat = X/l the low spectrum converges to the finite triple with Yukawa block M_F = Phi_Q Y_hat Phi_U (Theorem 12.7); graded singular values of F X G (Lemma 12.8).

**Verdict: PARTIAL.** The physics is in print, in more elaborate (warped) form: exact KK determinant equations with brane Yukawa matrices, the effective Yukawa F(c_Q) Y F(c_u), masses from minors and mixings F(c_Qi)/F(c_Qj). The one-flavour flat spectrum is Csáki et al. eq. (5.6) (KNOWN). We found no NCG paper that derives the finite Dirac operator D_F, or its Yukawa texture, from localization on an extra dimension.

## D. KO-dimension of products: 9, 10 or 11

**Claim** (lemmas 06, 07, 08). A finite KO-6 real triple is T_n x P_k with (s, n, k) = (+1, 7, 7) or (-1, 5, 1) iff a grading-reversing involution U commutes with A, A°, D and satisfies UJ = sJU (Theorem 06.2); J alone does not fix s; an unpaired nu_L forces the KO-7 reduction (No-go 06, with [U, Q] = 0); in the internal-first product with the interval, the bare reflection has s = -1 (Theorem 08.5).

**Verdict: PARTIAL.** Products of real spectral triples of all parities, including the doubled odd x odd product on H_1 x H_2 x C^2, the freedom J versus J gamma in even dimension, and the total KO-dimension 10 = 2 mod 8 of the Standard Model are in print. The converse direction, a criterion for when a given even KO-6 triple factors as (odd) x (odd point), was NOT FOUND, nor were the nu_L statement, the reflection sign, or any discussion of 9 or 11 as alternatives to 10.
