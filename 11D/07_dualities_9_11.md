## 1. The dualities (recalled)

**Referee note (GPT-6.1 Terra, 4 Nov 2026).** The duality descriptions are conditional on the recalled sources. The M2 wrapping number and the auxiliary torus flux are separately specified; a map identifying them remains open. See the [referee report](../reviews/2026-11-04_terra_check_11d.md#files-06-and-07-recalled-dualities).

1. **M-theory on T^2 and type IIB on S^1** (Schwarz, hep-th/9508143; Aspinwall, hep-th/9508154). The modulus tau of the torus is the IIB coupling and SL(2, Z) is IIB S-duality; the IIB radius scales as a negative power of the area A of the torus. M2-branes wrapped on T^2 are IIB momentum states, and momenta on T^2 are (p, q)-strings wound on S^1. Eleven dimensions at A -> infinity, ten (IIB) at A -> 0, nine in between.
2. **Sixteen supercharges, rank 17** (Polchinski and Witten, hep-th/9510169; Hořava and Witten, hep-th/9510209). The two heterotic strings on S^1 are T-dual through Wilson lines; their type I' form is an interval with two orientifold planes O8 and sixteen D8-branes whose positions are the Wilson-line eigenvalues; at strong coupling, M-theory on an interval times a circle, with E8 walls.
3. **Sixteen supercharges, lower rank.** The CHL string (rank 9) and M-theory on the Möbius strip; a rank-1 component related to the Klein bottle (Aharony, Komargodski and Patir, hep-th/0702195); further components with discrete theta angles (Montero and Parra de Freitas, arXiv:2209.03361).
4. **Light-cone M-theory.** On a light-like circle with momentum N, M-theory is the U(N) quantum mechanics of nine Hermitian matrices (Banks, Fischler, Shenker and Susskind, hep-th/9610043; Susskind). On T^2 with a light-like component of C_3, the torus becomes noncommutative and SL(2, Z) acts by Morita equivalence (Connes, Douglas and Schwarz, hep-th/9711162).

## 3. Proposition 7.1 (the angle of a channel lives on an interval)

**Referee note (GPT-6.1 Terra, 4 Nov 2026).** The spectral quotient and endpoint multiplicities are independently checked. Theorem 22.1 uses the strict interior; the antiperiodic endpoint is a limit, with a degenerate window. Equality of one channel spectrum does not by itself identify full physical backgrounds. See the [referee report](../reviews/2026-11-04_terra_check_11d.md#proposition-71-channel-angle-fundamental-domain).

**Statement.** In the parent of [lemma 22](../lemmas/22_dirac_operator_as_boundary_condition.md), a channel with singular value σ of the Yukawa block and angle θ = l σ has the signed spectrum {(n pi + θ)/l, (n pi − θ)/l : n in Z} (Theorem 22.1). As a set, this depends only on the class of θ in R modulo the group generated by θ -> −θ and θ -> θ + pi, whose fundamental domain is [0, pi/2]. At the endpoints:

1. θ = 0: the spectrum is n pi/l with a zero mode of multiplicity two (periodic);
2. θ = pi/2: the spectrum is (n + 1/2) pi/l, each value twice (antiperiodic), two copies of the spectrum of the chiral interval of Proposition 1.1; it is the limit X -> infinity of the wall data X = tan(θ), where the lowest eigenspace is degenerate, and in lemma 23 the point of antipodal gradings, where M_W = M_Z = 0 at fixed l with the fermion at pi/(2l).

**Proof.** The set is invariant under θ -> −θ (it exchanges the two progressions) and θ -> θ + pi (it shifts n). The orbit of θ meets [0, pi/2] in one point. The endpoint values are substitutions; for θ -> pi/2 the wall conditions Q_R(l) = X U_R(l), U_L(l) = −X^* Q_L(l) tend to U_R(l) = Q_L(l) = 0. The statements at θ = pi/2 are those of lemma 23 (Theorem 23.6 and the reading after Proposition 23.7). ∎

So the parameter space of one channel of the finite Dirac operator, for a given parent, is an interval with two distinguished ends, and lemma 22's restriction l ||D_F|| < pi/2 is the choice of the fundamental domain.

## 4. Proposition 7.2 (the far end is the displaced D8-brane on the other wall)

**Referee note (GPT-6.1 Terra, 4 Nov 2026).** At \(a=1/2\) the full \(SO(32)\) centralizer is \(S(O(30)\times O(2))\), whose identity component is \(SO(30)\times SO(2)\). The stated one-loop minimum uses the hypotheses of lemma 24 and \(n_f>0\); the D8 dictionary remains a proposal. (Second check.) In Spin(32)/Z_2 the component of determinant -1 sends the lift of W_{1/2} to its negative, so the heterotic centralizer is connected; a -> a + 1 holds for SO(32) matrices, and the lift has period 2. See the [referee report](../reviews/2026-11-04_terra_check_11d.md#proposition-72-wilson-line-endpoint-and-one-loop-minimum).

**Statement.** Put a = θ/pi in [0, 1/2]. By Proposition 6.1 the channel spectrum is that of the states (30)_{±1} of SO(32) on a circle of radius R = l/pi with the Wilson line W_a = 1_30 ⊕ R(2 pi a). The interval [0, 1/2] is the moduli interval of one Wilson-line eigenvalue modulo a -> −a and a -> a + 1; the unbroken group is SO(32) at a = 0 and SO(30) x SO(2) for 0 < a <= 1/2; at a = 1/2 the holonomy is −1 on the plane of SO(2), W_{1/2} = diag(1_30, −1_2), and the (30)_{±1} tower is (n + 1/2)/R, the antiperiodic tower of Proposition 1.1. The one-loop minimum of [lemma 24](../lemmas/24_commutative_parent.md), Theorem 9, θ = pi/2, is the point a = 1/2.

**Proof.** Proposition 6.1 and Proposition 7.1 with θ = pi a; the commutant of W_a in SO(32) for 0 < a < 1/2 is SO(30) x SO(2), and at a = 1/2 its identity component is still SO(30) x SO(2), since only two eigenvalues are −1. Lemma 24, Theorem 9, item 1, places the minimum at t = sin^2 θ = 1. ∎

**Reading (proposal).** In type I' the Wilson line W_a is fifteen D8-branes, with images, on one orientifold plane and the sixteenth at distance proportional to a from it (proportional only at constant dilaton; see [10](10_dilaton_and_second_scale.md), Proposition 10.1); a = 1/2 puts it on the other orientifold plane (recalled: hep-th/9711098, hep-th/0108135). The dictionary of file 06 then reads: the top angle is the position of the top's D8-brane on the type I' interval, the two ends of the fundamental domain are the two orientifold walls, and the one-loop vacuum of the flat family of lemmas 23 and 24 carries the top brane to the far wall, where the tower of the stack-brane strings is antiperiodic. In supersymmetric type I' the D8 positions are flat directions; the potential of lemma 24 belongs to the non-supersymmetric flat family, the setting in which a force on the brane is expected.

Under the strong-coupling duality of item 2 of section 1, the D8 position is a Wilson-line eigenvalue of the heterotic string on S^1, and in eleven dimensions an E8 holonomy around the circle on a Hořava-Witten wall. So the same datum, the top angle, is a brane position at 9 and a holonomy at 11.

## 5. Proposition 7.3 (a torus with flux, doubled, has the colour form)

**Referee note (GPT-6.1 Terra, 4 Nov 2026).** The signs, kernel dimensions and generated matrix corner check out, with a spin-line twist for nontrivial spin structures. The displayed action is degenerate and nonunital on the doubled Hilbert space, so the claimed unital finite triple needs an extra scalar summand. See the [referee report](../reviews/2026-11-04_terra_check_11d.md#proposition-73-doubled-torus-and-the-finite-algebra).

**Statement.** Let T^2 be a flat torus of area A with a spin structure, L a line bundle of degree p >= 1 with a connection of constant curvature, and Lbar = L^{-1} with the conjugate connection. On H = L^2(S ⊗ L) ⊕ L^2(S ⊗ Lbar) put D = D_L ⊕ D_Lbar, gamma = gamma_S ⊕ gamma_S, and J(ψ, χ) = (Cχ, −Cψ), where C is the charge conjugation of the spin structure (antiunitary, C^2 = −1, C D = D C, C gamma_S = −gamma_S C, mapping S ⊗ Lbar to S ⊗ L). Let C^infinity(T^2) act by multiplication on the first summand only. Then:

1. J^2 = 1, J D = D J and J gamma = −gamma J, the signs of KO-dimension six, and the algebra satisfies order zero;
2. ker D = H^0(L) ⊕ H^1(Lbar) ≅ C^p ⊕ conj(C^p), with gamma = +1 on the first summand and −1 on the second; the other eigenvalues are the Landau levels, of order (p/A)^{1/2}, which go to infinity as A -> 0 at fixed p;
3. the compressions of the functions to the kernel generate M_p(C) on C^p ⊕ 0, so the window is the finite triple (M_p(C), C^p ⊕ conj(C^p), 0, J, gamma), with KO-6 signs and order zero: M_p(C) acts on the first summand and, through J, by its opposite on the second.

**Proof.** (1) J^2(ψ, χ) = (C(−Cψ), −C(Cχ)) = (ψ, χ) since C^2 = −1; JD = DJ and J gamma = −gamma J follow from the properties of C; order zero holds because the algebra acts on the first summand and J b J^{-1} on the second. (2) On a torus the spinor bundle is Λ^{0,*} up to a flat factor, so ker D_L = H^0(L) ⊕ H^1(L) = C^p ⊕ 0 by Riemann-Roch in genus one, and ker D_Lbar = 0 ⊕ H^1(Lbar) ≅ conj(C^p) by Serre duality; the Landau levels of a constant field of total flux p on area A scale as (p/A)^{1/2}. (3) The compression of a character e_{m,n} to H^0(L) is a nonzero multiple of a clock-and-shift Weyl operator U^m V^n with UV = e^{2 pi i/p} VU (Berezin-Toeplitz quantization of the torus; recalled; in the theta-function basis the compression is a Gaussian factor times the Weyl word, checked for p = 2 to 5 in the second check), and these operators span M_p(C). ∎

The construction is the torus analogue of lemma 16 (a doubled parent with restricted algebra and a J covering the flip; Example 16.9 for the twistor case), and its window at p = 3 has the form of the colour sector of the Standard Model triple: M_3(C) on the quarks and its opposite on the antiquarks. Berezin-Toeplitz quantization of the torus is known; the KO-6 doubling is the corpus device.

**Reading for duality 1 (proposal).** The area A -> 0 at fixed flux keeps the window and sends the tower to infinity, the towerless limit; A -> infinity brings the Landau levels down. In M-theory on T^2, flux and wrapping number of M2-branes on T^2 are IIB momentum states, so the dimension p of the window counts IIB momentum on the nine-dimensional circle, and the SL(2, Z) of the torus acts on the family by Morita equivalence of the noncommutative tori of Connes, Douglas and Schwarz when a C_3 background is on. Whether p = 3 for colour has a reason in this picture is open.

## 6. A remark on duality 4 (known)

For U(N) matrices X^i (i = 1, ..., 9) that are block-diagonal, X^i = ⊕_a x_a^i 1_{n_a} with distinct points x_a in R^9, the commutant of all X^i is ⊕_a M_{n_a}(C), and the fermion operator Γ^i ad(X^i) on the adjoint, with the sixteen-component spinor of Spin(9), vanishes on the diagonal blocks and has eigenvalues ±|x_a − x_b|, each with multiplicity 8 n_a n_b per ordered block (a, b), that is 16 n_a n_b per unordered pair. With stacks of sizes 1, 2, 3 the commutant is C + M_2(C) + M_3(C), the complex form of A_F, the off-diagonal blocks are bifundamentals as in the Krajewski diagrams of the finite geometry, and the masses are the distances between stacks; coincident stacks restore U(N), the analogue of massless W and Z, and large N is the eleven-dimensional limit. A quaternionic factor H needs an orientifold projection on the two-stack, and chirality needs more than the nine Hermitian matrices.
