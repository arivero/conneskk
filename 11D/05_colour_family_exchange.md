# The exchange of the top and the neutrinos as a colour-family transposition in E8

Part of the 11D line of research ([README](README.md), [PLAN](PLAN.md), step 4). Attempted by Claude (Anthropic), 4 Nov 2026. Proposition 5.1 (a negative result in its scope, pending an independent check) and Proposition 5.2 are proved by hand; an exploratory search in Python with exact fractions, in a scratch directory and not kept, found the same results (no isometry for three families, signed coordinate permutations for four). ## Setting

Proposition 4.6 showed that the M2/M5 exchange of the graded e8 acts as J and cannot exchange the top with the neutrinos, and Proposition 4.2 that any such exchange must trade the colour index of the top for the generation index of the neutrinos. This file tests the colour-family exchanges inside E8.

Use E8 ⊃ SO(16) ⊃ SO(10) × SO(6), with the roots of E8 in the coordinates of R^8: ±e_i ± e_j, and (1/2)(±1, ..., ±1) with an even number of minus signs. The 128 half-integer roots form the chiral spinor of SO(16), which under SO(10) × SU(4) is (16, 4) + (16bar, 4bar): four families of the Standard Model with ν^c, with their conjugates. Write a root as (χ | w | φ), with χ the signs of the three colour coordinates, w those of the two weak coordinates and φ those of the three family coordinates; a particle has an even number of minus signs among χ, w and an even number among φ. The four family patterns are 𝟙 = (+++) (family 0) and the three patterns with two minus signs (families 1, 2, 3); the colour patterns with one minus sign are the quark colours, the pattern −𝟙 is the lepton. Then, for a colour pattern c with one minus sign and a family f,

| state | root (times 2) |
|---|---|
| u_L, d_L | (c \| −+ \| f), (c \| +− \| f) |
| u^c, d^c | (−c \| ++ \| f), (−c \| −− \| f) |
| ν_L, e_L | (−𝟙 \| −+ \| f), (−𝟙 \| +− \| f) |
| ν^c, e^c | (𝟙 \| ++ \| f), (𝟙 \| −− \| f) |

and antiparticles are the negatives. Three families give the 96 roots of H_F, closed under negation; the four family slots are permuted by the Weyl group of SU(4), so the choice of three is immaterial. The same 96 roots are (16, 3) + (16bar, 3bar) in E8 ⊃ E6 × SU(3) ⊃ SO(10) × U(1) × SU(3), the embedding of file 04.

## Proposition 5.1 (the two families of 84 are not isometric)

**Statement.** Let H_F be the 96 roots of three families, the top block T the 12 roots of the up-type quark of one family f_t with its conjugate, and the neutrino block N the 12 roots of the neutrinos of the three families with their conjugates; F1 = H_F − T and F2 = H_F − N. Then F1 and F2 have the same numbers of pairs at each inner product (42 at −2, 816 at −1, 1812 at 0, 816 at +1), but no isometry of R^8 maps F1 onto F2. In particular no element of the Weyl group of E8 does, and no isometry of H_F maps T onto N.

**Proof.** For a root v of a set S let d_S(v) be the number of roots of S at inner product +1 with v; it is preserved by isometries. Two half-integer roots have inner product +1 exactly when their signs agree in six of the eight places.

1. d_{H_F} = 22 for every root. H_F is a single orbit of the group generated by the Weyl group of SO(10) (transitive on the 16, a minuscule representation), the permutations of the three families and the negation. For ν^c of family 0, (𝟙 | ++ | 𝟙), the roots of H_F with exactly two minus signs are: two in the first five places with family 𝟙 (10), two among the family places (2, the patterns of families 1 and 2 in H_F), and antiparticles with one minus sign in each block (5 x 2). The total is 22.
2. Removing N. For u_L = (c | −+ | f): the inner product with ν_L(g) is (1 + f·g)/4 and with ν^c(g) is (1 + f·g)/4, where f·g = 3 for g = f and −1 otherwise; so u_L has exactly 2 roots of N at +1 (its own family's ν_L and ν^c), and the same holds for u^c and the conjugates. For d_L = (c | +− | f): the inner product with ν_L(g) is (−3 + f·g)/4, which is −1 for g ≠ f, so the two roots −ν_L(g), g ≠ f, are at +1, and ν^c(f) is at +1: 3 in all; the same count holds for d^c, e_L, e^c and the conjugates. So in F2, d = 20 on the 36 up-type quark roots and d = 19 on the 48 others.
3. Removing T. For a neutrino of the top's family, ν_L(f_t) = (−𝟙 | −+ | f_t): its inner products with t_L(c) = (c | −+ | f_t) and with t^c = (−c | ++ | f_t) are (−1 + 2 + 3)/4 = 1 and (1 + 0 + 3)/4 = 1, six roots at +1; for g ≠ f_t, ν_L(g) has inner product (−1 + 2 − 1)/4 = 0 with t_L and (1 + 0 − 1)/4 = 0 with t^c. The same holds for ν^c. So in F1, d = 16 on the 4 roots of the neutrinos of the top's family and d = 22 on the 8 roots of the other neutrinos (the up-type quarks of the other families have d = 20 and the down-type and charged leptons d = 19, by the computation of item 2 with the roles of the blocks exchanged).

F1 has roots with d = 16 and d = 22, and F2 has none. The sums of d agree, 4·16 + 48·19 + 24·20 + 8·22 = 48·19 + 36·20 = 1632 = 2 x 816, which is the equality of the pair counts at +1; the other pair counts follow in the same way. ∎

**Scope.** The roots of H_F in E8, through the embedding above, which is canonical up to the Weyl group of E8; isometries of R^8 acting on the roots. Escapes: maps that are not linear on the roots (bijections of states with no geometric meaning), other realizations of H_F in a larger group, and a fourth family (Proposition 5.2). The tau neutrino carries the obstruction: in F1 the neutrino of the top's own family is the only state whose neighbourhood changes by six.

## Proposition 5.2 (four families: the transposition of colour and family)

**Statement.** Let P be the permutation of coordinates that exchanges the three colour coordinates with the three family coordinates and the two weak coordinates with each other, and M = −P. Then M is an involution in the Weyl group of E8 that preserves the 128 roots of the four families and SU(2)_L, and acts on the up-type and down-type states as the transposition of the four Pati-Salam colours (r, g, b, lepton) with the four families (1, 2, 3, 0), with the lepton paired with family 0 and the colour c paired with the family −c. In particular:

1. the up-type quark of family 0 (t_L and t^c, three colours, with conjugates) goes onto the neutrinos of families 1, 2, 3 (ν_L and the conjugate of ν^c), and back;
2. the down-type quark of family 0 goes onto the charged leptons of families 1, 2, 3;
3. the leptons of family 0 are fixed, and the quarks of families 1, 2, 3 go to quarks of families 1, 2, 3 with colour and family transposed.

So the four-family sets 128 − T(0) and 128 − N(1, 2, 3), each of 116 roots, are exchanged.

**Proof.** P is an even permutation of coordinates and −1 is in the Weyl group of E8, so M is in it; M^2 = P^2 = 1. Both preserve the set of half-integer vectors with an even number of minus signs. P maps (χ | w_1 w_2 | φ) to (φ | w_2 w_1 | χ), so M maps it to (−φ | −w_2 −w_1 | −χ). The weak part (−+) goes to (−+) and (++) to (−−), so T_3 of SU(2)_L, (w_2 − w_1)/2, is preserved and T_3 of SU(2)_R is reversed. Then t_L(c) = (c | −+ | 𝟙) goes to (−𝟙 | −+ | −c) = ν_L(−c); t^c = (−c | ++ | 𝟙) goes to (−𝟙 | −− | c) = −ν^c(−c); b_L(c) = (c | +− | 𝟙) goes to (−𝟙 | +− | −c) = e_L(−c); ν_L(0) = (−𝟙 | −+ | 𝟙) and e_L(0) are fixed; u_L(c, f) = (c | −+ | f) goes to u_L(−f, −c), with −f a quark colour and −c a family pattern. ∎

The search found further signed permutations with the same effect on the two blocks; P itself maps T(0) onto N(1, 2, 3) up to conjugation, and M = J ∘ P, with J = −1 the real structure of Proposition 4.6.

## Readings (proposals)

1. **Three families are the obstruction.** The exchange of lemma 21 is a symmetry of the E8 geometry exactly in the four-family completion, where the colour-family square is 4 x 4 and the top belongs to the family paired with the lepton colour. With three families the square is 4 x 3 and the exchange is not an isometry (Proposition 5.1). Lemma 21's 96 = 84 + 12 in two ways is the three-family shadow of 128 = 116 + 12 in two ways, exchanged by M.
2. **The two heavy directions of the algebra chain.** Canonical §2.2 and §3 obtain the chain Pati-Salam -> Standard Model -> colour plus electromagnetism from two heavy operators: the Majorana direction, which selects the lepton among the four Pati-Salam colours and ν_R among the SU(2)_R states, and the top Yukawa coupling, which selects the heavy family and the up-type state. M exchanges the lepton colour with family 0 and keeps the up-type direction: it exchanges the direction selected by the first heavy operator with the direction selected by the second. If the 11 label is the grand algebra M_2(H) + M_4(C) (lemma 28) and the 9 label colour plus electromagnetism, M exchanges the two steps of 11 -> 10 -> 9. This needs a finite geometry in which the generation space is C^4 with an algebra exchangeable with M_4(C), and a Dirac operator compatible with the exchange.
3. **Four families and eleven dimensions.** The 128 of the four families is the chiral spinor of SO(16) ⊂ E8; under Spin(9) ⊂ SO(16), embedded through its spinor, it decomposes as 44 + 84, the graviton and the three-form of eleven-dimensional supergravity (Pengpan and Ramond, hep-th/9808190; Kostant's cubic Dirac operator on F4/Spin(9); recalled). The two families of 116 are not Spin(9) subrepresentations; whether the exchange M has a Spin(9) meaning is open.
4. **NCG.** In the finite geometry with three generations the exchange is not an automorphism (Proposition 4.2). A Pati-Salam finite geometry with C^4_colour ⊗ C^4_family, where the flip of the two factors exchanges the σ field with the top Yukawa direction, is the candidate in which it is (PLAN, step 5).

## What remains open

- A finite spectral triple, or a Hořava-Witten configuration, with four families in which M is a symmetry and the fourth family is decoupled in a controlled way, so that the three-family statement of lemma 21 follows as a limit.
- Whether the fourth family can be made heavy by the same mechanism as the top (lemma 21 counts only one heavy up-type quark).
