1. **Proposition 25.1 — Correct with amendment.**

   The unfolding in [lemma 25](../lemmas/25_sum_rule_effective_dimension.md) is correct after multiplying it by \(1/\sqrt2\). It maps the \(H^1\) domain of \(D_0\) onto the periodic \(H^1\) domain on the circle. With
   \[
   D_F=\begin{pmatrix}0&M^*\\M&0\end{pmatrix},
   \]
   the connection term satisfies
   \[
   E\Gamma_FD_F:\quad
   \Psi_Q\mapsto M^*\Psi_U,\qquad
   \Psi_U\mapsto M\Psi_Q.
   \]
   Thus the unfolded operator is \(-i\partial_x+D_F\), with signed spectrum \(n\pi/l+\mu_i\).

   The amendment is to name the frame. Corollary 22.2 states
   \[
   D_X=R(D_0+E\Gamma_FD_F)R^*,\qquad R(x)=e^{x\Gamma_FD_F}.
   \]
   An even \(T\) that is constant **after transport** unfolds to \(1\otimes T\). In the original frame it is \(R(x)TR(x)^*\), which generally varies with \(x\). Conversely, a constant original-frame \(T\) becomes \(R^*TR\) after transport and generally does not unfold to \(1\otimes T\). For broken \(T\), it also need not preserve the original coupled wall domain. The transported field generally has an odd bulk component and therefore requires an admissible enlarged gauge algebra; it is not automatically a field of the even algebra in lemma 22.

   There is a separate error in the holonomy sentence at [line 31](../lemmas/25_sum_rule_effective_dimension.md). With the stated convention, put \(U=e^{-i\pi\Gamma_0/4}\). The correct relation is
   \[
   \Gamma_1\Gamma_0
   =e^{2l\Gamma_0D_F}
   =Ue^{2ilD_F}U^*.
   \]
   Conjugation by \(\Gamma_0\) instead sends \(e^{2ilD_F}\) to its inverse. The circle holonomy can acquire the inverse sign under the opposite parallel-transport convention.

2. **Theorem 25.2 — Correct with amendment.**

   The exact kernel at [line 39](../lemmas/25_sum_rule_effective_dimension.md) and its quadratic coefficient have the stated signs. Lemma 18 states its theorem for a finite internal trace; an extension to this infinite tower must be supplied. Here it is available: \(V\) and \(T\) split into finite-dimensional blocks indexed by \(n\). Applying the finite theorem to a symmetric finite set of blocks and passing to the limit is justified by Gaussian bounds, uniform for \(D_F\) in a bounded neighborhood of zero. The same bounds justify differentiation and Taylor expansion under the sum.

   Let \(t=1\), \(d=\mu_i-\mu_j\), \(b=u(1-u)\), and
   \[
   E(u)=e^{-[(1-u)(k+\mu_i)^2+u(k+\mu_j)^2]}.
   \]
   An exact integration-by-parts form of the \(q=0\) bracket is
   \[
   d^2\int_0^1E(u)
       \bigl[1-b(2k+\mu_i+\mu_j)^2\bigr]\,du.
   \]
   Its expansion begins
   \[
   d^2e^{-k^2}\left[
       1-\frac23k^2+
       \frac{2k^3-5k}{3}(\mu_i+\mu_j)+O(\|\mu\|^2)
   \right].
   \]
   The displayed cubic term is odd in \(k\) and cancels between \(n\) and \(-n\). Similarly, the \(q^2\) bracket begins
   \[
   e^{-k^2}\left[\frac23-\frac{2k}{3}(\mu_i+\mu_j)
       +O(\|\mu\|^2)\right],
   \]
   and its linear term cancels. The grading symmetry gives the same evenness for the summed expression.

   The proof should include these cancellations and remainder terms. At fixed \(s=l\Lambda\), for fixed nonzero Hermitian \(T\), the stated \(O(\|D_F\|^3/\Lambda)\) mass remainder is valid but can be sharpened to
   \[
   M_{\rm Taylor}^2
   =\left(\frac32-\langle k^2\rangle\right)
     \frac{\|[D_F,T]\|_{\rm HS}^2}{\|T\|_{\rm HS}^2}
     +O\!\left(\frac{\|D_F\|^4}{\Lambda^2}\right).
   \]
   Here \(k_n=n\pi/(l\Lambda)\) is dimensionless. Equations presented later without a remainder must be labeled leading-order formulas.

3. **Spectral dimension — Error.**

   The factor at [line 60](../lemmas/25_sum_rule_effective_dimension.md) is wrong. Direct differentiation gives
   \[
   \boxed{\displaystyle
   \langle k^2\rangle
     =\frac12\,\frac{d\log S}{d\log s}
     =\frac{s}{2}\,\frac{d\log S}{ds}.}
   \]
   The extra \(s\) multiplying the logarithmic derivative must be deleted. The subsequent coefficient \((3-d_{\rm eff})/2\) uses the corrected identity.

   The spectral-dimension interpretation is correct for the **unshifted unfolded circle**, equivalently the paired Neumann-plus-Dirichlet interval trace. It should not be called the spectral dimension of an arbitrary interval boundary problem. Indeed, their separate heat traces are
   \[
   S_N=\frac{S+1}{2},\qquad S_D=\frac{S-1}{2},
   \]
   and have different logarithmic derivatives. For nonzero \(D_F\), the actual parent heat trace contains \(e^{-t(k_n+\mu_i)^2}\); \(S\) is the unperturbed trace used in the leading expansion.

4. **The two limits and covariance — Correct with amendment.**

   The limiting factors \(3/2\) and \(1\) are correct. The exponential corrections have polynomial prefactors. More precisely,
   \[
   d_{\rm eff}(s)\sim\frac{4\pi^2}{s^2}e^{-\pi^2/s^2}
       \quad(s\to0),\qquad
   d_{\rm eff}(s)=1-4s^2e^{-s^2}
       +O(s^2e^{-2s^2})\quad(s\to\infty).
   \]
   State the order of limits: first take the small-\(D_F/\Lambda\) coefficient at fixed \(s\), then take either limit.

   The five-dimensional covariance argument is valid on the unfolded circle for this operator and these insertions. With Hermitian connections the curvature convention gives \(F_{5\mu}=i[D_F,A_\mu]\), up to the sign convention for the covariant derivative; its norm gives the quoted commutator quotient.

   On a general space with boundary, the bulk local coefficient alone does not rule out boundary contributions. In the present calculation the full operator with the specified transported fields is unitarily equivalent to the circle problem, so its trace has the required cancellation. State that specific reason rather than a general boundary assertion.

5. **Corollary 25.3 — Correct with amendment.**

   The coefficients agree with [Remark 16 of lemma 18](../lemmas/18_heavy_limit_near_cutoff.md) and with the [CCM mass relation, Eq. (4.16)](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0610241#S4.SS3) at leading order. With the literal particle-and-antiparticle trace,
   \[
   Q_{11}=12,\qquad K_{11}=\sum_fN_fm_f^2,
   \]
   which gives \((3-d_{\rm eff})/24\). For the top alone, \(K_{11}=3m_t^2\), giving the displayed ratio and
   \[
   y_t=\frac{2g}{\sqrt3}\quad(d_{\rm eff}=0),\qquad
   y_t=\sqrt2\,g\quad(d_{\rm eff}=1).
   \]
   The first is also CCM’s top-dominance approximation, [Eq. (5.27)](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0610241#S5.SS4).

   The neutral result \(M_W^2/M_Z^2=5/8\) follows **to this order**, with the common trace and normalization. “Massless directions are unaffected” should say that their mass coefficient remains zero; their kinetic coefficients can change.

   The twelve doublets have the same tower factor \(S\) in the stipulated flat, bulk-mass-free model, including when only the top has nonzero Dirac mass. With bulk masses used to localize light fields, their spectra and overlap matrix elements change. The kinetic coefficient must then be recomputed from all eigenstates and overlaps; it cannot generally be obtained by assigning the same \(S\) to every doublet. Light zero modes can still contribute even when their excited modes are suppressed. For an explicit localization mechanism and profiles, see [Adachi, Kurahashi and Maru, arXiv:1404.4281v1, Eqs. (2.10)–(2.11)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.4281).

6. **General cutoffs — Correct with amendment.**

   The weighted formula is correct for the positive Laplace class and moment hypotheses in [lemma 18, Eq. (4)](../lemmas/18_heavy_limit_near_cutoff.md). It is a leading small-\(D_F\) formula. A precise version is
   \[
   S_\nu(s)=\int S(s/\sqrt{\tau})\,d\nu(\tau),\qquad
   d_\nu=\frac{d\log S_\nu}{d\log s}
        =2\langle\tau k^2\rangle_\nu,
   \]
   so that the coefficient is \((3-d_\nu)/2\). Under those moment assumptions, dominated convergence gives the stated endpoints. The crossover and its corrections depend on \(\nu\).

   Replace “general cutoffs” by “positive Laplace cutoffs satisfying (4).” A general smooth spectral cutoff, including a nonconstant cutoff flat near zero, need not belong to that class.
