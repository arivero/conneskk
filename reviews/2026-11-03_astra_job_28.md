**Proposition 1 — Endpoint restrictions and the twist.**

Let \(X=D(\Lambda^2_-T^*S^4)\), with zero section \(B=S^4\) and boundary \(Y=\mathbb{CP}^3\). Then restriction is surjective:
\[
C^\infty(X,\mathbb R)\longrightarrow
C^\infty(B,\mathbb R)\oplus C^\infty(Y,\mathbb R).
\]
Moreover, in smooth normal coordinates \(v\),
\[
a(y,v)=a(y,0)+O(|v|),
\]
uniformly on bounded subsets of \(C^1(X)\).

*Proof.* Extend a function on \(B\) by the bundle projection. Extend a function on \(Y\) into a collar of \(Y\). Cutoffs with disjoint supports near \(B\) and \(Y\) combine the extensions. Taylor’s formula proves the estimate. ∎

Thus the restriction to base functions at the collapsed end, and independence of the two endpoint values, follow from the geometry.

Proposition 28.1 stands as a statement about bundles: \(X\) retracts onto \(S^4\), so nontrivial \(\mathcal O(k)\) cannot extend as a line bundle. The extension
\[
E=p^*\operatorname{Sym}^3 S,\qquad
E|_Y\simeq\mathcal O(3)\oplus\mathcal O(1)
 \oplus\mathcal O(-1)\oplus\mathcal O(-3)
\]
also stands. Its splitting on \(Y\) is a bundle splitting. **The pulled-back connection does not preserve this splitting.**

**Theorem 2 — A smooth self-adjoint realization.**

Choose the connection metric
\[
g=dr^2+h(r)^2\pi^*g_{S^4}+\rho(r)^2g_{S^2},
\]
where the vertical sphere has unit radius, \(h\) is smooth and even near zero, and
\[
h(0)>0,\qquad \rho(r)=r+O(r^3),
\]
with \(\rho\) extending as a smooth odd function near zero. Choose the metric to be a product near \(r=\ell\).

Let \(J_i\) denote **Hermitian** spin-\(3/2\) generators and put
\[
\Phi_M(y,v)=M\,b(|v|^2)\sum_i v_iJ_i,
\]
where \(b\) is smooth and positive. This is a smooth global endomorphism under the associated-bundle construction. Its zero set is \(B\).

Use skew-adjoint Riemannian Clifford multiplication \(c\), so \(c(\xi)^2=-|\xi|^2\), and define
\[
T_M=D_X^E+i\Phi_M,\qquad
\mathscr D_M=
\begin{pmatrix}0&T_M^*\\ T_M&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\widehat\Gamma=
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
At the outer boundary set
\[
Z=-ic(dr),\qquad \chi=\widehat\Gamma Z,
\]
and impose \(\chi\psi=-\psi\).

Then \(\mathscr D_M\), on the resulting \(H^1\) domain, is self-adjoint, elliptic, odd for \(\widehat\Gamma\), and has compact resolvent. Every smooth scalar function preserves its domain.

*Proof.* The involution \(\chi\) anticommutes with the normal principal symbol of \(\mathscr D_M\). Its negative eigenspace is consequently maximal isotropic for the boundary form. It also anticommutes with the principal symbol of the adapted tangential operator. This gives local ellipticity. Elliptic regularity supplies the \(H^1\) graph estimate, and the compact embedding \(H^1(X)\hookrightarrow L^2(X)\) gives compact resolvent. Scalar multiplication commutes with \(\chi\).

The boundary-symbol criterion is precisely the one in Bär–Ballmann, Corollary 4.18; their Example 4.20 discusses boundary chirality. [Guide to boundary value problems, §4.6–4.7](https://arxiv.org/html/1307.3021). ∎

Near \(B\), in Cartesian normal coordinates, the leading expression is
\[
D_X^E=
D_{S^4}^{\,\Sigma_N\otimes E}
+\gamma_{S^4}\otimes D_{\mathbb R^3}
+\text{terms vanishing to first order in the normal coordinate},
\]
where the horizontal connection includes the normal-bundle connection. The apparent radial singularity is the ordinary polar-coordinate expression of \(D_{\mathbb R^3}\):
\[
c(dr)\left(\partial_r+\frac1r+\frac1rD_{S^2}\right),
\]
up to the convention for the adapted sphere operator.

There is no boundary condition at \(B\). More precisely, after fixing the outer boundary condition, removing \(B\) does not create another self-adjoint extension parameter. For a cutoff supported outside the normal \(\varepsilon\)-tube,
\[
\int |d\chi_\varepsilon|^2\,d\mathrm{vol}=O(\varepsilon).
\]
Hence smooth sections can be approximated in the Dirac graph norm by sections vanishing near \(B\). This proves uniqueness across the removed zero section.

The hypothesis \(\rho(r)\sim r\) alone would be insufficient for smoothness; the parity and smooth-extension conditions above supply it.

**Proposition 3 — The three slice operators must be distinguished.**

For the fixed standard complex structure on \(\mathbb{CP}^3\):

1. For every Hermitian metric,
   \[
   \ker\sqrt2(\bar\partial_{\mathcal O(1)}
                 +\bar\partial_{\mathcal O(1)}^*)
   \simeq \bigoplus_q H^{0,q}(\mathbb{CP}^3,\mathcal O(1))
   =\mathbb C^4.
   \]
2. For the Levi-Civita spin Dirac operator twisted by the **separate line bundle** \(\mathcal O(3)\), with its invariant line connection, the fundamental representation \(\mathbf4\) is in the kernel for every invariant squashing.
3. For the Levi-Civita spin Dirac operator twisted by the **full pulled-back connection on \(E\)**, sufficiently small fibres give an invertible slice operator.

*Proof of 1.* Dolbeault Hodge theory holds for compact Hermitian manifolds. The Kähler condition is needed for the usual identification with the Levi-Civita spin Dirac operator, rather than for metric independence of Dolbeault cohomology. Positivity of \(\mathcal O(1)\), or its elementary projective-space cohomology calculation, gives the stated cohomology.

*Proof of 2.* In the charge convention of the displayed isotropy representations in Lemma 28, the counts are
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
\text{twist charge}&-3&-1&1&3\\ \hline
\operatorname{mult}_{\Sigma^+}(\mathbf4)&1&2&2&0\\
\operatorname{mult}_{\Sigma^-}(\mathbf4)&0&2&2&1.
\end{array}
\]
Here the \(\mathcal O(3)\) convention corresponds to the first column. An equivariant odd operator must vanish on its unmatched copy of \(\mathbf4\).

This proves a protected kernel of dimension four. It does not exclude additional kernels in other representations. For the analytic one-parameter family of canonical squashed metrics, additional zeros can occur only at a locally discrete set of parameters: remove the protected \(\mathbf4\), use invertibility at the Fubini–Study metric, and apply analytic elliptic perturbation theory. Determining whether this exceptional set is empty requires a further spectral calculation.

*Proof of 3.* The pulled-back twist has no vertical curvature. For fixed horizontal scale, the scalar curvature of the connection metric satisfies
\[
\operatorname{scal}(g_\rho)=\frac2{\rho^2}+O(1)-O(\rho^2).
\]
The twisting curvature term in the Lichnerowicz formula remains bounded as \(\rho\to0\). Therefore
\[
(D_\rho^E)^2
\geq \frac1{2\rho^2}-C_0-C_1\rho^2,
\]
which is positive for sufficiently small \(\rho\). ∎

This verifies the \(5+5\) count in Proposition 28.2 and limits its consequence. **The full \(E\)-twisted slices do not carry a four-dimensional kernel throughout the collapse.** The proposed Callias window must arise from the competition between the normal Dirac operator and the hedgehog mass.

The scope of this statement is the pulled-back connection and the Levi-Civita Dirac operator. Separate line connections, Dolbeault Dirac operators, and modified collar connections remain available.

**Proposition 4 — The linear normal model has exactly \(3\oplus1\) zero modes.**

Let the normal mass be linear and let
\[
Q=\sigma\cdot\partial_y+y\cdot J
\quad\text{on }L^2(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^4).
\]
Then
\[
\ker Q=0,\qquad
\ker Q^*\simeq\mathbf3\oplus\mathbf1
\]
as representations of the normal \(\mathrm{Sp}(1)\), after fixing the corresponding orientation convention. Changing the mass sign exchanges the two kernels. The normal operator has a positive gap above these zero modes.

*Proof.* Direct multiplication gives
\[
Q^*Q=-\Delta+(y\cdot J)^2-\sigma\cdot J.
\]
For spin \(3/2\),
\[
(y\cdot J)^2\geq \frac{|y|^2}{4},
\qquad
\sigma\cdot J\leq\frac32.
\]
Consequently
\[
Q^*Q\geq -\Delta+\frac{|y|^2}{4}-\frac32\geq0.
\]
Equality would force
\[
\psi(y)=e^{-|y|^2/4}v
\]
and \(J_i^2v=\tfrac14v\) for all three \(i\). Summing contradicts
\[
\sum_iJ_i^2=\frac{15}{4}I
\]
unless \(v=0\).

The equivariant Callias index is obtained from the positive eigenbundles of \(n\cdot J\) on the normal sphere. Their degrees are \(3\) and \(1\). The corresponding sphere spin-Dirac indices are
\[
H^0(\mathbb{CP}^1,\mathcal O(2))
\oplus H^0(\mathbb{CP}^1,\mathcal O(0))
=\mathbf3\oplus\mathbf1.
\]
The vanishing just proved converts this index into an actual kernel count. Finally, the quadratic confining potential gives compact resolvent for the squared normal operators and hence a positive next eigenvalue.

The boundary-index principle used here is the Callias theorem; a families formulation identifying the index with the Dirac index twisted by the positive eigenbundle appears in Kottke, Theorem 11. [Callias index theorem, §5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/0909.5661). ∎

This strengthens Proposition 28.3 for the linear normal model. An index alone would not establish its asserted kernel count.

For \(\Phi_M=M b(0)y\cdot J+O(M|y|^3)\), rescaling \(y=M^{-1/2}z\) gives this normal model. Thus the expected core localization scale is
\[
r=O(M^{-1/2}),
\]
whereas a nonzero mass at the outer wall gives scale \(O(M^{-1})\).

The induced bundle of normal modes is
\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2S\oplus\mathbf1.
\]
On round \(S^4\), the twisted Dirac operator on
\[
\Sigma_{S^4}\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^2S
\]
has exactly four zero modes in one chirality. Indeed, the instanton curvature term vanishes on the opposite spin chirality, whose Lichnerowicz formula is strictly positive; the index has absolute value four. The untwisted summand has no kernel.

The identification with twistor spinors needs the following precision. There is a parallel Clebsch–Gordan inclusion
\[
S\hookrightarrow S\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^2S.
\]
On this subbundle, the twisted Dirac equation is a nonzero multiple of the chiral twistor equation. Its solution space is \(\mathbf4\). These are harmonic spinors **for the twisted operator**; as sections of \(S\), they are twistor spinors.

This also verifies the limiting core compression:
\[
Q_B(x_a)=\pm\frac{\Gamma_a}{5}.
\]
The sign records the chosen identification of the twistor fibration and Clifford generators.

\]

**Lemma 5 — Boundary localization retains tangential mixing.**

Consider a product collar, with inward coordinate \(t=\ell-r\), and two stable mass eigenvalues \(Mq_i>0\). Their normalized normal profiles are
\[
e_{i,M}(t)=\sqrt{2Mq_i}\,e^{-Mq_i t}.
\]
If \(A_{ij}\) is a tangential coupling between the corresponding eigenbundles, its compression to these profiles is
\[
\langle e_{i,M},e_{j,M}\rangle A_{ij}
=
\frac{2\sqrt{q_iq_j}}{q_i+q_j}A_{ij}.
\]
In particular, for \(q_i=\tfrac12\) and \(q_j=\tfrac32\), the factor is
\[
\frac{\sqrt3}{2},
\]
independently of \(M\).

*Proof.* Integrate the two exponentials on the half-line. ∎

Thus increasing \(M\) does not eliminate tangential coupling between the stable \(\mathcal O(3)\) and \(\mathcal O(1)\) channels. In the pulled-back connection, the vertical coupling is of order \(1/\rho(\ell)\). The limiting boundary operator therefore needs to be computed on the combined stable channels.

This explains why the fixed-radius boundary values in Result 28.5 need not equal the level-one Toeplitz values. That result itself reports a mixed boundary mode and an additional large-radius limit. It does not establish the endpoint map in the question at fixed radius.

There is a concrete collar repair. Choose an invariant connection on \(E\) that preserves the four line summands in a collar, and choose the boundary metric to be Fubini–Study. Such a connection extends smoothly into \(X\), using a cutoff between it and the pulled-back connection. The stable boundary model then separates into line-twisted Dirac operators. The \(\mathcal O(3)\) channel has the required four zero modes; the adjacent \(\mathcal O(1)\) channel has none. Its boundary compression is exactly the level-one map.

This repairs the boundary model. A global gluing estimate is still needed to turn the repaired core and boundary models into a theorem about the full compact operator.

**Lemma 6 — A sufficient spectral criterion.**

Let \(U_M:\mathbb C^8\to\operatorname{Dom}\mathscr D_M\) be an isometry and suppose
\[
\|\mathscr D_MU_M\|\leq\varepsilon_M,
\qquad
\|\mathscr D_M\psi\|\geq\delta\|\psi\|
\quad
\text{for }\psi\perp\operatorname{Ran}U_M,
\]
where \(\delta>0\) is independent of \(M\) and \(\varepsilon_M\to0\).

Then, for sufficiently large \(M\), \(\mathscr D_M\) has exactly eight eigenvalues, counted with multiplicity, in \((-\delta,\delta)\), provided the interval endpoints are placed strictly between \(\varepsilon_M\) and \(\delta\). The corresponding spectral space approaches \(\operatorname{Ran}U_M\), with projection error \(O(\varepsilon_M/\delta)\).

*Proof.* Apply the min–max principle to \(\mathscr D_M^2\). The eight-dimensional trial space gives eight small eigenvalues. The coercive estimate on its orthogonal complement excludes a ninth. The projection estimate follows by expanding the trial vectors in the spectral decomposition. ∎

**Theorem 7 — Compression and a quantitative endpoint limit, conditional on localization.**

Assume an eight-dimensional spectral window exists and splits under \(\widehat\Gamma\) as
\[
H_M=W_M\oplus V_M,\qquad \dim_\mathbb C W_M=\dim_\mathbb C V_M=4.
\]
Choose equivariant isometries \(U_{B,M},U_{Y,M}\) from fixed copies of \(\mathbb C^4\).

Then scalar compression is exactly block diagonal:
\[
Q_M(a)=
\begin{pmatrix}
U_{B,M}^*M_aU_{B,M}&0\\
0&U_{Y,M}^*M_aU_{Y,M}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Suppose these isometries differ in operator norm by at most \(\eta_M\) from normalized model embeddings whose angular compressions are \(Q_B,Q_Y\), and whose normal probability measures satisfy
\[
\int r\,d\mu_{B,M}\leq C M^{-1/2},\qquad
\int(\ell-r)\,d\mu_{Y,M}\leq C M^{-1}.
\]
Then
\[
\left\|Q_M(a)-
\bigl(Q_B(a|_B)\oplus Q_Y(a|_Y)\bigr)\right\|
\leq
C\|a\|_{C^1}M^{-1/2}
+4\|a\|_\infty\eta_M.
\tag{1}
\]

*Proof.* Scalars commute with \(\widehat\Gamma\), proving block diagonality. Replacing an isometry by another changes compression by at most twice its norm difference times \(\|a\|_\infty\). Taylor’s estimate from Proposition 1, followed by the normal first-moment bounds, proves (1). ∎

The comparison is on fixed four-dimensional spaces, through the specified isometries. For the desired maps,
\[
Q_B(f)=\int_{S^4}f(y)(I\pm y_a\Gamma_a)\,d\mu_B(y),
\qquad
Q_Y(g)=4\int_{\mathbb{CP}^3}g(z)p_z\,d\mu_Y(z),
\tag{2}
\]
where \(p_z\) is the rank-one projector and both measures are probability measures.

Equation (1) is a proved implication. The required \(\eta_M\) estimate and the identification of \(Q_Y\) with the second map in (2) remain unproved for the original connection.

For the local boundary condition of Theorem 2, the domain algebra is all \(C^\infty(X)\). For the spectral walls of Lemma 24, the domain algebra has constant boundary values. If \(a|_Y=c\), then any boundary-localized family satisfying the moment bound obeys
\[
\|Q_{Y,M}(a)-cI\|
\leq C M^{-1}\|\partial_ra\|_\infty.
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently, localization just inside a spectral wall does not recover arbitrary boundary functions for a fixed smooth \(a\).

Mass-dependent functions can evade (3) by changing on the localization scale. Their derivatives grow with \(M\), so they do not give the asserted limit on a fixed \(C^1\)-bounded scalar algebra. A local wall, or an explicitly different comparison algebra, is needed for that limit.

**Proposition 8 — The generated algebra requires an exact symmetry test.**

For an equivariant copy of \(\mathbf4\), write its radial evaluation map as
\[
s_v(r,g)=F(r)\lambda(g)^{-1}v,
\]
absorbing the radial volume density into \(F\). Let
\[
B(r)=F(r)^*F(r).
\]
Since
\[
\mathbf4|_H=\mathbf2_0\oplus\mathbf1_1\oplus\mathbf1_{-1},
\]
we have
\[
B(r)=
\operatorname{diag}\bigl(b_a(r)I_2,b_+(r),b_-(r)\bigr).
\tag{4}
\]

For all real scalar functions to compress to operators commuting with the standard quaternionic antiunitary \(j\), the necessary and sufficient condition is
\[
b_+(r)=b_-(r)\quad\text{for almost every }r.
\tag{5}
\]

If the \(\mathbf5\) component is nonzero, condition (5) gives generated real algebra \(M_2(\mathbb H)\). If both the \(\mathbf5\) and \(\mathbf{10}\) components are nonzero, the generated real algebra is \(M_4(\mathbb C)\).

*Proof.* The antiunitary \(j\) preserves the two-dimensional summand and exchanges the two one-dimensional summands. Therefore \(jB(r)j^{-1}=B(r)\) exactly when (5) holds. Testing arbitrary scalar functions, including radial cutoffs, makes this pointwise density condition equivalent to commutation of every compressed multiplier.

Under conjugation by \(\mathrm{Sp}(2)\),
\[
\operatorname{Herm}(\mathbb C^4)
=\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf5\oplus\mathbf{10}.
\]
The first two pieces are \(\operatorname{Herm}_2(\mathbb H)\); their products generate \(M_2(\mathbb H)\). Adding a nonzero equivariant \(\mathbf{10}\) component supplies its full orbit and hence all Hermitian complex matrices. ∎

In particular, a single reported value
\[
\int\operatorname{tr}(B(r)\widehat t)\,dr=0
\]
does not prove (5). Radially weighted test functions can detect cancellation in that integral.

Independence of the two **generated** blocks has a simple exact criterion. If some radial \(h\) satisfies
\[
Q_M(h)=\alpha I_{W_M}\oplus\beta I_{V_M},
\qquad \alpha\ne\beta,
\]
then
\[
e_W=\frac{Q_M(h)-\beta I}{\alpha-\beta}
\]
is the first central block projection. Separated localization guarantees such an \(h\) for sufficiently large mass.

Thus, at finite mass, the algebra can be
\[
M_4(\mathbb C)\oplus M_4(\mathbb C)
\]
if the core block has any nonzero symmetry-breaking \(\mathbf{10}\) density. It equals the proposed real algebra only after proving the exact core condition (5). Grading always keeps it inside the two-block algebra; it cannot become \(M_8(\mathbb C)\) under these hypotheses.

At the desired limit, the real linear image is
\[
\operatorname{Herm}_2(\mathbb H)\oplus
\operatorname{Herm}_4(\mathbb C),
\]
of real dimension \(6+16=22\). The generated real algebra has dimension \(16+32=48\).

**Counterexample 9 — Algebra generation is discontinuous under compression-map convergence.**

Let \(E_{\mathrm{fib}}\) denote fibre averaging on \(\mathbb{CP}^3\), and define
\[
R_0=Q_B E_{\mathrm{fib}},\qquad R_1=Q_Y,\qquad
R_\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)R_0+\varepsilon R_1.
\]
These are unital completely positive maps and
\[
\|R_\varepsilon-R_0\|\leq2\varepsilon.
\]
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), real functions generate \(M_4(\mathbb C)\) under \(R_\varepsilon\). At \(\varepsilon=0\), they generate \(M_2(\mathbb H)\).

*Proof.* Both maps retain the nonzero \(\mathbf5\) component. Fibre averaging kills the \(\mathbf{10}\), while \(R_1\) retains it. Apply Proposition 8. ∎

The correct limiting assertion is therefore convergence of specified compression maps, such as (1). It does not by itself assert convergence of the generated algebras or of their unit balls.

**Proposition 10 — Charge conjugation and the wall.**

Let \(C_7\) be spin charge conjugation in dimension seven, and let \(j_E\) be the quaternionic structure of \(\operatorname{Sym}^3S\). With the Hermitian-generator convention,
\[
C_7^2=1,\qquad C_7D_X=D_XC_7,
\]
\[
j_E^2=-1,\qquad j_EJ_i j_E^{-1}=-J_i.
\]
Thus \(C=C_7\otimes j_E\) satisfies, as a differential-expression symmetry,
\[
C^2=-1,\qquad
CT_M=T_MC,\qquad
C\mathscr D_M=\mathscr D_MC.
\]
It commutes with \(\widehat\Gamma\) and reverses \(Z\):
\[
CZ=-ZC.
\]
Consequently
\[
C\chi=-\chi C.
\]
It exchanges the two local wall conditions and does not preserve the chosen single-wall domain.

*Proof.* Antilinearity reverses \(i\), while \(j_E\) reverses the Hermitian Higgs generators, so their product preserves \(i\Phi_M\). Since \(Z=-ic(dr)\), charge conjugation reverses \(Z\). The boundary assertion follows. ∎

This checks the main signs argument of Remark 28.7, while correcting its ambiguous statement about “commuting with the generators”: quaternionic conjugation commutes with the anti-Hermitian Lie algebra action and reverses Hermitian angular-momentum generators.

The limiting core quaternionic structure is supplied by the normal-mode construction and the twisted \(S^4\) kernel. The formal parent symmetry does **not** prove that the finite-mass core spectral subspace is preserved by \(C\), because \(C\) changes the wall domain.

There is also an exact restriction: if a pointwise parent antiunitary preserves a window and commutes with real multiplication, every real compressed multiplier commutes with its restriction. Such a restriction cannot commute with a generated full \(M_4(\mathbb C)\) block, which contains \(iI\). The complex boundary block therefore requires the stated loss of that inherited symmetry, or a different real correspondence.

A phase choice makes \(\lambda_M\geq0\), and

in that choice.

*Proof.* The off-diagonal map is an intertwiner between irreducible complex \(\mathrm{Sp}(2)\) representations. Apply Schur’s lemma. ∎

*Construction and proof.* Let

P_y=\frac12(I+y_a\Gamma_a)

be the rank-two quaternionic projector over \(S^4\), and let \(p_z\leq P_{\pi(z)}\) be the tautological rank-one projector over \(\mathbb{CP}^3\).

Use scalar multiplication on

Invariant integration makes both maps isometries.
