Throughout, let \(\ell>0\), and write \(D=D_F\), \(\Gamma=\Gamma_F\), and \(J=J_F\) when no confusion can arise.

**1. [Setting](../lemmas/22_dirac_operator_as_boundary_condition.md) — Correct.**

In bases related by \(c\), the condition on \(M\) says that its matrix is complex symmetric. Let its Takagi factorization be
\[
M=U\Sigma U^T.
\]
Then
\[
|M|=\overline U\,\Sigma U^T,\qquad V=UU^T
\]
give \(M=V|M|\), with \(V\) unitary and \(V=cV^*c\). This construction includes the kernel: zero entries of \(\Sigma\) still have corresponding columns in the unitary \(U\). The extension of the polar factor on the kernel need not be unique.

The formula
\[
X=\tan(\ell|M|)V^*
\]
and the reality condition \(cX=X^*c^{-1}\) follow. Conversely, for every \(X\) satisfying that condition, the principal functional calculus gives the unique operator
\[
D_F=\ell^{-1}\arctan
 \begin{pmatrix}0&X\\X^*&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \ell\|D_F\|<\pi/2.
\]
Thus the claimed bijection is valid. The expansion \(X=\ell M^*+O(\ell^3)\) assumes fixed \(M\).

**2. [Theorem 22.1](../lemmas/22_dirac_operator_as_boundary_condition.md) — Correct with amendment.**

The boundary subspaces are Lagrangian for the first-order boundary form, giving self-adjointness; compactness of the \(H^1\)-to-\(L^2\) embedding gives compact resolvent. Direct substitution verifies the stated signs and all three intertwining relations. The \(L\)- and \(R\)-components are orthogonal, while \(R(x)=e^{x\Gamma_FD_F}\) is unitary, so \(W\) is an isometry.

For each singular value \(\sigma_k\), with \(\theta_k=\ell\sigma_k\), the signed spectrum is
\[
\frac{n\pi\pm\theta_k}{\ell},\qquad n\in\mathbb Z,
\]
with multiplicities added when values coincide. This gives the stated spectrum of \(|D_X|\), the window, and the lower bound on the next level. The amendment is to state multiplicities, particularly when \(\sigma_k=0\): the zero mode then has multiplicity two per zero singular channel, and the two excited branches coincide.

For arbitrary signed momentum \(k\), the scalar equation is
\[
|\tan(k\ell)|=\sigma_k(X).
\]
For the positive light mass \(k=\sigma_k(D_F)\), its tangent is nonnegative and equals the corresponding singular value of \(X\).

**3. [Corollary 22.2](../lemmas/22_dirac_operator_as_boundary_condition.md) — Correct.**

On the uncoupled domain, multiplication by \(R\) maps the endpoint conditions to those of \(D_X\), and

R^*D_XR=D_0+E\Gamma_FD_F.
\]
For one real channel, \(\Gamma_FD_F=mF\), with \(F\) as defined in [equation (21) of lemma 20](../lemmas/20_connecting_operator.md). The sign and the placement of the spin matrix \(E\) agree.

**4. [Standard Model form and boundary types](../lemmas/22_dirac_operator_as_boundary_condition.md) — Error.**

The type assignment holds **at \(X=0\)**: \(Q\) has type (a) and \(U\) has type (b). For \(X\ne0\), the wall conditions couple the fields, and the corresponding zero modes generally lift. For example, one channel with \(X=t>0\) has lowest masses \(\pm\arctan(t)/\ell\) and no zero mode.

The correction is to say that the coupled modes continue from the type-(a) and type-(b) zero modes of the uncoupled problem.

One matrix \(X\) does encode the full finite operator, including Yukawa and Majorana data. Its entries need not have only the original block pattern: the tangent introduces higher powers of \(D_F\). For example, with real symmetric
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
the corresponding entry of \(X=\tan(\ell M)\) is
\[
X_{22}=\frac{\ell^3}{3}ab^2+O(\ell^5).
\]
Thus the original zero block can acquire an induced entry.

**5. [Theorem 22.3](../lemmas/22_dirac_operator_as_boundary_condition.md) — Correct with amendment.**

Assume explicitly that the finite algebra and its faithful even representation are unital. The two endpoint intertwining equations are equivalent to
\[
[\pi(f(\ell)),D_F]=0.
\]
The commutant of a self-adjoint operator is closed under products and adjoints. Hence \(C_F\), and consequently \(A_\ell\), are unital \(*\)-algebras.

For every \(f\in A_\ell\), multiplication preserves the whole \(H^1\) domain and
\[
[D_X,\pi(f)]=E\pi(f')
\]
there. The right side extends to a bounded operator on all of \(H_\ell\). The opposite action also preserves the domain: this follows from domain preservation by \(g^*\) and \(J^\wedge D_X=D_XJ^\wedge\). Finite order zero then gives
\[
[[D_X,\pi(f)],\pi^\circ(g)]
 =E[\pi(f'),\pi^\circ(g)]=0.
\]
No finite order-one hypothesis is needed.

This agrees with [Counterexample 4 of lemma 20](../lemmas/20_connecting_operator.md): its parent has order one, while its two-point compression fails finite order one. The statement concerns this parent and its pointwise algebra; it does not assert that arbitrary compressions preserve either order condition.

The Standard Model identification of \(C_F\) also needs the usual nonzero Yukawa data and the specified representation. For example, if all Yukawa blocks vanish and only an algebra-commuting Majorana block remains, the commutant is larger.

**6. [Theorem 22.4](../lemmas/22_dirac_operator_as_boundary_condition.md) — Correct with amendment.**

Both compression formulas, including the minus sign in \(Q_\ell(ET)\), and the symmetric exponential average for constant \(a\) are correct. The four items hold with the stated constants. In particular, interpreting \(\|a\|\) as \(\|\pi(a)\|\),
\[
\|Q_\ell(a)-\pi(a)\|
 \le \frac{\ell^2}{6}\|[D,[D,\pi(a)]]\|
 \le \frac23\ell^2\|D\|^2\|\pi(a)\|.
\]
The constants in item 4 are respectively \(8/3\) and \(16/3\).

For item 1, compactly supported bulk functions suffice to test every smooth matrix-entry density. Finite-dimensional linear algebra then gives equality of the two images. The constant function \(a\) itself need not belong to \(A_\ell\); the theorem supplies a domain-preserving function with the same compression.

The general warning about generated algebras is sound. It also has a simple analytic example. Let
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\0&0&1\\1&1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
D=\begin{pmatrix}0&M\\M&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
\pi(\mathbb C^3)=\{\operatorname{diag}(a,a):a\text{ diagonal}\},
\]
with \(J=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&K\\K&0\end{psmallmatrix}\). If \(P_1,P_2\) are the first two diagonal channel projections, then on either graded block
\[
[Q_\ell(P_1),Q_\ell(P_2)]_{12}
   =-\frac{\ell^2}{3}+O(\ell^4).
\]
The original projections commute, whereas their compressed images generate a noncommutative algebra for sufficiently small nonzero \(\ell\).

The specific dimensions 15 and 30 remain **numerical claims**: the matrices and closure calculation were not retained, and the reported tolerance alone does not establish an exact rank. They should be accompanied by reproducible data and an exact or certified rank calculation. The general role of operator systems under spectral truncation is already developed in [Connes and van Suijlekom, §1 and §3, arXiv:2004.14115v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2004.14115).

**7. [Theorem 22.5](../lemmas/22_dirac_operator_as_boundary_condition.md) — Correct with amendment.**

Every represented self-adjoint one-form is \(E\pi(h)\), with \(h^*=-h\). In fact **every smooth** such \(h\) arises, including those with nonzero boundary value: set
\[
b(x)=\int_\ell^x h(t)\,dt.
\]
Then \(b(\ell)=0\), so \(b\in A_\ell\), and \([D_X,b]=E\pi(h)\).

Thus imposing compact support would exclude some connection profiles, although it loses no endpoint holonomy in the identity component: any smooth path from \(1\) to such a unitary can be made constant near both endpoints.

The transport formula and wall condition are correct. The proof should separate its two cases. For a general \(w\), \(\pi(w)^*D_F\pi(w)\) need not commute with \(J_F\), so the non-real part uses the spectral and isometry proof of Theorem 22.1 **without** its real-structure hypothesis. For the real fluctuation, \(U_w=\pi(w)J_F\pi(w)J_F^{-1}\) commutes with \(J_F\), and the claimed real window follows.

There is a useful stronger statement. Put
\[
\widetilde u(x)=u(x)w^*.
\]
Then \(\widetilde u(\ell)=1\), so \(\widetilde u\in A_\ell\). With
\[
\widetilde U=\pi(\widetilde u)J_F\pi(\widetilde u)J_F^{-1},
\]
direct differentiation and the endpoint condition give
\[
D_X+A+J^\wedge A(J^\wedge)^{-1}
   =\widetilde U D_X\widetilde U^*.
\]
Thus every scalar connection in this interval model is gauge equivalent to the original parent under its allowed endpoint gauge group.

**8. [Corollary 22.6](../lemmas/22_dirac_operator_as_boundary_condition.md) — Error.**

The gauge-orbit comparison itself is correct. [Chamseddine–Connes–van Suijlekom, §III.2, equations (10)–(11), arXiv:1304.7583v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1304.7583) give the quadratic fluctuation and its gauge transformation. Here the correct substitution is \(v=w^*\), so the moving window carries
\[
U_vD_FU_v^*=U_w^*D_FU_w.
\]
For the two-point example, if \(w=\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\alpha},e^{i\beta})\), the upper link is \(m e^{-2i(\alpha-\beta)}\). It is \(m e^{2i\psi}\) in lemma 19 when \(\psi\) is defined using \(v=w^*\).

The error is the assertion that fixed compression is the first derivative of the transported window for the **same** connection. Let \(h\) be constant and replace it by \(\varepsilon h\). At fixed \(D\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_0D_{\rm transported}
   &=-\ell[D,h],\\
Q_\ell(Eh)&=-\frac{\ell}{2}[D,h]+O(\ell^3).
\end{aligned}
\]
For example, take \(D=m\sigma_1\) and \(h=i\sigma_3\). Their leading terms differ by a factor of two. The real fluctuation doubles both expressions in this example and retains the discrepancy.

The correction is to state that the displayed fixed-compression expression is a tangent commutator with its own weighted generator. The moving spectral projection contributes to the derivative of the transported operator. The comparison with lemma 14 is a common transport principle, with its global path qualification; lemma 13 computes a distinct fixed compression. The first open point of lemma 19 is answered only on the specified gauge orbits and with the moving window.

**9. [No-go theorem 22.7](../lemmas/22_dirac_operator_as_boundary_condition.md) — Correct with amendment.**

For the stated one-interval, zero-bulk-mass parent, fixed \(X\), even scalar inner one-forms, and the **moving spectral window**, the finite operator is unitarily conjugate to \(D_F\). Its singular values are therefore fixed. The author’s argument and the direct endpoint-preserving gauge transformation in item 7 give the same conclusion in this scope. A fixed compression can show changes in singular values at second order, so the window must be named.

For the usual Standard Model representation with nonzero Yukawa data, the normalized shifted Higgs quaternion runs through unit quaternions, a three-real-dimensional gauge orbit. These are gauge-orbit directions. Calling them physical or eaten modes requires a spacetime gauge action and its boundary conditions; the interval calculation alone supplies no such mode count. Degenerate data, including vanishing Yukawas, can have a smaller orbit.

The listed changes of wall coupling, admissible odd bulk terms, bulk masses, or internal dimension lie outside the theorem’s hypotheses and are directions for further constructions. Curvature in higher dimensions alone does not prove a radial mode. Likewise, a quantum effective action on the same fixed configuration space does not by itself add a radial coordinate; an additional composite or other degree of freedom would have to be identified.

**10. [Proposition 22.8](../lemmas/22_dirac_operator_as_boundary_condition.md) — Correct with amendment.**

Evaluation shows that the unsymmetrized gauge one-form has boundary value in \(C_F\). For the displayed masses, assume a flat product interval, a positive Yang–Mills kinetic form, the trivial background, unrestricted endpoint variation in the allowed directions, and no additional boundary kinetic or mass terms. Variation then gives Neumann conditions at \(0\), and Neumann on the wall Lie algebra and Dirichlet on its orthogonal complement at \(\ell\). The stated integer and half-integer towers follow.

The physical gauge Lie algebra is obtained from skew-adjoint algebra elements through the real gauge representation, with its kernel and any imposed unimodularity condition accounted for. For the usual Standard Model representation, \(u(C_F)\) has dimension 10 before unimodularity, whereas photon plus gluons have dimension 9. The extra abelian direction must be addressed. The role of unimodularity and the hypercharge embedding is explicit in [Chamseddine–Connes–Marcolli, §2.5, arXiv:hep-th/0610241](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0610241).

The literature comparison is valid at the level of the boundary mechanism. The Neumann/Dirichlet towers appear in [Csáki et al., §5.1, arXiv:hep-ph/0305237v1](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-ph/0305237), and the fermion equation \(\tan(m_nL)=M_DL\) is [equation (5.6) of Csáki et al., arXiv:hep-ph/0310355v1](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-ph/0310355). **11. [Corollary 22.9](../lemmas/22_dirac_operator_as_boundary_condition.md) — Correct with amendment.**

Items 1, 2, 4 and 5 hold for fixed, orthogonal, \(J_F\)- and grading-compatible light and heavy channels, with \(0<\Theta<\pi/2\). For a general non-normal light block, write the upper wall block as
\[
X_0=\tan(\ell|M_0|)V_0^*,
\]
or state the formula in terms of singular values. The light-channel coupling is \(\ell M_0^*+O(\ell^3)\). Identifying it with a four-dimensional Yukawa coupling remains an additional hypothesis.

For item 3, let \(P_L\) reduce \(D_F(\ell)\), and suppose \(D_L=D_F(\ell)|_{P_LH_F}\) is fixed. Then the precise statement for a constant observable is
\[
P_LQ_\ell(a)P_L
 =\operatorname{sinc}(\ell\,\operatorname{ad}_{D_L})
       \bigl(P_L\pi(a)P_L\bigr),
\]
and
\[
\|P_LQ_\ell(a)P_L-P_L\pi(a)P_L\|
 \le \frac23\ell^2\|D_L\|^2\|\pi(a)\|.
\]
This remains true when \(\pi(a)\) mixes light and heavy channels. The limiting light compression need not be a representation: products can pass through the heavy subspace.

The full observable estimate does **not** follow from Theorem 22.4, since \(\ell\|D_F(\ell)\|\to\Theta\). An explicit counterexample uses
\[
D_F(\ell)=\sigma_1\otimes\operatorname{diag}(m,\Theta/\ell),
\qquad \pi(a)=1\otimes\sigma_1,
\]
with the commutative algebra generated by \(1\) and \(\pi(a)\) and the compatible real structure \(J=(\sigma_1\otimes1)K\). Then
\[
Q_\ell(a)=\operatorname{sinc}(\Theta-\ell m)\,\pi(a),
\]
which tends to \(\operatorname{sinc}(\Theta)\pi(a)\), rather than \(\pi(a)\).

The ratio \(m_t/M_W=2\Theta/\pi<1\) is a result for the stated lowest-level, zero-bulk-mass fermion channel and strict-wall gauge spectrum. Bulk fermion masses or a changed gauge wall condition are possible directions beyond that family.
