1. **CONFIRMED — Proposition 29.1.** For the connection metric with \(h=1\) and \(\rho=r\), the normal fibres are flat and totally geodesic. In a horizontal/vertical orthonormal frame, the difference between the Levi-Civita connection and the split connection is the O’Neill tensor. Its curvature component is linear in \(y\), and its spin contraction gives, in the convention of [Proposition 29.1](../lemmas/29_window_on_the_disc_bundle.md),
   \[
   R=-\frac{i}{8}\sum_{a,b,\alpha}
       \langle[\widetilde e_a,\widetilde e_b],f_\alpha\rangle
       c(e_a)c(e_b)c(f_\alpha).
   \]
   Thus \(T_M=T_0+R\) exactly on the tube. There is no additional first-order term for this metric and the pull-back connection. In the moving radial frame this is \(rA_2\); the apparent polar mean-curvature term belongs to the ordinary vertical Dirac operator.

   The bracket normalization used in the radial system reduces \(A_2\), on the nontrivial horizontal chirality, to a unitary conjugate of
   \[
   \tfrac12(s_1\otimes\sigma_1+s_2\otimes\sigma_2),
   \]
   with \(s_i\) Pauli matrices. Its norm is \(1\); it vanishes on the other horizontal chirality. Hence \(\|R\|\le |y|\).

   The common principal connection causes no extra derivative of \(\widehat Q\). In the equivariant-map realization, the horizontal fields differentiate the \(G\) argument and \(\widehat Q\) acts by the same operator on the fibre at every \(g\). Diagonal \(K\)-invariance of the actions on \(y\), the normal spinors and \(E\) makes this construction descend. Individual horizontal derivatives have a frame index that transforms under \(K\); the contracted \(D_H\) is equivariant.

   With \(c(f_\alpha)=\gamma_5\sigma_\alpha\), the relevant algebra is
   \[
   D_H\widehat Q=\widehat Q^{*}D_H,\qquad
   D_H\widehat Q^{*}=\widehat QD_H.
   \]
   It gives both displayed sums of squares and commutation of \(D_H^2\) with the two normal squares. Finally,
   \[
   \Sigma_7|_K
   =\bigl((2,1)\oplus(1,2)\bigr)\otimes(1,2)
   =(2,2)\oplus(1,3)\oplus(1,1),
   \]
   so the stated spin representation is correct.

2. **AMENDMENT — Proposition 29.2.** The spectrum and kernel conclusions follow, with a distinction between two projections. The projection \(P_W\) onto the normal \(K\)-representation \(W_0=(1,3)\oplus(1,1)\), extended over *both* base chiralities, commutes with \(D_H\). The projection onto the actual fibre kernel of \(\widehat Q\) also selects the chirality \(\gamma=-\epsilon\):
   \[
   P_0=P_{\gamma=-\epsilon}\otimes P_W .
   \]
   This projection need not commute with \(D_H\). Rather, \(D_HP_0=P_1D_H\), where \(P_1\) projects onto the fibre kernel of \(\widehat Q^*\), and \(P_0\) commutes with \(D_H^2\). The proof should use \(P_0\) and the latter commutation for the normal spectral gap.

   For the stated normalization, the twisted round-\(S^4\) formula is
   \[
   D_H^2=C_2^G(V)+\tfrac32-C_2^K(j),
   \qquad C_2^K(j)=2j(j+1).
   \]
   The twist has \(j=0,1\). Only representations with odd central character occur. The least \(G\)-Casimir is \(5/2\) on the \(4\); the next is \(15/2\) on the \(16\). The \(j=1\) zero on the \(4\) occurs in the base chirality of type \((1,2)\), exactly the chirality selected by (H4). The adjoint normal kernel selects the opposite base chirality, where that zero is absent. The \(j=0\) part on the \(4\) gives the next squared value \(4\).

   On noncompact \(N\), the calculation should first be stated on compactly supported smooth sections and extended through the closed graph forms. The doubled model is essentially self-adjoint on that core. The normal estimate \((y\cdot J)^2\ge |y|^2/4\) gives compact normal resolvent; the compact base and the commuting squared decomposition give the required discrete model spectrum. No boundary condition is imposed at infinity.

3. **CONFIRMED — Lemma 29.3.** Coupling spin \(1/2\) to spin \(3/2\) gives
   \[
   \sigma\cdot J=F^2-\tfrac34-\tfrac{15}{4},
   \]
   so its eigenvalues are \(3/2\) and \(-5/2\). For each \(y\), rotational invariance gives eigenvalues \(\pm |y|/2,\pm3|y|/2\) for \(y\cdot J\); hence the squared lower bound is pointwise valid. Consequently
   \[
   \frac{M^2}{4}\||y|u\|^2
      \le \|T_0u\|^2+\frac{5M}{2}\|u\|^2,
   \]
   which gives exactly the stated constants after taking square roots. The adjoint estimate follows in the same way. The positive \(\mu_1\), rather than its numerical value, is all that the subsequent proof needs.

   D_k^2=C_2(L)+\tfrac32-\tfrac38k^2.
   \]

   The invariant unitary connection on each line is the Chern connection: the difference of two invariant line connections is an invariant one-form, and the \(H\)-isotropy representation has no trivial summand.

8. **AMENDMENT — Proposition 29.8.** The tunnelling identity is correct for a singular pair with real nonnegative \(\lambda\). For a real scalar weight preserving the domain, the quadratic-form identity
   \[
   \|T_M(\xi u)\|^2

   is also correct.

   The Agmon proof needs two explicit choices. First, choose the IMS cutoffs so that the region where the exponential weight varies is covered by the bulk estimate, including the transition near the collar. Second, the final cutoff loses the integral across its support. Apply the weight with a smaller parameter, for example \(\eta'=\eta/2\), and choose the support width \(s\) so that
   \[
   (1-\eta')\!\left(I-\int_{r_*}^{r_*+s}f\,dr\right)
       \ge (1-\eta)I,\qquad
   I=\int_\varepsilon^{l-a}f\,dr.
   \]
   This supplies the exponent stated in the proposition. The absorption uses \(\lambda\to0\), supplied by the trial states.

   The factor \(1/2\) is justified uniformly across channels by
   \((n\cdot J)^2\ge1/4\); it does not require every channel to have eigenvalue \(1/2\). 10. **ERROR — Proposition 29.10 and Theorem 29.11 as written.** At [the definition of \(A_{\rm eff}\)](../lemmas/29_window_on_the_disc_bundle.md), \(P\) contains only \(Z=-1\) states and \(Y\) only \(Z=+1\) states. Therefore
    \[
    F=\Pi_Y(A_d+A_o)P:P\longrightarrow Y
    \]
    is a chiral operator. It is not the self-adjoint operator subsequently described. If regarded as an operator on the ambient space, it kills all of \(Y\); if regarded as \(P\to Y\), its kernel lies in \(Z=-1\). In either reading, the stated Condition \(K(\rho)\), requiring its kernel to be exactly a \(4\) in \(Z=+1\), cannot hold.

    The correction is to define the full operator on \(P\oplus Y\) by
    \[
    \mathcal A_{\rm eff}=
      \begin{pmatrix}0&F^*\\ F&0\end{pmatrix},
    \]
    and impose \(K(\rho)\) on this operator. The matrix \(A(\rho)\) of Proposition 28.13 represents \(F^*:Y\to P\) on the \(4\); \(F\) is its adjoint.

    With this correction, the proposed block argument has the required structure. The profile overlap is \(\sqrt3/2\). The stable-to-stable block has norm \(\sqrt3/\rho\), so its contribution has norm \(3/(2\rho)\); the full perturbation instead has \(\|A_o\|=2/\rho\), as required in the elimination estimate. The complementary block has inverse norm at most \((m/2-\|A_o\|)^{-1}\) for large \(m\), and its Schur correction is bounded by
    \[
    \frac{\|A_o\|^2}{m/2-\|A_o\|}.
    \]
    On \(V\ne4\), the corrected effective operator at \(\rho=1\) is bounded below by \(\sqrt5-3/2\). On the \(4\), the nonzero squared singular values of \(A(1)\) are
    \[
    \frac{45\pm3\sqrt{33}}8,
    \]
    and its one-dimensional multiplicity kernel lies in \(Z=+1\). This proves the intended corrected \(K(1)\). The quoted \(1.863\) is the value on the \(4\); the displayed argument gives the smaller uniform lower bound \(\sqrt5-3/2\) off that representation. The cut-off trial state must use the exact corrected half-cylinder kernel; its finite \(4\)-block equation gives exponential decay.

    There is a separate sign error in the reported fibre part. At \(\rho=1\), the kernel vector is \((-2,1,1)\), and the signed fibre trace is
    \[
    \frac{-4-1+1}{6}=-\frac23.
    \]
    In general it is \(-w(\rho)\). The positive \(w(\rho)\) is its magnitude.
