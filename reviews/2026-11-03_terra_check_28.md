**1. Slice operator — Correct with amendment.**

With Hermitian Clifford generators \(c_jc_k+c_kc_j=2\delta_{jk}\), the spin connection is

\[
\nabla_i=E_i-\frac14\Gamma_{ijk}c_jc_k,
\qquad
\Gamma_{ijk}=\langle\nabla_{E_i}E_j,E_k\rangle .
\]

Indeed, the commutator of the connection matrix with \(c_j\) gives \(\Gamma_{ijk}c_k\). The Koszul expression in [the script](../tools/lemma28_radial.py) has the correct order of indices. The \(\mathfrak h\)-part of a bracket is omitted in these reductive-frame coefficients; the \(H\)-action remains necessary in the equivariance condition.

For \(s_v(g)=F\lambda(g)^{-1}v\), differentiation along \(g\exp(t e_i)\) gives \(-FV_i\), with the appropriate scale divisor. The pull-back connection contributes \(+E_iF/\mathrm{scale}_i\) in the vertical directions. Its sign in the script is consistent with the spin term.

The displayed radial form is correct for the script’s \(h=1\) metric after absorbing the half-density. For the general metric in Theorem 28.9, let

\[
u=h^2\rho\,\psi .
\]

Before this conjugation the radial derivative is

\[
iZ\left(\partial_x+2\frac{h'}h+\frac{\rho'}\rho\right).
\]

After it, there is no extra \(h'/h\) or \(\rho'/\rho\) term, and the precise operator is

\[
T=iZ\partial_x+\frac{A_0}{h(x)}
       +\frac{A_1}{\rho(x)}
       +\frac{\rho(x)}{h(x)^2}A_2+i\phi(x)\tau .
\]

A collar modification of the connection adds \(-s(x)A_E/\rho(x)\). Thus the notation \(D_6(\rho(x))\) in [Result 28.5](../lemmas/28_two_walls_connected_parent.md) needs the \(h=1\) qualification. The file should also distinguish its Hermitian Clifford convention from the skew-Clifford \(c(dr)\) used in Theorem 28.9.

**2. Exact tests — Correct with amendment.**

The stated anticommutation and Casimir identities have the right form:

\[
\{A_0,A_1\}=0,\qquad
D_{S^4,\tau_{\rm tw}}^2
 =\operatorname{Cas}_G(V)+\frac32-\operatorname{Cas}_K(\tau_{\rm tw}).
\]

Here \(\tau_{\rm tw}\) denotes the relevant \(K\)-twist; it should be distinguished from the Higgs matrix \(\tau\). Python reproduced the printed \(A_0^2\) spectra and anticommutator norms in all four representations tested by the script. The zero in the \(A_0^2\) spectrum on the \(\mathbf{20}\) by itself is not a core zero mode of the full normal problem.

For the Fubini–Study metric with base \(S^4\) of radius \(1\), the fibre radius is **\(\rho=1\)**. The homogeneous strict nearly Kähler metric has \(\rho=1/\sqrt2\). More generally these radii are \(h\) and \(h/\sqrt2\). The squashing conventions and the two special metrics are described in [Appendix A.1, especially (A.1)](https://repository.up.ac.za/server/api/core/bitstreams/03d3f4aa-b281-4608-bba0-49ee7c63d7b8/content#page=22).

At \(\rho=1\), \(j(j+4)\), \(j=0,1,\ldots\), is the correct \(D^2\) eigenvalue on \((0,0)\)-forms with values in \(\mathcal O(1)\). It is not the spectrum of the *entire* \(\mathcal O(3)\)-twisted spinor bundle: the \(\mathbf{20}\) also contains \(D^2=10\). The last sentence of the [Correction paragraph](../lemmas/28_two_walls_connected_parent.md) needs that restriction. In addition, the script prints the Casimir and Fubini–Study spectra; it does not assert their expected numerical values.

The tests do not separately determine all three coefficients. For example, as an algebraic test of the listed identities, replacing

\[
A_1\mapsto tA_1,\qquad A_2\mapsto A_2+(1-t)A_1
\]

preserves \(\{A_0,A_1\}=0\) and the tested line-connection operator at \(\rho=1\). The curvature construction supplies an additional test of \(A_2\). With \(a,b=0,\ldots,3\) and \(\alpha=4,5\), Koszul’s formula gives

\[
A_2=-\frac{i}{8}\sum_{a,b,\alpha}
          C_{ab}^{\alpha}c_ac_bc_\alpha .
\]

The nonzero \(C_{ab}^{\alpha}\) for \(a<b\) are
\(C_{02}^{5}=-1\), \(C_{03}^{4}=-1\), \(C_{12}^{4}=1\), and \(C_{13}^{5}=-1\).
An in-memory Python calculation compared this contraction on the full eight-dimensional spinor space with the script’s \(\rho\)-coefficient. The Frobenius residual was \(4.8\times10^{-16}\); reversing the coefficient gave residual \(2.83\). This check shares the script’s Lie-bracket input, while using a separate curvature contraction. It is a numerical check of the displayed algebraic derivation.

**3. Proposition 28.13 — Error.**

The finite wall matrix, its kernel, and its trace calculation are correct for the channel convention used in that calculation. The error is its identification of those channels with the right singular vector for the boundary condition stated in Theorem 28.9.

For the stated \(\widehat\Gamma=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\) and
\(\widehat\Gamma Z=-1\), the boundary domains are

\[
Zu=-u\quad\text{for }T,\qquad Zv=+v\quad\text{for }T^*.
\]

Let \(T_\eta=iZ\partial_x+D_6+i\eta\phi\tau\), with \(\phi>0\), and write a wall profile as \(e^{q(x-\ell)}\), \(q>0\). The leading equations give

\[
q=-\eta\phi Z\tau\quad\text{for }T_\eta,\qquad
q=+\eta\phi Z\tau\quad\text{for }T_\eta^*.
\]

Consequently, with the **positive mass and boundary condition of Theorem 28.9**, the right wall channels have \(Z=-1,\tau>0\), and their fibre trace is \(+w\) in the script’s \(\widehat t\) convention. The \(Z=+1,\tau<0\) channels of Proposition 28.13 give \(-w\); they describe the adjoint wall problem for the reversed mass, or a problem with the opposite wall condition. Python reproduced \(+2/3\) and \(-2/3\), respectively, at \(\rho=1\). Changing the mass sign alone cannot make a right \(T\)-domain vector satisfy \(Z=+1\).

For the matrix calculation, let \(E_{s,t;j}\) be the rank-one map from input basis vector \(j\) to spinor basis vector \(s\) tensored with twist basis vector \(t\), using the zero-based bases of the script. Choose

\[
\begin{aligned}
(f_0,f_1,f_2)&=(E_{2,2;2},E_{2,3;3},E_{4,2;3}),\\
(g_0,g_1)&=\left(E_{3,2;3},
                 \frac{E_{0,2;0}+E_{6,2;1}}{\sqrt2}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

Direct Clifford contraction, with the stable-profile overlaps, gives

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1/\rho&-3/(2\rho)&-(\rho+1/\rho)\\
-i\sqrt2&0&-i\sqrt2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Reordering and changing unit phases gives the displayed \(A(\rho)\). The entry \(3/(2\rho)\), for example, contains the \(\sqrt3/\rho\) vertical connection matrix element and the \(\sqrt3/2\) overlap. Python/SymPy reproduced this matrix using exact symbolic arithmetic from the bracket and Clifford data.

Its kernel is as stated. The \(-3/2\) channel and one \(-1/2\) channel have input support \((1)_{-1}\), while the remaining channel has support \((1)_{+1}\); their output spaces are orthogonal. This yields

\[
w(\rho)=\frac{2(\rho^2+2)^2}{9+2(\rho^2+2)^2},
\]

including \(w(0)=8/17\), \(w(1)=2/3\), and \(w(\infty)=1\). For horizontal radius \(h\), replace \(\rho^2\) in this formula by \(\rho^2/h^2\).

The line-preserving connection replaces the first entry of the first row by zero. Its remaining block has the stated sum and product of squared singular values, is invertible for \(\rho>0\), and has squared singular values \(3,6\) at \(\rho=1\). The Fubini–Study cohomology statement in the proposition has the stated scope.

For fixed positive radius, the overlap matrix is the correct **leading formal wall operator**. Since its kernel on the \(\mathbf4\) is one-dimensional and separated from the other two singular values, the next correction is \(O(M^{-1})\) in the fixed-block expansion and does not change its limiting kernel. A uniform global reduction still needs proof, as the file acknowledges.

The sentence calling \(1.863\) and \(2.789\) “the next values on the 4” needs to say **wall values**. In a Python radial calculation at \(\phi_0=40\), \(\ell=3\), the levels were approximately \(1.865\) at the wall, \(2.000\) at the core, and \(2.789\) at the wall.

**6. Concentration literature — Correct with amendment.**

Let \(\mathcal A=i\Phi\) and let \(\sigma(\xi)\) be the symbol of the self-adjoint Dirac operator. Since \(\Phi\) acts on the twist factor, it commutes with the Clifford symbol, and

\[
\mathcal A^*\sigma(\xi)=\sigma(\xi)^*\mathcal A .
\]

Thus this is a concentrating pair in the sense of [Maridakis, arXiv:1510.07004v1, Definition 2.1 and Lemma 2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1510.07004). The commutator \([D,\Phi]\) is order zero, and the doubled self-adjoint operator satisfies the corresponding anticommutation condition. On a closed manifold, the concentration principle applies to suitable low modes near the singular set of \(\Phi\).

The stronger spectral-separation theorem of [Maridakis, arXiv:2110.11654v2, §2, Assumption 3 and the Spectral Separation Theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.11654) has an additional hypothesis that fails for this spin-\(3/2\) mass. At the core, its required direction-independent leading endomorphism would have to represent \((n\cdot J)^2\) for every normal unit vector \(n\). These matrices depend on \(n\): in a \(J_3=3/2\) state, the expectations of \(J_3^2\) and \(J_1^2\) are \(9/4\) and \(3/4\). An anisotropic extension of that theorem, or a separate core analysis, is therefore needed. [Prokhorenkov–Richardson, arXiv:math/0307251v3, §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0307251) supplies the related symbol condition and treats its stated localization setting.

Adding the wall requires boundary estimates and the stable boundary model. The closed-manifold energy argument acquires a boundary term: on a half-line,

\[
\|(\partial_t+M)f\|^2
 =\|f'\|^2+M^2\|f\|^2-M|f(0)|^2,
\]

and \(e^{-Mt}\) is a boundary-localized zero mode although the mass has no zero there. A proof for this problem must combine the core analysis with that boundary layer, control the \(O(M^{-1})\) reduction errors, and establish coercivity on the complement uniformly across representations.
