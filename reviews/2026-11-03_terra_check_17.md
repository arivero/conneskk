## [Theorem 17.1](../lemmas/17_explicit_masses_chiral_limit.md)

**Verdict: Correct with amendment.** The identities hold for both signs. The trace statement needs a hypothesis ensuring that the traces exist, for example that \(f(D^{2}/\Lambda^{2})\) is trace class.

Write \(P_\pm=(1\pm\gamma _5)/2\) and \(\widetilde U=1\otimes U\). Since \(U\) is unitary and exchanges the two eigenspaces of \(\gamma_F\), \(R\) is an isometry onto
\[
(S^+\otimes H_F^+)\oplus(S^-\otimes H_F^-)=\mathcal H^+.
\]
In particular, it is surjective.

Using \([U,D_F]=0\), \(J_M\gamma _5=\gamma _5J_M\), and \(UJ_F=sJ_FU\), direct calculation gives
\[
\widetilde U D R=R\mathcal D,\qquad
\widetilde U J R=R\mathcal J,
\]
where
\[
\mathcal D=D_M\otimes1+\gamma _5\otimes d,\qquad
\mathcal J=
\begin{cases}
J_M\otimes j,&s=+1,\\
\gamma _5J_M\otimes j,&s=-1.
\end{cases}
\]
The second identity includes the sign on the negative-spin-chirality component. These identities prove the displayed bilinear-form equality.

The claimed real structures have the expected signs:
\[
\mathcal J^2=-s,\qquad
\mathcal J\mathcal D=s\,\mathcal D\mathcal J.
\]
The square of \(\mathcal D\) has no cross term because \(d\) is constant and \(\{D_M,\gamma _5\}=0\).

For the trace, the two \(\widetilde U\)-eigenspaces carry operators equivalent to
\(\mathcal D_\pm=D_M\pm\gamma _5d\). Since
\(\gamma _5\mathcal D_+\gamma _5=-\mathcal D_-\), their squares are unitarily equivalent. This gives the factor \(2\), subject to the trace-class qualification.

## [Corollary 17.2](../lemmas/17_explicit_masses_chiral_limit.md)

**Verdict: Correct with amendment.** The two displayed forms give the same Dirac action after an identification of the Grassmann variables. They are not literally the same formula in the same coordinates.

The bilinear form \(\langle\mathcal J\psi,\mathcal D\psi'\rangle\) is antisymmetric for both signs: \(\mathcal J^2=-s\) and \(\mathcal J\mathcal D=s\mathcal D\mathcal J\). On Grassmann variables, exchanging the two cross terms introduces a second minus sign. They therefore agree, and their sum cancels the prefactor \(1/2\).

The two components \(\psi_1,\psi_2\) are independent Grassmann generators. After the charge-conjugation matrix is absorbed into a row variable, the first argument defines an independent Euclidean \(\bar\psi\). This is a Grassmann-bilinear construction; it does not impose ordinary complex conjugacy between integration variables. It agrees with the Grassmann construction in [CCM, §4, equations (4.3)–(4.7)](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0610241).

For the two choices in the corollary,
\[
S_-(\psi_1,\psi_2)=S_+(\psi_1,\gamma _5\psi_2).
\]
Thus the equality is an equality under an invertible change of variables. Also,
\[
R_5(D_M+\gamma _5m)R_5=D_M+im.
\]
This is a formal chiral change of both independent fermion variables. Its quantum Jacobian must be included; the identity alone does not identify the quantum measures.

## [Proposition 17.3](../lemmas/17_explicit_masses_chiral_limit.md)

**Verdict: Correct with amendment.** The fluctuation and reduction are correct. The statement about \(JAJ^{-1}\) should distinguish its Clifford and finite factors.

For a one-form \(A=\sum_i a_i[D,b_i]\), the displayed formula follows directly from the product Dirac operator. If \([D_F,\mathcal B]=0\), the finite scalar part vanishes. The remaining terms are Clifford one-forms with coefficients in the represented algebra. In \(JAJ^{-1}\), the **finite coefficients** lie in the opposite representation; the whole operator also contains a Clifford one-form.

Because \(U\) commutes with \(D_F\) and \(\mathcal B\), it commutes with \([D_F,b]\) and hence with \(A\). The relation \(UJ_F=sJ_FU\) then implies \([U,JAJ^{-1}]=0\). Thus \([D_A,\widetilde U]=0\), and the proof of Theorem 17.1 applies.

When \([D_F,\mathcal B]\ne0\), the same argument permits a finite scalar fluctuation \(d_A(x)\). It may vanish for a particular one-form \(A\); noncommutation permits a scalar field and does not force every fluctuation to contain one. The usual self-adjointness condition on the fluctuating one-form is understood.

## [Corollary 17.4](../lemmas/17_explicit_masses_chiral_limit.md)

**Verdict: Error.** The bimodule and fixed-mass Yukawa statements are correct, but the asserted heavy-top conclusion does not follow, and “photon, gluons” needs an additional gauge-group restriction.

In the representation of [lemma 06, Result 5](../lemmas/06_vectorlike_odd_factorization.md), restriction to
\(\mathcal B=\{(\lambda,Q_\lambda,m)\}\) gives the same left action on \(u_L\) and \(u_R\), namely \(\lambda\), and the same opposite colour action on both. Likewise, \(d_L,d_R\) both carry the left character \(\bar\lambda\) and the same opposite colour action. The surviving electron pair also has identical left and opposite actions. Hence the kept mass blocks commute with \(\mathcal B\), including when a vectorlike top pair alone is removed.

For a canonically normalized radial Higgs field, the coupling to a fermion of fixed mass \(m_f\) is \(m_f/v\). It tends to zero if \(v\to\infty\). But, at fixed nonzero gauge coupling, the matching relation in [lemma 11](../lemmas/11_spectral_action_heavy_w.md) is
\[
8M_W^2=\sum_{\rm generations}
(m_\nu^2+m_e^2+3m_u^2+3m_d^2).
\]
Consequently, keeping **all** Dirac masses fixed while \(M_W\to\infty\) is incompatible with that relation. If only a specified light mass is kept fixed, some Dirac mass must become unbounded, but it need not be the top mass. For example, the relation permits an unbounded charged-lepton Dirac mass with zero top mass. Top dominance is an additional hypothesis. The relation also belongs to the stated asymptotic matching regime, not automatically to the full finite-cutoff action.

Finally, the unrestricted unitary group of \(\mathcal B\) contains \(U(1)\times U(3)\). Before unimodularity or an inherited restriction from the Standard Model gauge group, two independent abelian gauge fields can act on the surviving charged fermions. For example, the central colour phase acts on quarks and not leptons. The phrase “photon, gluons” therefore requires specifying the residual gauge group and its unimodularity condition.

## [Proposition 17.5](../lemmas/17_explicit_masses_chiral_limit.md)

**Verdict: Correct.**

In the specified Standard Model representation, the left colour action is zero on particles and defining on antiparticles; the opposite colour action is defining, as an opposite representation, on particles and zero on antiparticles. The represented colour identity therefore separates the particle and antiparticle sectors. The commutant of the full colour matrix algebra is scalar on colour.

Commutation with the grading then gives independent flavour unitaries \(V_L,V_R\), and commutation with \(J_F\) fixes their complex conjugates on antiparticles. The remaining condition is
\[
V_LM=MV_R.
\]
Its adjoint, together with unitarity, gives the other mass-block equation. The listed groups follow. For example, with two zero masses and a third nonzero mass, the group contains \(U(2)_L\times U(2)_R\); this statement is for the colour-only algebra.

In the \(u,d\) sector, the \(\lambda\)-representation distinguishes the two electric charges. Requiring flavour transformations to commute with it makes each \(V_L,V_R\) diagonal, giving \(U(1)^4\) at \(M=0\). The physical electromagnetic subgroup also requires the gauge-group normalization discussed under Corollary 17.4.

The following unnumbered sentence, saying that “these groups are not inner,” should be read with care. The nonabelian flavour transformations are not colour gauge transformations. If the central \(U(1)\subset U(3)\) is retained in the lifted gauge group, however, its action contains a common vector phase; an imposed unimodularity condition can remove that continuous subgroup.

## [Theorem 17.6](../lemmas/17_explicit_masses_chiral_limit.md)

**Verdict: Correct with amendment.** The classification, signs, critical-point equation and four numbered items are correct. The final charge sentence must retain the mass-compatibility condition, and the colour identity factors should be displayed or explicitly suppressed.

Commutation with both colour actions separates particles and antiparticles and makes every block scalar on colour. Grading reversal and unitary involutivity give the stated particle block. From \(UJ_F=sJ_FU\), the antiparticle block is **\(s\) times** its complex conjugate, as printed.

On particles, commutation with \(D_F\) is equivalent to
\[
\Sigma M^\dagger=M\Sigma^\dagger,\qquad
\Sigma^\dagger M=M^\dagger\Sigma.
\]
The equations are equivalent: if \(H=\Sigma^\dagger M\) is Hermitian, then
\(\Sigma M^\dagger=\Sigma H\Sigma^\dagger\). Conversely, conjugating the first equation by \(\Sigma^\dagger,\Sigma\) gives the second.

For a Hermitian \(X\), differentiation along \(\Sigma e^{itX}\) gives
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{0}\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}
(M e^{-itX}\Sigma^\dagger)
=\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{tr}(HX).
\]
This vanishes for every Hermitian \(X\) exactly when \(H\) is Hermitian. With colour shown, the reduced mass is
\[
d_{\Sigma,s}
=(\Sigma M^\dagger\otimes1_3)\ \oplus\
\bigl(s\,\overline{\Sigma^\dagger M}\otimes1_{\bar3}\bigr).
\]

At \(M=mI\), the condition is \(\Sigma^\dagger=\Sigma\), so the components are the Grassmannians indexed by the number of negative eigenvalues. If \(M\) is positive diagonal with distinct entries, \(H^2=M^\dagger M\) forces \(H\) to be diagonal with entries \(\pm m_i\), giving \(2^{N_f}\) choices. For invertible \(M\), \(H>0\) gives the unique polar unitary and the unique maximum of \(\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(M\Sigma^\dagger)\). The first block of \(d_{\Sigma,s}\) is \(\Sigma H\Sigma^\dagger\), so it is positive in exactly that case.

With electric charge included, the admissible set is the **intersection** of this set with the charge commutant. For two differently charged flavours the latter consists of diagonal phases, but the mass condition remains. For instance, positive nonzero diagonal masses leave signs \(\pm1\), not arbitrary phases.

## [Remark 17.7](../lemmas/17_explicit_masses_chiral_limit.md)

**Verdict: Error.** The coset and stationarity comparisons are valid with the interpretation in Part 2(i), but the statement about all other critical points and mass signs is too broad.

For singular \(M\), other critical points need not introduce a negative mass eigenvalue. Take
\[
M=\operatorname{diag}(1,0),\qquad
\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{i\theta}).
\]
Every \(\theta\) gives \(\Sigma^\dagger M=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)\); all are global minima, and none introduces a negative eigenvalue. For invertible \(M\), every critical point other than the positive polar choice has at least one negative eigenvalue of \(\Sigma^\dagger M\). Its numerical signature \(n_+-n_-\) need not be non-positive.

Changing \(\Sigma\) changes the chosen reduction of a fixed even triple. A negative entry in the reduced mass block is therefore not, by itself, a physical mass-sign change at fixed fermion measure. A theta-shift statement requires the associated change of variables and its Jacobian.

The commutator with \(D_M\) in the last paragraph is nonzero for varying \(\Sigma\). Its consequences are worked out in Part 2(iii); a dynamical pion field does not follow from that commutator alone.

## [Proposition 17.8](../lemmas/17_explicit_masses_chiral_limit.md)

**Verdict: Error.** The formal logarithmic-derivative identity and the fixed-background conclusion are correct with a compatible regularization. The hypothesis in item 2 is insufficient for its limit.

For a finite spectral cutoff preserving the \(\lambda,-\lambda\) pairs,
\[
\left|\det(D_M(A)+\gamma _5m)\right|
=m^{n_0}\prod_{\lambda>0}(\lambda^2+m^2).
\]
Differentiation gives the stated sum, counting both signs of every nonzero eigenvalue. On a compact four-manifold the sum requires ultraviolet regularization and subtraction. In general the divergent terms include both an \(m\Lambda^2\) term and an \(m^3\log\Lambda\) term, together with possible curvature-dependent terms. With a subtraction scheme regular at \(m=0\), the positive spectral gap away from zero gives
\[
c_V(m;A)=\frac{n_0}{Vm}+O(m).
\]

Uniform continuity of \(\rho_m(\lambda)\) at \(\lambda=0\) does not imply that \(\rho_m(0)\) has a limit as \(m\to0\). For example,
\[
\rho_m(\lambda)=
\left(1+\tfrac12\sin(1/m)\right)e^{-\lambda^2}
\]
is nonnegative and uniformly continuous at zero, but its Poisson-kernel integral has no limit as \(m\downarrow0\). A sufficient amendment includes \(\rho_m(0)\to\rho_0(0)\), control of the spectral-density tails and ultraviolet subtraction uniformly in \(m\), and justification for taking the infinite-volume limit inside the integral. Under those conditions the Poisson-kernel argument gives \(\pi\rho_0(0)\). The sign and any flavour normalization of a condensate depend on its convention.

A fixed operator \(D_M(A)\) determines its own spectral measure. The gauge-averaged density, a regulated fermion determinant or Pfaffian measure, the sequence of volumes, and the order and existence of limits require further input. A spectral action may contribute to a *proposed* gauge weight, but an individual compact spectral triple does not by itself specify the required functional integral and thermodynamic limit.

The later assertion that \(\det(D_M+im)\) is positive is also convention-dependent and is false literally for a finite paired cutoff: a single \(\pm\lambda\) pair contributes \(-(\lambda^2+m^2)\). The standard positive expression uses the anti-self-adjoint operator \(\slashed D=-iD_M\):
\[
\det(\slashed D+m)
=m^{n_0}\prod_{\lambda>0}(\lambda^2+m^2)>0
\quad(m>0),
\]
with a compatible regulator. The factor relating this determinant to \(\det(D_M+im)\) carries a phase convention.

## [Proposition 17.9](../lemmas/17_explicit_masses_chiral_limit.md)

**Verdict: Error.** The stated interval mass and boundary stabilizer are correct under their respective hypotheses, but both routes need qualifications.

The first displayed reduced operator follows from [Theorem 08.7](../lemmas/08_product_selects_ko.md) for its **scalar-algebra minimal truncation**, with compatible real structure and interval pair. In the localized regime of [Theorem 9.4](../lemmas/09_massive_chiral_interval.md), \(M_{\rm bulk}=-\mu\) and \(\mu\ell>1\),
\[
m=\mu\,\operatorname{sech}(\kappa_0\ell)
\sim2\mu e^{-\mu\ell}.
\]
It is positive at finite parameters. At fixed \(\mu>0\), \(\ell\to\infty\) gives the stated chiral limit.

Without fixing \(\mu\), the claim that the limit is reached **only** when \(\mu\ell\to\infty\) is false. Let \(\ell\to\infty\) and \(\mu=2/\ell\). Then \(\mu\ell=2\), while \(m\) is a fixed positive constant times \(1/\ell\) and tends to zero. Conversely, \(\mu\ell\to\infty\) alone does not ensure \(m\to0\) if the prefactor \(\mu\) grows sufficiently fast. The localization asymptotic controls the dimensionless suppression; an absolute mass limit needs a scaling hypothesis.

For the boundary-Yukawa problem, \(X=0\) gives exact zero modes of boundary types (a) and (b). With flavour-degenerate bulk masses, the boundary domain is invariant exactly when
\[
V_QX=XV_U.
\]
If \(N_Q=N_U\), this is the group of Proposition 17.5 for the corresponding limiting mass matrix. More generally the same formula defines the rectangular stabilizer.

The identification \(M_F\propto X\) in [lemma 12](../lemmas/12_boundary_yukawa_interval.md) is a **towerless-limit** statement, with \(X=\ell\widehat Y\), and not an exact finite-\(\ell\) mass formula. For one flavour and zero bulk masses, the exact light eigenvalue is \(\arctan|X|/\ell\), not a constant multiple of \(|X|\). The full boundary-coupled operator is a different construction from the fixed tensor-product truncation of Theorem 08.7; an identification of the two at finite \(\ell\) requires additional proof.

## Part 2(i): Coset and alignment

The massless coset has substantive equivariant content. At \(M=0\), the chiral group acts on reductions by
\[
(V_L,V_R):\Sigma\longmapsto V_L\Sigma V_R^\dagger.
\]
The action is transitive, and the stabilizer of \(\Sigma=1\) is the diagonal \(U(N_f)\). Thus the reductions form a homogeneous \(U(N_f)_L\times U(N_f)_R\)-space, not merely a set with the same dimension as a chiral coset.

The mass condition has an elementary linear-algebraic origin: it says that \(H=\Sigma^\dagger M\) is Hermitian. For invertible \(M\), \(H^2=M^\dagger M\), and the positive choice gives the polar unitary. The equality with the critical-point equation for \(-\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(M\Sigma^\dagger)\) is exact. It identifies *all stationary points*, including saddles and maxima; vacuum alignment normally asks which stationary point minimizes a physical energy.

We therefore read the outcome paragraph as somewhat too strong when it says masses align the coset as a physical vacuum would. The remark correctly disclaims a proof of condensation, but identifying a reduction with a possible condensate direction still needs an explicit map to a quantum order parameter. The spectral action of the fixed even operator supplies no \(\Sigma\)-dependent potential merely from changing the reduction. In QCD, the anomalous singlet axial direction also means that the full classical \(U(N_f)\) space cannot be identified without qualification with the pion manifold.

## Part 2(ii): The sign, Pfaffian and anomaly

For the paired sector of Corollary 17.2, \(s\) is invisible in the classical fermion action **up to the displayed change of Grassmann variables**. The two reductions are coordinate descriptions of the same original bilinear form.

At finite dimension, if \(B_-=W^TB_+W\), then
\[
\operatorname{Pf}(B_-)=\det(W)\operatorname{Pf}(B_+).
\]
For the corollary one may take \(W=\operatorname{diag}(1,\gamma _5)\). The Berezin measure transforms inversely, so a consistently transported measure gives the same integral. Separately chosen Pfaffian orientations or regulators can display a sign or phase; comparing those choices requires their specification. No physical anomaly or theta shift follows from changing the reduction sign \(s\) alone.

A **physical** change of a quark mass sign at fixed measure is different. For one massive fundamental Dirac flavour in a gauge background of index \(Q\), the paired nonzero eigenvalues contribute equally for \(m\) and \(-m\), while zero modes give the ratio
\[
\frac{\det(\slashed D-m)}{\det(\slashed D+m)}
=(-1)^{n_0}=(-1)^Q.
\]
With the usual topological normalization, this is the factor associated with a \(\pi\) shift of \(\theta\). More generally, anomalous chiral rotations relate mass phases and \(\theta\); the conventionally invariant combination is written \(\bar\theta=\theta+\arg\det M\), with signs depending on conventions. None of this makes \(s\) an additional observable.

## Part 2(iii): Spacetime-dependent \(\Sigma\)

Let \(c^\mu=[D_M,x^\mu]\) locally, and suppose \(U(x)\) remains an admissible finite involution pointwise. Then
\[
[D_M,\widetilde U]=c^\mu\otimes\partial_\mu U.
\]
Set \(B_\mu=(U\partial_\mu U)|_{H_0}\). With the same pointwise definition of \(R\), direct differentiation gives
\[
R^{-1}\widetilde U D R
=D_M+c^\mu P_-B_\mu+\gamma _5d(x).
\]
Here \(B_\mu^\dagger=-B_\mu\). Since \(c^{\mu\dagger}=-c^\mu\) and \(P_-c^\mu=c^\mu P_+\), the derivative term has adjoint \(c^\mu P_+B_\mu\). The displayed reduced operator is therefore generally **not self-adjoint**.

If \(\Sigma(x)\) merely changes coordinates for the original fixed operator \(D\), the original spectral action is independent of \(\Sigma\). Likewise, conjugating \(D\) by a smooth unitary \(U(x)\) preserves its spectrum exactly. Neither operation generates a genuine two-derivative kinetic term.

One can instead *define a new operator*
\[
D_\Sigma^{\rm new}=D_M+\gamma _5d_\Sigma(x)
\]
and omit the derivative term above. That is an additional choice, rather than the exact reduction of the fixed operator. For illustration, on a flat four-manifold, with \(M=mI\), \(\Sigma^\dagger=\Sigma\), \(\Sigma^2=1\), and spectral action
\(\operatorname{Tr}f((D_\Sigma^{\rm new})^2/\Lambda^2)\), its heat expansion contains
\[
\frac{N_c f(0)m^2}{4\pi^2}
\int \operatorname{tr}_{f}
(\partial_\mu\Sigma^\dagger\partial^\mu\Sigma).
\]
This normalization traces over both reduced particle and antiparticle blocks and uses \(N_c=3\); its sign is positive when \(f(0)>0\). At \(M=0\), \(d_\Sigma=0\), so this particular new operator has no \(\Sigma\)-dependence either. A pion kinetic term therefore requires a specified new operator or a quantum effective-action calculation.
