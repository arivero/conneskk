## 1. Proposition 49.1

**What holds.** All the linear algebra was reproduced. Gamma is Hermitian, squares to one and anticommutes with each c(e_a), so Gamma_1, ..., Gamma_5 are five Hermitian anticommuting involutions on C^4, with Gamma_1 ... Gamma_5 = +1. The antiunitary j with j c_a j^{-1} = c_a exists, is unique up to a phase, satisfies j^2 = -1 and commutes with Gamma, as a product of four j-linear operators. The 16 elements 1, Gamma_a, Gamma_a Gamma_b are linearly independent over C, hence over R, and j-linear; End_H(Sigma_4) has real dimension 16; so the real span is End_H(Sigma_4) = M_2(H). ## New insights

1. **The mirror stratum carries C_F, and the invariance of the Yukawa block is the photon condition.** On the mirror of tau the stabilizer algebra span_R(1, x) is the restriction to Sigma_4 of C_F = {(lambda, lambda, 0)}, and the blocks commuting with it are the Yukawa blocks of Chamseddine-Connes-Marcolli, Definition 2.20 (sections 3 and 4). In their model C_F is a datum imposed on D_F. In the torus it is the stabilizer algebra of a stratum, and it is the same for the three mirrors on Sigma_R and different on Sigma_L, where each transposition has its own circle. Direction: a derivation of the condition [D_F, C_F] = 0 from the geometry, by restricting the torus Dirac operator to a point of a mirror (item (b) of Remark 45.3), with the three mirrors as three candidates for the photon circle on Sigma_L.

2. **The circle of C is the complex structure of A.** For each transposition the lift agrees on Sigma_R, up to sign, with c(omega_A)/2, where omega_A is the Kähler form of the complex structure 1 (x) J_E that A inherits from E (computed for the three transpositions). By hand: S_3 acts on T^{1,0} A = a (x)_R C by holomorphic isometries, the three-cycles with determinant 1 and the transpositions with determinant -1. With Sigma_4 = Lambda^{0,*} (x) K^{1/2}, the three-cycles act trivially on Lambda^{0,0} (x) K^{1/2} and Lambda^{0,2} (x) K^{1/2}, while a transposition acts there by the square roots +-i of the determinant. So the two right-handed singlets are K^{1/2} and K^{-1/2}, the left doublet is Lambda^{0,1} (x) K^{1/2}, and C + H is the real span of the spin lift U(1)_R x SU(2)_L of the unitary group U(2) of the complex structure of A; Dic_3 is a finite subgroup of that lift spanning the same real algebra. This is the four-dimensional part of Baez-Huerta's Theorems 4, 8 and 9 (section 7), realized on the torus. It also fixes the orientation question: with the complex orientation, Sigma_R is the chirality on which the Kähler form acts. Direction: the circle that lemma 42 marked by hand becomes the complex structure of E, which the Weyl action preserves; the two weight lines are exchanged by the lift of any reflection of T E, which reverses J_E.

3. **Neutral and charged directions, and the splitting as an area.** Under exp(t x), T A splits into the mirror plane, whose Clifford blocks are diagonal and of charge 0, and the normal plane, whose blocks are off-diagonal, anticommute with x and have charge +-2 (computed): the neutral and the charged components of the bidoublet. For delta, delta' in the mirror plane, the general x-invariant block is c(delta) + i c(delta') Gamma, and with the isometry iota of the mirror plane onto C given by c(delta)|_{Sigma_R} = L_{iota(delta)}, Proposition 49.3 gives a = iota(delta), c = +- iota(delta'), hence

    m_+^2 + m_-^2 = 2 (|delta|^2 + |delta'|^2),    m_+^2 - m_-^2 = 4 |det(delta, delta')|

   (checked on random pairs). The up-down splitting is four times the area of the parallelogram spanned by the displacement and the direction dual to the three-form; equal masses exactly when the two are parallel. This gives the custodial-breaking measure of lemma 46 (referee lead (2)) a geometric form on the torus.

4. **The anti-quaternionic half as a three-form.** i c(delta') Gamma = +- i c(*delta'). A constant three-form term in a Dirac operator on A, of the kind that torsion or a flux adds, has exactly this form, and with the geometric real structure J_M = j Gamma of A it is the J_M-linear half (Amendment 1.3). Direction for item (b) of Remark 45.3: a Dirac operator on A with a constant three-form term whose dual lies in a mirror plane, restricted to a point of that mirror, would supply both halves of an x-invariant Yukawa block. This is a lead, untested.

5. **What a lepton sheet needs besides an algebra C on a line.** With V_Y = C + C^3 and A_{F,Y} = C + M_3(C), Corollary 16.8 gives 32 states over C + H + C + M_3(C), which is A_LR with H_R reduced to C, a four-summand algebra. Chamseddine-Connes-Marcolli reach A_F = {(lambda, q_L, lambda, m)} by identifying the two copies of C through the order-one condition for an off-diagonal operator between particles and antiparticles (Proposition 2.11 and its proof, where the commutant condition is the graph of an embedding of C in H). Corollary 16.8 gives a D_F that commutes with the sheet projections. So the lepton generation needs, besides a sheet with C on a line, a connecting operator whose order-one condition identifies the two copies of C; in the project this is the role of the connector of lemma 20, whose centralizer is C + H + M_3(C). The degenerate quark pairing points the same way: Corollary 2.31 of Chamseddine-Connes-Marcolli needs the lepton class for nondegeneracy.

6. **The whole fibre of T^6 carries the quark bimodule twice.** Sigma_6 restricted to Dic_3 is 2 Sigma_4 (lemma 48), so V_X = Sigma_6 gives two copies of the 24-state bimodule, with the spinors of E as a two-dimensional multiplicity space on which T E and the inversion act; the Yukawa block becomes a 2 x 2 matrix in that space. This is a computation of multiplicity, with no claim about generations: the count three does not arise this way.
