## Lemma 08

### [08.1 — Classification under pointwise reality](../../lemmas/08_product_selects_ko.md)

**Correct.** Write \(J_I=WK\). Pointwise reality makes \(W\) a multiplication operator. Comparing derivative and zeroth-order terms in \(J_ID_I=tD_IJ_I\) gives \(WE=tEW\) and \(W'=0\). The endpoint conditions then force \(W\) to be diagonal, and \(WE=tEW\) gives \(\alpha=t\beta\). Thus the signs are \((+,t,+)\).

For \(t=-1\), the designation \(0_-\) is an extended convention; the standard Connes KO-0 signs have \(t=+1\).

### [08.2 — Spectral compression](../../lemmas/08_product_selects_ko.md)

**Correct.** The relation \(J_ID_I=tD_IJ_I\) implies commutation with \(D_I^2\) and its spectral projections. Solving the boundary problem gives eigenvalues \(\pm(r-\tfrac12)\pi/l\), \(r\ge1\). Direct differentiation of the displayed normalized profiles gives \(D_Iu_L=mu_R\) and \(D_Iu_R=mu_L\), with \(m=\pi/(2l)\). The diagonal antiunitary compresses as claimed.

### [08.3 — Reflection sign](../../lemmas/08_product_selects_ko.md)

**Correct.** Reflection reverses the derivative and exchanges the endpoint conditions. For the off-diagonal phase matrix, commutation with \(E\partial_x\) requires \(a=b\); involutivity then gives \(a=b=\pm1\). The relation \(\alpha=t\beta\) gives \(\sigma J_I=tJ_I\sigma\), and the lowest profiles are exchanged, so the compressed reflection is \(\pm\sigma_1\).

### [08.4 — Two counterexamples](../../lemmas/08_product_selects_ko.md)

**Correct.** The antiunitary \(\sigma_1R_lK\) preserves the domain, commutes with \(D_I\), squares to \(1\), and anticommutes with \(\Gamma_I\). Its opposite action is multiplication by the reflected function, so order zero and first order hold for the scalar interval algebra.

For the compressed full diagonal algebra, \(e^o=e\) for \(e=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)\), and
\[
 [[m\sigma_1,e],e]=m\sigma_1\ne0.
\]
The first-order failure is therefore exact.

### [08.5 — Internal-first KO-6 product](../../lemmas/08_product_selects_ko.md)

**Correct.** For the stated grading-twisted tensor structures,
\[
 J_{a,b}^2=(-1)^a,\qquad
 J_{a,b}(\Gamma_0\otimes D_I)
 =-t(-1)^b(\Gamma_0\otimes D_I)J_{a,b}.
\]
The standard KO-6 signs therefore require \(a=0\) and \(t(-1)^b=-1\). Since \(\sigma\Gamma_I=-\Gamma_I\sigma\), the bare reflection has
\[
 (1\otimes\sigma)J_F=-J_F(1\otimes\sigma).
\]
This conclusion uses the stated product convention and the specified class of real structures.

### [08.6 — Grading and lift of reflection](../../lemmas/08_product_selects_ko.md)

**Correct.** The interval grading alone commutes with the displayed tensor real structures, so it cannot supply the standard KO-6 grading. For \(U_1=\Gamma_0\otimes\sigma\),
\[
 [U_1,D_F]=[\Gamma_0,D_0]\otimes\sigma
           =2\Gamma_0D_0\otimes\sigma,
\]
which vanishes exactly when \(D_0=0\). The displayed unitary \(W\) transports both \(D_F\) and \(U\); consequently it preserves the sign against the transported \(J\).

### [08.7 — Reconciliation with the odd Clifford point](../../lemmas/08_product_selects_ko.md)

**Correct.** With the scalar interval algebra, the displayed map \(R\) is unitary onto the positive-grading space and gives
\[
 R^*UD_FR=D_0+m\Gamma_0,\qquad
 R^*UJ_FR=J_0\Gamma_0.
\]
The latter antiunitary squares to \(-1\) and anticommutes with \(d_5=D_0+m\Gamma_0\). Its opposite action is that of \(J_0\), and \([d_5,a]=[D_0,a]\), so the order conditions descend.

In the resulting two-block basis, the matrices are those of the odd–odd product with \(B=-i\sigma_2\). The zero Dirac operator on the point makes the \(P_1\) label a KO-sign convention. The factorization does not extend to the full compressed two-sheet algebra with its inherited real structure: 08.4 shows the first-order failure there.

### [08.8 — Literal collapse](../../lemmas/08_product_selects_ko.md)

**Correct.** Anticommutation gives
\[
 D_F^2=D_0^2\otimes1+1\otimes D_I^2
       \ge \frac{\pi^2}{4l^2}.
\]
Thus every fixed finite energy window is empty for sufficiently small \(l\). This is a conclusion about the stated product, with no compensating bulk mass or modified cutoff prescription.

### [08.9 — Spacetime grading twists](../../lemmas/08_product_selects_ko.md)

**Correct.** The two summands of \(D_M\otimes1+\gamma_M\otimes d\) have signs \((-1)^a\) and \(s\) against the proposed real structure, so \((-1)^a=s\) gives the stated odd-product convention. Its square is \(-s\), giving the claimed KO-1 and KO-3 signs. The displayed unitary \(V\) also transports the complete products because \(\sigma_1B_s=sB_s\sigma_1\). Neither calculation selects a finite involution.

### [08.10 — Euclidean and Lorentzian charge conjugation](../../lemmas/08_product_selects_ko.md)

**Correct.** The stated Euclidean and Lorentzian operators have the displayed squares and chirality relations in the specified matrix basis; in particular \(C_L=-i\gamma_E^0C_E\). Charge conjugation preserves the real Dirac mass while changing the charge, whereas subsequent multiplication by \(\gamma_5\) reverses the scalar mass sign.

The source identifications are supported by [CCM, arXiv v1, §§3.1–3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/0610241#page=20) and [D’Andrea–Kurkov–Lizzi, arXiv v2, equations (3.13)–(3.15)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.03231#page=9).

### [08.11 — Fixed real structure with both signs](../../lemmas/08_product_selects_ko.md)

Both \(U_-=1\otimes\sigma_1\) and \(U_+=\tau_3\otimes\sigma_1\) commute with \(D_F,Q\) and the two algebra actions, reverse the grading, and square to \(1\). Direct multiplication gives the opposite signs against \(J_F\). The block unitary \(W\) gives the displayed electrodynamics matrices.

### [08.12 — Particle-number pairing](../../lemmas/08_product_selects_ko.md)

**Correct.** Under all the stated hypotheses, \([Q,U]=0\) makes \(QU\) a unitary involution. The commutators of \(Q\) with \(D,\Gamma\), the algebra and its opposite preserve the remaining requirements, while
\[
 (QU)J=sQJU=-sJ(QU).
\]
The final bimodule obstruction also follows: a particle-preserving, grading-reversing \(U\) must identify the two particle bimodules.

## Lemma 13

### [13.1 — Inner fluctuations and boundary conditions](../../lemmas/13_gauge_sector_truncation.md)

**Correct.** The commutator with a matrix-valued function has the five displayed Clifford components, and matrix-valued one-forms are generated locally by sums \(a\,db\). Self-adjointness gives Hermitian \(A_\mu,A_5\). The fluctuation is bounded and self-adjoint, hence preserves self-adjointness on the original Dirac domain without having to map that domain into itself.

Applying the original boundary conditions to \(D_A\psi\) yields the two covariant derivative conditions in the proof. They constrain \(\psi\), not endpoint values of the potentials.

### [13.2 — Fixed-projection compression](../../lemmas/13_gauge_sector_truncation.md)

**Correct.** The diagonal spacetime matrix elements give the two weighted averages, and the vertical off-diagonal element is
\[
 m+i\int_0^l \frac{\sin(\pi x/l)}{l}A_5(x)\,dx.
\]
The proposed cosine lift realizes arbitrary Hermitian \(A_L,A_R\), and \(A_5=(\pi/2)B\) realizes arbitrary Hermitian \(B\). Thus the exact link image is \(\phi+\phi^*=2mI_n\). An unrestricted finite two-sheet fluctuation can in particular set \(\phi=0\), so its image is larger.

### [13.3 — Failure of gauge covariance of fixed compression](../../lemmas/13_gauge_sector_truncation.md)

**Correct.** For \(A_5=a\cos(2\pi x/l)\), the integral of \(A_5\) vanishes, hence \(W(l)=1\). But
\[
 B=\frac a\pi\int_0^\pi\sin t\cos(2t)\,dt
  =-\frac{2a}{3\pi}.
\]
The resulting compressed eigenvalues are \(\pm\sqrt{m^2+4a^2/(9\pi^2)}\), rather than \(\pm m\). Thus even an endpoint-fixed gauge transformation changes the fixed compression. The transported-frame link and the Schur-complement term in the proof follow by direct substitution.

### [13.4 — Mixed-boundary vector mass](../../lemmas/13_gauge_sector_truncation.md)

**Correct.** Neumann at \(0\) gives \(\cos(kx)\), and Dirichlet at \(l\) gives \(kl=(r+\tfrac12)\pi\). The lowest mass is \(\pi/(2l)\). These gauge boundary conditions are additional data; 13.1 does not derive them from the fermion domain.

### [13.5 — Spectral-action vector-mass formula](../../lemmas/13_gauge_sector_truncation.md)

**Correct.** In the stated convention, the Clifford traces in the proof give
\[
 \mathcal L_{a_4}
 =\eta\,\operatorname{Tr}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
  +3\eta\,\operatorname{Tr}D_\mu V D^\mu V,
 \qquad \eta=\frac{f_0}{24\pi^2}.
\]
At \(V_0\), the quadratic terms are \(\eta Q_{ab}f^af^b+3\eta K_{ab}A^aA^b\). Setting \(A_{\rm can}=2\sqrt\eta\,Q^{1/2}A\) gives
\[
 \mathcal M^2=\frac32Q^{-1/2}KQ^{-1/2}.
\]
The coefficient agrees with [CCM, arXiv v1, equation (6.10)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/0610241#page=68). It is conditional on the stated trace, vacuum and \(a_4\) approximation.

### [13.6 — Minimal two-sheet action](../../lemmas/13_gauge_sector_truncation.md)

**Correct.** For each generator, the stated trace matrices are
\[
 Q=\kappa I_2,\qquad
 K=2\kappa m^2
 \begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The mass-squared eigenvalues are therefore \(0\) and \(6m^2\), independent of the common multiplicity. For the additional profile restriction, \(c_L:c_R=2:1\), and the one-field formula gives \(3m^2/5\). That restricted field is not the massive eigenvector of the unrestricted two-sheet action.

### [13.7 — Conditional CCM comparison](../../lemmas/13_gauge_sector_truncation.md)

**Correct.** The full three-generation trace gives \(Q_{11}=12\), while one massive colour-triplet Dirac fermion gives \(K_{11}=3m^2\). Hence
\[
 M_W^2=\frac32\frac{3m^2}{12}=\frac38m^2.
\]
Keeping the massless charged states in \(Q\) is essential. This reproduces the conditional relation in [CCM, arXiv v1, Lemma 4.6, equation (4.16)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/0610241#page=39). The normalized interval matrix element is already \(m\); the separate \(A_5\) overlap does not rescale that background mass.

### [13.8 — Electroweak doublet and neutral-vector ratio](../../lemmas/13_gauge_sector_truncation.md)

**Correct with amendment.** The doublet calculation gives the displayed \(W\), \(Z\), and \(\rho\) formulas. The full trace has \(Q_2=12\), \(Q_Y=20\), so \(g_Y^2/g_2^2=3/5\) and \(M_W/M_Z=\sqrt{5/8}\).

The last paragraph must say that **the link of the independently gauged finite two-sheet completion** transforms as \(g_L\phi g_R^*\). The fixed compression of the original continuum field does not in general obey that endpoint transformation law: 13.3 gives an endpoint-fixed gauge transformation that changes \(\phi\). The \(1\oplus3\) decomposition under the diagonal \(SU(2)\) remains correct.

## Lemma 14

### [14.1 — Covariant projection and spectral data](../../lemmas/14_gauge_covariant_reduction.md)

**Correct with amendment.** The operator identity
\[
 D_{A^g,\mathsf M^g,\mathcal B^g}=G_gD_{A,\mathsf M,\mathcal B}G_g^\dagger
\]
and the Riesz formula prove covariance of the gapped projection. Conjugation by \(W\) gives the stated axial-gauge bulk matrix and domain. In particular, a gauge-invariant bulk mass and the mixed conditions are unchanged, so their spectrum is independent of \(A_5\).

The double-coset statement needs the hypothesis that endpoint transformations act **independently** through \(H_0\) and \(H_l\), with the corresponding extendability understood; it is a local pointwise classification unless global topology is specified. For example, periodic boundary conditions require \(g(l)=g(0)\). Their holonomy is classified by conjugation, not by \(G\backslash G/G\). For \(G=U(1)\), a periodic constant \(A_5\) has a nontrivial gauge-invariant Wilson phase despite both endpoint projections being \(U(1)\). The lemma’s subsequent discussion of identified endpoints gives the appropriate distinction.

### [14.2 — Induced connection and finite operator](../../lemmas/14_gauge_covariant_reduction.md)

**Correct.** Acting on \(U\chi\) gives
\[
 \nabla^\mathcal E_\mu\chi
 =\partial_\mu\chi+U^\dagger\partial_\mu U\,\chi
   +iU^\dagger A_\mu U\,\chi,
\]
which is the displayed \(\mathcal A_\mu\). Expansion of \(P\nabla_\mu P\nabla_\nu P-(\mu\leftrightarrow\nu)\) gives the curvature formula, and \(U^g=G_gUh^\dagger\) gives the stated frame transformations.

The sums involving \(\nabla^S\) and the internal connection are tensor-product connection sums; in a local spin frame the derivative appears once. The Schur-complement correction is the standard result of eliminating the complementary block.

### [14.3 — Finite algebra and fluctuation image](../../lemmas/14_gauge_covariant_reduction.md)

**Error.** The displayed formulas, the compression range, and the **local** fixed-singular-value statement are correct. The unqualified claim that the global continuum link image consists *exactly* of all links with \(\Phi^\dagger\Phi=\lambda^2I\) is too strong.

Take \(M_4=S^1\times S^3\), \(n=1\), and the globally defined fixed-singular-value link
\[
 \Phi(\theta,z)=\lambda e^{i\theta}.
\]
For any globally smooth real \(A_5\) on the stated trivial bundle,
\[
 \Phi=\lambda W_l^\dagger
 =\lambda\exp\!\left(i\int_0^l A_5(y,x)\,dx\right)
\]
has a globally defined real phase and zero winding around \(S^1\). It cannot equal the displayed link. More generally, \(W(y,x)\) is a homotopy from the identity to \(W_l(y)\).

The corrected statement is that every fixed-singular-value link is obtained **locally in spacetime**; globally, the unitary link must admit the corresponding path from the identity in the chosen trivialization. The reducing-subalgebra criterion is correct: the compression identity in the proof forces both \(a\) and \(a^*\) to preserve the range. Since \(p_L\) is nonzero in the interior, this gives precisely \(a(x)=W(x)bW(x)^\dagger\), and conversely such multipliers reduce the light space.

### [14.4 — Boundary Yukawa data](../../lemmas/14_gauge_covariant_reduction.md)

**Correct with amendment.** The transformation law for \(X\), its axial-gauge form, and the limiting texture follow from the boundary equations. In the towerless scaling, the normalized wall values are \(l^{-1/2}\Phi_Q\) and \(l^{-1/2}\Phi_U\), giving
\[
 Y_0=\Phi_Q\widehat X\Phi_U.
\]
Expanding the transfer functions at fixed bounded \(\lambda\) changes this matrix by \(O(l^2)\); after grading-preserving changes of frame the light block converges to \(Y_0\). Positive diagonal \(\Phi_Q,\Phi_U\) give the stated attainability.

Two hypotheses must be made explicit:

1. To obtain the **entire** displayed limiting block, retain the full zero-mode-descended cluster of dimension \(N_Q+N_U\), not an arbitrary smaller gapped grading-invariant cluster.
2. The **nonzero** singular values of \(Y\) are the retained positive eigenvalues. Zero singular values and possible unmatched chiral zero modes must be included separately.

For the first point, with two massless flavors and \(X=l\,\operatorname{diag}(1,2)\), the cluster containing only the lowest \(\pm\) pair is gapped but has a \(1\times1\) limiting block, not the full \(2\times2\) texture.

### [14.5 — Quadratic matching condition](../../lemmas/14_gauge_covariant_reduction.md)

**Correct with amendment.** On a common retained profile space where both kinetic forms are positive, the two mass matrices are the stated generalized eigenvalue problems. If the KK mass matrix is \(\kappa^2I\), equality of all its masses is equivalent to
\[
 K=\frac23\kappa^2Q.
\]
The equality of actions in the same coordinates gives the stronger conditions \(G^{(5)}=4\eta Q\), \(H^{(5)}=6\eta K\). Block multiplication gives the stated \(C_a\), \(Q\), \(K\), and one-vector sum rule.

The comparison must explicitly restrict **both** quadratic forms to the same profile space on which \(G^{(5)}\) and \(Q\) are positive. A KK profile can be invisible to compression. For the massless mixed fermion pair, \(f(x)=\cos(2\pi x/l)\) satisfies Neumann conditions at both endpoints and has positive five-dimensional norm and derivative norm, but
\[
 \int w_Lf=\int w_Rf=0.
\]
Thus \(Q=K=0\) for that nonzero KK mode, and the finite action has no corresponding canonically normalized vector.

### [14.6 — Covariant lowest-mode reduction](../../lemmas/14_gauge_covariant_reduction.md)

**Correct.** The displayed gauge transformation closes on the retained Abelian \(a_\mu,b\) fields. The transported frame gives
\[
 D\Phi=i\Phi(c_L-c_R)\left(a+\frac{db}{\kappa}\right),
\]
including the Berry terms. Canonical normalization then gives
\[
 M_{\rm spec}^2
 =3\lambda^2\frac{(c_L-c_R)^2}{c_L^2+c_R^2}.
\]
At \(M=0\), \(c_L=8/(3\pi)\), \(c_R=4/(3\pi)\), and \(\lambda=\kappa\), so the ratio is exactly \(3/5\).

### [14.7 — Tuned bulk mass](../../lemmas/14_gauge_covariant_reduction.md)

**Correct.** For \(M\ge0\), the lowest root satisfies \(M=-k\cot(kl)\), with \(t=kl\in[\pi/2,\pi)\), and \(\lambda=k/\sin t\). At \(t=3\pi/4\), the displayed normalization integral and overlaps give
\[
 c_L=\frac9{3\pi+2},\qquad c_R=\frac6{3\pi+2},
 \qquad
 \frac{M_{\rm spec}^2}{M_{\rm KK}^2}=\frac{27}{26}.
\]
The ratio is \(3/5\) at \(M=0\) and varies continuously, so an \(M_*\) exists in the stated open interval. No uniqueness follows from this argument. At such a solution, \(\kappa<\lambda<(3/\sqrt2)\kappa\), so the retained fermion is at the tower scale.

### [14.8 — Bounded-fermion no-go](../../lemmas/14_gauge_covariant_reduction.md)

**Correct.** For any Hermitian linear combination \(T\) of retained couplings,
\[
 \|[T,V_0]\|_{\rm HS}\le2\|V_0\|\,\|T\|_{\rm HS}.
\]
Consequently \(K\le4\|V_0\|^2Q\) as quadratic forms, and every finite mass satisfies
\[
 M_{\rm spec}^2\le6\|V_0\|^2.
\]
The constant is sharp: take \(V_0=C\sigma_3\) and \(T=\sigma_1\). Thus bounded \(V_0\) cannot match the divergent Neumann–Dirichlet mass within the stated leading-action approximation.

### [14.9 — Gauge and scalar normalizations](../../lemmas/14_gauge_covariant_reduction.md)

**Correct.** With fixed generator and profile normalizations, \(\eta q_sF_s^2\) gives \(g_{4,s}^{-2}=4\eta q_s\), whereas integrating the five-dimensional kinetic term gives \(J_s/g_{5,s}^2\). Rescaling a five-dimensional coupling changes both of its quadratic forms by the same factor and leaves its KK mass unchanged, so mass matching alone does not determine the relative coupling.

At vanishing ambient \(A_\mu\), \(D_\mu V=H_i\partial_\mu\varphi^i\), giving \(Z_{ij}=6\eta\,\operatorname{ReTr}(H_iH_j)\). In the eaten-scalar example, imposing both displayed gauge-kinetic and mass equalities gives \(Z_b=G\). No corresponding conclusion follows for an independent physical scalar direction.

### Section 6: Theorem D

- **D.1 — Correct, with its finite-triple scope made explicit.** The criterion agrees with [Lemma 06.2](../../lemmas/06_vectorlike_odd_factorization.md). The point has zero Dirac operator; its KO label is part of the product convention.

- **D.2 — Error as a general statement.** The assertion about *a finite triple with an unpaired left-handed neutrino* is broader than the particular reduced representations treated in Lemma 06. For a counterexample, take
  \[
  H=\mathbb C^2_{\rm gen}\otimes\mathbb C^2,\quad
  A=\mathbb C,\quad D=0,\quad
  \Gamma=1\otimes\sigma_3,\quad J=(1\otimes\sigma_1)K.
  \]
  Regard the two positive-grading states as two unpaired left neutrinos and the negative-grading states as their conjugates. There is no Majorana term. Both
  \[
  U_+=1\otimes\sigma_1,\qquad U_-=\sigma_2\otimes\sigma_1
  \]
  satisfy the factorization criterion, with signs \(+1\) and \(-1\), respectively. Conversely, an unpaired neutrino does not alone guarantee the \(+1\) factorization: the full chiral algebra can obstruct both, as in [Lemma 06.8](../../lemmas/06_vectorlike_odd_factorization.md). The paper must specify the reduced algebra, charged bimodules and neutral multiplicity for its KO-7-only claim.

- **D.3 — Correct with amendment.** The sign calculation is correct. Calling both interval choices “KO-dimension 0” needs the extended \(0_+\), \(0_-\) convention: under the paper’s stated standard convention only \(t=+1\) has the standard KO-0 signs. The bare reflection is generally not linear over the full interval algebra.

- **D.4 — Correct with amendment.** The exact factorization is for the **scalar-algebra** interval truncation. With the full compressed two-sheet algebra, the inherited real data fail first order and the reflection does not commute with that algebra.

- **D.5 — Correct with amendment.** The fixed-product example is correct. The general pairing statement also needs \(Q\) to be a self-adjoint involution commuting with \(\Gamma\), the algebra and its opposite, in addition to the stated conditions and particle preservation. The omitted algebra hypothesis matters: with \(A=\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C\) acting diagonally on a multiplicity factor, \(D=0\), \(\Gamma=1\otimes\sigma_3\), \(J=(1\otimes\sigma_1)K\), \(U=1\otimes\sigma_1\), and \(Q=\sigma_2\otimes1\), the conditions printed in D.5 hold and \(U\) preserves \(Q\), but \(QU\) does not commute with \(A\).

The [paragraph after Theorem D](../../paper/towerless_intervals.tex) repeats the unqualified neutrino claim and the claim that adding \(\nu_R\) with Dirac masses gives both signs; both need the reduced-representation hypotheses. Its statement that the Majorana case is open is also less precise than Lemma 06: that lemma gives a one-generation result and a singular-value multiplicity criterion for its specified neutral sector. A broader model can remain open.

### Section 7: Propositions E and F and the following paragraph

[Proposition E](../../paper/towerless_intervals.tex) states its CCM mass relation as an assumption and correctly limits its use to the matching-scale heat-kernel regime.

[Proposition F](../../paper/towerless_intervals.tex) is **correct with amendment** for its fixed-compression and gauge-covariance claims, but its mass sentence contains a dimensional error: \(\mathcal M^2\) cannot equal \(\sqrt6\,m\) or \(\sqrt{3/8}\,m\). Those are masses; their squares are \(6m^2\) and \(3m^2/8\). It must also say that the minimal two-sheet model has a massless diagonal vector, and that the CCM value is for the \(W\) under the full three-generation trace, specified vacuum and one-mass assumption. The Neumann–Dirichlet vector boundary conditions are separate inputs.

The [following paragraph](../../paper/towerless_intervals.tex) reports the \(3/5\) example, tuned mass, and norm bound correctly, but its opening sentence is too general: the two masses can agree in the tuned example described immediately afterward. The \(3/5\) and \(M_*\) claims concern the specified one-fermion Abelian mixed-boundary reduction. The no-go requires **all fermion masses retained in the finite trace** to be uniformly bounded, and applies to the stated leading spectral action. Covariant projection and one mass equality do not establish equality of the full gauge or scalar actions.

The [abstract](../../paper/towerless_intervals.tex) and [introduction](../../paper/towerless_intervals.tex) repeat the overly broad claims that an unpaired neutrino selects KO-7 and that the gauge sector does not reproduce the tower. They need the same qualifications.
