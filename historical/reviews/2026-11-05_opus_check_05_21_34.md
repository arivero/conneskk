## 1. Row l05_chain: the deconstructed chain and its continuum limit

### 1.1 The text of the ledger row

The ledger row reads "The staggered N-site chain with hopping h_N = (2N-1)/(2l) has spectrum +-2 h_N sin((2r-1) pi/(4N-2)), r = 1..N". The brief restates it the same way. The lemma, equation (1), and the results file use h_N = pi/(4 l sin theta_N), with theta_N = pi/(4N-2), and r = 1..N-1. The lemma is right on both points.

- **Range of r.** dim H_N = 2(N-1), so D_N has N-1 positive eigenvalues. At r = N the formula gives 2 h_N sin((2N-1) theta_N) = 2 h_N sin(pi/2) = 2 h_N, which is outside the spectrum.
- **Hopping.** (2N-1)/(2l) is the leading term of the exact hopping, since pi/(4 l sin theta_N) = ((2N-1)/(2l)) (1 + theta_N^2/6 + O(theta_N^4)). With the hopping set exactly to (2N-1)/(2l), the spectrum formula, the distances and the three limits still hold. The smallest positive eigenvalue is then ((2N-1)/l) sin(pi/(4N-2)), which equals 3/(2l) at N = 2 and 1.5451/l at N = 3, instead of pi/(2l). The exact Connes-Lott member with |m| = pi/(2l), used in Parts 3 and 4, requires normalization (1).

### 1.2 Part 1: spectrum, heat trace and distances

**Spectrum.** In the basis L_1, R_2, L_2, R_3, ..., L_{N-1}, R_N, the operator D_N is h_N times the adjacency matrix of the path with 2N-2 vertices, with edge signs +, -, +, ... . A diagonal unitary removes the signs, so the spectrum is 2 h_N cos(j pi/(2N-1)), j = 1, ..., 2N-2. The positive values are those with j <= N-1, and with r = N - j they are 2 h_N sin((2r-1) theta_N). An independent route: B_N = h_N (I - S_n) has singular values 2 h_N sin((2r-1) pi/(2(2n+1))) with n = N-1, the same numbers. Equation (3) follows. Numbers computed: for N = 2, ..., 39 and l = 1 the eigenvalues agree with (2) to a relative error of 2.5e-15, there are 2(N-1) of them, and the smallest positive one equals pi/(2l) to 1e-12.

**Distances.** Write B_N = h_N I - h_N S_n. The term h_N I couples L_i to R_{i+1}, and the term -h_N S_n couples L_i to R_i at the same site. Hence

    B_N pi_R(a) - pi_L(a) B_N = h_N diag(a_2 - a_1, ..., a_N - a_{N-1}) =: C(a),

because the shift part gives (S_n pi_R(a) - pi_L(a) S_n)_{i,i-1} = a_i - a_i = 0. The other off-diagonal block of [D_N, pi_N(a)] is -C(a), also for complex a, so the commutator norm is the largest absolute value on the diagonal of C(a):

    ||[D_N, pi_N(a)]|| = h_N max_i abs(a_{i+1} - a_i)    for every a in C^N.

The supremum of abs(a_j - a_i) under this constraint is abs(i - j)/h_N, attained by a_i = (i-1)/h_N, which proves (4). Then (5) follows from (1). Numbers computed: for random complex a and N <= 39 the two sides of the norm identity agree to a relative error of 4e-16. The endpoint distances for l = 1 are d_N(1, N) = 0.63662, 0.78691, 0.88438, 0.93984 at N = 2, 3, 5, 9, which agree with the independent check recorded in the lemma.

**The other constructions of Part 1.** The geometric normalization (6) is immediate. For the one-component path, the proof of (7) holds as written. For the weights (q, 1, q-1, 2, ..., 1, q)/sqrt(q(q+1)), each vertex of the larger class has weight sum q/sqrt(q(q+1)), and each vertex of the smaller class has weight sum (q+1)/sqrt(q(q+1)). So the normalized constant vectors are singular vectors with singular value 1, and Perron-Frobenius gives norm 1. Numbers computed: for k = 1, ..., 8 the attained values equal (7), and a numerical maximization of the weight sum over the norm (Nelder-Mead, 20 starts per k) found no larger value. For the incidence operator, the spectrum {0} together with +-2h sin(k pi/(2N)) agrees for N = 2, ..., 6 to 1.2e-15. The tail-value representation gives the commutator block h (a_{e+1} - a_e) on distinct rows and columns, hence distance abs(i - j)/h.

**Verdict on Part 1.** Theorem, proved, as written.

### 1.3 Part 2: the three convergence statements

**The continuum operator.** The boundary form is [conj(L) R~ - conj(R) L~]_0^l. The conditions R(0) = 0 and L(l) = 0 define a two-dimensional isotropic, hence Lagrangian, subspace of the boundary data in C^4, so D_l is self-adjoint. The eigenfunctions are c (cos(lambda x), sin(lambda x)) with cos(lambda l) = 0, the kernel is zero, and (9) and (10) hold. The boundary conditions of D_l^2 are those stated: Neumann at 0 and Dirichlet at l for L, and the reverse for R.

**(a) Spectrum.** Equation (11) gives lambda_{N,r} -> (2r-1) m_1 for each fixed r. Two facts make the statement sharper.

- For 0 < k theta <= pi/2 the ratio sin(k theta)/sin(theta) decreases in theta, since tan(k theta) >= k tan(theta). Moreover (2r-1) theta_N <= (2N-3) pi/(4N-2) < pi/2. So each lambda_{N,r} increases with N, and the convergence is from below.
- Together with (12), for all N and r we get (2r-1)/l <= lambda_{N,r} <= (2r-1) pi/(2l). Numbers computed: both bounds hold for all r at every N = 2, ..., 199.

Consequently, for every E outside spec(D_l), the number of eigenvalues of D_N in [-E, E] is eventually equal to that of D_l. On the window r <= R the relative error is O(R^2/N^2). The scope is eigenvalue-by-eigenvalue convergence on bounded energy windows, for operators on different finite-dimensional spaces. The lemma gives no common Hilbert space or identification maps, so it proves no resolvent or operator convergence, and it says nothing about eigenvectors.

**(b) Heat trace.** The proof of (13) is correct. The majorant exp(-eps (2r-1)^2/l^2) is summable, and each term converges. For fixed r and t > 0, abs(exp(-t lambda^2) - exp(-t mu^2)) <= sup_t t exp(-t c^2) abs(lambda^2 - mu^2) = abs(lambda^2 - mu^2)/(e c^2) whenever lambda, mu >= c. So the convergence is uniform on [eps, infinity) for every eps > 0, which is stronger than [eps, T]. It is uniform on no interval reaching t = 0, since K_N(0) = 2(N-1). Poisson summation (14) holds with the sum over all integers k of exp(-t pi^2 (k + 1/2)^2/l^2). Numbers computed, for l = 1:

**(c) Gromov-Hausdorff.** The statement concerns the pure-state spaces ({1, ..., N}, d_N), which converge to ([0, l], abs(x - y)), the metric (10) of D_l. An explicit bound follows from the lemma's identification. Take the correspondence of pairs (i, x) with abs(x - x_i) <= l/(2(N-1)). Its distortion is at most (1 - rho_N) l + l/(N-1). By sin(theta) >= theta - theta^3/6 we have 0 < 1 - rho_N <= 1/(2N-1) + theta_N^2/6 <= 1/(N-1), hence

    d_GH(({1, ..., N}, d_N), ([0, l], abs(x - y))) <= l/(N-1).

Numbers computed: 1 - rho_N = 0.36338, 0.21309, 0.11562, 0.06016 and 0.0050355 at N = 2, 3, 5, 9 and 100, each below the bound 1/(2N-1) + theta_N^2/6. This is Gromov-Hausdorff convergence of the classical metric spaces of pure states. Quantum Gromov-Hausdorff convergence, or a propinquity of the spectral triples that involves D_N and the Hilbert spaces, is a stronger notion that the lemma does not claim (section "New insights", item 4).

**(d) Heat window.** The sketch after (16) is complete once one identity is written out. So the ratio sqrt(t_N) lambda_{N,r}/u_r tends to 1 uniformly on u_r <= U exactly when N sqrt(t_N)/l -> infinity. Bound (12) gives the tail majorant exp(-4 u^2/pi^2). With mesh delta = pi sqrt(t_N)/l, the Riemann sums give delta sum_r exp(-t_N lambda^2) -> sqrt(pi)/2 and delta sum_r t_N lambda^2 exp(-t_N lambda^2) -> sqrt(pi)/4, which yields both claims of (16). ## 2. Row l21_exotic: Proposition 21.4 and Corollary 21.5

### 2.1 The counting

Let n = n_u + n_d and V = C^n (x) C^3, with Q = 2/3 on up-type and Q = -1/3 on down-type flavours. The real vector is the real form of V + conj(V), of real dimension 6n, and the complexified adjoint is

    Lambda^2(V + conj V) = Lambda^2 V + Lambda^2 conj(V) + V (x) conj(V),    Lambda^2 V = (S^2 n, 3bar) + (Lambda^2 n, 6).

So the colour antitriplets of the adjoint are exactly (S^2 n, 3bar), the diquarks q_i q_j symmetric in flavour, with charge Q_i + Q_j. The counts n_d(n_d+1)/2 at -2/3, n_u n_d at +1/3 and n_u(n_u+1)/2 at +4/3 are correct.

**Error in the statement.** Proposition 21.4 says "span the vector of SO(3(n_u + n_d))". For (2, 3) this would be SO(15). The vector has 6(n_u + n_d) real dimensions, as the proposition itself requires with "36 = 30 + 6" and "A coloured quark adds 6 real dimensions".

**Antisquarks.** Per up-type flavour there are two complex antitriplet scalars of charge -2/3, the conjugates of the left and right squarks. Per down-type flavour there are two of charge +1/3. Three generations need 6 and 6, counted as complex antitriplet fields, which is the count of the (15, 3bar) entries. The condition n_d(n_d+1) = 12 has the nonnegative root n_d = 3, and then n_u n_d = 6 gives n_u = 2. The charge 4/3 count is 3. Numbers computed: an exhaustive search over 0 <= n_u, n_d < 60 finds (2, 3) as the only solution. The counts are (6, 6, 3) at (2, 3) and (6, 9, 6) at (3, 3).

**SO(36) and SO(32).** With the top, the vector has 36 real dimensions. In SO(32) ⊃ SO(30) x SO(2), the SO(2) plane commutes with the colour SU(3) inside SO(30), so it is colour-neutral and cannot hold a coloured quark, which needs 6 dimensions. Correct.

### 2.2 Corollary 21.5: pairing and anomalies

A Dirac fermion of a chiral-multiplet pairing has two Weyl components and needs two complex scalars, so three generations need 6. Correct. In reading (b) the six charge-4/3 diquarks can be assigned as three scalars in (3bar, 4/3) and three conjugates of scalars in (3, -4/3). Their fermions then form three vectorlike Dirac fermions, so the charge-4/3 sector is anomaly free in that reading.

For three left-handed Weyl fields in (3bar, 4/3), SU(2) singlets so that Y = Q = 4/3, with T(3) = 1/2 and A(3) = 1, the traces are as follows.

| trace | value |
|---|---|
| SU(3)^3 | 3 A(3bar) = -3 |
| SU(3)^2 U(1) | 3 x (1/2) x (4/3) = 2 |
| U(1)^3 | 3 x 3 x (4/3)^3 = 64/3 |
| gravitational U(1) | 3 x 3 x (4/3) = 12 |

The three traces listed in the corollary are nonzero, and the SU(3)^3 trace, also nonzero, is missing from the list.

**The meson sector cannot cancel them.** The meson sector (24, 1) + (1, 1) carries charges +1 (6 complex dimensions), -1 (6) and 0 (13). As fields, it consists of six complex charged scalars and thirteen real neutral ones, which contains the slepton count of the lemma with one neutral state left over. Give it one left-handed Weyl field per complex scalar, with either chirality. Then:

1. its fermions are colour singlets, so they contribute nothing to SU(3)^3 or SU(3)^2 U(1);
2. their charges lie in {0, +-1}, so their cubic charge sum is an integer, and 64/3 is not;
3. at most six of them are charged, so their linear charge sum has absolute value at most 6, and 12 is out of reach.

So the meson sector cancels none of the four traces, for every chirality assignment of its fermions. In the Standard Model reading, with the (6, 6) fermions as the quarks and the meson fermions as the leptons, the quarks and leptons together are anomaly free and leave the exotic traces as they are. The same holds with hypercharge in place of charge.

For other chirality assignments of the three exotic multiplets, each scalar is either the scalar of a multiplet in (3bar, 4/3) or the conjugate of one in (3, -4/3). The SU(3)^3 trace is then odd, with value +-1 or +-3. Numbers computed, over all eight assignments: (SU(3)^3, SU(3)^2 U(1), U(1)^3, gravitational) = (-3, 2, 64/3, 12) for the corollary's assignment, (-+1, +-2/3, +-64/9, +-4) for the mixed ones, and (3, -2, -64/3, -12) for the opposite one. ## 3. Row d11_spin9_conjugate: the so(9) of the Chevalley involution and the Spin(9) of Reading 3

### 3.1 Proposition and proof

The statement holds, and we propose it for lemma 34 in the following form, as a Proposition after Proposal 5.5.

**Proposition 5.6 (the so(9) of the Chevalley involution is conjugate to the Spin(9) of Reading 3).** Let h be a Cartan subalgebra of e8 contained in the regular sl(9) of lemma 33, section 3, and let theta be the Chevalley involution of e8 for h. In a Chevalley basis, theta acts as -1 on h and maps e_alpha to -e_{-alpha}. Let sigma be the involution of the root table of lemma 34, which is +1 on h and on the integer roots and -1 on the half-integer roots. Its fixed algebra is so(16)_sigma, and its -1 eigenspace is the 128 of the four families. Then:

1. theta preserves sl(9) and acts on it by an outer involution, whose fixed algebra so(9)_theta is conjugate in SL(9) to the so(9) of lemma 33.
2. The fixed algebra k_theta of theta on e8 is isomorphic to so(16). As so(9)_theta-modules, k_theta = 36 + 84 and p_theta = 44 + 84, where p_theta is the -1 eigenspace. The 44 equals p_theta ∩ sl(9) and lies in the gravity sector. The two 84 are the graphs {x + theta x} and {x - theta x}, with x in Lambda^3 C^9, and each meets the C_3 and C_6 sectors equally.
3. so(9)_theta acts on the 16-dimensional vector of k_theta by the spinor.
4. so(9)_theta is conjugate in E8 to the Spin(9) of Reading 3, the image of Spin(9) under the spinor 16 inside so(16)_sigma, in the class with 128 -> 44 + 84. Under both, 248 = 36 + 44 + 84 + 84.
5. In E8 both connected subgroups are isomorphic to SO(9). The other spinor class, with an irreducible 128 and 248 = 36 + 84 + 128, is isomorphic to Spin(9).

*Proof.* (1) theta maps the root system A_8 to itself, since it acts as -1 on h, so it preserves sl(9). On sl(9) it acts as -1 on a Cartan subalgebra, and -1 lies outside W(A_8), so the involution is outer. Since 9 is odd, every outer involution of sl(9) has the form X -> -B^{-1} X^T B with B symmetric. Its fixed algebra so(B) has dimension 36 and is conjugate in SL(9) to the so(9) of lemma 33. The conjugating element lies in SL(9), which commutes with the centre of SU(9), so it preserves the three sectors.

(2) The fixed algebra of theta is spanned by the e_alpha - e_{-alpha}, so it has dimension 120, the number of positive roots. It is the complexified maximal compact subalgebra of the split form e8(8), which is so(16) (recalled: Cartan's classification, Helgason, chapter X). On sl(9), theta has fixed part so(9)_theta (36) and -1 part 44, which is h plus the span of the e_alpha + e_{-alpha} for alpha a positive root of A_8. theta maps Lambda^3 to Lambda^6. For Y in so(9)_theta we have theta [Y, x] = [Y, theta x], so the maps x -> x +- theta x from Lambda^3 are so(9)_theta-equivariant and injective, and their images are the fixed and -1 parts of Lambda^3 + Lambda^6, each isomorphic to Lambda^3 C^9 = 84.

(3) As k_theta-modules, k_theta = Lambda^2 W with W the vector 16. So Lambda^2(W restricted to so(9)_theta) = 36 + 84. The 16-dimensional representations of so(9) are 16, 9 + 7·1 and 16·1. Their exterior squares are 36 + 84, 36 + 7·9 + 21·1 and 120·1. Only the spinor fits.

(4) Complex e8 has two conjugacy classes of involutions, with fixed algebras so(16) (dimension 120) and e7 + sl(2) (dimension 136) (recalled: Cartan; Kac's classification of automorphisms of finite order). So there is g in E8 with g theta g^{-1} = sigma. Then g maps k_theta to so(16)_sigma and p_theta to the 128, and g(so(9)_theta) is a spin(9) in so(16)_sigma. By (3) it acts on the 16 by the spinor, and by (2) it acts on the 128 as 44 + 84. Now let rho_1 and rho_2 be two embeddings of spin(9) in so(16)_sigma through the spinor, both with 128 -> 44 + 84. So A lies in SO(16), and the image in E8 of either lift of A to Spin(16) conjugates rho_1 to rho_2. Hence the class with 128 -> 44 + 84 is a single class under the subgroup Spin(16)/Z_2 of E8, and g(so(9)_theta) lies in it.

(5) The modules 36, 44 and 84 are tensor representations, on which -1 of Spin(9) acts trivially.
