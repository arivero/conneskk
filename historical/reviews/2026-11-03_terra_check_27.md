## Part A

**1. Theorem 27.1 and the index formula — Correct with amendment.**  
In [Theorem 27.1](../../lemmas/27_anomaly_reduced_action.md), the nonzero eigenspaces of \(D_f^2\) pair between the two chiralities. Trace class makes the sum of their contributions legitimate, so the stated trace identity holds at every cutoff.

The index and bundle conventions need to be explicit. Let \(D_f^+:S^+\otimes E_f\to S^-\otimes E_f\), and choose \(\gamma_5=+1\) on \(S^+\). Then \(\operatorname{ind}_f=n_{f,+}-n_{f,-}\). With the corresponding orientation and Hermitian-curvature convention, the displayed formula is

\[
\operatorname{ind}_f
 =\frac{1}{8\pi^2}\int_M\operatorname{tr}_{E_f}(F_f\wedge F_f)
 -\frac{N_c}{24}\langle p_1(TM),[M]\rangle .
\]

Reversing the chirality convention reverses the index. The curvature decomposition follows because \(G\) is traceless:

\[
\operatorname{tr}_{E_f}(F_f^2)
=N_cQ_f^2e^2F_{\rm em}^2+g^2\operatorname{tr}_c(G^2).
\]

Here the products are wedge products. For topologically nontrivial backgrounds, replace the literal trivial bundle \(M\times\mathbb C^{N_cN_f}\) by the appropriate colour and electromagnetic bundles, with charges normalized so that the flavour representations are globally defined.

**2. Corollary 27.2 — Correct with amendment.**  
The factor \(2\) in the regulated Jacobian comes from transforming both independent Grassmann variables. With \(T^3=\frac12\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\), \(N_c=3\), and \(Q=\operatorname{diag}(2/3,-1/3)\),

\[
2N_c\operatorname{tr}(T^3Q^2)=1,
\qquad
F_{\rm em}\wedge F_{\rm em}
=\tfrac12F_{\mu\nu}\widetilde F^{\mu\nu}\,d{\rm vol}.
\]

Thus the electromagnetic coefficient in [Corollary 27.2](../../lemmas/27_anomaly_reduced_action.md) is correct. The gluonic and gravitational terms cancel for \(\operatorname{tr}T=0\), provided the flavours have the same colour bundle. For \(T=1\), they remain; the integrated gravitational contribution to \(2\operatorname{Tr}\) is

\[
-f(0)\frac{N_cN_f}{12}\langle p_1(TM),[M]\rangle .
\]

The limit requires continuity of \(f\) at zero. The standard anomaly normalization additionally sets \(f(0)=1\); trace class alone supplies neither condition.

For the local statement, assume a smooth cutoff class for which the heat expansion can be used, and define \(E,\Omega\) by the Laplace-type square of the Dirac operator. The displayed \(a_4\) terms then give the stated density. The omitted lower-coefficient and other \(a_4\) terms have vanishing \(\gamma_5\) spin trace in this vector-coupled Dirac problem.

**3. Proposition 27.3 — Correct with amendment.**  
The identity in [Proposition 27.3](../../lemmas/27_anomaly_reduced_action.md) is correct for \(m_f>0\). Conjugation by \(\gamma_5\) makes the \(D_M\) term odd and hence gives it zero trace; the remaining nonzero eigenspaces cancel by chirality pairing. The surviving zero modes contribute \(n_{f,+}-n_{f,-}\). The scalar density in Proposition 17.8 instead involves \(n_{f,+}+n_{f,-}\).

This has a Ward-identity interpretation if the Jacobian and the composite mass insertion are given the **same specified regularization**. The trace identity alone does not construct an independently regulated functional measure or a local current. In the present mass convention,

\[
\{\gamma_5T,D_M+\gamma_5d\}=2dT,
\]

so the mass insertion is \(\bar\psi dT\psi\); after changing to a conventional scalar-mass basis, the corresponding insertion is pseudoscalar. The local identity also has a current-divergence term.

**4. Proposition 27.4 — Error.**  
The displayed coefficient is correct when \(f(0)\ne0\). For the heat cutoff it is

\[
\frac{4e^{-x}-1}{3},
\]

with limits \(1\) and \(-1/3\). The gluonic and gravitational regulated traces are proportional to \(f(x)-f(0)\).

The interpretation following [Proposition 27.4](../../lemmas/27_anomaly_reduced_action.md) goes beyond that calculation. An explicitly massive flavour breaks the axial symmetry, and the trace identity alone does not establish a Wess–Zumino term in its low-energy theory. Such a term can be required after specifying the retained symmetry, its background fields or spurions, and the anomaly-matching prescription.

The weights in this calculation depend on the chosen regulator. Making \(f\) part of a finite-scale spectral action does not by itself identify the fermion-measure regulator with that action or make these trace weights observables. The fermion measure and matching calculation must also be specified.

**5. Proposition 27.5 — Error.**  
The chiral pure-gauge form in [equation (1)](../../lemmas/27_anomaly_reduced_action.md) is valid after the paired sector is expressed in independent quark and antiquark variables. More precisely, the operator couples \(B=\Sigma\,d\Sigma^\dagger\) to one chirality and is generally non-self-adjoint. On the full \(H_0=(q_L,\bar q_R)\), the two blocks of \((U_\Sigma dU_\Sigma)|_{H_0}\) are

\[
\Sigma\,d\Sigma^\dagger,
\qquad
\Sigma^{T}d\overline{\Sigma}.
\]

A nontrivial \(\Sigma:S^4\to SU(2)\) cannot be joined to \(1\) by a continuous path of such maps. To define the relative Weyl Pfaffian sign, use a path of **connections**, such as \(A_t=tB\), with the endpoint fibres compared by the gauge transformation. For one Weyl doublet, [Witten’s equation (6), p. 325](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-2693(82)90728-6), gives “the opposite sign of the square root”; \(N_c\) doublets give \((-1)^{N_c}\).

That is an anomalous relative sign for the chosen Pfaffian transport and endpoint identification. The pointwise \(R_\Sigma\) also changes fermion coordinates. Pulling the original measure through that change preserves the value of the same integral; the measure Jacobian must accompany the transformed action. Accordingly, “the quark integral in that frame changes by \((-1)^{N_c}\)” is too strong without specifying the comparison of measures. Likewise, the fixed classical form is unchanged as a geometric form, while its expression in fixed reduced variables acquires the connection in (1).

The quoted two-flavour sigma-model coupling is correctly attributed: [Lee, Ohmori and Tachikawa, arXiv:2009.00033v2, §2.5 and equation (2.29)](https://arxiv.org/html/2009.00033) define it as \((-1)^{N_c[\sigma]}\) on spin four-manifolds. They explicitly note that “there is no appropriate five-form” for this case. Matching that term to the quark anomaly requires the sigma-model and its anomaly-matching assumptions; Proposition 17.10 alone constructs neither a pion action nor its measure.

**6. Remark 27.6 — Correct with amendment.**  
For the flow from \(A=0\) to the gauge-equivalent \(A=B\), Witten relates the Pfaffian sign to the mod-two index on \(S^4\times\mathbb R\). The two ends can be compactified, with the appropriate gauge trivializations, to the nontrivial \(SU(2)\) bundle on \(S^5\). Witten’s footnote 2 describes an invariant zero mode for a special connection; the general protected statement is the **parity** of the kernel.

The nontrivial bundle is, after choices of identification, the bundle \(SU(3)\to S^5\) used in [research-state §6.12](../corpus/connes_kk_research_state.md). Its twisted Dirac operator has the same mod-two index. For isospin \(j\), the parity is that of \(2j(j+1)(2j+1)/3\), odd for \(j=2k+\tfrac12\).

The outcome paragraph should distinguish the bordism groups: the sigma field represents a class in \(\widetilde\Omega^{\rm Spin}_4(SU(2))\), whereas the five-dimensional anomaly is represented in \(\Omega^{\rm Spin}_5(BSU(2))\). Their signs agree in this construction through the anomaly comparison; they are not literally one element of one group. The abstract bundle identification also does not identify the internal gauge group or dynamics with the flavour construction.

## Part B

**7. Proposition 26.3c — Correct with amendment.**  
The boundary equation in [Proposition 26.3c](../../lemmas/26_interior_vacuum_matching_scale.md) follows from a self-adjoint bulk-plus-wall problem. For example, normalize \(u=(S+A')/\sqrt2\), \(v=(S-A')/\sqrt2\), and take

\[
H(f,g,\chi)=(-g',\,f',\,w g_u(\ell)),
\qquad f_u(\ell)=w\chi,
\]

along with \(g_{O'}(\ell)=0\), \(f_v(\ell)=0\), and the stated conditions at zero. The boundary form cancels. Eliminating \(\chi\) for \(m\ne0\) gives \(f_u=(w^2/m)g_u\).

Let \(a=m\ell\), \(s=\sin2\theta\), and
\(\Delta=\sin^2a-s^2\). Direct elimination gives

\[
2a\Delta\sin a
=\omega\cos a\,(\Delta+\sin^2a).
\]

The two nonzero-mode limits reproduce \(\Delta\sin a=0\) and the first referee’s \(\cos a(2\sin^2a-s^2)=0\). At \(w=0\), the decoupled wall Weyl zero mode must also be counted.

At \(\theta=\pi/4\), the equation factorizes as stated. The written proof divides by \(\cos2\theta\), so it misses this angle; use the determinant before division. For every \(0<\theta<\pi/2\), put \(u=M_W\ell=\arcsin(s/\sqrt2)\). On \(0<a<u\),

\[
\omega
=2a\tan a\,
 \frac{s^2-\sin^2a}{s^2-2\sin^2a}
\]

is continuous and strictly increasing from \(0\) to \(+\infty\). This proves the stated light root, including at \(\theta=\pi/4\).

For the **displayed** \((10,6)\) charge-\(2/3\) channel, a state lies below \(M_W\) for every finite positive wall mass; in the infinite-mass limit the lowest root of this isolated channel is \(M_W\). The same construction works for the specified \((5,15)\) plane with chiralities exchanged and the corresponding wall Weyl field. A complete gauge-invariant wall multiplet, other boundary mixings, bulk masses and the other towers require separate analysis. Their presence can add still lighter states, so equality for the lightest state of a completed model is unproved.

**8. Result 26.8 — Correct with amendment.**  
The numbers in [Result 26.8](../../lemmas/26_interior_vacuum_matching_scale.md) reproduce for the stated tree-level model. At the connector wall, \(R\) in the hypercharge combination must mean the **rotated wall direction**

\[
L_\ell=(S+A')/\sqrt2,\qquad R_\ell=(S-A')/\sqrt2.
\]

With \(p=\cos\phi\), \(q=\sin\phi\), the wall conditions are

\[
O'=0,\quad -qR_\ell+pB=0,\quad
\partial_xL_\ell=0,\quad
\partial_x(pR_\ell+qB)=rm^2(pR_\ell+qB).
\]

At \(x=0\), \(S,A,B\) are Neumann and \(O\) is Dirichlet. Eliminating these four amplitudes gives, for the lowest neutral root \(a=M_Z\ell\), with \(t=r/\ell\),

\[
(1+t\,a\cot a)\sin^2a
=\frac{\sin^2(2\theta)}2
 \bigl(1+p^2+t\,a\cot a\bigr).
\]

The definitions of \(p^2\) and \(r/\ell\) in the result follow from the stated zero-angle coupling prescription. Using its inputs, the two recomputed rows are:

| \(\theta\) | \(M_Z\ell\) | \(r/\ell\) | \(M_W^2/M_Z^2\) | 4D reference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(\pi/4\) | 0.990308 | 1.6336 | 0.62898 | 0.76230 |
| 1.13774393 | 0.675757 | 1.5957 | 0.70868 | 0.76067 |

The quoted shifts, approximately \(-0.13\) and \(-0.05\), therefore reproduce. The four-dimensional number is a meaningful **reference within this matching prescription**. A physical precision comparison needs the model’s coupling definitions, wavefunction overlaps, threshold matching and radiative corrections: those can affect the comparison at the same order as a small finite-interval shift. The “about \(1.2\) TeV” threshold should remain a numerical statement for this prescription, with its parameter range stated.

**9. Revised outcome and two regimes — Error.**

The proposition proves a lighter charge-\(2/3\) partner in its displayed channel. It does not exclude identifying a higher eigenstate with the top.

Indeed, let \(u=M_W\ell<\pi/4\). Equation (3) has an eigenvalue \(m=2M_W\) when

\[
\omega=\frac{4u\sin2u}{1+2\cos2u}.
\]

For example, Python gives \(\theta=1\), \(\omega=2.04350\), a state at \(2M_W\), and another at \(0.84972M_W\). The higher state has nonzero \(O_L\) and \(R_R\) components. Its physical identification and the lighter state’s couplings still need analysis. Replace the summary by the proved lighter-partner statement.

The low-tower potential, the wall-mass spectrum, the wall kinetic term and the step-threshold running also use different simplified spectra. A combined model needs the altered complete-multiplet determinants and thresholds before its vacuum and matching scale can be asserted together. The qualifications in “The top against the matching scale” correctly treat the Yukawa table as an illustration.
