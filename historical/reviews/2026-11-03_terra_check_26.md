1. **Lemma 26.1 and Theorem 26.2 — Correct with amendment.**

   Equation (1) is the correct extension of the determinant in lemma 23 for an independently specified pure frequency-two Dirac channel: its contribution is \(-n_2F(\sin^2 2\theta)=-n_2F(4u)\). The conclusion assumes precisely that tower and its stated multiplicity; a full representation can contain additional towers.

   The strict monotonicity proof is valid. In the referee amendment to [lemma 24](../../lemmas/24_commutative_parent.md), the covariance is strictly positive: both functions in it are strictly decreasing and the measure has nontrivial support. Thus \(e(z)\) is strictly increasing, making each \(J(cu)/J(4u)\), \(c<4\), strictly increasing. The argument remains valid at \(u=1/4\), where the arguments of \(J\) are positive and \(J\) is smooth. Python quadrature gives

   \[
   n_c=2.8855033120557163.
   \]

   All cases stated in Theorem 26.2 follow. At \(n_2=n_c,\ n_1=0\), the minimum at \(\theta=\pi/4\) is degenerate: its leading displacement is quartic. At \(n_2=27/10,\ n_1=0\), the endpoint minima are also degenerate, with leading behavior

   \[
   \mathcal V(\theta)-\mathcal V(0)
   =\frac{99}{25}\theta^4\log(1/\theta^2)+O(\theta^4).
   \]

   In item 2, both factors of \(|G|=4J(4u)(n_2-R(u))\) decrease as \(u\) increases. Hence \(\Psi\) increases with \(t\) as claimed. At \(n_1=n_*\), the endpoint is a degenerate minimum and the potential still decreases strictly on the open interval. The endpoints are stationary in the \(\theta\) variable because \(dt/d\theta=0\) there; this does not add interior stationary points.

   **Amendment:** state \(n_1,n_2\geq0\), distinguish endpoint and interior stationarity, and say that the theorem leaves the general case \(n_1>0,\ n_2<n_c\) unclassified. Physical multiplicities have further integrality and representation constraints.

3. **Proposition 26.3b — Gap.**

   The representation and charge calculations are correct under the stated embedding. With multiplicities for eigenvalues of \(F\),

   \[
   \begin{array}{c|c}
   \text{representation}&\text{frequencies}\\ \hline
   \mathbf4&\pm1\text{, each twice}\\
   \mathbf5&0\text{ three times},\ \pm2\text{ once each}\\
   \mathbf{10}&0\text{ four times},\ \pm2\text{ three times each}.
   \end{array}
   \]

   For \(\mathbf5=(2,2)\oplus(1,1)\), a singlet \(U_R\) of hypercharge \(2/3\) needs \(B-L=4/3\); the smallest \(SU(4)\) irreducible representation containing the required color triplet is \(\mathbf{15}\). The ordinary doublet has \(R_3=-1/2\), and the exotic doublet has \(Y=7/6\).

   For \(\mathbf{10}=(3,1)\oplus(1,3)\oplus(2,2)\), \(U_R=R^+\) needs \(B-L=-2/3\), supplied by a color triplet in the smallest choice \(\mathbf6\). Here the ordinary doublet has \(R_3=+1/2\). These are minimality claims for the specified \(\mathbf5\) or \(\mathbf{10}\) embeddings and irreducible \(SU(4)\) representations. Also,

   \[
   \Lambda^2E
   =(\mathbf{10},\mathbf6)\oplus
     ((\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf5),\overline{\mathbf{10}}),
   \]

   so the stated \((\mathbf{10},\mathbf6)\) inclusion is correct.

   Let \(\phi=2\theta\), \(a=m\ell\), and use the rotated fields \(A'=\cos\phi\,A+\sin\phi\,O\), \(O'=-\sin\phi\,A+\cos\phi\,O\). An explicit self-adjoint fermion problem is:

   - At \(x=0\), let \(\psi_R\) have Neumann conditions on \(S,A\) and Dirichlet on \(O\); impose the complementary conditions on \(\psi_L\).
   - At \(x=\ell\), impose Dirichlet on \(\psi_R(O')\) and \(\psi_R(S+A')\), and Neumann on \(\psi_R(S-A')\); again impose complementary conditions on \(\psi_L\).

   Its determinant is proportional to

   \[
   \cos a\,\bigl(2\sin^2a-\sin^2\phi\bigr).
   \]

   Thus the angle-dependent tower has the stated equation, and there is also an angle-independent half-integer tower. With both \(S\) and \(A'\) Neumann at the second wall, \(S_R\) is an exact massless mode and the other plane has the pure frequency-two tower. The same construction applies to the appropriate \(\mathbf5\) plane with the chirality roles exchanged. These statements require the complementary chiral conditions and, for a gauge-invariant completion, conditions on the complete wall multiplets.

   The \(n_d\geq27/5\) argument is correct for the displayed determinant with \(n_1=0\). Python gives \(0.858512\) and \(1.001144\) for the two quoted minima, and found no interior stationary point in the two other quoted cases.

   **Gap:** no finite wall-mass boundary problem has been specified or solved. Replace the claim that the top *lies between* \(M_W\) and \(2M_W\), and that the exotic reaches the tower scale along the interpolation, by an open calculation. The full multiplet’s other modes and their contribution to the potential also remain to be included.

4. **Tower table and Propositions 26.4–26.5 — Error.**

   At \(\theta=0\), the listed gauge boundary conditions and coefficients are correct. The table should also show the neutral hypercharge combination as \(N,N\) and its orthogonal neutral combination as \(N,D\); both have zero contribution to the displayed unbroken-group traces. The coefficient \(-\tfrac72\operatorname{tr}Q^2\) includes the massive vector, its fifth component and ghosts. The \(A_5\) Higgs zero mode is counted in the Standard Model coefficient; its nonzero modes are the gauge partners already counted in the vector towers.

   For three complete, massless bulk \(E\) generations, \(F=8\) per integer level is correct in the \(\theta\to0\) limit. At nonzero angle, frequency-one masses are shifted to \((n\pi\pm\theta)/\ell\); at finite angle their thresholds and couplings cannot generally be replaced by an exactly degenerate integer tower. Consequently Proposition 26.4 is an algebraic property of equation (3), conditional on its common \(F I_n\) term.

   Equations (3)–(5), the stated asymptotics, and Proposition 26.5 follow algebraically from the displayed step-threshold model. The cancellation of the power term in (4) concerns the **left–right difference within \(Sp(2)\)**. The full bulk group is \(Sp(2)\times SU(4)\), so differences involving color can retain a power contribution.

   There are two material errors in applying these equations:

   - Equation (4) itself gives, at \(S=1\), \(I_h=\log2\). With the stated inputs and \(M_{\rm KK}=321.44\) GeV it gives
     \[
     \alpha_R^{-1}-\alpha_2^{-1}=61.69,
     \]
     rather than 60.2. The latter is the Standard Model extrapolation before adding the half-integer tower.
   - The frequency-two multiplet required for the proposed vacuum is omitted from (3). For example, a complete five-dimensional Dirac \((\mathbf{10},\mathbf6)\) has leading level contributions
     \[
     \Delta b_i=\left(\frac{296}{15},\,24,\,\frac{40}{3}\right),

     before its boundary-dependent threshold splitting and zero-mode subtractions. These are not a universal \(F\).

   Replace “the tower of the parent” and the claimed physical independence from bulk localization by statements conditional on the exact spectrum in (3). Recompute the full representation, including its zero modes and split thresholds, before applying the result to the proposed parent. Boundary kinetic counterterms also need a renormalization prescription; they are generally generated by orbifold loops, as shown by [Carena, Tait and Wagner, sections 1–2](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-ph/0207056).

6. **Gauge-Higgs Yukawa matching and finite angles — Error.**

   For the specified flat pure channel, canonical normalization and the small-angle mass formulas give the **tree-level** relation \(y_q=(q/2)g\). The same higher-dimensional covariant derivative fixes the corresponding bulk gauge and \(A_5\) vertices. Physical four-dimensional gauge and Yukawa couplings nevertheless receive different finite corrections and threshold effects. In particular, [Lim and Maru, equations (1)–(3)](https://arxiv.org/html/0904.0304) calculate a finite one-loop violation of gauge–Yukawa universality. The claim of no separate running must therefore be restricted to the local bulk coupling and a specified matching scheme.

   The first row’s finite-angle identification is incorrect. Let \(v_{\rm ang}\) be the canonically normalized flat Wilson-line background, normalized at small angle. Then

   \[
   v_{\rm ang}=\frac{2\sqrt2\,\theta}{g\ell},\qquad
   y_{\rm physical}=\sqrt2\,\frac{\partial m}{\partial v_{\rm ang}}.
   \]

   At \(\theta=\pi/4\), \(m_2=2M_W\) gives \(\sqrt2\,m_2/v_F=\sqrt2 g\) if \(v_F\) is defined by \(M_W=gv_F/2\). It does **not** give the Higgs coupling. The pure frequency-two branches have slopes corresponding to \(|y|=g\) at tree level. They are degenerate at this angle, so the coupling is a matrix on the degenerate subspace. For the tower with \(m=M_W\), the mass derivative vanishes at \(\theta=\pi/4\).

   There is a second obstruction to interpreting 164 GeV as a prediction: the pure channel has the exact massless colored exotic and a second fermion at \(M_{\rm KK}/2\). Other half-integer thresholds also occur below \(M_{\rm KK}\). Standard Model-only running below 321 GeV is therefore an illustrative boundary-value calculation, not the running of that spectrum. The independent-bulk-Higgs calculation in steer item 11 has a different action and remains conditional on its own content. The finite-angle distinction between a Higgs background and its couplings is also explicit in [Hosotani et al., equations (6.1)–(6.2)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0806.0480).

7. **Quadratic coefficient and cancellation — Correct with amendment.**

   The coefficient

   \[
   \frac32\zeta(3)\left(\frac{54}{5}-n_1-4n_2\right)
   \]

   is correct, and it cannot vanish for integer \(n_1,n_2\) in equation (1). The statement about added bosons needs a multiplicity convention: a real scalar determinant carries a \(1/2\) coefficient and a real vector carries \(3/2\); mixed boundary projections can also yield non-integer prefactors. Integer \(nq^2\) contributions hold only for an explicitly specified normalization and class of pure channels.

   “Cancellation of the quadratic coefficient” is appropriate. It does not mean cancellation of the one-loop potential. In particular,

   \[
   F(z)=J(0)z+\tfrac14z^2\log z+O(z^2),
   \]

   so the remaining \(\theta^4\log\theta^2\) term must be retained when discussing a tuned small angle.

8. **“Two regimes” — Error.**

   The numerical estimates describe the simplified equations, but several sentences extend beyond what those equations establish.

   - At the low tower, the quoted hypercharge discrepancy of about 60 is the Standard Model-only extrapolation. In the formal step model (3), it is \(61.69\) at \(S=1\). At \(\theta=\pi/4\), the actual mixed profiles and thresholds must be used. A wall term of order \(60\) in \(\alpha_Y^{-1}\) is therefore an estimate, with normalization and matching conditions specified.
   - Separate bulk couplings are required if the only admitted non-universal correction is the stated hypercharge wall term. General non-universal wall kinetic terms provide another way to fit the couplings, so separate bulk couplings are not a general requirement.
   - For the formal high-scale meeting point, the small-angle relation gives \(\theta=5.67\times10^{-12}\). For integer counts, equation (1) has no **global** minimum at a small positive angle: for \(n_1>0\),
     \[
     V(1-t)-V(t)=-n_1[F(1-t)-F(t)]<0
     \quad(0<t<1/2),
     \]
     while the \(n_1=0\) integer cases follow from Theorem 26.2. This proves the global statement. It does not prove the absence of every metastable small-angle minimum in the cases left to scans.
   - The statement that warping leaves the left–right difference unchanged is too broad. The flat threshold sums do not determine warped spectra, boundary counterterms or their matching. Warping, bulk masses, antiperiodic conditions, extra representations, boundary interactions and wall kinetic terms remain model-dependent escapes.

   The unqualified sentence that frequency-two fermions give a minimum at \(\theta\geq\pi/4\) also misses the \(\theta=0\) endpoint minima noted in item 2. The numerical top predictions require the corrections in items 3–6.

   There is no direct contradiction with [lemma 25](../../lemmas/25_sum_rule_effective_dimension.md): its \(y_t/g=\sqrt2\) endpoint is a leading small-mass **spectral-action trace** result with its stated matter normalization; lemma 26 assumes a Yang–Mills gauge action and particular frequency channels. The steer note’s universal bulk-Higgs running and the main paper’s towerless, classical statements likewise have different hypotheses. They should not be combined into one matching calculation without constructing the common action and spectrum.
