**1. Theorem 23.1** ([statement and proof](../../lemmas/23_two_gradings_interval.md))

**1a. Item 1 — Correct.** Each wall condition is a maximal isotropic subspace for the boundary form. The resulting first-order operator is self-adjoint with compact resolvent, and the spin grading preserves its domain and anticommutes with it.

**1b. Item 2 — Correct.** Under the equal-dimension and no-\(-1\)-eigenvalue hypotheses, the principal logarithm gives an anti-Hermitian \(K\) satisfying \(\Gamma _0K\Gamma _0=-K\). The stated \(D_F=\Gamma _0K\), its singular values, and
\[
\Gamma _1=e^{lK}\Gamma _0e^{-lK}

follow from the two-projection decomposition.

**1c. Item 3 — Correct with amendment.** The graph boundary condition and the converse hold on the stated branch \(l\|D_F\|<\pi/2\). Define \(X\) explicitly as the \(H_-\to H_+\) block of \(\tan(lD_F)\). The spectral part of Theorem 22.1 applies without a real structure; its real-structure conclusion additionally needs the hypothesis in item 4. At an angle \(\pi/2\), the principal logarithm and the isolated window used here cease to be unique.

**1d. Item 4 — Correct.** The antiunitary \(J_1\) is an additional hypothesis. With \(\widehat J=EJ_1\), the signs are \((+,+,-)\), and \(J_F=\Gamma _0J_1\) has the stated finite signs. In particular,
\[
J_F\Gamma _1J_F^{-1}
=\Gamma _0(J_1\Gamma _1J_1^{-1})\Gamma _0
=-\Gamma _0\Gamma _1\Gamma _0.
\]
The antilinearity of \(J_1\) is compatible with \(J_1KJ_1^{-1}=K\) on the principal branch.

**2. Theorem 23.2** ([statement and proof](../../lemmas/23_two_gradings_interval.md))

**2a. Items 1 and 2 — Correct with amendment.** Domain preservation is equivalent to commuting with the respective wall grading, and \([D,f]=Ef'\). The compression formula in item 2 requires the equal-dimension and branch hypotheses under which the isometry \(W\) of Theorem 22.1 exists. State those hypotheses at the item.

**2b. Item 3, aligned walls — Error as stated for an arbitrary \(*\)-subalgebra \(P\).** A constant odd \(a\in P\) generally fails the endpoint conditions and is an ambient multiplier, rather than an element of the parent algebra. More seriously, \(E_0(P)\) need not equal \(P\cap\{\Gamma _0\}'\).

Let \(H=\mathbb C^2\), \(\Gamma _0=\sigma_z\), and let \(P\) be the unital \(*\)-algebra generated by \(\sigma_x+\sigma_z\). Then
\[
E_0(P)=\operatorname{span}\{1,\sigma_z\},
\qquad
P\cap\{\Gamma _0\}'=\mathbb C1.
\]
The first space is the compression image, while the second is the even part of \(P\). Compactly supported scalar profiles show that the image of the actual boundary algebra is \(E_0(P)\).

The correction is to assume \(\Gamma _0P\Gamma _0=P\). Then \(E_0(P)=P^{\mathrm{ev}}\), and the aligned-wall conclusion holds. Continue to distinguish constant ambient multipliers from parent-algebra elements.

**2c. Item 4 — Correct.** Under the stated \(J_1\) hypothesis and \([P,P^\circ]=0\), the parent has a real structure and satisfies order zero and order one, even when \(P\) contains elements odd for \(\Gamma _0\). The parent grading is \(\widehat\Gamma\), which commutes with those elements. Pointwise,
\[
[\pi(f),J\pi(g)^*J^{-1}]=0,\qquad
[[D,\pi(f)],J\pi(g)^*J^{-1}]
=E[f',g^\circ]=0.

- Agostino Devastato and Pierre Martinetti, “[Twisted spectral triple for the Standard Model and spontaneous breaking of the Grand Symmetry](https://arxiv.org/html/1411.1320v3),” *Mathematical Physics, Analysis and Geometry* **20** (2017), article 2. The correction is to describe a **structural analogy** between involutive automorphisms and compare the actual representations, opposites and first-order conditions before claiming an identification.

It is undefined at both poles, and at \(\theta=\pi/2\) the finite logarithm and lowest-window choice are degenerate. **5. Theorem 23.6** ([boundary conditions and theorem](../../lemmas/23_two_gradings_interval.md))

**5a. Connector at one wall — Correct with amendment.** The stated gauge boundary conditions form a consistent boundary-value problem with the connector treated as prescribed wall data. A real fermion connector can also be placed at one wall. For example, choose a unit vector \(e\) in the \(+1\) eigenspace of \(\Gamma _1\), put \(f=J_1e\), and set
\[
K_c=|f\rangle\langle e|-|e\rangle\langle f|,
\qquad
\Gamma_{1,c}=e^{\eta K_c}\Gamma _1e^{-\eta K_c}.
\]
Then \(K_c\) anticommutes with \(\Gamma _1\), commutes with \(J_1\), and \(J_1\) anticommutes with \(\Gamma_{1,c}\). Restricting the wall algebra to the common stabilizer gives the connector conditions and retains the parent order conditions.

The amendment is to specify whether the connector is prescribed gauge-breaking data or a term in the fermion boundary operator. In the latter case, \(\Gamma_{1,c}\) changes the affected fermion tower; the universal fermion product later in the lemma must be recalculated for that channel.

**5b. Charged and neutral masses — Correct.** An independent solution uses principal angles between the Neumann subspaces at the two walls. Put \(s=\sin(2\theta)\). For each charged direction the squared sine of the angle is \(s^2/2\). In the neutral sector the two Neumann directions are \(L'\) and \(\sqrt{3/5}\,R'+\sqrt{2/5}\,B\); their projection onto the Dirichlet complement at the first wall has one nonzero squared singular value \(4s^2/5\). Consequently
\[
\sin^2(M_Wl)=\frac{s^2}{2},\qquad
\sin^2(M_Zl)=\frac{4s^2}{5},
\]

**5c. Massless modes — Correct with amendment.** For \(0<\theta<\pi/2\), the intersection has dimension nine: the photon and eight gluons. **5d. Normalization — Correct.** With the stated fundamental trace forms, equal five-dimensional coefficients, flat profiles and no boundary kinetic terms,

\frac1{e^2}=\frac{8}{3g_2^2}.
