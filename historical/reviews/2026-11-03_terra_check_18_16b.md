## 9. Lemma 18, No-go theorem 9

**Correct.** Differentiation of the heat-trace density gives (41), which is strictly negative for \(c>0\), nonzero \(V_*\), and the positive measure in (4). The theorem is fairly limited by the phrase “positive Laplace class.”

The conclusion can nevertheless be sharpened on flat \(\mathbb R^4\). For a sufficiently integrable real cutoff \(f\), let \(\lambda_j^2\) be the eigenvalues of \(V_*^2\). Direct integration of the momentum variable gives

\[
\mathcal U'(c)=
-\frac{c\Lambda^2}{2\pi^2}
\sum_j\lambda_j^2
\int_{c^2\lambda_j^2/\Lambda^2}^{\infty}f(z)\,dz. \tag{R1}
\]

For **nonnegative** \(f\), a finite \(c>0\) is stationary exactly when every tail in (R1) associated with a nonzero \(\lambda_j\) vanishes. Thus:

- if \(f\) has nonzero support arbitrarily far out, there is no finite stationary point on this ray;
- if \(f\) has compact support ending at \(R\), there is a stationary plateau once every nonzero \(c^2\lambda_j^2/\Lambda^2\geq R\).

This argument uses the continuum momentum integral; it is not the proof given for a finite torus. Sign-changing cutoffs can have cancellations in (R1), and a radial path with an additional fixed, noncommuting mass matrix need not have the form \(cV_*\).

A nonconstant cutoff flat near zero cannot satisfy (4): a nonzero positive measure on \((0,\infty)\) gives \(f'(0)=-\int s\,d\nu(s)<0\). CCM use positive test functions and their asymptotic moments; profiles flat near zero are allowed and are outside the theorem’s stated class. For nonnegative profiles, however, (R1) supplies the separate conclusion above. See [CCM, Introduction and §6.2](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0610241).

\gamma_X=\sigma_3,\quad d_X=\sigma_1,\quad

## 14. Lemma 16, Example 16.9

**Error.** The two algebra-generation calculations and the abstract classified bimodule are correct. The claimed application of Corollary 16.8 with the subsequently chosen nontrivial grading is not justified by the specified even parent, and the compressed potential does not repair it.

For the generation calculations:

- The matrices \(1,\Gamma_a,\Gamma_a\Gamma_b\) for \(a<b\) are \(16\) real-linearly independent elements commuting with \(j\). Since \(\dim_{\mathbb R}\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb H}(W)=16\), the real algebra generated by the five \(\Gamma_a\) is \(M_2(\mathbb H)\).
- For Hermitian \(B\), the fourth moment gives
  \(Q(w^*Bw/w^*w)=(B+\operatorname{tr}(B)1)/5\). This ranges over all Hermitian matrices. Their products generate \(M_4(\mathbb C)\) as a real algebra.

The use of two copies of the four-space is mathematically legitimate. It supplies independent direct summands and the \(32\)-dimensional bimodule. For this particular grading of \(W\) and trivial grading of \(V\), the graded flip is the ordinary adjoint flip, and

\[
\Gamma_F(\xi,\eta)=(\Gamma_5\xi,-\eta\Gamma_5),
\]

exactly the graded representation in [Chamseddine–Connes, Proposition 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/0706.3688). The full algebra is a **graded prealgebra**; its even part is

\[
\mathbb H\oplus\mathbb H\oplus M_4(\mathbb C).
\]

The obstruction is that an even parent has \([P\gamma_XP,P aP]=0\) for every compressed parent function. But the proposed grading \(\Gamma_5=5Q(x_5)\) anticommutes with \(Q(x_1)=\Gamma_1/5\). Thus the full compressed \(M_2(\mathbb H)\) and that grading cannot both arise from the stated even parent. They can be chosen **after** truncation as abstract graded-algebra data, and the even subalgebra then gives the real zero-cycle orientability relation.

Similarly, \(d=\mu\Gamma_1\) is the compression of a bounded real multiplication operator, but adding that operator to the original parent Dirac operator does not retain the original kernel as a spectral projection: multiplication by the nonconstant real coordinate does not preserve the holomorphic kernel. It also does not preserve the original parent grading as an odd perturbation.

Finally, \(d\) commutes with the centre of the full prealgebra, namely \(\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb C\). It does **not** commute with the whole centre after passing to the even algebra: for \(e_+=(1+\Gamma_5)/2\), \([\Gamma_1,e_+]\ne0\). It still commutes with the two sheet projections.

## Part 2: scope of the heavy-limit conclusion

Within the three-generation, zero-Majorana, top-only family of Proposition 4, at fixed cutoff and for the heat function, **both** the charged Taylor mass and its unique positive timelike pole tend to zero. The neutral Taylor and pole masses also tend to zero. The result refutes an infinitely heavy \(W,Z,t\) limit **for these background data and these quadratic mass definitions**. It also rules out a stationary nonzero radial vacuum in the positive-Laplace class. It does not rule out other finite data, a different action or constraint, a joint \(\Lambda\to\infty\) limit, or a quantum effective theory.

The top-only Taylor conclusion is broader than (4). For a nonnegative smooth compactly supported \(f\) with \(f(0)>0\), define

\[
M_1=\int_0^\infty zf(z)\,dz,\qquad
M_2=\int_0^\infty z^2f(z)\,dz.
\]

The heat formulas extend by linearity and smooth functional calculus to

\[
A_f(x)=\frac2x\int_0^x zf(z)\,dz,\qquad
w_f(x)=\frac3{x^3}\int_0^x z^2f(z)\,dz. \tag{R2}
\]

One way to justify the extension is to approximate a smooth compactly supported \(f(z)\), with the derivatives needed for the quadratic variation, by finite real linear combinations of \(e^{-nz}\): write \(f(z)=e^{-z}g(e^{-z})\) and approximate the smooth \(g\) and its derivatives by polynomials. The divided-difference formula for the variation permits passage to the limit. Thus

\[
\frac{M_{W,\mathrm{Taylor}}^2}{\Lambda^2}
\sim\frac{M_1}{f(0)x}.
\]

This includes a nonnegative cutoff flat near zero. For a nonanalytic cutoff there need not be a prescribed analytic continuation defining the pole used in Theorem 5.

Several heavy doublets change the answer. Suppose, for the heat cutoff, that \(P\) is the total particle-trace weight of complete massless doublets, and heavy doublet \(i\), of weight \(d_i>0\), has one Dirac member of mass \(m_i\) and one massless member. Then

\[
C_W(y)=\frac{\Lambda^2}{4\pi^2}
 \left(Pg(y)+\sum_i d_i h_{x_i}(y)\right),\qquad
\frac{M_{W,\mathrm{Taylor}}^2}{\Lambda^2}
=\frac{3}{2}\,
\frac{\sum_i d_i A(x_i)}
     {P+\sum_i d_i w(x_i)}. \tag{R3}
\]

For any fixed finite number of doublets, if \(P>0\) and all \(x_i\to\infty\), the Taylor mass tends to zero. If \(P=0\) and all \(x_i=x\), (R3) instead gives

\[
M_{W,\mathrm{Taylor}}^2\sim\frac{m^4}{2\Lambda^2}.
\]

The full heat form factor also has a unique positive pole in this equal-mass case. To find its scale, put \(z=cx\), \(0<c<1\), in (21). Concentration near \(u=0\) gives

\[
h_x(-cx)\sim
\frac{2(1-2c)}{x(1-c)^2}.
\]

Monotonicity then places the root at \(z/x\to1/2\), so
\(M_{W,\mathrm{pole}}^2\sim m^2/2\). At every fixed Euclidean momentum, however, the whole unnormalized quadratic kernel tends to zero. The divergent Taylor and pole scales differ, and neither alone establishes a healthy limiting gauge theory.

There is also a fully massive example. Give **both** members of every Standard Model doublet the same Dirac mass \(m\), with no Majorana masses. The charged form factor is proportional to

\[
e^{-x}\bigl[g(y)+2xH(y)\bigr].
\]

Its positive pole obeys \(2x<z_W<2x+2\). The neutral massive pole obeys
\((16/5)x<z_Z<(16/5)x+2\). Both pole masses therefore diverge with \(m\). Yet the fixed-momentum action tends to zero, and the charged Taylor kinetic coefficient is proportional to \(e^{-x}(1-x/2)\), which is negative for \(x>2\). This is a counterexample to a general fixed-cutoff no-go for diverging pole locations, while leaving the physical quality of that limit open.

The phrase “the heavy fermion leaves the loop” is only a shorthand for the suppression in this **classical spectral trace**. The light complete doublets supply the surviving charged kinetic form, but the top-only light space also retains \(b_L\) after removing \(t_L\). If \(P_{\rm light}\) is the projection off the top mass block, then on \(b_L\)

\[
P_{\rm light}T_1P_{\rm light}
=P_{\rm light}T_2P_{\rm light}=0,\qquad
P_{\rm light}T_3P_{\rm light}=-\tfrac12.
\]

These compressed operators do not obey the \(SU(2)\) commutation relations. Correspondingly, the limiting particle-trace coefficients have \(K_W=9/2\), \(K_{33}=21/4\), and \(K_{3Y}=-1/4\); a linearly realized unbroken electroweak gauge action would require equal weak coefficients and no \(3Y\) mixing. Vanishing vector masses therefore do **not** prove unbroken \(SU(2)\times U(1)\) on the entire light space.

The remaining **complete** massless doublets do carry the ordinary electroweak representation, and their separate contribution is gauge invariant. If finite data instead make both members of every removed doublet heavy, the retained light subspace can be invariant under the full group; a full limiting theory still requires control of the remaining fields, normalization, interactions, and vacuum.

## Part 4: what the two-sheet construction establishes

Example 16.9 is substantive as a concrete compression calculation: real base functions generate a quaternionic matrix algebra, real total-space functions generate a complex matrix algebra, and two copies give the classified \(32\)-dimensional bimodule. The calculation does not select those ingredients dynamically. The restriction to base functions on one sheet, the second independent sheet, real algebra generation, the four-dimensional window, and the grading axis are inputs.

At the finite algebraic level, the two-sheet bimodule is the standard construction from two representations:
\[
(W\otimes\bar V)\oplus(V\otimes\bar W).
\]
The graded flip supplies the real structure and the block sign supplies the KO-6 relation. The calculation’s additional content is its realization from the particular compressed function systems and its explicit matching to the classified data. The abstract bimodule and its adjoint-flip real structure are already stated in [Chamseddine–Connes, Remark 2.9](https://arxiv.org/html/0706.3688); the report makes no broader novelty claim.

The note appropriately lists several choices and says that no ordinary Kaluza–Klein vacuum is derived. It overstates the reach where it treats the chosen nontrivial grading and nonzero compressed \(d\) as consequences of the stated even-parent truncation. It also labels the centre of Corollary 16.3 too generally. Lemma 16’s later caveat that its generated algebras are complex and a quaternionic summand still needs additional real data is stale after Example 16.9’s real-algebra construction.

For [canonical section 4](../corpus/canonical_research.md), the example closes the **abstract finite-module** problem after adding a second sheet: it turns the nested actions on one four-space into independent summands acting on two Hom spaces. It does not turn four global sections into rank-four fibres over \(S^4\), derive the two sheets from the fibration, or produce the centre-connecting Dirac operator required for the subsequent algebra selection. The fibre-rank problem remains open.
