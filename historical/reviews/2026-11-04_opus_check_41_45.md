## Lemma 41

### Lemma 41.1 (the trace form is skew). Verdict: Correct.

With J gamma J^-1 = -gamma, J p_i J^-1 = p_i°, J p_j° J^-1 = J^2 p_j J^-2 = p_j and Tr(J A J^-1) = conj(Tr A), one gets Q_ij = -conj Tr(gamma p_i° p_j), and order zero turns this into -conj(Q_ji). Q is real because gamma, rho(p_i) and rho(p_j)° are commuting self-adjoint operators; gamma commutes with the opposite action because J gamma = -gamma J. Two remarks: the hypothesis J^2 = 1 can be weakened to J^2 = +-1, since only conjugation by J^2 enters, and the evenness of the algebra under gamma is used and belongs in the hypotheses. The attribution to Ćaćić agrees with the corpus record (connes_kk_research_state section 6.5); Ćaćić's text was not checked here.

### Proposition 42.3 (the moduli of the connector). Verdict: Error, in the count of the connector moduli.

The classification of pairs of lines is correct. Sp(2) acts transitively on the unit sphere S^7 of H^2, and the stabilizer of a unit vector w is the Sp(1) acting on L^perp (it fixes j w as well, since Sp(2) commutes with j); with v = alpha w + beta j w + u, Sp(1)_a is transitive on the spheres of L^perp, and the rephasings of w and v remove the phases of alpha and beta. So the pairs (Cw, Cv) are classified by a = |h(w, v)| and b = |omega(w, v)|. With omega(x, y) = h(j x, y) as defined, beta = omega(w, v) exactly.

The connector T_kappa is not classified by (a, b, |kappa|). When G acts on both copies by the same representation, the identity 1 in Hom(V, W) is G-fixed and c(1) = 1, so

    <c(1), T_kappa(1)> = tr T_kappa(1) = kappa (w* v)^2 = kappa alpha^2

is a G-invariant of T_kappa. It is also unchanged by the rephasings w -> e^{i phi} w, v -> e^{i theta} v, kappa -> e^{-2i(theta - phi)} kappa that leave T_kappa fixed. Its phase is a fourth real modulus when alpha != 0. *Counterexample:* T_kappa and T_{-kappa} on the same pair of lines with alpha != 0 have the same a, b, |kappa| and the invariants kappa alpha^2 and -kappa alpha^2, so they lie in different G-orbits. Python confirms the invariance under a random element of Sp(2) and under the rephasing. The proof's last sentence, "the coupling kappa is unchanged by G and rephased with the lines", uses the rephasing a second time after it has been spent on the phases of alpha and beta. Amend: the moduli are a, b, |kappa| and arg(kappa alpha^2), three when alpha = 0; a symmetry larger than the diagonal Sp(2), such as an independent phase on V, removes the phase. The Outcome ("two real numbers ... and the coupling") and STRATEGY T14 ("the connector moduli under Sp(2) are two overlaps and the coupling") need the same change.

## Lemma 43

### Proposition 43.1 (the change of the Berry connection). Verdict: Correct with amendment.

For fixed fibre operators the formula is exact, with no order involved: P nabla'_{X^h} s = P nabla_{X^h} s + P alpha(X^h) P s. The amendment concerns what is left out. A fibrewise twisted Dirac operator uses only the vertical part of the coefficient connection, so for alpha vanishing on vertical vectors the family D_b and the kernels are unchanged and the statement is exact. A vertical component of alpha changes D_b and moves the kernels, and a first-order motion of the kernels changes the Berry connection at first order: with U = P'P as identification and P (delta P) P = 0, the change contains P [nabla, delta P] P + P (delta P)(1 - P) nabla P, built from the second fundamental form. For instance, a kernel line n(b) in C^2 over S^2 moved to n(phi_eps(b)) along the flow of a vector field xi changes the Berry curvature by eps L_xi F, nonzero unless xi preserves the area form. So "do not enter at this order" should read: the statement is exact for horizontal alpha, and vertical components are excluded. The same restriction applies to "any vertical component can be added" in Theorem 43.2.

### Theorem 43.2 (which connections are Berry connections). Verdict: Correct with amendment.

The equivalence and the right inverse are correct after three amendments.

1. *Unitarity.* nabla^K_X s = P nabla_{X^h} s is unitary for the fibrewise L^2 product only when horizontal transport preserves the fibre measure: in general d<s, s'> contains the term int <s, s'> L_{X^h}(dy), and the unitary connection on the pushed-forward bundle adds (1/2) k(X), with k the mean curvature form of the fibres (Bismut; Berline-Getzler-Vergne, chapter 9; recalled from memory). Without this term all Berry connections share the same nonzero Hermitian part, and statement 1 fails as worded. Either add the term to the definition, or assume volume-preserving horizontal transport; the homogeneous examples (Riemannian submersions with totally geodesic fibres) satisfy it.
2. *The adjoint is transposed.* With C_b(X)_{ij} = int <s_i, X s_j>, the adjoint for the stated pairings is C_b^*(Y) = sum Y_ij s_i s_j^*, the density of Y; the lemma's sum Y_ij s_j s_i^* is C_b^* composed with transposition. On a discrete model in Python, <Y, C(X)> = -0.2163 + 0.0335i agrees with the pairing against sum Y_ij s_i s_j^* and differs from the pairing against the lemma's formula, -0.7685 + 0.0120i. Criterion 3 is unaffected, since the two families of products coincide as sets, and C_b^*(C_b C_b^*)^-1 remains a right inverse with either convention, since transposition preserves u(K_b).
3. *The step from 2 to 3.* The parenthetical should be replaced by: C_b^* is complex linear and C_b^*(Y^*) = C_b^*(Y)^*, so its kernel is closed under the adjoint and meets u(K_b) trivially exactly when it is zero.

### Corollary 43.3 (scalar changes). Verdict: Error, in its last clause; the two computations are correct.

Correct. The scalar criterion follows by the same argument as in amendment 3. On CP^3 the sixteen functions conj(z_i) z_j / |z|^2 are the entries of P_z and are independent, and Q(w* B w / w* w) = (B + tr(B) 1)/5 is invertible on Herm(4) (eigenvalues 1/5 and 1). The error is in the last clause, "the complementary ten directions need matrix-valued alpha with components between the summands of E, as in the corpus construction and in the collar connection of lemma 29, case (b)", which the Outcome repeats, together with the Scope ("the collar connection that preserves the line summands is a choice forced by the base") and STRATEGY T15 ("which is why the collar connection of lemma 29 is matrix-valued").

### Proposition 44.2 (the light mass along the scaling). Verdict: Correct with amendment.

On the neutral block (nu_L, nu_R, J nu_R, J nu_L) the operator t T_R + t^alpha V_nu is the path with weights (s, t, conj s), s = t^alpha y. Its characteristic polynomial is lambda^4 - (t^2 + 2|s|^2) lambda^2 + |s|^4, with roots +-mu_+ and +-mu_-, where mu_+- = (t +- sqrt(t^2 + 4|s|^2))/2 are the roots of mu^2 - t mu - |s|^2 = 0. So the spectrum is plus and minus the roots of the displayed polynomial, and the light mass is the singular value |mu_-|; the 2 x 2 matrix [[0, s], [s, t]] is the symmetric mass matrix M_N of lemma 06, Proposition 6, whose eigenvalue mu_- is negative. In Python, at t = 10^4 and alpha = 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, the eigenvalues agree with the closed formula (light roots -0.0049, -0.4900, -48.76) and approach the asymptotics -y^2 t^{2 alpha - 1} (-0.0049, -0.49, -49). Amendments: write |y|^2 for complex y. In one generation the other Yukawa blocks act on other species and leave the neutral block alone, so the sentence "S ... move these values by O(1) relative corrections" should be removed; a coupling S to the neutral block would move eigenvalues by at most ||S|| in absolute value (Weyl's inequality), which is no relative bound for the vanishing light root.
