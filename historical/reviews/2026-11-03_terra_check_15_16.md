**Correct.**

### [Theorem 15.2](../../lemmas/15_propinquity_collapse.md)

**Gap, closed here.**

The commutativity argument in item 1 is correct. Bounded-Lipschitz lifts of two self-adjoint elements have uniformly bounded norms. Their Lie product maps to zero in every commutative approximating algebra, so Lemma 15.1 forces the Lie product of the two limit elements to vanish.

In item 2, the stated conditions on \(g\) do **not** imply that \(b_n\) stays within \(3/8\) of an integer. They control neighborhoods of the half-integers, but permit values beyond the outermost integers.

We can repair the proof with the same constants. Choose \(g:I\to[0,1]\) such that
\[
g=0\quad\text{on }\bigcup_{j=0}^k[j-\tfrac18,j+\tfrac18],
\qquad
g=1\quad\text{outside }\bigcup_{j=0}^k(j-\tfrac38,j+\tfrac38).
\]
A polynomial \(p\) with \(\|p-g\|_I<1/8\) has \(\|p(a)\|<1/8\). Lemma 15.1 then gives \(\|p(b_n)\|<1/4\) for large \(n\), so the range of \(b_n\) lies in the union of the disjoint open \(3/8\)-neighborhoods. Each connected component of \(X_n\) maps into one neighborhood. At most \(k\) neighborhoods are reached, and a fixed one is missed along a subsequence. The polynomial \(q\) constructed in the note then gives the stated contradiction.

The clopen partition used at the start exists because components and quasicomponents agree in a compact Hausdorff space. Both conclusions follow.

### [Theorem 15.3](../../lemmas/15_propinquity_collapse.md)

**Correct with amendment.** The estimates hold under the standard inner Leibniz definition. The supplied source’s literal definition must be corrected before its tunnels can be checked as stated.

At source lines 602–604 (arXiv:2404.00240v3 LaTeX source), the inner product is required to lie in the domain of \(L_B\), although that domain was defined on self-adjoint elements; the displayed inequality then names \(L_A\). Literally, the first requirement fails for every nonzero complex Hilbert module: \(\langle\omega,i\omega\rangle\) is a nonzero skew-adjoint element. The required condition is
\[
\max\!\left\{L_B(\operatorname{Re}\langle\Theta,\Theta'\rangle),
L_B(\operatorname{Im}\langle\Theta,\Theta'\rangle)\right\}
\le 2\,\mathrm{DN}_P(\Theta)\mathrm{DN}_P(\Theta').
\]
This is the condition in [Latrémolière’s Definition 2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.10843#page=12).

With that correction, the tunnel has the required properties:

- The top seminorm is Leibniz for Jordan and Lie products, has scalar kernel and a closed unit ball. Its centered unit ball is compact. Choosing the midpoint of the spectrum of \(a\) proves the quotient condition onto \(A\); scalar lifts prove it onto \(\mathbb C\). Its extent is at most \(\rho\).
- The base seminorm on \(\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C\) is an L-seminorm with extent at most \(s\).
- The module D-norm dominates the module norm and has a compact unit ball. The displayed modular estimate is valid without assuming \(P\) commutes with \(A\).
- The inner-product calculation proves the stronger complex estimate
  \[
  |\langle\xi,\xi'\rangle-\langle\eta,\eta'\rangle|
  \le 2s\,\mathrm{DN}_P(\xi,\eta)\mathrm{DN}_P(\xi',\eta'),
  \]
  which implies the corrected real-and-imaginary-parts condition.
- The lifts \(\eta=cW\xi\) and \(\xi=cW^*\eta\) attain the two D-norm quotients. The coordinate maps also preserve the left and right actions and inner products.

The pseudo-state choices in both directions are valid and are independent of \(t\). Consequently they bound the **Hausdorff distance between families**, as required, by
\[
g^{-2}+\rho+\Delta+\Delta/\varepsilon .
\]
The conclusion follows by taking \(\varepsilon\) larger than
\(g^{-2}+\rho+\Delta+\sqrt{\Delta}\) and then decreasing \(\rho\). The lower bound correctly includes the cap:
\[
\Lambda_{\rm spec}\ge
\min\!\left\{\frac{\sqrt2}{2},\frac{\delta}{2}\right\}.
\]

### [Theorem 16.2](../../lemmas/16_coarse_truncation.md)

**Correct with amendment.** The Weyl-exponent assertion requires the original exponent to exist.

The graded flip is well defined on the complex tensor product. On each homogeneous summand it is the ordinary conjugate flip times a sign; it is therefore antiunitary. Direct application to homogeneous tensors gives
\[
J^2=1,\qquad J\mathcal D=\mathcal D J,\qquad J\Gamma=\Gamma J.
\]
For even \(a\), \(a^\circ=1\otimes(a^*)^c\). Hence order zero and order one hold, and
\[
[\mathcal D,\pi(a)]=[D_0,a]\otimes1.
\]
The signs \((+,+,+)\) are the usual KO-\(0\) signs.

An eigenbasis of \(D_0\) decomposes \(\mathcal D\) into finite-dimensional invariant blocks. On each block,
\[
\mathcal D^2=D_0^2\otimes1+1\otimes(D_0^c)^2.
\]
This proves essential self-adjointness on the algebraic tensor core and compact resolvent of the closure.

Writing \(N_0(R)\) and \(N_{\mathcal D}(R)\) for absolute-eigenvalue counting functions with multiplicity,
\[
N_0(R/\sqrt2)^2\le N_{\mathcal D}(R)\le N_0(R)^2.
\]
Thus, if \(\lim_{R\to\infty}\log N_0(R)/\log R=p\) exists, the new exponent is \(2p\). Compact resolvent alone does not guarantee such a limit: eigenvalue multiplicities can be chosen to make the logarithmic ratio oscillate.

## Part 2. Interpretation

### (i) Unbroken symmetry and the Standard Model

**Error, for the unrestricted claim.** A metric spectral triple excludes nonscalar elements of its represented algebra that commute with \(D\). That does not exclude every symmetry implemented on its Hilbert space.

For a counterexample within the spectral-propinquity domain, let \(H=M_2(\mathbb C)\) with Hilbert–Schmidt inner product, let \(A=M_2(\mathbb C)\) act by left multiplication, and let
\[
D(x)=x-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(x)I.
\]
If \([D,L_a]=0\), applying the commutator to \(I\) gives \(a\) scalar. Finite dimensionality then makes this a metric spectral triple. Yet every conjugation \(x\mapsto uxu^*\), \(u\in SU(2)\), commutes with \(D\). With \(Jx=x^*\), these are the transformations \(L_uJL_uJ^{-1}\). This example fails order one, which spectral propinquity itself does not require.

A narrower statement does hold for a **finite real first-order** triple. If \(uJuJ^{-1}\) commutes with \(D\), order one and \(JD=\varepsilon'DJ\) imply that \(a=[D,u]u^*\) commutes with \(u\). Thus
\[
u^nDu^{-n}=D-na.
\]
Since \(D\) is bounded, \(a=0\). If the triple is metric, \(u\) is scalar and the induced transformation is trivial.

The Standard Model finite triple is outside the metric class. In the usual construction, the color action commutes with the finite Dirac operator; independently, the nonscalar subalgebra \(C_F\) is explicitly required to commute with it in [Chamseddine–Connes–Marcolli, §2.6](https://www.math.fsu.edu/~marcolli/SMpaper.pdf#page=12). The constant copy of such an element also obstructs the almost-commutative product. The original algebra is a real algebra, so applying a framework formulated for complex \(C^*\)-algebras also requires specifying a complexification.

### (ii) Morita equivalence

**Error, as an argument that removes the scalar-algebra restriction.** The Morita equivalence
\(C(K)\sim C(K,M_n(\mathbb C))\) is correct, but Morita equivalence does not make the two algebras isomorphic or identify their spectral propinquity limits. Theorem 15.2 remains a restriction on limits of the whole **scalar** algebra \(C(K)\).

A vector bundle \(E\) can carry matrix-valued connections while \(C(K)\) still acts by scalar multiplication on sections. Replacing the represented algebra by \(\operatorname{End}(E)\) is an additional change of data. It may be a useful enlargement of T0, but it is not a consequence of the presence of \(E\).

The product-collapse example in Farsi–Latrémolière (arXiv:2404.00240v3 LaTeX source) concerns an already noncommutative finite algebra \(B\) in \(B\otimes C(K_\ell)\). Its finite triple must be metric, and the limiting Hilbert space has the additional factor \(\ker D_K\), which must be nonzero. A one-dimensional kernel recovers the stated finite multiplicity. The example does not produce a noncommutative whole-algebra limit from scalar parent algebras.

### (iii) The geometry of the doubled parent

**Correct with amendment.**

For a compact even-dimensional spin manifold, an appropriate charge-conjugation identification of \(\overline{H_0}\) with \(H_0\) identifies the displayed \(\mathcal D\) with the product Dirac operator on \(X\times X\). The grading may acquire an overall sign under that identification. The real structure in Lemma 16 covers the exchange of factors and is not the usual pointwise spin real structure.

The spectral triple used in the construction represents only
\(C(X)\otimes1\). It is therefore a product-manifold Dirac operator equipped with a restricted observable algebra. If “commutative Kaluza–Klein data” includes that restriction and the flip real structure, the description is accurate. If it means the ordinary spin triple of the total manifold with its full function algebra, or a finite-rank spinor twist over \(X\), it is too broad. With the full product algebra, the generated compression is \(A_F\otimes\overline{A_F}\), and the flip generally fails order zero when \(A_F\) is noncommutative.

### (iv) What Lemma 16 advances

**Correct with amendment.**

For every finite even representation \((A,V,d,\gamma)\), the construction
\[
V\longmapsto V\otimes\overline V,\qquad
d\longmapsto d\otimes1+\gamma\otimes d^c
\]
gives a real bimodule satisfying order zero and order one. It uses no pre-existing finite real structure. Consequently, those two order conditions follow formally from the chosen tensor-square representation; the construction does not recover an arbitrary prescribed finite \(H,D,J\), or their multiplicities.

The examples supply more than that formal observation. In particular, the \(\mathbb{CP}^3\) kernel example gives a simple tower-decoupling family under the note’s generated-compression rule. Put
\[
\mathcal D_\ell=\ell^{-1}\mathcal D
\]
on the doubled parent. Its kernel is \(V\otimes\overline V\), its complementary gap grows like \(1/\ell\), and compression followed by algebra generation gives the constant finite data
\[
A_F=M_4(\mathbb C),\qquad \dim H_F=16,\qquad D_F=0.
\]
After adding the stated Clifford point, the dimension is \(32\) and the KO-\(6\) signs hold. Here order one is automatic because the finite Dirac operator vanishes. The whole parent triple has scalar algebra in its spectral-propinquity limit, consistently with Lemma 15.

Thus Lemma 16 advances T0 for a specified truncation procedure, including a noncommutative finite algebra and an explicit bimodule. It does not establish the target under every meaning of ordinary Kaluza–Klein data: the parent algebra is restricted, the real structure flips factors, and algebra generation is an additional operation. For T0b, it gives examples with the requested signs and first-order relations, but leaves the desired algebra and representation, orientability, nonzero mass data, classification, and the beyond-first-order and twisted conditions unestablished. The Clifford point also fails the finite orientability condition described above. The assessment is right to reject an obstruction inferred from one metric; its description of a “concrete origin” should retain these qualifications.
