## A2. Proposition 47.1

Verdict: Correct.

- K_T(z) = i T z is tangent to the sphere: Re<z, i T z> = Re(i <z, T z>) = 0, since <z, T z> is real for Hermitian T. Its component along the unit vertical vector i z is Re<i z, i T z> = <z, T z>, and the horizontal part has squared round norm |T z|^2 - <z, T z>^2. Hence g_eta(K_T, K_T') = Re<T z, T' z> + (eta^2 - 1) <z, T z><z, T' z>, as stated.
- The fourth moment: for a standard complex Gaussian vector in C^n, E[z_a conj(z_b) z_c conj(z_d)] = delta_ab delta_cd + delta_ad delta_cb, and E|z|^4 = n(n + 1); since the radius and the direction are independent, the sphere moment is (delta_ab delta_cd + delta_ad delta_cb)/(n(n + 1)), as the lemma uses. Then int <T z, T' z> = tr(T T')/3, and int <z, T z><z, T' z> = sum T_ij T'_kl E[conj(z_i) z_j conj(z_k) z_l] = (tr T tr T' + tr(T T'))/12. Both were reproduced by Monte Carlo (relative deviation below 10^-3), the second also for matrices with trace.

## B3. Theorem 48.3

**Item 1.** Correct. Through the evaluation representation on l^2(O) (x) Sigma_6, the fibre at O_1 has real dimension 24 with a centre of dimension 3, which is M_2(C + H). The corner at one point has dimension 6 with a centre of dimension 3, which is C + H. The fibre at O_2 has dimension 36 with a centre of dimension 4, which is M_3(C + C).

One point should be recorded. The corner at a point of O_1 is a hereditary subalgebra of the fibre, the compression by a function equal to 1 at that point and 0 at the other, while on the second sheet the theorem uses the quotient M_3(C) itself. With corners on both sheets the algebra would be C + H + C, and with full fibres on both sheets M_2(C) + M_2(H) + M_3(C). Suggested addition to item 1: "The first sheet uses the corner at one point of O_1 and the second the full quotient at O_2; this is a choice, like the choice of characters."

**Item 2.** Correct, with one wording error. The proof of Corollary 16.8 uses arbitrary even complex-linear operators. C + H, acting by a character on C and by left multiplication on C^2 = H, consists of such operators inside C + M_2(C). So the KO-6 signs, order zero, order one and the nonzero one-forms hold for the real subalgebra, and the use of the formal result is legitimate. G = (1, -1) is central in C + H. J Gamma = -Gamma J holds: J Gamma (x, y) = (-G y^dagger, x^dagger G) and Gamma J (x, y) = (G y^dagger, -x^dagger G). The orientability relation needs only G in A_{F,X} and 1 in M_3(C). On minimal projections e = (a_e, b_e), the intersection form is <e, f> = tr(G a_e) tr(b_f) - tr(b_e) tr(G a_f). With tr(G a) = 1, -2, 0 and tr b = 0, 0, 1 for 1_C, 1_H and e_11, it is [[0, 0, 1], [0, 0, -2], [-1, 2, 0]], skew of rank 2, the corpus form. All of this was also checked numerically on the 18 states with random algebra elements: J^2 = 1, J D = D J, J Gamma = -Gamma J, D Gamma = -Gamma D, b° = J b* J^{-1}, order zero, order one, a nonzero one-form, Gamma = pi(z c) c°, and the form from traces over a basis.

The wording error is in the proof: "C + H is a real form of the complex algebra C + M_2(C)". H is a real form of M_2(C). C is not a real form of C, since C (x)_R C = C + C. C + H has real dimension 6, while a real form of C + M_2(C) has real dimension 5. Replacement: "C + H is a real *-subalgebra of the complex algebra C + M_2(C) of Theorem 45.2, acting on the same V_X, whose complex span is C + M_2(C)".

**Item 3.**

(a) Error in the last sentence: "At the fixed point the spinorial condition selects C + C + M_2(C), three summands over the complex numbers, which is the sheet lemma 45 used." (b) "The spinorial condition selects the first sheet" overreads. The spinorial condition fixes the algebra on each orbit type. Taking O_1 as the first sheet is a choice made with the target C + H in view. The orbit types of W on X, with the spinorial parts of the lifted stabilizer algebras, are:

## B4. The spinorial quotient

The definition is consistent. u_{-1} is central in B-tilde, because -1 is central in W-tilde and acts trivially on X. ## New insights

1. **W-tilde = S_3^tw-lift x Z_4, and the scope of rs_N20.** The obstruction rs_N20, that no homomorphic lift exists at the transposition fixed point, holds for S_3 and for every subgroup of W that contains a transposition without the inversion. The twisted copy S_3^tw = {(sigma, iota^{sgn sigma})}, isomorphic to S_3, acts on the spinors of T^6 by an honest representation. The reason is that tau iota is the rotation by pi on the four-dimensional subspace (n (x) R^2) + T E, and its lift squares to +1, as in the T^4/Z_2 orbifold. This was verified in Cl(6) (B1). Direction: an untwisted spin action by S_3^tw is available for item (b) of Remark 45.3, on an orbit structure different from that of W. Under S_3^tw the stabilizer of a point of O_1 is A_3.
2. **The whole spinor fibre at O_1 carries one quark generation.** Sigma_4 restricted to Dic_3 is chi_1 + chi_3 + V_f. On it C acts by (lambda, conj(lambda)) on chi_1 + chi_3 and H acts on V_f, with the two parts in opposite chiralities of the A-directions. This is the action of C + H on the right-handed singlets (lambda on one, conj(lambda) on the other) and on the left-handed doublet of one quark generation in the spectral Standard Model; which singlet carries lambda is a convention not rechecked here. We took V_X = Sigma_4, G = (1, 1, -1, -1), D_v with v a 2 x 2 matrix, and V_Y = C^3 with M_3(C). Corollary 16.8 then gives a 24-state bimodule over C + H + M_3(C), with M_3(C) acting on the right of Hom(C^3, V_X), which is the bimodule form of the quark sector of one generation. It satisfies the KO-6 signs, order zero, order one, nonzero one-forms and the orientability relation (checked numerically). Its intersection form on 1_C, 1_H and e_11 is [[0, 0, 2], [0, 0, -2], [-2, 2, 0]], of rank 2. The corpus's 18-state bimodule is its restriction to one character. A lead for the leptons: a sheet with algebra C acting on C, for instance the corner of an orbit with stabilizer <tau> or <iota>, whose spinorial algebra is C. Untested.
3. **The grading from the geometry.** The chirality e_1 e_2 e_3 e_4 of the A-directions lies in the image of R[Dic_3] on Sigma_6 (checked numerically), and on the first sheet it equals G = (1, -1) up to sign. So the input G of Theorem 48.3 is the A-chirality restricted to the stabilizer algebra. The full chirality of T^6 is outside that image, since Dic_3 acts trivially on the spinors of E.
4. **The orbit types are forced by the target.** By the table in B3 (b), H occurs only at stabilizer S_3 and matrix size 3 only at stabilizer <tau, iota>. This is the positive form of "the spinorial condition as selection rule".
5. **Lemma 47: the round point is source-independent.** By Schur's lemma, 3/8 at eta = 1 holds for any SO(6)-invariant contribution to the gauge kinetic terms. The eta-dependence, the range (3/32, infinity) and the monotonicity belong to the Einstein term alone, as the Ricci-weighted example in A4 shows. Direction: the round point is the one point of the family where the normalization test of T18 is independent of what holds the shape.
6. **Lemma 47: the charge unit is the superconformal R-charge.** c = 2/3 puts the holomorphic 3-form of C^3 at charge 2 and the singlet Killing spinor at charge 1, which is the normalization of Barnes, Gorbatov, Intriligator and Wright, and 3/8 = tau_SU(3)/tau_RR for N = 4 SYM (A5). Their methods compute such ratios on other Sasaki-Einstein Y_5 with flavour symmetry, which gives further geometric data for candidate 5 of T18. This is a lead.
7. **Lemma 47: the general rule asked in the results file.** At any enhanced point where su(3) + u(1) sits in a simple algebra g, the ratio is tr_V T^2 / tr_V Q^2 for any representation V of g. So the matching value 3/8 appears exactly when the embedding gives Q the SU(5) index, tr Q^2 = (8/3) tr T^2 in every representation. The round S^5 has this index because Q on the 4 is the SU(5) charge on (d, d, d, e^+). The question in "What it leaves open" thus reduces to an index computation for each geometric family.
