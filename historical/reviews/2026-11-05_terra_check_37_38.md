1. **ERROR — Proposition 10.1.** The nonlinear map and radius formula are correct, but the assertion that the linear dictionary holds “exactly when \(z\) is constant” needs qualification.

   Polchinski–Witten equations (14) and (15) give, by taking the ratio,

   \[
   \lambda_k=\frac{W(x_k)}{2W(L)},\qquad
   \theta_k=\pi\lambda_k=\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{W(x_k)}{W(L)}.
   \]

   Equation (16), with the piecewise-linear profile normalized to slope \(8-N(x)\), gives

   \[
   R_h^2=\frac34
   \frac{\int_0^L h^{1/3}\,dx}
        {\left(\int_0^L h^{-1/3}\,dx\right)^2},
   \quad L=2\pi.
   \]

   The distinction between the two profile normalizations matters: the physical function in \(e^\phi=z_{\rm phys}^{-5/6}\) is
   \(z_{\rm phys}=(3C\mu_8/\sqrt2)h\). Polchinski–Witten set **heterotic** \(\alpha'_h=2\); they keep the type-I′ \(\alpha'_{I'}\) separate.

   **Counterexample to the stated “exactly when”:** put fifteen branes at \(0\) and one at \(L\). The two brane positions obey the linear dictionary exactly, since their angles are \(0\) and \(\pi/2\), although \(h'=-7\) in the bulk. The nearest correct statement is that the map is linear **as a function of every \(x\)** exactly when the profile is constant. The proof should also use the brane-to-brane mass differences in equations (14)–(15); a string ending on the wall is not an available physical state for every configuration.

2. **AMENDMENT — Proposition 10.2.** The formulas, endpoint values, and the lower bound at fixed Wilson angle check out.

   Replace:

   > “In units of the string length, \(R_*/\sqrt{\alpha'}\) lies in \([0.935,1]\). In this chamber, \(R\geq R_*\).”

   with:

   > “In heterotic string units, \(R_*/\sqrt{\alpha'_h}\in[\sqrt{7/8},1]\). At each fixed Wilson angle, a nonnegative far-wall value of the normalized profile gives \(R_h\geq R_*(\theta)\).”

   An independent algebraic check is \(R_*^2=2-(\theta/\pi)^2\), equivalent to the displayed \(\rho\)-formula. More generally, put \(A=h(0)^{2/3}\), \(T=h(x_t)^{2/3}\), \(E=h(L)^{2/3}\), and \(a=\theta/\pi\). Direct integration gives

   \[
   R_h^2=2-a^2+\frac{28E}{8A-T-7E},
   \]

   which proves the bound. The critical point uses the classical profile at divergent coupling.

   A Python 3.14.4/SciPy 1.16.3 calculation gives \(\theta=0.585918469\) and \(R_h^2=1.965216392\) at \(x_t/L=1/2\), agreeing with the reported rounded values.

3. **ERROR — Proposition 10.3.** The source identifies a particular heterotic BPS momentum state with a type-I′ winding state. Identifying the interval-parent tower with those string states requires the proposed string embedding; equality of the spectra \(n/R\) alone does not establish the identification. Bergman–Gaberdiel–Lifschytz also show that Wilson-line charges shift the momentum-to-winding dictionary.

   The proof then extends flat-background radius and coupling formulas beyond their stated domain. The source explicitly restricts its simple equations (1.2)–(1.3) to eight D8-branes at each wall. In the \(15+1\) background, the metric and coupling vary. The applicable mass relation instead contains the Weyl factor and the integral

   \[
   \frac1{R_h}=\frac{D}{\pi\alpha'_{I'}}
       \int_0^L\Omega^2(x)\,dx,
   \qquad
   L_p=\int_0^L\Omega(x)\,dx.
   \]

   The flat relations \(R_I L_p=\pi\alpha'_{I'}\) and
   \(\ell L_p=\pi^2\alpha'_{I'}\sqrt{\lambda_h}\) hold under the stated constant-dilaton and frame identifications. Equality of two lengths at a self-dual value does not identify the walls of the two descriptions. The large-\(R_h\), fixed-\(\lambda_h\) heterotic decompactification is a meaningful conditional reading, but the exact flat formulas do not prove it throughout the \(15+1\) chamber.

5. **ERROR — Proposition 10.5 and the following inference.** The large-radius estimate is correct after defining the normalization and the denominator in “relative variation.” For example, let \(B=\min h>0\). Then \(0\leq h-B\leq16\pi\), so

   \[
   R_h^2=\frac{3B}{8\pi}+O(1),\qquad
   \frac{\max h-\min h}{B}\leq\frac{16\pi}{B}
      \sim\frac6{R_h^2}.
   \]

   This is an **upper bound**. In the \(8+8\) profile the variation is zero. The bound implies that differences of the dilaton along the interval tend to zero; a finite limiting value requires control of \(C\). Since \(z_{\rm phys}\propto C h\), a path can have \(R_h\to\infty\) while the coupling diverges uniformly.

   The proof breaks at “So the corrections to any potential from the gradient are powers of \(\alpha'/l^2\).” Small variation alone gives no regular expansion of an effective potential. As a mathematical example, a correction proportional to the square root of the relative variation has a different power. A regular, differentiable expansion in the relevant small parameter is an additional hypothesis, as is the frame identification relating \(l\) to \(R_h\). The estimate guides a test of gradient effects; it does not by itself determine them.

6. **ERROR — Proposition 10.6.** Item 3 has a counterexample to the stated deduction. Let

   \[
   G_0(\theta)=\theta^6,\qquad G_1(\theta)=\theta^2.
   \]

   The potential is even in \(\theta\), vanishes at \(\theta=0\) for every \(l\), and has \(a_0=a_1=b=0\). With \(\theta=lm\),

   \[
   V=m^6l^2+\frac{\alpha'm^2}{l^4}.
   \]

   It has a strict minimum at

   \[
   l^6=\frac{2\alpha'}{m^4},\qquad
   l\asymp m^{-2/3},\qquad lm\to0.
   \]

   Thus the stationarity equation can balance powers of \(\theta\) against powers of \(s=\alpha'/l^2\); it does not generally reduce to an \(m\)-independent root. This is a counterexample to the mathematical inference from the displayed assumptions. It does not construct a supersymmetric field theory producing this potential.

   The nearest correct statements are:

   - If \(a_0\ne0\), and the joint expansion and its derivatives are controlled near \((\theta,s)=(0,0)\), there is no stationary joint branch with \(\theta,s\to0\).
   - For the **truncated** potential \(b m^2/l^2+a_1\alpha'/l^6\), item 2 has the stated minimum. With sufficiently regular higher terms, the formula is its leading asymptotic, not generally an exact equality.
   - If \(a_0=a_1=0\) and \(b\ne0\), regular higher terms cannot balance the leading \(\theta^2\) term in that joint limit. The case \(b=0\) needs the higher terms.

10. **ERROR — Theorem 11.3 as a consequence of the displayed spectral data.** The local zero-mode calculation is correct **after** specifying the usual gauge action and the Yang–Mills boundary variational problem. The stated representation alone does not supply the claimed gauge group: for the nonunital corner action,

    \[
    \pi(u)\,\widehat J\pi(u)\widehat J^{-1}=0.
    \]

    A unital completion such as that in item 9, or an independently specified gauge action, repairs this gap.

    At the trivial background, with the positive flat Yang–Mills action, unrestricted allowed endpoint variations, and no additional wall terms, the scalar zero-mode space before quotienting by residual four-dimensional gauge transformations is

    \[
    (\mathfrak k_0+\mathfrak k_l)^\perp,
    \]

    and the vector zero-mode space is \(\mathfrak k_0\cap\mathfrak k_l\). For two \(U(15)\) walls this gives **210 real scalar** and **225 real vector** components. The action supplies the quadratic form and the natural boundary conditions; order one alone does not supply the scalar kinetic term. The complementary Dirichlet/Neumann conditions for \(A_x\) require a compatible gauge fixing.

    The global double coset also needs a component qualification. If \(K_0\) or \(K_l\) contains reflections, it does not act separately on \(SO(30)\). The endpoint pair must satisfy \(\det g(0)=\det g(l)\). For nonaligned walls, the zero-mode statements should be given as subspaces; the principal-angle formula \(kl=n\pi\pm\phi\) for the displayed two-dimensional example is correct. Nontrivial holonomy can change the zero-mode spaces.

11. **ERROR — Proposition 11.4 and the “Reading.”** The matrix identities and compression formula check out for a bounded constant perturbation, with the window condition of Theorem 22.1 and \(\Lambda\) self-adjoint and real for \(j\). In particular,

    \[
    S=[\Lambda,a],\qquad
    B_{ij}\longmapsto
    -\frac{1-\cos(l(m_i-m_j))}{l(m_i-m_j)}B_{ij},
    \]

    with the continuous value zero at equal masses. The leading term is
    \(-\tfrac l2(m_i-m_j)B_{ij}\).

    The proof breaks when this perturbation is identified with an allowed inner one-form of the **displayed nonunital algebra at nonzero mass**. For invertible \(M\), every algebra element has \(f(l)=0\); consequently every coefficient \(\sum f_i g_i'\) of a one-form also vanishes at \(l\). A nonzero constant \(a_{\rm tot}\) therefore does not arise from those one-forms. Also, \(f_{\rm tot}\) in the Reading is not an element of the represented corner algebra. A unital completion and a corresponding construction of the real one-form can repair the issue; the compression identity itself remains a valid operator calculation.

    For distinct flavour masses, the commutator has the stated image and kernel as vector spaces: the antitriplet kernel consists of **five colour-antitriplet multiplets**, and the image has counts \((3,6,1)\). Distinct fixed masses break flavour \(SU(5)\), so the map is not an \(SU(5)\)-equivariant map between the named representations unless the mass matrix is treated as a spurion. The alternative wall condition on the full gauge unitaries gives the stated unequal-mass scalar directions, but is an additional boundary construction. For universal mass, the commutator vanishes, as claimed.
